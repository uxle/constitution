# Section 21.5 — Protection of Journalists

> **Part 21 — Media and Information** · Articles 21.5.1 – 21.5.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section protects journalists against violence and intimidation, secures source protection in every proceeding, and shields journalists' digital security against intrusion.*

## Article 21.5.1 — Protection from Violence and Intimidation

1. The State shall protect journalists against violence, threats, harassment, and intimidation connected with their work, consistent with Article 1.3.1.
2. An offense committed against a journalist because of their journalism shall be treated as an aggravating circumstance.
3. Investigations of attacks on journalists shall be expeditious, thorough, and independent, and their outcomes shall be made public.

## Article 21.5.2 — Source Protection in Proceedings

1. The protection of sources under Article 21.1.2 applies in every proceeding, civil, criminal, or administrative, before any authority.
2. An order compelling disclosure may issue only on the strict judicial test of Article 21.1.2, using the least intrusive means.

## Article 21.5.3 — Digital Security of Journalists

1. Devices, accounts, and communications of journalists shall be protected against unlawful intrusion; access to them requires a judicial warrant under the standards of Article 1.5.5, consistent with Article 1.13.3.
2. Spyware and comparable intrusive tools shall not be deployed against journalists save under a judicial warrant based on individualized suspicion of a grave crime, with prompt notification of the journalist and subsequent review.

## Article 21.5.4 — Access and Safety Measures

1. Accreditation schemes for journalists shall be content-neutral and may not be used to exclude critical voices from public events or places.
2. Protective measures may be provided to journalists at risk, on their request, under a risk assessment published in general terms.

## Design Note

*Journalists are the visible targets when powerful actors prefer intimidation to argument, so the State's protective duty is matched by aggravation of motive-driven offenses and mandatory public reporting of investigations. Restating source protection across all proceedings prevents the end-run of retrieving from a civil or administrative file what a criminal court would not compel. The digital rules answer modern spyware markets, under which the phone in a journalist's pocket is the new stakeout: only a warrant, only suspicion, and never silence.*
