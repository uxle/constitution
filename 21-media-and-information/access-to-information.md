# Section 21.3 — Access to Information

> **Part 21 — Media and Information** · Articles 21.3.1 – 21.3.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section states the right of every person to information held by public bodies, makes open data the default posture of the State, and fixes the narrow limits of the right; procedures lie in Part 27.*

## Article 21.3.1 — Right of Access

1. Every person has the right of access to information held by public bodies, on request and without stating reasons, subject only to Article 21.3.3.
2. Access shall be granted within a reasonable time fixed by law and at no or reasonable cost, consistent with Article 1.3.2.

## Article 21.3.2 — Open-Data Default

1. Public bodies shall publish information proactively in open, machine-readable formats that permit reuse, subject to privacy and security safeguards.
2. Open publication is the rule; individual requests are the residual channel. Request procedures, timelines, exemptions, and appeals are governed by Part 27.

## Article 21.3.3 — Limits on Access

1. Access to information may be limited only where necessary to protect privacy, public security, or another legitimate aim recognized by law, and only so long as necessary, consistent with Article 1.1.4.
2. The burden of justifying a refusal lies on the body refusing.
3. Every refusal shall be reasoned and may be appealed to the Information Commission and thereafter to a court, consistent with Article 20.7.2.

## Article 21.3.4 — Records and Documentation

1. Public bodies shall create, maintain, and document records of their activities in a manner that makes this right effective.
2. Destruction or concealment of records to defeat access is prohibited and shall be sanctioned by law.

## Design Note

*The right of access turns the abstract promise of an informed citizenry into an individual, enforceable claim, and this Section deliberately pairs it with an open-data default: the best freedom-of-information law is one fewer request because the data was already published. Placing the burden of justification on the refusing authority inverts the practice of regimes where secrecy needs no reason. The records-management duty closes the quiet loophole by which the right exists on paper while the files no longer exist.*
