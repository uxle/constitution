# Section 21.2 — Public Broadcasting

> **Part 21 — Media and Information** · Articles 21.2.1 – 21.2.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes an independent public broadcaster with a universal service mandate, pluralist governance, editorial freedom, and funding insulated from political interference.*

## Article 21.2.1 — Mandate and Universal Service

1. A public broadcaster shall provide a universal service of news, education, culture, and public affairs, accessible to all persons in the territory of the Republic, including through accessible formats for persons with disabilities.
2. The universal service shall reach the whole territory of the Republic and be provided free at the point of use.

## Article 21.2.2 — Independent Governance

1. The governing body of the public broadcaster shall be appointed through an open, pluralist process insulated from the executive; terms are fixed and staggered, and members may be removed only for cause by a qualified majority.
2. Holders of political office and officials of political parties are ineligible to serve on the governing body, consistent with Article 0.5.4.

## Article 21.2.3 — Pluralism and Editorial Independence

1. The public broadcaster shall present news fairly and reflect the diversity of opinion in society.
2. Neither the State nor any public authority may direct or interfere with programming decisions; election-related allocation of airtime shall follow rules fixed by law.
3. Programming shall serve the whole public, including linguistic and cultural communities within society.

## Article 21.2.4 — Insulated Funding

1. Funding shall be fixed by a multi-year statutory formula or charge, adequate to the universal service mandate, and may not be altered so as to influence editorial decisions.
2. The accounts of the public broadcaster shall be audited by the Office of the Auditor-General and published, consistent with Article 20.5.3.

## Design Note

*Public broadcasting earns its place in a constitution only if its governance and money are armored against the governments of the day, since a state station that answers to the incumbent is propaganda with a license fee. Staggered terms, cause-only removal, and ineligibility of party officials mirror the design used for the Electoral Commission. The multi-year funding formula, audited and published, is the single most decisive protection: the power to squeeze a budget is the power to schedule the news.*
