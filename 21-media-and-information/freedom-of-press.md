# Section 21.1 — Freedom of the Press

> **Part 21 — Media and Information** · Articles 21.1.1 – 21.1.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the institutional freedoms of the media: no licensing or censorship, protection of sources, judicial control of newsroom searches, and protection against strategic lawsuits.*

## Article 21.1.1 — No Licensing or Censorship

1. The press and all media are free; censorship is prohibited, and no license or authorization shall be required as a condition of practicing journalism or operating a medium, consistent with Article 1.3.2.
2. Editorial decisions rest solely with the editorial staff; no organ of the State may direct, suspend, or close a medium save by a final order of a court based on law, consistent with Article 1.3.4.

## Article 21.1.2 — Protection of Sources

1. Journalists and media staff shall not be compelled to disclose information identifying a source.
2. Disclosure may be ordered only by a court, only where essential to prevent a serious crime or to defend a person against a grave accusation, and only after weighing the public interest in the free flow of information; the least intrusive available means shall be used.

## Article 21.1.3 — Newsrooms and Materials

1. Premises, devices, and materials of media shall not be searched, seized, or copied except for grave offenses, under a judicial order that specifies what may be taken and why.
2. Material that identifies or is reasonably likely to identify a source may not be seized under any order, consistent with Article 21.1.2.

## Article 21.1.4 — Protection against Strategic Lawsuits

1. A civil action brought principally to silence participation in public debate may be dismissed at an early stage by a court on a showing that it lacks merit or that its purpose is suppression; costs shall normally fall on the claimant.
2. Courts shall give such motions priority and shall consider the chilling effect of the action on public debate, consistent with Article 1.3.3.

## Design Note

*Abolishing licensing resurrects the old constitutional settlement that journalism is a right, not a trade permitted by the State; the ban on prior restraint does the rest. Source protection and newsroom protection are the operational core of press freedom, because reporting that cannot be confidential produces no accountability, a point made forcefully in leading international case law on compelled disclosure. Anti-SLAPP protection addresses the modern replacement of censorship with litigation, where the process itself is the punishment.*
