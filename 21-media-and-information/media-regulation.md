# Section 21.4 — Media Regulation

> **Part 21 — Media and Information** · Articles 21.4.1 – 21.4.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section provides an independent media regulator bound by content-neutral rules, safeguards pluralism and ownership transparency, and protects minors without censoring adults.*

## Article 21.4.1 — Independent Regulator

1. An independent media authority shall regulate media services in the manner and within the limits of this Section, consistent with Article 0.5.4.
2. Its members shall be appointed for fixed, staggered terms through an open process; its decisions shall be reasoned, public, and subject to appeal to a court.
3. The authority shall cooperate with other regulators and with the Information Commission where media services process personal data.

## Article 21.4.2 — Content-Neutral Rules

1. Regulatory rules shall be general and content-neutral; they shall not impose licensing of journalists, prior restraint, or any penalty for lawful opinion.
2. No rule shall authorize censorship of lawful content for adult audiences, consistent with Article 1.3.4.
3. Rules shall apply equally to public and to private media services.

## Article 21.4.3 — Pluralism and Ownership Transparency

1. Ownership and control of media shall be transparent; the regulator shall maintain a public register of beneficial owners.
2. Concentrations of media ownership that endanger pluralism may be prevented or reversed by law, subject to judicial review.

## Article 21.4.4 — Protection of Minors and Media Literacy

1. Measures protecting minors, such as scheduling rules, labeling, and access controls, shall be proportionate and shall not operate as general censorship, consistent with Article 1.15.4.
2. The regulator and educational institutions shall promote media literacy among the public.
3. The effectiveness of protection measures shall be reviewed by the regulator at intervals fixed by law.

## Design Note

*The regulator is given teeth but muzzled from the worst uses of regulation: licensing journalism and prior restraint are expressly excluded because they are the instruments censorship always reaches for first. Ownership transparency and concentration rules answer the fact that private gatekeepers can suppress pluralism as effectively as any censor. Protection of minors is treated as a scheduling and labeling problem rather than a licensing problem, so that the ends are achieved without handing the State an editor's pen.*
