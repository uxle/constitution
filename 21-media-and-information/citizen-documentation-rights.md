# Section 21.6 — Citizen Documentation of Public Institutions (Anti-Capture Amendment)

> **Part 21 — Media and Information** · Articles 21.6.1 – 21.6.3 · Anti-Capture Amendment — added outside the original numbered sequence

*This Section extends a narrow, specific protection beyond registered journalists (Article 21.5) to any person who documents the condition of publicly funded institutions, closing a route by which criticism of public service delivery is recast as a criminal offense against the institution rather than addressed on its merits.*

**Article 21.6.1 (Protected Documentation).** Recording, photographing, or otherwise documenting the physical condition or service delivery of a publicly funded institution — including a school, hospital, or government office — from a place the person is lawfully present, is protected expression under Article 1.3 (Freedom of Expression) and shall not, by itself, constitute grounds for a criminal complaint.

**Article 21.6.2 (Bar on Reputational-Harm-Only Complaints).** A criminal complaint alleging that such documentation is false or misleading must identify a specific, falsifiable factual claim of fabrication or manipulation. A complaint asserting only reputational harm to the institution, without such a claim, shall be summarily dismissed and shall not proceed to arrest or detention of the person who created the documentation.

**Article 21.6.3 (No Blanket Entry Bars).** An administrative order barring members of the public from entering or recording within a publicly funded institution shall be content-neutral, time-limited, and shall not apply to persons entitled by law or relationship — such as a parent, guardian, or beneficiary — to be present in that institution.

*Design Note: The protection in Article 21.5 runs to journalists as such. The pattern this Amendment answers is broader — an ordinary person, a parent, or a local resident who documents a failing public service and is met not with a fix to the service but with a criminal complaint against the documentation. Article 21.6.2's specificity requirement is the operative safeguard: it does not prevent legitimate complaints about genuinely fabricated content, but it prevents "this makes us look bad" from functioning as a criminal charge.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.3 (Freedom of Expression); Article 21.5 (Protection of Journalists); Article 27.2 (Freedom of Information); Part 36 (Media Integrity).
