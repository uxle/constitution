# Section 6.5 — Election Observers

> **Part 06 — Elections and Referendums** · Articles 6.5.1 – 6.5.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the observation of elections and referendums by accredited domestic and international observers, their access to all stages, and their rights and duties.*

## Article 6.5.1 — Right of Observation

1. Domestic and international observers may observe all elections and referendums of the Republic.
2. Accreditation shall be granted by the Electoral Commission on uniform, published criteria and shall not be refused on arbitrary or discriminatory grounds; refusal shall state reasons.
3. Observation serves the public interest in the integrity of the electoral process and shall not be made dependent on the approval of any other organ of the State.

## Article 6.5.2 — Accreditation and Access

1. Accredited observers shall have access to all stages of the electoral process, including voter registration, the campaign period, polling, counting, tabulation, and the resolution of complaints.
2. Observers shall have access to the information and documents necessary for observation, subject to the secrecy of the ballot and to legitimate security requirements fixed by law.

## Article 6.5.3 — Rights and Duties of Observers

1. Observers may attend all proceedings, take notes, record procedures as permitted by law, and publish their findings freely.
2. Observers shall remain impartial, shall not interfere with the process, shall not campaign or display party symbols, and shall respect the secrecy of the ballot and the law.
3. A report of serious irregularity submitted by an observer shall be recorded by the Electoral Commission and addressed under Article 6.6.1 (Complaints to the Electoral Commission).

## Article 6.5.4 — Observer Codes of Conduct

1. The Electoral Commission shall adopt codes of conduct for observers, covering impartiality, non-interference, and transparency of methods.
2. Accreditation may be withdrawn only for a serious violation of the code, by a reasoned decision subject to review.

## Design Note

*Observation supplies independent evidence where neither the electorate nor the courts can be present at once, and international accreditation brings a comparative benchmark of good practice. Uniform criteria for accreditation prevent selective exclusion, while binding duties keep observers from becoming participants in the contest they watch. Withdrawal of accreditation is confined to proven misconduct so that unfavorable reporting can never be punished as such.*
