# Section 6.7 — Referendums

> **Part 06 — Elections and Referendums** · Articles 6.7.1 – 6.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs national referendums: the routes by which they are triggered, their subject-matter limits, the mandatory referendum on entrenched amendments, campaign rules, and binding effect.*

## Article 6.7.1 — Routes to a National Referendum

1. The Parliament may provide for a national referendum by law adopted by a majority of the members of each House.
2. A national referendum shall also be held on a citizens' initiative supported by the signatures of at least five percent of the persons registered to vote, verified by the Electoral Commission.
3. The law shall fix the procedure for initiatives, including the period for signature collection and verification.

## Article 6.7.2 — Subject-Matter Limits

1. No referendum shall be held on the annual budget, on taxation, or on the declaration or termination of a state of emergency.
2. No referendum may propose a measure that derogates from the rights listed in Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights).
3. A referendum shall not be used to evade the amendment thresholds of this Constitution, including the protection of entrenched provisions under Section 30.5 (Protected Provisions).

## Article 6.7.3 — Mandatory Referendum on Entrenched Amendments

1. A constitutional amendment affecting entrenched provisions shall not enter into force unless approved by a majority of the voters voting in a national referendum held for that purpose.
2. The referendum shall be combined with a general election where possible, unless the urgency of the matter justifies a separate vote.

## Article 6.7.4 — Campaign Rules

1. Public authorities shall not campaign for a particular outcome and shall ensure that supporters and opponents enjoy equal treatment in the use of public facilities.
2. The rules on campaign finance applicable to elections, including disclosure and oversight, apply to referendum campaigns under Article 3.7.5 (Oversight and Sanctions).
3. The Electoral Commission shall publish neutral explanatory material on each question and shall ensure fair access to public service media for both sides.

## Article 6.7.5 — Binding Effect and Review

1. A referendum result within its constitutional competence is binding and shall be implemented by the competent organs without delay.
2. The validity of a referendum may be challenged and finally decided by the Constitutional Court under Article 6.6.2 (Appeal to the Constitutional Court).
3. A referendum result unconstitutional in its content shall not be implemented; its conformity is reviewed under Article 5.6.3 (Abstract Review).

## Design Note

*Three trigger routes balance parliamentary control with citizen initiative, while the mandatory referendum on entrenched amendments gives the double lock of the entrenchment scheme its popular legitimacy. Subject-matter exclusions protect the budget and rights from majoritarian moments in which concentrated costs and diffuse benefits are hard for a single vote to adjudicate. Binding effect makes the referendum a genuine instrument of popular sovereignty rather than an advisory ornament.*
