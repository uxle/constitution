# Section 6.4 — Election Security

> **Part 06 — Elections and Referendums** · Articles 6.4.1 – 6.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section secures the technical integrity of elections: the paper record, chain of custody, cybersecurity of electoral technology, post-election audits, and publication of counting records.*

## Article 6.4.1 — Paper Record Requirement

1. Every vote cast shall be recorded on a paper ballot or shall produce a verified paper trail that the voter can confirm and that is protected from alteration.
2. The paper record shall be the authoritative basis for determining and recounting results.
3. Voting technologies may be used only where they support, and never where they displace, the paper record.

## Article 6.4.2 — Chain of Custody and Tamper-Evidence

1. Ballots, ballot boxes, memory media, and related election materials shall be secured by tamper-evident procedures from issuance through counting to retention.
2. Custody shall be documented at every transfer with the signatures of the transferring and receiving custodians and, where practicable, of witnesses.
3. Election materials shall be retained for the period fixed by law and shall be destroyed only after dispute procedures are exhausted.

## Article 6.4.3 — Cybersecurity Standards

1. Electoral information systems shall meet cybersecurity standards fixed by law, including certification before use, separation from public networks, and continuous monitoring.
2. The Electoral Commission shall test systems before each election and shall publish a summary of the results.
3. An incident affecting the integrity of electoral data shall be investigated, documented, and disclosed without undue delay.

## Article 6.4.4 — Post-Election Audits

1. Before certification of results, the Electoral Commission shall audit a sample of counting units by hand comparison against the paper record.
2. The audit shall be capable of detecting a wrong outcome and shall be expanded where discrepancies appear.
3. Audit results and their reconciliation with machine counts shall be published.

## Article 6.4.5 — Public Counting and Publication

1. Counting shall be public, and party and candidate witnesses and accredited observers shall be entitled to attend under Article 6.5.2 (Accreditation and Access).
2. Counting records shall be published together with the certified results in the manner provided by Article 3.4.4 (Administration and Validity).

## Design Note

*Paper ballots or verified paper trails keep the decisive evidence in a form that citizens, parties, and courts can examine without expert intermediaries. A risk-limiting audit before certification converts trust in technology into statistical verification of the outcome itself. Chain-of-custody documentation makes tampering not only difficult but detectable after the fact, which matters more than perfect prevention.*
