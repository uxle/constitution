# Section 6.3 — Voting

> **Part 06 — Elections and Referendums** · Articles 6.3.1 – 6.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs the conduct of voting: the secret ballot, accessibility, special voting arrangements, time to vote, and assistance to voters.*

## Article 6.3.1 — The Secret Ballot

1. Voting shall be by secret ballot in a booth or other place ensuring that no one can observe how a voter votes.
2. No person may obtain information about how a voter has voted, and a voter shall not be obliged to disclose it.
3. Exceptions necessary to render assistance shall be recorded without revealing the content of the vote.

## Article 6.3.2 — Accessibility of Voting

1. Polling stations shall be accessible to voters with disabilities, and the Electoral Commission shall provide reasonable accommodations suited to different forms of disability.
2. The law shall ensure that distance, terrain, and similar obstacles do not deprive remote communities of effective access to the poll.

## Article 6.3.3 — Special Voting Arrangements

1. The law shall provide for absentee, postal, and early voting, and for voting in hospitals, care facilities, and places of detention.
2. Detained persons retain the right to vote on the same terms as other voters.
3. Special voting arrangements shall observe the same standards of secrecy, security, and counting as ordinary voting.

## Article 6.3.4 — Time to Vote

1. Election day shall be fixed by law so that every voter has a reasonable opportunity to vote.
2. An employer shall grant an employee leave sufficient to vote without loss of pay and shall not penalize the exercise of the vote in any manner.

## Article 6.3.5 — Assistance to Voters

1. A voter who by reason of disability or illiteracy cannot vote without help may be assisted by a person of the voter's own choice or by an official of the polling station.
2. Assistance shall be rendered only in the presence of the voter, shall preserve secrecy, and shall express the voter's will alone.
3. The Electoral Commission shall prescribe uniform rules for assistance and shall train polling-station officials accordingly.

## Design Note

*The secret ballot is treated as an active guarantee rather than a mere absence of observers, so the State must provide booths, enforce non-disclosure, and record assistance without recording votes. Extending identical integrity standards to postal, early, and in-facility voting prevents convenience channels from becoming weaker gates into the count. Paid time to vote converts a formal right into a practical one for shift and hourly workers, for whom polling hours are otherwise a barrier.*
