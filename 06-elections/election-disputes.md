# Section 6.6 — Election Disputes

> **Part 06 — Elections and Referendums** · Articles 6.6.1 – 6.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section organizes the resolution of electoral disputes: complaints to the Electoral Commission, appeal to the Constitutional Court, expedited timelines, and remedies.*

## Article 6.6.1 — Complaints to the Electoral Commission

1. Any voter, candidate, political party, or accredited observer may complain to the Electoral Commission of an irregularity affecting an election or referendum.
2. The Commission shall decide complaints within short statutory periods, shall state reasons, and shall correct within the process what can be corrected.
3. Decisions of the Commission under this Article may be appealed as provided in Article 3.5.4 (Accountability and Cooperation).

## Article 6.6.2 — Appeal to the Constitutional Court

1. The validity of an election or referendum, and the decisions of the Electoral Commission on complaints, may be brought before the Constitutional Court, which decides finally under Article 5.3.3 (Exclusive Jurisdiction).
2. The persons and bodies entitled to complain under Article 6.6.1 shall have standing to appeal.

## Article 6.6.3 — Expedited Timelines

1. The law shall fix strict deadlines for filing, decision, and appeal, so that disputes are resolved before the certification of results or, exceptionally, before the newly elected body first meets.
2. Timelines shall balance thorough adjudication with the certainty of the result that democratic continuity requires.

## Article 6.6.4 — Remedies

1. The Electoral Commission and the Constitutional Court may order a recount, the correction of records, the repetition of counting, or a new election in the area affected.
2. A result may be annulled only where an irregularity occurred that was capable of affecting its outcome.
3. Remedies shall be proportionate to the violation and shall restore, so far as possible, the will of the voters.

## Article 6.6.5 — Finality and Publication

1. Decisions on electoral disputes shall be reasoned, published, and binding under Article 5.3.6 (Effect of Decisions).
2. Where an election is repeated, the organs of the State shall take the measures necessary to hold it without delay.

## Design Note

*Two tiers serve two functions: the Commission corrects operational defects quickly, while the Constitutional Court provides final and independent judgment on validity, matching the allocation in Article 5.3.3. Expedited timelines manage the tension between thorough adjudication and the prompt, certain result that a democracy needs to keep functioning. Annulment is reserved for outcome-capable irregularities so that technical errors do not disenfranchise an entire electorate.*

**Cross-references:** Article 5.3.3 (Exclusive Jurisdiction); Article 3.4.4 (Administration and Validity); Article 5.3.6 (Effect of Decisions).
