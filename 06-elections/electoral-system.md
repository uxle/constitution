# Section 6.1 — The Electoral System

> **Part 06 — Elections and Referendums** · Articles 6.1.1 – 6.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section defines the electoral system of the Republic: equality of the vote, the proportional method for the Lower House, the allocation of seats, and boundary principles.*

## Article 6.1.1 — Formal Equality of the Vote

1. Every voter possesses one vote of equal formal weight in every election and referendum, and every valid vote shall be counted equally.
2. The vote shall be general, equal, free, secret, and direct.
3. Public authority shall protect the free formation of the voter's will and the free expression of that will against every improper influence.

## Article 6.1.2 — The Electoral System for the Lower House

1. Members of the Lower House shall be elected by proportional representation with open candidate lists in Province-wide constituencies, consistent with Article 3.2.2 (Electoral Principle).
2. Each voter may cast a party vote and express a preference for one candidate on that list.
3. Seats shall be allocated among parties in proportion to the valid votes cast nationwide.
4. A party shall participate in the allocation of seats only if it obtains at least three percent of the valid national votes cast.

## Article 6.1.3 — Allocation of Seats

1. Seats shall be allocated on the basis of the Droop quota: valid votes in a constituency divided by its seats plus one.
2. A party shall receive as many seats as its votes cover whole quotas; remaining seats shall be distributed by the method fixed by law.
3. Candidates shall be declared elected in the order of the preferences they have received; where preferences do not distinguish, the party's list order shall govern.
4. A vacant seat shall be filled under Article 3.2.4 (Vacancies).

## Article 6.1.4 — Constituency Boundaries

1. The Lower House shall be elected in constituencies corresponding to the Provinces.
2. Constituencies for other elections shall be delimited so that each vote carries comparable weight; boundaries shall respect population equality, geographic contiguity, and the coherence of communities.
3. Boundaries shall be determined by an independent boundary commission, published with reasons, and reviewed at regular intervals under Section 30.2 (Electoral Districts).

## Article 6.1.5 — Stability of Electoral Law

1. The details of the electoral system shall be regulated by law.
2. Electoral law shall serve equal suffrage and shall not advantage particular parties or candidates.
3. A material change to electoral law shall not enter into force within one year before an ordinary election, unless the Constitutional Court finds that the change is necessary to remedy a violation of equal suffrage.

## Design Note

*Open-list proportional representation allocates seats strictly in proportion to votes while leaving the choice among candidates to the electorate, not to party headquarters. The three percent threshold screens out micro-parties without excluding substantial minorities, and the one-year stability rule prevents an incumbent majority from rewriting the rules mid-game. Boundary principles are stated in the Constitution so that districting cannot become a partisan instrument.*
