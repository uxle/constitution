# Section 32.2 — Animal Cruelty: Crimes and Punishments

## Articles

**Article 32.2.1 (Classification).** Animal cruelty is classified by severity: (I) neglect through ignorance — mandatory education order and civil fine; (II) reckless neglect causing suffering — fine and custody-license revocation; (III) intentional cruelty or torture — criminal conviction, custodial sentence proportionate to harm, and lifetime ban on animal ownership; (IV) organized cruelty for entertainment or profit (illegal fighting rings, poaching syndicates) — treated as organized crime under 38 (Penal Code Extended), with asset confiscation.

**Article 32.2.2 (Aggravating Factors).** Cruelty committed in the presence of a child, for profit, or as part of organized activity is an aggravating factor increasing the sentence within its class.

**Article 32.2.3 (Rehabilitation Consistency).** Consistent with the Constitution's rehabilitation-first sentencing philosophy (38-penal-code-extended/restorative-justice.md), Class I and II offenders are directed first toward education and supervised correction; Class III and IV offenders face custodial punishment because the harm and intent are severe.

*Design Note: The tiered structure mirrors how this Constitution treats human-harm crimes — proportionate to intent and harm, rehabilitative where possible, firm where the conduct is deliberate or organized — so that "low violence" as a constitutional value extends to violence against animals, not only between humans.*

**Cross-references:** 32.1, 38-penal-code-extended, 14-criminal-law/sentencing.md


## Implementation Detail

**Article 32.2.4 (Sentencing Guide).** Class I: mandatory education order plus fine up to one month's median regional income. Class II: fine up to six months' median income and licence revocation of up to five years. Class III: custodial sentence of six months to five years plus lifetime ownership ban. Class IV: treated under Part 38 organized-crime sentencing, minimum three years, with full asset confiscation under Part 37.
