# Section 32.4 — Meat Consumption and Dietary Customary Law

## Articles

**Article 32.4.1 (World Floor, Local Custom).** This Constitution does not impose a single world-wide diet. Every region retains authority to regulate meat consumption, slaughter methods, and dietary custom according to its own culture, religion, and ecology, subject only to the universal welfare floor in Article 32.1.2(d) — that any killing for food must be humane.

**Article 32.4.2 (Religious and Cultural Methods).** Slaughter methods tied to religious or cultural practice are lawful where they meet independently verified humane standards; where a traditional method and the humane-standard requirement are in tension, the region's relevant religious and veterinary authorities jointly certify a compliant protocol rather than the practice being banned outright.

**Article 32.4.3 (Sustainable Limits).** Regions may set sustainability-based limits on meat production (e.g., land, water, and emissions ceilings) under Part 13 and Part 24, separate from and in addition to welfare regulation.

**Article 32.4.4 (No Discrimination Against Diet).** No person may be denied employment, education, or public service on the basis of their dietary choice, whether vegetarian, vegan, or a diet including meat, consistent with Article 1.2 (Equality).

**Article 32.4.5 (Labelling and Transparency).** All meat and animal-product packaging must disclose welfare-standard compliance (e.g., "humane-certified"), giving consumers informed choice rather than the state dictating what they eat.

*Design Note: This is the clause that resolves the tension in a genuinely worldwide constitution between a universal animal-welfare floor and enormous cultural and religious diversity around food. The answer chosen here is subsidiarity: one floor (no cruelty), many ceilings (local custom decides everything above it).*

**Cross-references:** 32.1, 24-agriculture/farmers-rights.md, 1.4, 0.2 (Founding Principle: local custom respected above the floor)


## Implementation Detail

**Article 32.4.6 (Certification Renewal).** Humane-slaughter protocol certification under Article 32.4.2 is reviewed and renewed every three years by the joint religious-and-veterinary authority named in that Article.

**Article 32.4.7 (Labelling Standard).** Humane-certified labelling (32.4.5) must appear on packaging in a font no smaller than the product name, verified by random audit at a minimum rate of 5% of certified producers per year.
