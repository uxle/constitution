# Section 32.5 — World-Level Protected Species List

## Articles

**Article 32.5.1 (World Register).** A World Register of Protected Species is maintained by the Environmental Authority, listing species at risk of extinction across any region, updated at least every two years by independent scientific review.

**Article 32.5.2 (Baseline Prohibitions).** Hunting, trading, or trafficking a listed species is a crime under this Constitution regardless of regional law, enforced alongside regional wildlife authorities.

**Article 32.5.3 (Habitat Linkage).** Listing a species automatically triggers heightened protection, under Article 32.3, for the ecosystems that sustain it.

**Article 32.5.4 (Community Compensation).** Where protection restricts a traditional livelihood (e.g., subsistence hunting), the state provides a transition and compensation program rather than leaving affected communities unsupported.

*Design Note: A worldwide register only works if it is scientific and updated, not political and frozen — hence independent review rather than legislative vote on the list itself.*

**Cross-references:** 32.1, 32.3, 13-environment/biodiversity.md, 24-agriculture/fisheries.md


## Implementation Detail

**Article 32.5.5 (Review Cycle).** The World Register is reviewed at minimum every two years, with emergency additions possible within 30 days on a documented, scientifically supported extinction-risk finding.
