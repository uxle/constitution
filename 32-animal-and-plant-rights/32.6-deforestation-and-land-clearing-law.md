# Section 32.6 — Deforestation and Land-Clearing Law

## Articles

**Article 32.6.1 (Permit Requirement).** Clearing of forest above a defined threshold size requires a permit following the impact review of Article 32.3.2; unpermitted clearing is a crime under 38-penal-code-extended/environmental-crime.md.

**Article 32.6.2 (Net-Zero Clearing Target).** Each region sets a binding schedule toward net-zero permitted deforestation, reported annually to the World Environmental Authority.

**Article 32.6.3 (Illegal Logging as Organized Crime).** Large-scale illegal logging, where linked to organized trafficking, is prosecuted as organized crime under Part 38, with confiscation of equipment, land-use rights, and proceeds.

*Design Note: Placing this alongside animal and plant rights, rather than burying it purely in environmental policy, reflects the Constitution's view that a forest's destruction is a rights violation against the ecosystem itself (32.3.1), not merely a regulatory infraction.*

**Cross-references:** 32.3, 38-penal-code-extended/environmental-crime.md, 13-environment/forests.md


## Implementation Detail

**Article 32.6.4 (Threshold Definition).** The permit threshold in Article 32.6.1 is set at 2 hectares of contiguous forest cover, reviewed every five years against updated ecological data.

**Article 32.6.5 (Net-Zero Milestones).** Each region's net-zero schedule (32.6.2) must show measurable reduction milestones at years 3, 6, and 10 from adoption, reported to the World Environmental Authority annually.
