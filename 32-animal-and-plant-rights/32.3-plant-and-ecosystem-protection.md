# Section 32.3 — Plant and Ecosystem Protection

## Articles

**Article 32.3.1 (Ecological Standing).** Significant ecosystems — old-growth forests, wetlands, coral reefs, and other officially designated systems — are recognized as holding protected legal status, and may be represented in court by a designated Guardian body when threatened, even absent a human plaintiff.

**Article 32.3.2 (Duty Not to Destroy Without Process).** No forest, wetland, or other designated ecosystem may be cleared, drained, or fundamentally altered without an independent environmental impact review and a public objection period.

**Article 32.3.3 (Restoration Duty).** Where destruction is unavoidable for a permitted public purpose, the responsible party bears a restoration or equivalent-offset duty, verified by the Environmental Authority.

**Article 32.3.4 (Traditional and Indigenous Stewardship).** Where indigenous or traditional communities have long-standing stewardship practices for a given ecosystem, those practices are given weight in the impact review and, where consistent with the welfare floor, preserved.

*Design Note: This Article gives plants and ecosystems a form of legal voice without pretending a tree can sue on its own — a designated Guardian body does that job, similar to how a guardian ad litem represents a child's interests in court.*

## Implementation Detail

**Article 32.3.5 (Review Period).** The independent environmental impact review (32.3.2) must be completed within 120 days of filing, with a public objection period of no fewer than 30 days before any final decision.

**Article 32.3.6 (Restoration Ratio).** Where destruction is authorized, the restoration or offset duty (32.3.3) is set at a minimum 3:1 ratio of restored-or-protected equivalent habitat to habitat destroyed.
