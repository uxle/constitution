# Section 14.9 — National Security Offenses

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.9.1 – 14.9.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section defines treason, espionage, and sabotage narrowly, and forbids any criminalization of journalism, dissent, or whistleblowing.*

## Article 14.9.1 — Narrow Definitions

1. Treason, espionage, and sabotage shall be defined by law only as concrete acts, committed with intent to prejudice the existence, security, or constitutional order of the Republic, in a manner that goes beyond the preparation of ordinary offenses.
2. The advocacy of ideas, the criticism of constitutional institutions, and the peaceful pursuit of political aims shall not fall within these definitions.

## Article 14.9.2 — Protected Activities

1. No provision of the Code or of any other law shall be construed to criminalize journalism, dissent, protest, academic work, artistic expression, or whistleblowing, in conformity with Article 21.5.2 (No Punishment for Journalism) and Article 27.5.1 (Protected Disclosures).
2. The publication of truthful information in the public interest shall not constitute espionage or a related offense merely because it is embarrassing to public authorities.

## Article 14.9.3 — Conformity with Free Expression

1. Offenses under this section shall be construed in conformity with freedom of expression under Article 1.3.1 (Freedom of Expression and Opinion).
2. A restriction on the disclosure of official information is lawful only where the information is genuinely capable of causing serious prejudice to national security and the disclosure is not justified by an overriding public interest.

## Article 14.9.4 — Relationship to Counter-Terrorism

1. Offenses of terrorism are defined separately under Article 18.7.1 (Definition) and are subject to the same principles of this Part.

## Article 14.9.5 — Review

1. Parliament shall review the definitions and penalties of this section at regular intervals, with public hearings, to verify that they remain narrow, necessary, and proportionate.

## Design Note

*National-security offenses are the historical instrument by which constitutions have been hollowed out, so this section writes the protection of journalists, dissenters, and whistleblowers directly into criminal law rather than leaving it to interpretation. The narrow-definitions clause demands concrete acts and specific intent, excluding treason by speech. The public-interest test in Article 14.9.3 gives courts a workable standard when the State invokes secrecy, and the mandatory periodic review ensures these provisions are re-justified rather than merely renewed.*

**Cross-references:** Article 21.5.2 (No Punishment for Journalism); Article 27.5.1 (Protected Disclosures); Article 1.3.1 (Freedom of Expression and Opinion).
