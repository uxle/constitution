# Section 14.7 — Cybercrime

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.7.1 – 14.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section defines offenses against computer systems and data and subjects every cybercrime investigative power to judicial warrant and ordinary evidence rules.*

## Article 14.7.1 — Illegal Access

1. The Code shall punish the intentional access to an information system without right.
2. Access affecting critical infrastructure or producing significant harm shall constitute an aggravated form of the offense.

## Article 14.7.2 — Data and System Interference

1. The Code shall punish the intentional damaging, deletion, deterioration, alteration, or suppression of data without right, and the serious hindering of the functioning of an information system.
2. The creation and distribution of tools adapted for such offenses shall be punishable only where committed with the intent that they be used for the commission of an offense, so that legitimate security research remains lawful.

## Article 14.7.3 — Computer-Related Offenses

1. Fraud, forgery, and extortion committed through information systems constitute offenses under the general provisions applicable to those offenses, read with the equal protection of digital property.

## Article 14.7.4 — Investigative Powers

1. Expedited preservation of data, production orders, and searches of information systems may be ordered only by judicial decision, in accordance with the warrant requirement of Article 15.2.1 (Warrant Requirement).
2. Real-time collection of traffic data and interception of content shall be subject to the judicial-authorization safeguards of Article 1.5.5 (Judicial Authorization of Intrusions).
3. There shall be no generalized monitoring of the population's communications.

## Article 14.7.5 — Evidence Standards

1. Digital evidence shall meet the authentication and integrity standards of Article 17.8.2 (Authentication and Integrity), and the defense shall have access to independent forensic verification.

## Design Note

*Cybercrime provisions are kept strictly separate from cyber-investigation powers, so that the seriousness of the threat cannot be used as an argument for surveillance shortcuts. The dual-use tools clause protects security research by demanding criminal intent, acknowledging that the same code secures systems and breaks them. Routing all digital evidence through the authentication standards of the digital-law Part keeps the constitutional boundary between investigation capability and evidentiary reliability visible to courts.*

**Cross-references:** Article 15.2.1 (Warrant Requirement); Article 17.8.2 (Authentication and Integrity); Article 1.5.5 (Judicial Authorization of Intrusions).
