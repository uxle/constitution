# Section 14.6 — Corruption

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.6.1 – 14.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section criminalizes bribery, trading in influence, embezzlement, and illicit enrichment, with liability for officials and private parties alike and no immunity for corruption offenses.*

## Article 14.6.1 — Bribery

1. The Code shall punish both the giving and the receiving of an undue advantage intended to induce or reward the improper performance of a public function.
2. The Code shall likewise punish bribery between private persons where it distorts honest business conduct.

## Article 14.6.2 — Trading in Influence

1. The Code shall punish promising, offering, or requesting an undue advantage by a person who claims to be able to exert an improper influence over a decision of a public authority, whether or not the influence is actually exercised.

## Article 14.6.3 — Embezzlement and Illicit Enrichment

1. The Code shall punish the misappropriation of funds or property entrusted to a public official or held by a public body.
2. The Code may punish a public official whose wealth increases significantly and lawfully cannot be explained by legitimate income, subject to the procedural safeguards of Article 27.3.2 (Content and Verification).

## Article 14.6.4 — Liability and Immunity

1. Officials and private participants are equally liable, and the official position of the offender shall be treated as an aggravating circumstance, never as a ground of mitigation.
2. No immunity may bar the prosecution of corruption offenses; procedural immunities may be lifted for this purpose by the authority competent under the law.

## Article 14.6.5 — Enforcement

1. The Anti-Corruption Commission shall investigate corruption offenses within its mandate under Article 20.3.2 (Mandate) and shall prosecute jointly with the Public Prosecution Service.
2. The recovery of proceeds of corruption shall follow the rules on the recovery of proceeds of crime.

## Design Note

*Criminal law completes the anti-corruption architecture built in Part 27 and Part 20: disclosure, ethics, and commission powers deter, and this section punishes. Punishing both sides of the bribe closes the defense that corruption is consented to by all participants. Illicit enrichment is included with a deliberately explicit anchor to the verification safeguards for asset declarations, acknowledging its contested character while preserving a tool against officials whose lifestyle cannot be explained. The no-immunity rule ensures that rank provides no shield for the offenses that most corrode public trust.*

**Cross-references:** Article 20.3.2 (Mandate); Article 27.3.2 (Content and Verification); Article 14.5.4 (Recovery of Proceeds).
