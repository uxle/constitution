# Section 14.8 — Organized Crime

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.8.1 – 14.8.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section addresses criminal associations through conduct-based liability, judicially authorized infiltration powers, and asset forfeiture with strong safeguards.*

## Article 14.8.1 — Criminal Association

1. Participation in an association of three or more persons, structured over time and established for the commission of serious offenses, constitutes an offense when accompanied by an intentional act of participation.
2. Mere membership by belief, family tie, or social association, without an act of participation, shall not be punishable.

## Article 14.8.2 — Infiltration and Controlled Deliveries

1. Undercover operations and controlled deliveries may be authorized only by prior judicial decision, for limited periods, and only where the evidence sought cannot reasonably be obtained by less intrusive means.
2. The authorization shall specify the permitted acts, and agents shall not incite the commission of an offense that the person would not otherwise have committed.

## Article 14.8.3 — Asset Forfeiture

1. Proceeds and instrumentalities of organized crime shall be subject to forfeiture by judicial decision, consistent with the protection of property under Article 1.8.1 (Right to Property).
2. The law may provide for extended forfeiture of assets whose lawful origin cannot be plausibly demonstrated by a person convicted within a criminal association, subject to judicial control and to the protection of bona fide third parties.

## Article 14.8.4 — Collaborators of Justice

1. The law shall provide for the protection of persons who cooperate with the prosecution of organized crime.
2. Sentencing reduction for cooperation shall remain within the limits of proportionality set by Article 14.2.5 (Proportionality).

## Article 14.8.5 — Review of Special Techniques

1. The use of special investigative techniques under this section shall be reported annually to Parliament in a form that protects ongoing investigations.

## Design Note

*Organized-crime law risks trading away legality and property guarantees in the name of effectiveness, so every special device here is fenced by a judicial gatekeeper and a conduct requirement. The act-of-participation rule rejects pure status offenses, keeping association liability anchored in what the accused did. Extended forfeiture is admitted as the one asymmetric tool, since criminal enterprises compartmentalize ownership, but only after conviction and under judicial control, with third-party bona fide interests protected.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.8.1 (Right to Property); Article 14.2.5 (Proportionality); Article 15.4.1 (Lawfulness and Exclusion).
