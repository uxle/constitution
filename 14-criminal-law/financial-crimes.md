# Section 14.5 — Financial Crimes

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.5.1 – 14.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section defines offenses against the integrity of public funds and markets, establishes liability of legal persons, and provides for the recovery of criminal proceeds.*

## Article 14.5.1 — Fraud Against the Public

1. The Code shall punish the obtaining of public funds, subsidies, or benefits by deception, and the fraudulent evasion of obligations owed to the public purse.
2. The scale of the deception and the abuse of official positions shall be treated as aggravating circumstances.

## Article 14.5.2 — Market Manipulation

1. The Code shall punish insider dealing and the manipulation of financial markets, protecting market integrity under Article 9.7.3 (Market Integrity).

## Article 14.5.3 — Money Laundering

1. The Code shall punish the conversion, transfer, concealment, or acquisition of property known to be the proceeds of crime.
2. Money laundering shall be punishable irrespective of the type of predicate offense.
3. The law shall require reporting of suspicious transactions by financial institutions and by designated professions.

## Article 14.5.4 — Recovery of Proceeds

1. Conviction of a financial or property offense shall entail the confiscation of proceeds and instrumentalities of the offense, consistent with the protection of property under Article 1.8.1 (Right to Property).
2. Confiscated value shall be applied first to the satisfaction of victims' claims.

## Article 14.5.5 — Liability of Legal Persons

1. Legal persons shall be liable for offenses committed for their benefit by persons exercising control or acting in a leading position within them.
2. Sanctions on legal persons shall include fines proportionate to the advantage gained and may include exclusion from public procurement and public funding; dissolution shall be reserved for grave and repeated offenses.

## Design Note

*Financial crime is defined around the abuse of trust in markets and public administration rather than around archaic distinctions between forms of money. The all-offense predicate rule for laundering follows the international standard, preventing laundering law from being narrowed by treating some crimes as acceptable sources. Corporate liability is deliberately sanction-based rather than vicarious: the enterprise answers for abuse of its structures, while confiscation-with-victim-priority keeps recovery focused on restitution rather than treasury enrichment.*
