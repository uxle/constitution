# Section 14.2 — Principles of Criminal Law

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.2.1 – 14.2.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section states the binding principles of criminal law: legality, non-retroactivity with lenity, culpability, individual guilt, proportionality, and double jeopardy.*

## Article 14.2.1 — Legality

1. No one shall be held guilty of an offense on account of conduct that did not constitute a criminal offense under the law at the time it was committed.
2. Criminal law shall be construed strictly, and any ambiguity shall be resolved in favor of the accused.

## Article 14.2.2 — Non-Retroactivity and Lenity

1. No penalty shall be imposed that was not provided for by law at the time of the offense.
2. Where the law in force after the commission of an offense provides for a lighter penalty, the lighter penalty shall apply.

## Article 14.2.3 — Culpability

1. Criminal liability requires culpability, that is, intention or negligence, as defined by law.
2. The law may attach liability to a state of affairs only where the law so expressly provides, only for minor regulatory matters, and never for conduct punishable by imprisonment.

## Article 14.2.4 — Individual Guilt

1. Criminal liability is personal. No person shall be held criminally liable for the conduct of another, and no collective, family, or group liability shall exist.

## Article 14.2.5 — Proportionality

1. The nature and degree of punishment shall be proportionate to the gravity of the offense and the culpability of the offender.
2. Offenses and penalties shall observe the master limitation clause of Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Article 14.2.6 — Double Jeopardy

1. No person shall be tried or punished again for an offense for which that person has already been finally acquitted or convicted.
2. Narrow exceptions for the retrial of finally acquitted persons may be established by law only where grave new evidence of guilt emerges, in conformity with Article 15.7.3 (Retrial and Double Jeopardy).

## Design Note

*The six principles form the constitutional grammar of the entire Part and bind every offense defined in it. Strict construction and the lenity rule protect the citizen against retroactive or expansive interpretation, while the culpability clause prevents the quiet growth of strict liability into serious crime. The double-jeopardy exception is drafted narrowly and by cross-reference, so that the constitutional rule is never softened by judicial construction alone.*

**Cross-references:** Article 15.7.3 (Retrial and Double Jeopardy); Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights); Article 0.4.1 (Legality).
