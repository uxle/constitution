# Section 14.10 — Sentencing

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.10.1 – 14.10.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section governs the purposes and individualization of sentencing, promotes alternatives to imprisonment, subjects life sentences to periodic review, and makes rehabilitation the goal of the penal system.*

## Article 14.10.1 — Purposes of Sentencing

1. Sentencing serves just retribution proportionate to the offense, the protection of the public, reparation toward victims and society, and the rehabilitation of the offender.

## Article 14.10.2 — Individualization

1. Every sentence shall be individualized according to the gravity of the offense, the culpability of the offender, and the personal circumstances relevant to the case.
2. Sentencing courts shall state the reasons for the penalty imposed and for its position within the range provided by law.

## Article 14.10.3 — Alternatives to Imprisonment

1. For offenses of lesser gravity, non-custodial penalties shall be presumed, including fines scaled to income, community service, restorative justice measures, and treatment programs.
2. Custodial sentences shall be reserved for serious offenses or where non-custodial measures have failed or are plainly inadequate.

## Article 14.10.4 — Life Sentences and Review

1. Life imprisonment may be imposed only for the gravest offenses.
2. Every life sentence and every long custodial sentence shall be subject to periodic review by a court, which shall order release where continued detention is no longer justified by the purposes of sentencing under Article 14.10.1 (Purposes of Sentencing).

## Article 14.10.5 — Parole and Rehabilitation

1. The law shall establish a conditional-release system based on individualized assessment of the progress and risk of the person.
2. The penal system shall provide education, work, and treatment programs supporting reintegration, consistent with the rehabilitation mandate of Article 15.8.5 (Reintegration) and the vocational pathways of Article 11.5.1 (Vocational Pathways).

## Design Note

*The section refuses to choose between retribution and rehabilitation by naming both, but its institutional choices lean clearly toward reintegration: a presumption of non-custodial penalties, reviewable life sentences, and a parole system with real criteria. Periodic review of life sentences follows the principle that punishment must remain justifiable over time and that persons can change, a position taken by several apex courts in comparative human-rights jurisprudence. Reasoned individualization gives appeals courts and the public a basis for checking both excessive severity and unexplained leniency.*

**Cross-references:** Article 15.8.5 (Reintegration); Article 11.5.1 (Vocational Pathways); Article 14.2.5 (Proportionality).
