# Section 14.4 — Property Offenses

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.4.1 – 14.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section defines the core property offenses and guarantees that intangible and digital property receive protection equal to tangible property.*

## Article 14.4.1 — Offenses of Taking

1. The Code shall punish the dishonest appropriation of property belonging to another, including theft, robbery, and extortion.
2. Robbery and extortion involving violence or threats shall be punished as offenses against both property and personal liberty.

## Article 14.4.2 — Fraud and Deception

1. The Code shall punish obtaining property, services, or financial advantage by deception, including through false identities, forged instruments, and fraudulent representations.
2. Fraud committed against particularly vulnerable persons or through organized means shall constitute an aggravated form of the offense.

## Article 14.4.3 — Criminal Damage

1. The Code shall punish the destruction of or damage to property belonging to another.
2. Damage endangering the life or safety of persons, including arson, shall be treated as an aggravated form of the offense.
3. Damage of minimal significance may, as the law provides, be reserved to civil remedies.

## Article 14.4.4 — Intangible and Digital Property

1. Intangible property, data, virtual assets, and electronic financial instruments shall receive protection equal to that of tangible property.
2. Unlawful interference with data and systems may additionally constitute offenses under Article 14.7.2 (Data and System Interference).

## Article 14.4.5 — Restitution and Recovery

1. The Code shall provide for restitution to victims of property offenses as a consequence of conviction.
2. Proceeds of property offenses shall be recoverable under the rules on the recovery of proceeds of crime.

## Design Note

*The section keeps the classical offense structure of taking, deception, and damage but modernizes the object of protection: the equal-treatment clause ensures that a stolen database or emptied digital wallet is not treated as a lesser wrong than a stolen bicycle. Linking digital interference to the dedicated cybercrime provisions prevents gaps and double definitions between the two offense groups. Restitution is constitutionalized because the injury of property crime is primarily to the victim's patrimony, and punishment alone does not repair it.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.8.1 (Right to Property); Article 14.7.2 (Data and System Interference); Article 14.5.4 (Recovery of Proceeds).
