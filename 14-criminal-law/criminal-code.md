# Section 14.1 — Criminal Code

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.1.1 – 14.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section mandates a single Criminal Code as the exclusive source of offenses and penalties, with special criminal laws exceptional and subordinate to the Code's general principles.*

## Article 14.1.1 — Codification Mandate

1. Parliament shall enact and maintain a single Criminal Code defining every criminal offense, its elements, and the penalties attached to it.
2. No person shall be prosecuted for conduct that the Code does not define as an offense.

## Article 14.1.2 — Exclusivity of the Code

1. Special criminal laws may be enacted only where the Code expressly so provides, and they shall be interpreted subject to the general principles of the Code.
2. No court may create offenses by analogy or extend an offense beyond its terms.

## Article 14.1.3 — Accessibility

1. The Code shall be written in plain and definite language so that persons of ordinary understanding can know what conduct is prohibited.
2. The definitive text of the Code shall be published promptly and remain permanently available to the public free of charge.

## Article 14.1.4 — Specificity of Penalties

1. The Code shall prescribe the kind and range of penalties for every offense, and no penalty shall be imposed outside that range.
2. Delegated instruments may specify technical elements, such as regulated substances or safety standards, only where the Code expressly identifies the delegation and fixes the maximum penalty; no instrument may create a new offense or aggravate an existing one, consistent with Article 4.6.3 (Delegated Legislation).

## Article 14.1.5 — Maintenance and Review

1. Parliament shall review the Code at regular intervals.
2. Every proposal to create or aggravate an offense shall be accompanied by an assessment of its necessity, proportionality, and expected effects on fundamental rights.

## Design Note

*Criminal law is where State coercion is at its maximum, and so codification is treated as a constitutional duty rather than a drafting preference: one Code, plainly written, free to read, with no offense beyond its terms. The special-laws clause preserves legislative flexibility for urgent regulation while preventing the quiet fragmentation of criminal law into inaccessible statutes. Mandatory rights impact assessments discipline the recurring political temptation to answer public anxiety with new offenses.*
