# Section 14.3 — Offenses Against Persons

> **Part 14 — Criminal Law** · Articles 14.3.1 – 14.3.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section defines the protection of life, bodily integrity, liberty, and dignity, establishes consent-based sexual offenses, and removes limitation periods for grave offenses.*

## Article 14.3.1 — Offenses Against Life

1. The Code shall protect human life through offenses of intentional homicide, with graver forms for homicide committed with particular cruelty or against especially defenseless victims.
2. The death penalty shall not exist, and no penalty may be imposed that violates the prohibition of cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment.

## Article 14.3.2 — Bodily Integrity

1. The Code shall punish assaults on bodily integrity, with the gravity of the offense measured by the seriousness and permanence of the harm.
2. Domestic violence, female genital mutilation, and other forms of gender-based violence constitute offenses, and the law shall treat violence committed by a close relation as an aggravating circumstance.

## Article 14.3.3 — Liberty and Dignity

1. The Code shall punish kidnapping, unlawful coercion, stalking, human trafficking, and forced labor, the last two consistently with the prohibition of forced labor under Article 1.9.3 (Prohibition of Forced Labor).

## Article 14.3.4 — Consent-Based Sexual Offenses

1. Sexual offenses shall be defined by reference to the absence of freely given consent.
2. Consent shall not be inferred from silence, passivity, or the absence of resistance, and no prior or existing relationship shall imply consent.
3. Marriage or a comparable relationship shall not constitute a defense to a sexual offense.

## Article 14.3.5 — Offenses Against Children

1. The Code shall provide special protection for children, including offenses of sexual abuse, exploitation, grooming, and child pornography.
2. The vulnerability of the victim by reason of age or dependency shall be an aggravating circumstance, consistent with the priority of the child's best interests under Article 22.7.2 (Protection from Violence and Exploitation).

## Article 14.3.6 — No Limitation Period

1. No limitation period shall apply to the prosecution of offenses against life, human trafficking, and serious sexual offenses, including offenses against child victims.

## Design Note

*Consent, defined as free and volitional agreement rather than the absence of resistance, is made the organizing element of sexual offenses; the explicit non-inference rules respond to the persistent evidentiary myths that have historically shielded perpetrators. Abolition of the death penalty is stated here because life is the protected interest at stake, and it aligns with the mandatory refusal of surrender under Article 19.6.2 (Mandatory Refusals) where the requesting jurisdiction retains capital punishment. Removing limitation periods for the gravest offenses acknowledges that victims of childhood abuse and trafficking often come forward only decades later.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.9.3 (Prohibition of Forced Labor); Article 22.7.2 (Protection from Violence and Exploitation); Article 14.10.2 (Individualization).
