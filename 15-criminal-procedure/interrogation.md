# Section 15.3 — Interrogation

> **Part 15 — Criminal Procedure** · Articles 15.3.1 – 15.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section disciplines the questioning of suspects: the caution, the presence of counsel, mandatory recording, the prohibition of coercion and inducements, and the exclusion of tainted statements.*

## Article 15.3.1 — The Caution

1. Before questioning begins, the person shall be cautioned that the person is not obliged to answer and that anything said may be used in evidence.
2. Questioning of a person deprived of liberty shall not begin before the person has had the opportunity to speak with counsel.

## Article 15.3.2 — Counsel During Questioning

1. Counsel shall be present throughout the questioning of a person deprived of liberty; the person may waive counsel only expressly, on the record, and after being cautioned.
2. Where the person cannot afford counsel, representation shall be provided at public expense consistent with Article 5.9.3 (Mandatory Criminal Legal Aid).
3. Where the person requests counsel, questioning shall be suspended until counsel is present.

## Article 15.3.3 — Recording

1. Every interrogation of a person deprived of liberty shall be recorded in full by audiovisual means, and the recording shall be preserved until the final conclusion of the case.
2. A statement made during an unrecorded interrogation may not be used in evidence unless the failure to record was not attributable to the authorities or the person consents to its use.
3. The absence of a recording shall weigh against the reliability of any statement offered in evidence.

## Article 15.3.4 — Prohibited Methods

1. Coercion, ill-treatment, threats, inducements, drugs, exhaustion, and any method capable of destroying the person's will or capacity to decide shall not be used at any stage of proceedings.
2. The law shall regulate the duration, rest periods, and conditions of questioning and shall provide for monitoring of compliance.

## Article 15.3.5 — Exclusionary Effect

1. A statement obtained in breach of this Section or of Article 1.7.3 (Rights of the Accused) shall be inadmissible.
2. Evidence derived from an inadmissible statement shall be inadmissible unless the derivation is so remote that the taint is dissipated, as provided by law.

## Design Note

*Mandatory recording is the cheapest safeguard in this Part: it protects the innocent against fabrication, the honest officer against unfounded complaint, and the court against disputes about what was said. The caution-plus-counsel sequence follows the model developed in the case law of the European Court of Human Rights and in the caution doctrine of United States law, with the waiver made express and recorded because a silent waiver is what coercion prefers. Derivative exclusion is calibrated: torture taint is treated as absolute in Section 15.4, while lesser breaches admit a remoteness test.*
