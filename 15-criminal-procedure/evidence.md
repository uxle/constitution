# Section 15.4 — Evidence

> **Part 15 — Criminal Procedure** · Articles 15.4.1 – 15.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section states how evidence is obtained and judged: lawful acquisition, the absolute exclusion of torture-derived material, reliability standards, the chain of custody, and disclosure.*

## Article 15.4.1 — Lawfulness and Exclusion

1. Evidence shall be obtained by lawful means only.
2. Evidence obtained through a serious violation of this Constitution or of the law shall be inadmissible where the violation impaired the reliability of the evidence or the fairness of the trial.
3. Exclusion shall not prevent the independent discovery of the same facts by lawful means.

## Article 15.4.2 — Torture-Derived Evidence

1. A statement or other evidence obtained by torture or by cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment shall be inadmissible for any purpose.
2. Other evidence derived from such a statement shall be inadmissible.
3. No consideration of reliability, gravity, or public security may qualify this Article.

## Article 15.4.3 — Reliability Standards

1. Evidence shall be assessed for authenticity, integrity, and reliability according to standards provided by law; electronic evidence is governed by Section 17.8 (Electronic Evidence).
2. Expert evidence shall be given by qualified and impartial experts, and the method underlying an opinion shall be disclosed and open to challenge.

## Article 15.4.4 — Chain of Custody

1. Exhibits and data shall be documented from seizure to presentation in court, and every transfer shall be recorded.
2. An unexplained gap in the chain of custody shall weigh against the admission or the weight of the exhibit.

## Article 15.4.5 — Disclosure

1. The Public Prosecution Service shall disclose to the defense, in due time to enable the preparation of the defense, all material evidence, including material favorable to the accused.
2. Failure to disclose favorable material shall be remedied by the court, including by adjournment, exclusion of the affected evidence, or discharge.

## Design Note

*The absolute exclusion of torture-derived evidence, including its derivatives, gives constitutional rank to the rule of the United Nations Convention against Torture that such statements shall not be invoked; a regime that admits torture-derived corroboration still purchases torture, so the taint is treated as incurable. Lesser violations are governed by a fairness-and-reliability test rather than automatic exclusion, which keeps the sanction proportionate to the wrong. Disclosure of favorable material stands beside the exclusion rules because a trial can be no fairer than the file the defense actually sees.*
