# Section 15.8 — Rights of Prisoners

> **Part 15 — Criminal Procedure** · Articles 15.8.1 – 15.8.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section protects persons deprived of liberty after conviction: humane treatment, the prohibition of degrading punishment, contact with family and counsel, independent inspection, and rehabilitation.*

## Article 15.8.1 — Humane Treatment

1. Every person deprived of liberty shall be treated humanely and with respect for dignity, consistent with Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights).
2. Conditions of detention shall not aggravate the deprivation of liberty beyond what its lawful purpose requires; accommodation, food, health care, exercise, and outdoor time shall meet the minimum standards provided by law.

## Article 15.8.2 — No Degrading Punishment

1. Corporal punishment, restraint in fixed positions, reduction of food, and collective punishment are prohibited in places of detention.
2. Solitary confinement may be imposed only within the strict limits fixed by law, for the shortest necessary time, and never against minors.
3. Discipline shall be individualized, proportionate, and subject to review before an independent body.

## Article 15.8.3 — Family Contact and Counsel

1. A prisoner shall be entitled to regular contact with family and to confidential communication with counsel and with the courts, subject only to reasonable restrictions required by security.
2. Restrictions shall be reasoned, recorded, and reviewable.

## Article 15.8.4 — Independent Inspection

1. Places of detention shall be inspected regularly by an independent body empowered to enter without notice, to speak with prisoners in private, and to report publicly.
2. Prisoners shall have an effective internal complaints channel and access to the courts.

## Article 15.8.5 — Reintegration

1. The penal system shall prepare every prisoner for release through education, work, treatment, and structured contact with society, consistent with the rehabilitation mandate of Article 14.10.5 (Parole and Rehabilitation).
2. Participation in such programs shall be voluntary and shall not be secured by coercion.
3. A spent criminal record shall not be used to defeat reintegration, as provided by law.

## Design Note

*The Section turns the United Nations Standard Minimum Rules for the Treatment of Prisoners — the Nelson Mandela Rules — from soft law into justiciable commands, and the inspection body borrows the unannounced-visit model of the Optional Protocol to the Convention against Torture. Rehabilitation appears as a duty of the system rather than a hope, with participation kept voluntary so that programs cannot become an instrument of pressure. The record clause answers the quiet life-long punishment that follows release when the file never closes.*
