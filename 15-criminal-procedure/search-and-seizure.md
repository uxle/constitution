# Section 15.2 — Search and Seizure

> **Part 15 — Criminal Procedure** · Articles 15.2.1 – 15.2.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs searches and seizures: warrants with particularity, narrow exigent exceptions, heightened protection for digital data, professional privilege, and self-incrimination.*

## Article 15.2.1 — Warrant Requirement

1. A search or seizure shall be carried out only under a warrant issued in advance by a judge, on the basis of reasonable grounds, and subject to Article 1.5.2 (Inviolability of the Home).
2. A warrantless search or seizure is lawful only where imminent danger to life, imminent destruction of evidence, or immediate flight of a suspect makes prior authorization impossible; the authority shall seek judicial confirmation without delay.

## Article 15.2.2 — Particularity

1. A warrant shall identify the place or person, the objects of the search, and the offense concerned, and shall describe the items to be seized with reasonable particularity.
2. General exploratory warrants are prohibited.
3. A search shall be executed within the period fixed in the warrant, shall be recorded, and the person affected shall receive a copy of the record.

## Article 15.2.3 — Digital Searches

1. Searches of information systems, devices, and accounts require a separate judicial authorization stating the systems concerned, the data categories, and the period covered.
2. Data not covered by the authorization shall be secured and shall be examined only under a further warrant.
3. Interception of communications is governed by Article 1.5.3 (Confidentiality of Communications) and by law.

## Article 15.2.4 — Privilege Against Self-Incrimination

1. No person shall be compelled to make statements or to produce documents or data held for personal use that would incriminate that person, consistent with Article 1.7.3 (Rights of the Accused).
2. The privilege does not extend to existing physical evidence independent of the person's will, as provided by law.

## Article 15.2.5 — Privileged Relationships

1. Seizure shall not extend to materials protected by professional confidentiality between counsel and client, physician and patient, or confessor and believer, or to such other confidential relationships as the law protects.
2. An exception may be established by law only where the material is essential to a serious charge and no less intrusive means remain.

## Article 15.2.6 — Seized Property

1. Seized items shall be inventoried in the presence of the person affected where practicable.
2. Items seized without or beyond lawful authority shall be returned without delay; the law shall regulate retention of items needed as evidence.

## Design Note

*Particularity is the working core of the warrant: a warrant that says "search everything" is a general warrant in disguise, which is why this Section restates the particularity discipline familiar from the Fourth Amendment of the United States Constitution and adds an express rule for digital data. The separate-authorization rule for devices reflects that a phone now holds more private material than a house. Exigent exceptions must be confirmed by a judge promptly, so necessity begins the exception and judicial review ends it.*
