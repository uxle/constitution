# Section 15.7 — Appeals

> **Part 15 — Criminal Procedure** · Articles 15.7.1 – 15.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees appeal as of right in criminal cases, channels constitutional questions to the Constitutional Court, confines retrial to the accused's detriment to narrow grounds, and bars worsening on the accused's appeal.*

## Article 15.7.1 — Appeal as of Right

1. A party may appeal a first-instance judgment on questions of fact and of law to the next higher court.
2. The appeal lies as of right for the prosecution and for the accused; no leave requirement may apply to an appeal against a conviction or a sentence of imprisonment.

## Article 15.7.2 — Scope and Safeguards

1. The appellate court shall review the assessment of evidence, the application of the law, and the sentence.
2. A conviction may be reversed or a sentence varied only after the appellant has been heard; new evidence shall be admitted where it may affect the outcome.

## Article 15.7.3 — Retrial and Double Jeopardy

1. No person shall be tried again for an offense of which the person has been finally acquitted or convicted, consistent with Article 14.2.6 (Double Jeopardy).
2. A retrial to the detriment of a finally acquitted person may be authorized by law only where grave and compelling new evidence of guilt emerges, only within a period fixed by law, and only with the approval of a superior court.

## Article 15.7.4 — Constitutional Questions

1. A question of constitutionality arising in criminal proceedings may be referred to the Constitutional Court as provided by Article 5.3.3 (Exclusive Jurisdiction).
2. A person who has exhausted the ordinary remedies may challenge the final judgment by individual complaint consistent with Article 5.3.4 (Individual Complaints).

## Article 15.7.5 — No Worsening on Appeal

1. On an appeal brought by the accused alone, the appellate court shall not impose a heavier sentence than the judgment under appeal.

## Design Note

*Appeal as of right on facts and law follows the two-instance tradition of continental systems, while the prohibition of worsening on the accused's own appeal — the continental rule against reformatio in peius — keeps the right of appeal from becoming a trap. The retrial exception is drafted as a judicial valve rather than a legislative licence: grave new evidence, a fixed period, and superior-court approval together protect the finality that makes acquittals safe. The constitutional-question route ties the ordinary courts to the Constitutional Court without dismantling the single appeal path.*
