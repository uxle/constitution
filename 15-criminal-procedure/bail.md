# Section 15.5 — Bail and Pre-Trial Detention

> **Part 15 — Criminal Procedure** · Articles 15.5.1 – 15.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs liberty before judgment: the presumption of release, proportionate conditions, prompt periodic review, statutory caps on detention, and a non-punitive remand regime.*

## Article 15.5.1 — Presumption of Release

1. A person awaiting trial shall be presumed entitled to release.
2. Detention pending trial may be ordered only where release would create a concrete risk of flight, of interference with evidence or witnesses, or of serious reoffending, and only where less restrictive means are inadequate to the risk.

## Article 15.5.2 — Conditions of Release

1. Conditions imposed on release shall be proportionate to the risk addressed and shall be the least intrusive available; financial conditions shall be set at a level the person can actually meet.
2. Conditions shall be recorded in writing with reasons and may be varied or lifted on application or on the court's own motion.

## Article 15.5.3 — Prompt Review

1. An order of detention pending trial shall be reviewed by a court at intervals not exceeding thirty days.
2. The detained person may seek earlier review where new facts emerge, and the review shall consider whether the grounds for detention persist.

## Article 15.5.4 — Detention Periods

1. The law shall fix maximum periods of detention pending trial according to the gravity of the offense; detention beyond the statutory maximum shall be unlawful.
2. A person detained beyond the applicable maximum shall be released without delay, without prejudice to the continuation of the trial.

## Article 15.5.5 — Remand Regime

1. A person detained pending trial shall be kept apart from convicted persons and shall be treated as innocent, consistent with Article 1.7.2 (Presumption of Innocence).
2. Remand detention shall not be punitive; access to counsel, family, and health care shall be ensured.

## Design Note

*Bail is drafted around the risk to be managed, not the wealth of the accused; the can-meet rule closes the cash-bail trap that jails the poor while freeing the dangerous, following the just-cause structure of the Canadian Charter's pre-trial detention clause. Fixed review intervals prevent remand from quietly becoming the sentence before any conviction. The separate remand regime gives the presumption of innocence institutional form, because a person treated as guilty in custody has already lost the trial that has not yet happened.*
