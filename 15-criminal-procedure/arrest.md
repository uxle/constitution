# Section 15.1 — Arrest and Judicial Control

> **Part 15 — Criminal Procedure** · Articles 15.1.1 – 15.1.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section regulates arrest and executive deprivation of liberty: the warrant rule, information of rights, the prompt judicial appearance, habeas corpus, and remedies.*

## Article 15.1.1 — Arrest on Warrant

1. No person shall be arrested except under a warrant issued in advance by a judge, on reasonable suspicion, stated with particularity, that the person has committed or is about to commit an offense.
2. A warrantless arrest is lawful only where a person is caught in the act or in immediate flight, or where delay would create an imminent danger to life; every such arrest shall be reported without delay to a judge.
3. The law may define further narrow exceptions for serious offenses; every exception shall be construed strictly.

## Article 15.1.2 — Information of Rights

1. Upon arrest, the person shall be informed promptly, in a language the person understands, of the reasons for the arrest and of the person's rights, including the right to remain silent, to counsel, and to an interpreter.
2. The person shall be enabled to notify a relative of the person's choice and to contact counsel without delay.

## Article 15.1.3 — Prompt Judicial Appearance

1. Every arrested person shall be brought before a judge within forty-eight hours of the arrest.
2. The judge shall review the lawfulness and the necessity of the detention without delay and shall order immediate release unless detention is justified by law.

## Article 15.1.4 — Habeas Corpus

1. Every detained person may at any time challenge the lawfulness of the detention before a court, consistent with Article 1.7.4 (Habeas Corpus and Liberty).
2. The challenge shall be simple, prompt, and free of charge, and no act of the executive may limit it; where the court finds the detention unlawful, it shall order release.

## Article 15.1.5 — Treatment and Custody Record

1. An arrest shall be carried out with the minimum force necessary and with respect for human dignity.
2. Every deprivation of liberty shall be entered in a single custody record stating the time, place, and authority of the arrest and every transfer; the record shall be open to the person, to counsel, to the judge, and to oversight bodies.

## Article 15.1.6 — Remedies for Unlawful Arrest

1. A person unlawfully arrested or detained has the right to compensation.
2. Unlawful conduct in connection with an arrest shall entail disciplinary responsibility and, where culpable, criminal responsibility.

## Design Note

*The forty-eight-hour appearance converts the vague "promptly" of many international texts into an enforceable deadline; comparative codes commonly allow twenty-four to ninety-six hours, and the constitutional text fixes the outer limit so that ordinary legislation cannot stretch it. Habeas corpus is kept free-standing because custody is where legality fails first, a lesson as old as the writ itself. The single custody record gives the deadline an audit trail and the detainee a document instead of a denial.*
