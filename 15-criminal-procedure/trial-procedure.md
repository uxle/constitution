# Section 15.6 — Trial Procedure

> **Part 15 — Criminal Procedure** · Articles 15.6.1 – 15.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section orders the criminal trial: adversarial process, the public hearing, equality of arms, reasoned judgments, and a narrow expedited procedure resting on informed consent.*

## Article 15.6.1 — Adversarial Process

1. The trial shall be adversarial: the prosecution and the defense shall have equal opportunity to present evidence, to examine and cross-examine witnesses, and to address the court.
2. The court shall decide only on the evidence presented and tested in the hearing.

## Article 15.6.2 — Public Trial

1. The trial shall be public, and judgment shall be pronounced in public, consistent with Article 1.7.1 (Right to a Fair Trial).
2. The public may be excluded only by reasoned order, only to protect minors, privacy, or the integrity of justice, and only for the duration strictly necessary.

## Article 15.6.3 — Equality of Arms

1. The court shall ensure that neither party enjoys a procedural advantage by reason of the resources or the official position of that party.
2. Where the defense lacks the means to meet the prosecution's case, the court shall secure the assistance that the interests of justice require, consistent with Article 5.9.1 (Right to Legal Aid).

## Article 15.6.4 — Reasoned Judgments

1. Judgments shall state the evidence accepted and rejected, the reasons for the finding of guilt or acquittal, and the reasons for the sentence.
2. Reasons shall be sufficient to permit effective review on appeal.

## Article 15.6.5 — Expedited Procedure

1. An expedited or simplified procedure may be used only with the informed consent of the accused, given after advice of counsel and on the record.
2. Consent shall not be induced by the threat of a heavier penalty in the ordinary procedure, and the accused may return to the ordinary procedure at any time before judgment.

## Design Note

*The adversarial core is stated as a duty of the court, not merely a privilege of the parties, because inequality of arms is usually structural rather than tactical. Reasoned judgment is treated as a fair-trial guarantee in its own right: it disciplines first-instance fact-finding and makes the appeal system of Section 15.7 meaningful rather than ornamental. The expedited procedure is admitted on consent, counsel, and no inducement, reflecting the comparative consensus that simplified justice is legitimate only where the accused surrenders nothing unknowingly.*
