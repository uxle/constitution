# Section 7.6 — Decentralization

> **Part 07 — Local Government** · Articles 7.6.1 – 7.6.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section declares subsidiarity the governing rule of devolution, provides for periodic devolution reviews, and protects local self-government against abolition.*

## Article 7.6.1 — The Subsidiarity Principle

1. Public functions shall be performed at the lowest level of government that can perform them effectively and efficiently.
2. A higher level may perform a function itself only where the objectives of the function cannot be sufficiently achieved at the lower level, or where scale or external effects justify central action.
3. The level relying on an exception under clause 2 bears the burden of justifying it.

## Article 7.6.2 — Allocation and Review of Functions

1. Functions among Provinces, Municipalities, and the organs of the Republic shall be allocated by law in accordance with Article 7.6.1 (The Subsidiarity Principle).
2. Transfers of functions shall be accompanied by the corresponding resources and shall be adopted after consultation with the tier concerned.
3. Assignments shall be drafted so that responsibilities can be attributed without ambiguity.

## Article 7.6.3 — Periodic Devolution Reviews

1. The Parliament shall review the distribution of functions and resources at intervals of no more than five years, with the participation of the Provinces and Municipalities.
2. The review shall assess the exercise of devolved functions, the adequacy of financing, and the effects of supervision, and its conclusions shall be published.
3. Independent evidence for the review may be commissioned from the Statistics Authority.

## Article 7.6.4 — Protection of Local Self-Government

1. The existence of the Provinces as self-governing units and the right of Municipalities to local self-government shall not be abolished except by constitutional amendment.
2. A law shall not dissolve, suspend, or merge a Province or Municipality as a sanction for the exercise of its lawful competences.
3. The Constitutional Court protects these guarantees under Article 5.3.3 (Exclusive Jurisdiction).

## Design Note

*Subsidiarity is written as an operational rule with a burden of justification, not as a preface, so centralizing statutes must argue rather than assert. Periodic review turns devolution into a living settlement that can follow demography and administrative experience, with evidence supplied by the Statistics Authority. The constitutional-amendment lock answers the historical record of local government abolished or hollowed out by ordinary statute in many real states.*
