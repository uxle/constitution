# Section 7.1 — The Provinces

> **Part 07 — Local Government** · Articles 7.1.1 – 7.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Provinces as the primary subnational units of the Republic, their elected councils, their devolved competences, and supervision limited to legality.*

## Article 7.1.1 — The Provinces

1. The Provinces are the primary subnational units of the Republic and enjoy self-government within the limits of this Constitution.
2. The number, boundaries, and names of the Provinces shall be fixed by law within constitutional principles of population equality and community coherence.
3. The Republic remains a unitary State; devolution allocates the exercise of public functions, not sovereignty.

## Article 7.1.2 — Provincial Councils

1. Each Province shall have a Provincial Council elected by the voters of the Province for a term of five years.
2. The Council shall exercise the devolved competences of the Province, adopt its budget, and supervise its administration.
3. The Council shall elect a presiding officer from among its members and shall establish its own rules of procedure.

## Article 7.1.3 — Devolved Competences

1. The law shall assign to the Provinces the functions that can be effectively and efficiently discharged at that level, applying the subsidiarity principle under Article 7.6.1 (The Subsidiarity Principle).
2. Devolved competences include, in particular, provincial infrastructure, spatial planning, implementation of environmental policy, and the coordination of schools and health services within the framework of national law.
3. Competences not assigned remain with the organs of the Republic; assignments shall be construed in favor of self-government where the text is open.

## Article 7.1.4 — Supervision Limited to Legality

1. Supervision of provincial action shall extend only to conformity with this Constitution and with law.
2. Supervision shall not review the opportunity or merit of provincial decisions and shall use the least intrusive adequate means.
3. Supervisory measures shall state reasons and shall be subject to judicial review; disputes between a Province and the organs of the State are decided by the Constitutional Court under Article 5.3.3 (Exclusive Jurisdiction).

## Article 7.1.5 — Representation of Provincial Interests

1. The Provinces participate in national decision-making through the election of members of the Upper House under Article 3.3.2 (Election by Provincial Councils).
2. The Parliament shall hear the Provinces before adopting legislation affecting their core competences.

## Design Note

*Provinces carry the main devolution because a unitary State can decentralize functions without fragmenting sovereignty, and the Upper House of three members per Province loops provincial interests back into national law-making. Confining supervision to legality, as distinct from review of expediency, preserves genuine political responsibility at the provincial level. Judicial protection before the Constitutional Court turns supervision from a political privilege of the center into a reviewable legal act.*

**Cross-references:** Article 3.3.2 (Election by Provincial Councils); Article 5.3.3 (Exclusive Jurisdiction); Article 7.6.1 (The Subsidiarity Principle).
