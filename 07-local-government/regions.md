# Section 7.2 — Regions

> **Part 07 — Local Government** · Articles 7.2.1 – 7.2.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs Regions as cooperative groupings of Provinces for shared services, with voluntary composition and no separate sovereignty.*

## Article 7.2.1 — Nature of Regions

1. Regions are cooperative associations of Provinces established for the joint performance of shared services.
2. A Region possesses no sovereignty and no independent legislative power; it exercises only the functions conferred on it.
3. Membership of a Region shall be voluntary; a Province may join or leave under the conditions fixed by law and by the founding agreement.

## Article 7.2.2 — Competences

1. Regions may perform functions delegated to them by their member Provinces or assigned by law where a Province alone cannot perform them effectively.
2. Fields of cooperation include transport networks, health planning, environmental and water management, and disaster-response coordination.
3. A Region shall not be assigned functions that interfere with the core self-government of its member Provinces.

## Article 7.2.3 — Regional Organs and Finance

1. Each Region shall have a regional council composed of delegates appointed by the Provincial Councils of its members, and such offices as the council establishes.
2. The founding agreement shall fix the manner in which the regional council decides so that it fairly reflects the member Provinces.
3. A Region shall be financed by contributions of its member Provinces and by revenue of its own undertakings; it shall publish its budget and accounts.

## Article 7.2.4 — Supervision and Disputes

1. Acts of a Region are subject to supervision limited to legality in the same manner as provincial action under Article 7.1.4 (Supervision Limited to Legality).
2. Disputes among member Provinces, or between a Region and a Province or the organs of the State, shall be decided by the Constitutional Court under Article 5.3.3 (Exclusive Jurisdiction).

## Design Note

*Regions are given the weakest institutional form on purpose: they solve genuine scale problems — river basins, transport corridors, tertiary health planning — without creating a new sovereign tier or a chamber of regional veto. Voluntary membership keeps cooperation from hardening into imposed bureaucracy, while delegate councils preserve the democratic chain back to the Provincial Councils. The design borrows the logic of functional cooperation without importing the rigidity of a fixed federal layer.*
