# Section 7.3 — Municipalities

> **Part 07 — Local Government** · Articles 7.3.1 – 7.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes Municipalities as the local tier of government: their councils and mayors, their general competence for local services, their by-laws, and community participation.*

## Article 7.3.1 — Right to Local Self-Government

1. Municipalities shall have the right to regulate and manage local affairs within the limits of this Constitution and the law.
2. Municipalities are established and structured by law; their boundaries shall respect the coherence of settlements and communities.
3. A Municipality may not be abolished or merged without a hearing of the populations concerned.

## Article 7.3.2 — General Competence and Services

1. Municipalities shall provide the services of the local tier, including water supply, sanitation, local roads, local public transport, and the maintenance of public spaces.
2. A Municipality possesses general competence for local affairs not reserved by this Constitution or assigned by law to another level.
3. Municipal services shall be provided without discrimination among residents and at standards fixed by law.

## Article 7.3.3 — Municipal Councils and Mayors

1. Each Municipality shall have a municipal council elected by its residents.
2. The council shall elect a mayor from among its members; the mayor conducts the administration of the Municipality and represents it.
3. The council shall adopt the municipal budget and may establish committees and local offices.

## Article 7.3.4 — Local By-Laws

1. Municipal councils may adopt by-laws within their competence, subject to publication and to supervision limited to legality under Article 7.1.4 (Supervision Limited to Legality).
2. A by-law shall not take effect before its publication in the manner fixed by law.

## Article 7.3.5 — Community Participation

1. Municipalities shall inform their residents regularly about plans and decisions and shall provide for consultation before major local decisions.
2. The law shall provide for petitions, public hearings, and other instruments of participation, which may include participatory budgeting for part of the investment budget.
3. Decisions of local administration affecting individual rights or interests shall observe the guarantees of fair administrative action.

## Design Note

*Municipalities receive the services closest to daily life — water, sanitation, local roads — because the tier that observes demand is best placed to supply it. A council-elected mayor keeps the local executive accountable to the representative body, a choice real constitutions resolve differently without affecting democratic quality. Participatory instruments are constitutionalized as duties of process rather than transfers of final decision, preserving elected accountability while opening it to residents.*
