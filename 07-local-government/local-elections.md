# Section 7.4 — Local Elections

> **Part 07 — Local Government** · Articles 7.4.1 – 7.4.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs elections to the Provincial Councils and the municipal councils: administration by the Electoral Commission, common integrity standards, timing, and common rolls.*

## Article 7.4.1 — Administration by the Electoral Commission

1. The Electoral Commission shall organize, conduct, and supervise elections to the Provincial Councils and to the municipal councils.
2. The Commission shall have the same exclusive competence for local elections as for national elections under Article 3.5.3 (Exclusive Administration).
3. No organ of a Province or Municipality may interfere with the conduct of a local election.

## Article 7.4.2 — Common Integrity Standards

1. Local elections shall observe the same constitutional standards as national elections: universal and equal suffrage, the secret ballot under Article 6.3.1 (The Secret Ballot), paper records, observation, and the remedies of Part 06.
2. Voting rights and rolls shall be identical to those used in national elections.
3. Candidates and parties shall compete on equal terms, and public facilities shall be used neutrally.

## Article 7.4.3 — Terms and Timing

1. Provincial Councils shall be elected for five-year terms consistent with Article 7.1.2 (Provincial Councils); municipal councils shall be elected for five-year terms.
2. Provincial and municipal elections shall ordinarily be held on the same day, at a date fixed by law.
3. Early elections of a council shall be held only where the council cannot function, in the manner fixed by law.
4. A vacancy in a council shall be filled for the remainder of the term in the manner fixed by law.

## Article 7.4.4 — Common Electoral Rolls

1. A single voter roll maintained under Article 6.2.2 (Maintenance of the Rolls) shall serve national, provincial, and municipal elections.
2. No separate registration shall be demanded of any voter for a local election.

## Design Note

*Routing local elections through the national Electoral Commission removes the most common source of local manipulation, the incumbent council running its own election. A single roll and a shared election day lower the administrative cost of devolution and raise turnout, while identical integrity standards prevent the local tier from becoming a testing ground for practices intolerable at national level.*

**Cross-references:** Article 3.5.3 (Exclusive Administration); Article 6.3.1 (The Secret Ballot); Article 6.2.2 (Maintenance of the Rolls).
