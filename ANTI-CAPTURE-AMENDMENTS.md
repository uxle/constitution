## On "zero loopholes"

Every entry above closes a *specific, named* failure pattern. None of them, individually or together, make the system loophole-free — that isn't achievable by drafting, and a document claiming otherwise would be less honest, not more secure. Three risks remain open by design and are worth tracking as this edition evolves:

- **Sortition pools can be captured slowly**, by narrowing who is eligible to enter them, rather than defeated head-on. This requires periodic, independent audit of pool composition — not addressed by any single Article above.
- **Fast timelines (two-year trials, thirty-day reviews) create pressure to under-investigate** rather than to delay improperly. Article 31.7.8's judicial review is a partial check, not a complete one.
- **A sufficiently large constitutional-amendment majority can still rewrite any of this**, including the entrenchment clauses themselves. High thresholds (Article 5.5.6.3, Part 25) are a speed bump, not a wall — the durable safeguard is a public that would notice and object, which no Article can manufacture.

## Suggested next passes (not yet drafted)
