# Section 35.1 — Public Cleanliness Standard

## Articles

**Article 35.1.1 (Constitutional Standard).** Every municipality must maintain public spaces — streets, parks, transit, waterways — to a defined cleanliness standard, measured by litter density, waste-collection frequency, and public sanitation surveys conducted at least annually.

**Article 35.1.2 (Shared Duty).** Cleanliness is a shared civic duty as well as a government responsibility: littering, illegal dumping, and public sanitation violations are enforceable civil offenses with graduated fines, paired with accessible public bins and collection infrastructure so that the duty is realistic to meet.

**Article 35.1.3 (No Cleanliness Without Infrastructure First).** No cleanliness enforcement action may be taken in an area where the municipality has failed to provide adequate waste infrastructure — the duty runs on the state first, the citizen second.

*Design Note: This models the widely admired public-cleanliness culture of cities such as Japan's, but deliberately sequences the duty — infrastructure obligation first, citizen obligation second — so it functions as genuine civic culture rather than as a pretext for punishing poverty.*

## Implementation Detail

**Article 35.1.4 (Survey Frequency).** Public sanitation and litter-density surveys are conducted at minimum annually in every municipality above 10,000 residents, published in the Clean Cities Certification report (Article 35.4.1).
