# Section 25.5 — Constitutional Continuity

> **Part 25 — Constitutional Process** · Articles 25.5.1 – 25.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section preserves the continuity of the constitutional order: succession when organs fail, the prohibition of unconstitutional abolition, and the duty of loyalty of all organs.*

## Article 25.5.1 — Continuity of the Constitutional Order

1. The constitutional order of the Republic is continuous; it survives changes of government, elections, crises, and the incapacity of any organ.
2. The Constitution may be changed only through the procedures of Part 25; continuity and change are not in conflict, for amendment is the Constitution's own mode of continuing.

## Article 25.5.2 — Succession of Organs

1. Where a constitutional organ is permanently unable to act because its offices are unfilled or its functioning is otherwise obstructed, its essential functions shall be exercised temporarily by the organ designated by law, and only to the extent necessary to restore full capacity.
2. Temporary exercise under paragraph 1 shall be announced publicly, shall be reported to the Parliament without delay, and shall end as soon as the organ is able to act.
3. No organ may use the inability of another organ as a ground for assuming its ordinary powers.

## Article 25.5.3 — Prohibition of Unconstitutional Abolition

1. No organ, person, or group may abolish the constitutional order, dissolve the democratic institutions of the Republic, or remove the supremacy of this Constitution by any means other than amendment under Part 25.
2. Acts attempting such abolition are void from the outset and create no obligations.
3. Organs and officials shall not comply with such acts; the validity of measures taken in reliance on them may be tested under Section 5.6.

## Article 25.5.4 — Duty of Loyalty

1. All organs of the Republic, all officials, and all service members owe loyalty to the constitutional order and shall actively preserve it.
2. Loyalty includes the duty to lawful dissent and to the loyal opposition; it does not license obedience to unconstitutional commands.

## Article 25.5.5 — Continuity of the State

1. The Republic continues as a subject of law notwithstanding changes of government or organs; its treaties, obligations, and property remain in force, subject to this Constitution.

## Design Note

*Every constitution is ultimately a bet that institutions will outlast their occupants; this Section supplies the fallback rules for the day the bet is tested. The void-from-the-outset rule in Article 25.5.3 echoes the German Basic Law's defense of the democratic order and the constitutional identity doctrine of several European courts. Succession clauses are deliberately narrow — temporary, announced, and reported — because substituting organs is itself a danger if left open-ended.*
