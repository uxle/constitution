# Section 25.3 — Constitutional Interpretation

> **Part 25 — Constitutional Process** · Articles 25.3.1 – 25.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs how the Constitution is read: methods of interpretation, respect for precedent, evolution consistent with dignity, and the duty to give reasons.*

## Article 25.3.1 — Methods of Interpretation

1. All organs of the Republic shall interpret this Constitution in accordance with its text, structure, and purpose, and in harmony with the Constitution as a whole.
2. Interpretation shall begin with the ordinary meaning of the words in their context; purpose and consequences may refine, but not contradict, the text.
3. Interpretation consistent with the Republic's obligations under international law shall be preferred, in accordance with Article 19.3.3 (Consistent Interpretation).

## Article 25.3.2 — Precedent

1. Decisions of the Constitutional Court on constitutional questions bind all organs and courts.
2. A departure from a settled interpretation shall state expressly the grounds of the departure and the reasons why the earlier decision should no longer stand.

## Article 25.3.3 — Evolving Understanding

1. Constitutional provisions may be interpreted in the light of present conditions and experience, within the limits of their text and purpose.
2. Evolving interpretation shall remain consistent with human dignity and with the nature of this Constitution as the charter of a democratic republic.
3. Interpretation shall not effect an amendment that the procedures of Section 25.1 have not accomplished.

## Article 25.3.4 — Duty to Give Reasons

1. Every organ applying or interpreting this Constitution shall state the reasons for its understanding in decisions of public effect.
2. Reasons shall be intelligible to the public and shall identify the provision interpreted and the method relied upon.

## Article 25.3.5 — Conforming Interpretation

1. Statutes and subordinate law shall, wherever possible, be read so as to conform to this Constitution, following Article 0.8.4 (Conforming Interpretation).
2. Where no conforming reading exists, the law shall be applied as written and its validity may be tested under Section 5.6.

## Design Note

*Interpretation clauses are the quiet engine of constitutional longevity: they tell successors how to apply a text written decades earlier. The hierarchy of text, structure, purpose, and precedent condenses the methodology developed by courts across many systems, while the dignity anchor reflects the interpretive practice of the Federal Constitutional Court of Germany. Article 25.3.3 guards the boundary that comparative practice has found essential — courts may update meaning, but live-origin limitations and referendums, not judges, change the text.*
