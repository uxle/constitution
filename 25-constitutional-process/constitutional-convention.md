# Section 25.2 — Constitutional Convention

> **Part 25 — Constitutional Process** · Articles 25.2.1 – 25.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section enables the citizens and the Parliament to convene a Constitutional Convention and submits its outcome to the amendment procedures.*

## Article 25.2.1 — Citizens' Initiative

1. At least five per cent of the citizens entitled to vote may submit to the Parliament a reasoned proposal to convene a Constitutional Convention.
2. Within six months of receiving a valid proposal, the Parliament shall either resolve to convene the Convention or submit the question of convocation to a national referendum.
3. The question shall be approved in a referendum by a majority of the votes cast.

## Article 25.2.2 — Parliamentary Convocation

1. The Parliament may convene a Constitutional Convention on its own initiative by resolution of each House.
2. The resolution shall define the subject matters within the mandate of the Convention and the timetable for its work.

## Article 25.2.3 — Composition and Mandate

1. The Convention shall be composed so as to reflect the diversity of the Republic; its composition and procedure shall be determined by law adopted before its convocation.
2. The Convention may deliberate only within the mandate assigned to it; it may not itself amend the Constitution.
3. The Convention shall receive the administrative support it requires and shall publish its deliberations.

## Article 25.2.4 — Limits

1. The Convention shall observe the principles of Section 25.5 and shall not propose the abolition of the democratic constitutional order.
2. Proposals concerning entrenched provisions under Section 30.5 shall be identified as such in the Convention's report.

## Article 25.2.5 — Referendum on the Outcome

1. The Convention shall transmit its proposals to the Parliament with its final report.
2. Within twelve months, the Parliament shall introduce implementing amendment bills under Section 25.1 or shall publish its reasoned decision not to do so.
3. Proposals affecting entrenched provisions shall, if adopted by the Parliament, be put to a national referendum as required by Article 6.7.3 (Mandatory Referendum on Entrenched Amendments).

## Design Note

*A convention route inserts a deliberative reset valve that ordinary amendment votes lack: elected delegates with a mandate can rethink structures that parliamentarians are too invested in to review. The Icelandic assembly of 2011 and Ireland's Citizens' Assembly of 2016 show both the promise and the peril of such bodies, which is why this text hard-wires the parliamentary follow-up duty. Requiring the outcome to travel through Section 25.1 preserves the amendment tiers; the Convention advises the Constitution, it does not replace it.*
