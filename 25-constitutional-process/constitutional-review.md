# Section 25.4 — Constitutional Review Commission

> **Part 25 — Constitutional Process** · Articles 25.4.1 – 25.4.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes a periodic Constitutional Review Commission that examines the Constitution in public and feeds proposals into the amendment process; it is distinct from judicial review under Section 5.6.*

## Article 25.4.1 — Establishment and Cycle

1. A Constitutional Review Commission shall be convened every fifteen years.
2. The Commission shall be established by law in good time before its convocation and shall be composed so as to include the parliamentary groups and independent members with expertise in law and public affairs.
3. Its members shall serve in their personal capacity and shall declare any interests in matters before it.

## Article 25.4.2 — Mandate

1. The Commission shall examine the functioning of this Constitution, including the working of constitutional institutions, the realization of rights in practice, the coherence of the text, and the implementation gaps identified by constitutional bodies.
2. The Commission may consider any provision, including entrenched provisions, and shall report whether and how it should be amended.

## Article 25.4.3 — Public Participation

1. The Commission shall invite submissions from the public, hold hearings accessible throughout the Republic, and publish an interim report before finalizing its recommendations.
2. Hearings shall include the perspective of educators and civic organizations, so that the Constitution's operation is explained, not only evaluated.

## Article 25.4.4 — Report and Follow-Up

1. The Commission shall deliver a final report with reasoned recommendations.
2. Within twelve months, the Parliament shall process recommendations under Section 25.1 or shall publish its reasoned decision not to do so.
3. The follow-up given to each recommendation shall be recorded in a public register.

## Design Note

*This mechanism answers a real defect of constitutions without any of the usual risks: most texts are reviewed piecemeal or only after crisis. Periodic review follows the model of the Austrian and Dutch constitutional survey bodies and the standing review practice of some Nordic parliaments, scaled to a fixed fifteen-year cycle. The Section deliberately does not touch judicial review under Section 5.6 — courts invalidate unconstitutional acts case by case; the Commission evaluates the whole document in public, and its proposals carry only the ordinary force of proposals.*
