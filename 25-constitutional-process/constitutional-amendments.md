# Section 25.1 — Constitutional Amendments

> **Part 25 — Constitutional Process** · Articles 25.1.1 – 25.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section fixes the tiers of constitutional amendment: ordinary amendments, entrenched provisions, schedule matters, and the restrictions that apply during emergencies.*

## Article 25.1.1 — Initiative and Form

1. A proposal to amend the Constitution may be introduced by one fifth of the members of either House or by the Government.
2. A proposal to amend the Constitution may also reach the Parliament through the citizens' initiative of Section 25.2.
3. Amendments shall be adopted as a single text; what is adopted is the whole amended Constitution as it will read.

## Article 25.1.2 — Ordinary Amendments

1. The provisions of this Constitution not referred to in Article 25.1.3 or in Article 25.1.4 may be amended by a two-thirds majority of each House.
2. A constitutional amendment adopted under this Section shall be promulgated without delay and shall be published as part of this Constitution.

## Article 25.1.3 — Entrenched Provisions

1. The provisions listed in Section 30.5 may be amended only by a two-thirds majority of each House and by approval in a national referendum by a majority of the votes cast.
2. The referendum shall be conducted under the rules applicable to mandatory referendums in Article 6.7.3 (Mandatory Referendum on Entrenched Amendments).
3. No alternative to the referendum route may be used, and a referendum result may not be overridden by Parliament.

## Article 25.1.4 — Schedule Matters

1. Matters contained in the Schedules, insofar as they are not tied to provisions of Section 30.5, may be amended by a simple majority of each House.
2. Schedule amendments shall be published in the same manner as ordinary amendments.

## Article 25.1.5 — Restrictions During Emergencies

1. During a state of emergency declared under Part 26, no constitutional amendment may be introduced or adopted.
2. A vote on a constitutional amendment taken in violation of paragraph 1 is void.
3. Judicial review of the validity of an amendment under this Article may not be excluded, consistently with Article 5.6.5 (No Ouster of Review).

## Design Note

*Three amendment tiers balance stability and adaptability: a heavy ordinary route, a referendum lock for the identity of the polity, and a light route for schedules that would otherwise clog the amendment machinery. This mirrors the graded design of the German Basic Law, where a subset of principles is unamendable, and of constitutions that subject entrenched clauses to referendums, such as Denmark. The emergency moratorium answers the historical pattern that constitutions are most often rewritten under duress.*
