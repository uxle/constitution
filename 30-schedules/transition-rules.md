# Section 30.4 — Transition Rules

> **Part 30 — Schedules** · Articles 30.4.1 – 30.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Schedule orders entry into force of the Constitution: continuity of consistent law, first appointments, continuity of institutions and obligations, and repeal of inconsistent provisions.*

## Article 30.4.1 — Entry into Force

1. This Constitution enters into force upon its proclamation in accordance with the procedure that brought it into being.
2. Upon entry into force, the organs established by this Constitution are constituted and take up their functions under this Schedule.
3. The oath or affirmation under Section 30.1 shall be taken before the functions of the listed offices are exercised.

## Article 30.4.2 — Continuity of Existing Law

1. Existing law continues in force insofar as it is consistent with this Constitution.
2. To the extent of inconsistency, existing law ceases to apply, without need of express repeal.
3. Existing law shall be interpreted in conformity with this Constitution, consistent with Article 0.8.4 (Conforming Interpretation).

## Article 30.4.3 — First Appointments

1. The Parliament in office at the entry into force continues for its term and exercises the powers of this Constitution.
2. The President, the Prime Minister, and the ministers in office continue and are deemed appointed under this Constitution, subject to taking the oath or affirmation without delay.
3. The first judges of the Constitutional Court shall be designated by law; their terms are staggered under Article 5.3.2 (Terms of Judges).
4. The first holders of the offices in Section 30.3 shall be appointed without delay under the governing articles.

## Article 30.4.4 — Continuity of Institutions and Obligations

1. The courts, the Public Prosecution Service, and the public administration continue to function; pending proceedings are unaffected.
2. The property, contracts, and public debt of the State devolve upon the competent organs of the Republic.
3. Rights of individuals arising under existing law are preserved insofar as consistent with this Constitution.

## Article 30.4.5 — Repeal and Conforming Amendments

1. The Government shall lay before the Parliament, and the Parliament shall process within the periods fixed by the transition legislation, the measures required to repeal inconsistent provisions and conform existing law to this Constitution.
2. Until such measures are taken, the rule of Article 30.4.2 governs.
3. No provision of existing law shall be revived after ceasing to apply under this Schedule except by fresh enactment.

## Design Note

*Transition rules are the bridge every constitution must build from the old order to the new, and the continuity principle follows the maxim that the State outlives its constitutions: courts stay open, judges keep sitting, and debts are honored. The deemed-appointment clause avoids the first-hour paradox in which the offices that must install the new order are the very ones awaiting installation. The inconsistency rule, under which inconsistent law ceases rather than being repealed piecemeal, borrows the supremacy mechanics of Section 0.8, and the repeal program with fixed periods makes the cleanup a duty rather than an option.*
