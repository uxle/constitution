# Section 30.2 — Electoral Districts

> **Part 30 — Schedules** · Articles 30.2.1 – 30.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Schedule governs delimitation of constituencies and districts: the Boundary Commission's mandate, the principles of delimitation, procedure, and review.*

## Article 30.2.1 — Scope

1. This Schedule governs the delimitation of constituencies and districts for elections other than that of the Lower House, whose constituencies correspond to the Provinces under Article 6.1.4 (Constituency Boundaries), and any revision of the provincial constituency structure that an Act provides to preserve equality of the vote.
2. Its principles apply to districts for local elections and referendums, under Article 7.4.1 (Administration by the Electoral Commission).

## Article 30.2.2 — The Boundary Commission

1. An independent Boundary Commission shall delimit constituencies and districts under this Schedule.
2. Its members are appointed for fixed terms through an open, published process, may be removed only for cause, and may not hold party office.
3. The Commission is supported by professional staff and the data services of the Statistics Authority.
4. The Commission's budget shall be adequate and shall not be reduced so as to obstruct its work.

## Article 30.2.3 — Principles of Delimitation

1. The Commission shall observe, in order of priority:
   (a) the equality of the vote, so that electors per seat are comparable;
   (b) geographic contiguity and the accessibility of the district;
   (c) the coherence of communities of interest and of administrative and natural boundaries.
2. The Commission shall use the most recent census and electoral register data.
3. The Commission shall not take account of voting patterns, the prospects of parties or candidates, or the residence of incumbents.
4. A deviation from population equality shall not exceed the statutory tolerance and shall be reasoned.

## Article 30.2.4 — Procedure

1. The Commission shall publish draft delimitations with their data and reasons.
2. Any person may object within the period fixed by law, and the Commission shall hold public hearings where substantial objections are made.
3. The final determination is published with reasons and takes effect for the next election; the Parliament may correct only procedural defects.

## Article 30.2.5 — Review Cycle and Review by Courts

1. The Commission shall review all delimitations after each census, at intervals not exceeding ten years, and whenever population movements impair the equality of the vote.
2. A determination of the Commission is subject to review by the courts for breach of this Schedule, under Article 5.6.1 (Power of All Courts).

## Design Note

*Boundary rules are stated in the Constitution because districting is the classic instrument by which a majority entrenches itself, and the ban on using voting patterns or incumbents' residences is the rule's teeth. The priority ordering is deliberate: arithmetic equality first, geography and community second, so contiguity cannot be a pretext for salamander shapes. The independent commission model follows parliamentary democracies whose redistributions are accepted precisely because the commission takes no electoral advantage into account. Public drafts, objections, and reasoned determinations make the process accountable, and the ten-year cycle keeps maps honest as populations move.*
