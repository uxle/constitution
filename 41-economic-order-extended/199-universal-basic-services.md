# WC-199 · Universal Basic Services

> **Part 41 — Economic Order Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 199/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** The social guarantee: what everyone gets as of right

## Preamble

This document defines the universal services floor guaranteed to every resident.

## Articles

### Article 1 — The guarantee

1.1 Every resident has an enforceable claim to the essentials of participation: basic banking, digital identity and connectivity, a postal address, healthcare, education, emergency shelter, and legal aid.

1.2 Services are delivered as rights under the public services charter, not as charity with conditions.

1.3 Eligibility is residence, full stop - no status tests beyond registration.

### Article 2 — Standard and access

2.1 National service standards define minimum quality, with equalisation funding so geography does not decide quality.

2.2 Assisted channels under document 171 guarantee offline access.

2.3 Waiting-time maxima and remedies apply across the floor.

### Article 3 — Funding

3.1 Services are funded from general taxation with transparent cost publication per service.

3.2 User charges are capped at ability-to-pay with automatic waivers.

3.3 Outsourcing never transfers the rights - providers contract under the charter.

### Article 4 — Beyond the floor

4.1 Universal basic income pilots are a policy option for Parliament; the constitutional floor is services, not cash.

4.2 Food and transport vouchers in crisis follow humanitarian standards with dignity design.

4.3 The floor indexes to living-cost data published independently.

## Commentary

Universal services are the anti-corruption answer to welfare: no discretionary gatekeeping, no brokers, no favours - everyone gets the service by residence, so there is nothing to bribe for. Banking, address and connectivity are added to the classic list because exclusion from them now excludes from everything else. Keeping cash UBI as policy option rather than constitutional right preserves fiscal flexibility while the services floor is locked.
