# WC-197 · Fighting Tax Evasion and Avoidance

> **Part 41 — Economic Order Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 197/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Compliance strategy: data, treaties, penalties and promotion

## Preamble

This document sets the strategy against tax evasion and aggressive avoidance.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Detection

1.1 Third-party reporting is universal: employers, banks, platforms, notaries, casinos and crypto-asset providers report automatically.

1.2 International exchange of information, beneficial ownership and land registers are fused in the risk analytics of document 086.

1.3 Industry-specialist audit teams match sector patterns to returns.

### Article 2 — Legal tools

2.1 General anti-avoidance rule with economic substance; exit taxes; transfer-pricing with country-by-country reporting publication.

2.2 Promoter penalties for scheme design; privilege does not shield scheme marketing.

2.3 Offshore structures without substance are disregarded for tax.

### Article 3 — Penalties and prosecution

3.1 Graduated penalties: error, negligence, avoidance, evasion - with fraud prosecuted as the crime of document 132.

3.2 Voluntary disclosure programmes with full payment, interest and reduced penalties, one-time windows.

3.3 Public enforcement data by sector and taxpayer class publishes annually.

### Article 4 — Service-first compliance

4.1 The promotion ladder: make it easy, make it visible, make it hurt - in that order.

4.2 Advance rulings and cooperative compliance for complex taxpayers under document 173.

4.3 Compliance-gap targets for the top percentile are public policy, not tactics.

## Commentary

The evidence on tax compliance is unambiguous: service plus third-party data collects more than fear, so the strategy leads with promotion and reserves punishment for dishonesty. Publishing the top-percentile gap changes the politics of enforcement by making double standards visible. Promoter liability attacks the supply side of avoidance - the industry that industrialised it.
