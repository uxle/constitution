# WC-200 · Healthcare System and Financing

> **Part 41 — Economic Order Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 200/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Universal coverage, primary-care-first, honest pricing

## Preamble

This document designs the financing and organisation of universal healthcare.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Coverage

1.1 Single-payer coverage for the defined benefit basket: primary, specialist, hospital, pharmaceuticals, mental health, dental core, rehabilitation and palliative care.

1.2 No upfront payment at point of care; cost-sharing is limited to solidarity-calibrated co-payments with caps and exemptions.

1.3 Private complementary insurance is free but never buys queue-jumping in public services.

### Article 2 — Organisation

2.1 Primary care is the system's front door: registered family practices with gatekeeping-light, multidisciplinary teams and capitation-plus-performance payment.

2.2 Regional hospital networks with referral protocols; academic centres anchor quality.

2.3 Public health and prevention are funded as statutory programmes under document 217.

### Article 3 — Pricing and procurement

3.1 Reference pricing and health-technology assessment govern pharmaceuticals; negotiation is transparent with published outcomes.

3.2 Medical devices and services follow DRG-style case payment with quality adjustments.

3.3 Procurement follows document 098 with medical-specific integrity screens - a classic corruption hotspot.

### Article 4 — Workforce and quality

4.1 Workforce planning is statutory with training commissions and minimum staffing standards.

4.2 Clinical quality registries, outcomes publication and incident-reporting systems are mandatory and no-fault.

4.3 Patient rights under the charter include choice of practice, second opinion, records access and complaint redress.

## Commentary

Healthcare financing is where universality meets the hardest fiscal choices, and the chapter follows the strongest-performing systems: single payer for the basket, primary care first, and pricing power via assessment and reference pricing. Publishing negotiated pharma outcomes ends the opacity where the biggest health-corruption money historically moved. No-fault incident reporting is the cultural switch that turns error into learning instead of concealment.
