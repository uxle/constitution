# WC-195 · Wealth, Inheritance and Capital Taxes

> **Part 08 — Economic Order** · Document **195 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** Taxing the stock, not just the flow

## Preamble

This document designs wealth, inheritance and property taxes with anti-avoidance architecture.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Net wealth tax

1.1 A moderate annual net wealth tax above a high exemption - one percent above one thousand median household incomes, two percent above ten thousand.

1.2 Base: worldwide assets of residents, domestic assets of non-residents, with treaty relief.

1.3 Valuations: market-based with mass appraisal, appealed through the property tribunals.

### Article 2 — Inheritance tax

2.1 Inheritance tax with high family allowances - one hundred median incomes to children, fifty to others - and progressive rates above.

2.2 Business and farm relief with jobs-and-continuity conditions and clawback on break-up.

2.3 Lifetime gift aggregation closes the living-transfer escape.

### Article 3 — Property tax

3.1 Annual land value taxation on site value, split-rate discouraging speculation, with exemptions for subsistence farms.

3.2 Vacancy surcharges on empty housing beyond twelve months.

3.3 Registers make ownership and value public under document 155.

### Article 4 — Anti-avoidance

4.1 Exit taxes on expatriation of unrealised gains; anti-avoidance on trusts under document 159.

4.2 General anti-avoidance rule with economic substance doctrine.

4.3 Automatic exchange of financial information closes offshore opacity.

## Commentary

Taxing stocks as well as flows is the structural answer to the dynastic concentration documented across economies, and the design keeps the burden on the truly wealthy with high thresholds rather than middle-class property. Land value taxation is included as the least distortive and most transparent of all taxes, resting on registers the corpus already makes public. Exit taxes and gift aggregation close the two doors through which wealth-tax systems historically leaked.
