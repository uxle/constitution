# Section 16.5 — Family Law

> **Part 16 — Civil Law** · Articles 16.5.1 – 16.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section protects the family as the fundamental unit of society, orders the equality of partners and joint parental responsibility, secures the voice and welfare of the child, and assigns the State its supporting duties.*

## Article 16.5.1 — The Family

1. The family is the fundamental unit of society and is entitled to protection.
2. Family life may take diverse forms; the protection of this Section extends to every family without discrimination, consistent with Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination).

## Article 16.5.2 — Equality of Partners

1. Partners in a family have equal rights and equal duties in its decisions, property, and care work.
2. Household and care work shall be valued on equal terms with earning in the property and maintenance rules of this Part.

## Article 16.5.3 — Parental Responsibility

1. Parents hold joint responsibility for the upbringing, development, and property of their children, guided by the best interests of the child consistent with Article 1.15.2 (Best Interests of the Child).
2. Separation of the parents does not end either parent's responsibility; contact with both parents shall be maintained where consistent with the child's best interests.

## Article 16.5.4 — Voice and Welfare of the Child

1. A child capable of forming views shall be heard in family proceedings affecting the child, consistent with Article 1.15.3 (Participation of Children).
2. The maintenance of children takes priority over the other claims of their parents.

## Article 16.5.5 — State Support and Protection

1. The State shall support families through services, benefits, and leave arrangements as provided by law.
2. The State shall protect family members from violence within the family; protection measures shall be prompt and shall be designed so that, where possible, the person protected remains in the home and the violent person must leave.

## Design Note

*Equality of partners is made concrete by requiring that care work count in the property and maintenance rules, which answers the classic gap where formal equality left economically dependent partners exposed upon dissolution. The family is protected in its diverse forms rather than in a single prescribed model, and cross-references import the child's paramountcy and participation rights from Section 1.15. The violence clause places the burden of departure on the violent partner rather than the victim, the design behind modern protection-order statutes.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination); Article 1.15.2 (Best Interests of the Child); Article 1.15.3 (Participation of Children).
