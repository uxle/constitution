# Section 16.7 — Divorce

> **Part 16 — Civil Law** · Articles 16.7.1 – 16.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs the dissolution of marriage: no-fault divorce, mediation offered but never compelled, fair division of property, maintenance where needed, and the paramountcy of arrangements for children.*

## Article 16.7.1 — Dissolution by Law

1. Marriage may be dissolved only by a court or by another authority provided by law.
2. Divorce shall be granted upon the irretrievable breakdown of the marriage; neither the grant of divorce nor its terms shall depend on the attribution of fault.
3. Breakdown may be established by separation for a period fixed by law or by other evidence of the end of the marital relationship.

## Article 16.7.2 — Mediation

1. Mediation and counseling shall be offered to the parties at the outset of proceedings.
2. Participation in mediation shall never be compelled, and refusal shall not affect the outcome of the proceedings; an agreement reached in mediation shall be confirmed only after review for fairness.

## Article 16.7.3 — Division of Property

1. Property acquired during the marriage and the increase in the partners' estates shall be divided according to principles of fairness fixed by law, with regard to the care of children and household, earning capacity, and the needs of each partner.
2. Disadvantage suffered for the sake of the family shall be compensated.

## Article 16.7.4 — Maintenance

1. A partner who cannot maintain himself or herself adequately after divorce shall be entitled to maintenance from the other partner, according to need and capacity, as provided by law.

## Article 16.7.5 — Arrangements for Children

1. Arrangements for the care, residence, and contact of children shall be made according to the best interests of the child, consistent with Article 1.15.2 (Best Interests of the Child).
2. The child shall be heard, arrangements shall be reviewed where circumstances materially change, and protection services are stated in Part 22 (Social Policy).

## Design Note

*No-fault divorce removes the perjury economy of blame-based systems: once the marriage has genuinely ended, the State's interest is in orderly consequences, not in adjudicated guilt, and fault survives only as evidence relevant to safety and to children. Mediation is offered because negotiated outcomes age better than imposed ones, but compulsion would convert a service into a toll gate on access to court. The division rules recognize care work as a contribution of equal dignity with earning, applying within divorce the equality command of Article 16.5.2 (Equality of Partners).*
