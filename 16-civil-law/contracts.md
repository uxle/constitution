# Section 16.1 — Contracts

> **Part 16 — Civil Law** · Articles 16.1.1 – 16.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs private agreement: freedom of contract, good faith in negotiation and performance, the control of unfair standard terms, defects of consent, and consumer protection.*

## Article 16.1.1 — Freedom of Contract

1. Parties may conclude contracts and determine their content freely; a validly concluded contract binds the parties and may be set aside only on grounds provided by law.
2. Freedom of contract is subject to this Constitution, to the law, and to public policy, consistent with Article 9.1.3 (Freedom of Contract).

## Article 16.1.2 — Good Faith

1. Parties shall negotiate, perform, and enforce contracts in accordance with good faith.
2. Negotiations conducted in bad faith, including a break-off without reasonable grounds after creating reasonable reliance, may give rise to liability as provided by law.

## Article 16.1.3 — Unfair Standard Terms

1. A term that has not been individually negotiated and that causes a significant imbalance in the parties' rights and obligations to the detriment of the weaker party shall not bind that party.
2. A term that excludes or limits liability for intentional or grossly negligent conduct shall not bind any party.
3. Ambiguity in standard terms shall be construed against the party that supplied them; the remainder of the contract survives where it can stand without the unfair term.

## Article 16.1.4 — Defects of Consent

1. Consent obtained by fraud, duress, or material mistake may be set aside as provided by law.
2. The party that induced the defect shall make good the resulting loss.

## Article 16.1.5 — Consumers

1. Suppliers of goods and services shall observe the guarantees of Article 1.14.3 (Fair Contract Terms) in every contract with a consumer.
2. The law shall require plain language and pre-contractual information in consumer contracts and shall provide accessible redress consistent with Article 1.14.4 (Accessible Redress).

## Design Note

*The Section keeps the classical architecture — freedom, binding force, defects of consent — and adds two modern disciplines: a general good-faith duty in the tradition of section 242 of the German Civil Code, and a rule of non-binding unfair standard terms modeled on the European Union's Unfair Contract Terms Directive. Policing operates through invalidity of the term, not of the contract, which protects the weaker party without destabilizing exchange, and the construction rule against the drafter gives the test teeth. Consumer protection is implemented by cross-reference so that the constitutional guarantee of Section 1.14 and the private law speak with one voice.*

**Cross-references:** Article 9.1.3 (Freedom of Contract); Article 1.14.3 (Fair Contract Terms); Article 1.14.4 (Accessible Redress).
