# Section 16.6 — Marriage

> **Part 16 — Civil Law** · Articles 16.6.1 – 16.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section regulates marriage: free and full consent, a strict minimum age of eighteen without exceptions, equal rights of the partners during and after marriage, and non-discriminatory recognition of the forms marriage may take.*

## Article 16.6.1 — Freedom to Marry

1. Persons of marriageable age have the right to marry and to found a family.
2. Marriage requires the free and full consent of both partners, expressed before the competent authority; a marriage concluded without such consent is void.
3. No marriage shall be concluded under coercion, deception, or pressure that destroys genuine consent.

## Article 16.6.2 — Minimum Age

1. Marriage may be concluded only by persons who have reached the age of eighteen years.
2. No authority may grant a dispensation from the minimum age on any ground, including pregnancy, parental consent, or the celebration of a religious ceremony.

## Article 16.6.3 — Equal Rights in Marriage

1. Partners have equal rights and equal duties in marriage, during the marriage and upon its dissolution, in property, care, representation, and every other respect.
2. Each partner retains full legal capacity and may choose work, profession, and property dealings freely.

## Article 16.6.4 — Recognition of Forms

1. Marriage may be solemnized in civil or religious form; every form recognized by law carries the same legal effects.
2. All marriages shall be recognized without discrimination, consistent with Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination); the law shall provide for registered partnerships carrying equivalent effects and shall give effect to marriages validly concluded under the law of another state.

## Article 16.6.5 — Registration

1. Marriages shall be registered; registration may not be refused where the requirements of consent and age are met.
2. Failure to register shall not invalidate a marriage that was validly solemnized.

## Design Note

*The absolute age floor with no dispensations is deliberately stricter than systems that permit judicial or parental exceptions, because child marriage is the point where family autonomy collides with the child's rights under Section 1.15, and the harm is irreversible in a way that ordinary limitations cannot repair. Consent is made the essence of validity, voiding coerced marriages outright rather than merely annulling them at discretion. Equal treatment of all recognized forms follows from the equality guarantee and leaves religious communities their rites while reserving civil effects to the civil order.*
