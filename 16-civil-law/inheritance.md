# Section 16.4 — Inheritance

> **Part 16 — Civil Law** · Articles 16.4.1 – 16.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs succession upon death: testamentary freedom bounded by a compulsory family share, equality among heirs without discrimination, estate administration, and intestate succession.*

## Article 16.4.1 — Testamentary Freedom

1. Every person may dispose of the person's estate by will within the limits of this Section.
2. A will shall be made freely and with capacity; a will procured by fraud, duress, or material mistake may be set aside.

## Article 16.4.2 — Compulsory Family Share

1. The descendants, the spouse, and, as provided by law, the parents of the deceased shall be entitled to a compulsory share of the estate.
2. The compulsory share shall be fixed by law as a fraction of the share the person would have taken on intestacy and may be displaced only on the narrow grounds provided by law, such as grave misconduct toward the deceased.
3. Subject to the compulsory share, the deceased may dispose freely.

## Article 16.4.3 — No Discrimination Among Heirs

1. No person shall be excluded from succession or from the compulsory share on any ground listed in Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination), including sex, birth in or out of wedlock, or adoption.
2. Children shall stand equal in succession; adopted children shall be placed in the same position as children born within the marriage.

## Article 16.4.4 — Estate Administration

1. The estate passes on death subject to the payment of its debts; an heir may accept the estate subject to an inventory and shall not thereby become liable beyond its value.
2. Administration shall proceed under court supervision where disputes exist, and the executor or administrator shall account to the heirs.

## Article 16.4.5 — Intestate Succession

1. Where there is no effective will, succession follows the order of kinship fixed by law, with the spouse and the descendants taking first.
2. Where no kin entitled to succeed can be found, the estate passes to the State, which shall apply it to public purposes.

## Design Note

*The compulsory share reconciles the two great traditions — full testamentary freedom and the family's protected portion — by guaranteeing close kin a fraction of the statutory share while leaving the residue free, a settlement closer to the German Pflichtteil than to a fixed division of the whole estate. The nondiscrimination clause is not decorative: the exclusion of daughters, of children born outside marriage, and of adopted children was the daily business of succession law in many systems well into the twentieth century, and the clause closes that door permanently. The benefit-of-inventory rule prevents grief from becoming personal insolvency.*
