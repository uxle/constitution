# Section 16.9 — Civil Procedure

> **Part 16 — Civil Law** · Articles 16.9.1 – 16.9.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section secures civil justice: fair and oral hearings within a reasonable time, a small-claims track, alternative dispute resolution that is encouraged but never compelled, and effective enforcement of judgments.*

## Article 16.9.1 — Fair Hearing

1. Civil proceedings shall be fair, adversarial, and decided by an independent and impartial tribunal, consistent with Article 1.7.1 (Right to a Fair Trial).
2. The decisive hearing shall be oral; decision without an oral hearing may be taken only where the parties consent or for the categories of cases provided by law.

## Article 16.9.2 — Reasonable Time and Case Management

1. Proceedings shall be conducted within a reasonable time, consistent with Article 5.7.1 (Timely Justice).
2. Courts shall manage cases actively through early definition of the disputed points, proportionate exchange of evidence, and firm scheduling.

## Article 16.9.3 — Small Claims

1. The law shall provide a small-claims track with reduced formality, limited costs, and prompt decision, consistent with Article 5.7.2 (Simplified and Small-Claims Procedures).
2. Appeal from the small-claims track may be limited as provided by law, save where a fundamental defect is alleged.

## Article 16.9.4 — Mediation and ADR

1. Courts shall inform the parties of mediation and other methods of dispute resolution and may encourage their use.
2. No person shall be compelled to mediate; access to a court shall not be conditioned on prior settlement efforts.

## Article 16.9.5 — Enforcement

1. Judgments shall be enforced by the public authority without undue delay; the losing party may resist enforcement only on the narrow grounds provided by law.
2. Enforcement shall be proportionate: minimums protecting the debtor's basic subsistence and home shall be exempt, and enforcement measures shall be reviewable by a court.

## Design Note

*Civil procedure is drafted around proportionality: the small-claims track exists because a uniform procedure makes justice too expensive for small rights, and the ADR rule offers the modern settlement infrastructure without letting it become a condition of access, the error of compulsory-mediation experiments struck down in comparative practice. Case management duties place the fight against delay on the court rather than on the litigant. Enforcement closes the loop, because an unenforceable judgment is a recommendation; pairing effective execution with subsistence exemptions and judicial review keeps execution itself lawful.*
