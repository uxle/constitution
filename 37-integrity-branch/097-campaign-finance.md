# WC-097 · Campaign Finance

> **Part 37 — Integrity Branch** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 097/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Clean money for clean politics: caps, public funds, real-time disclosure

## Preamble

This document takes big and dark money out of elections through caps, public funding, disclosure and enforcement that bites.

## Articles

### Article 1 — The ceiling

1.1 Campaign spending is capped per party and candidate at statutory amounts indexed to inflation, with separate caps for the official campaign period.

1.2 Contributions from natural persons are capped per donor per cycle; contributions from legal persons, state contractors and foreign sources are prohibited.

1.3 Loans below market terms, in-kind services and price-discounted media count at full value within the cap.

### Article 2 — Public funding

2.1 Public campaign funds support parties by a formula of votes and seats, with additional support for new entrants qualifying signatures.

2.2 Public funds are paid early and on compliance with disclosure; misuse is recoverable with penalty.

2.3 Broadcast free time of document 057 substitutes for a share of the fund.

### Article 3 — Real-time disclosure

3.1 Donations above the statutory threshold are reported to the Electoral Commission within forty-eight hours and published online within seventy-two.

3.2 Full accounts, audited by chartered auditors, are filed within sixty days after elections with every expense itemised above threshold.

3.3 Sponsorship of events, third-party campaigns and issue advertising within ninety days of elections are within the regime and disclosed.

### Article 4 — Enforcement

4.1 Overspending voids public funding and attracts fines up to the overspend; knowing violations forfeit seats and annul elections under document 052.

4.2 Circumvention through proxies and associations is a corruption offence of document 108.

4.3 The Electoral Commission audits a random thirty percent of accounts and all accounts of winners.

## Commentary

Money is the oxygen of political capture, and the regime cuts both its supply - contribution caps, corporate prohibition - and its shadow - real-time, itemised, audited disclosure. Prohibiting state contractors from donating severs the direct quid pro quo channel documented in every serious procurement-fraud study. The seat-forfeiture sanction is the one that history shows changes behaviour: fines are budgeted as risk, lost seats are not.

## Notes for adopting states

- This Part is the zero-corruption architecture; it may be adopted alone as an organic statute where full constitutional reform is not yet possible.
- Build the registers before the courts: disclosure, beneficial ownership and open contracting are the foundation the prosecutions stand on.
- Expect resistance precisely where the old system was most profitable - that resistance is the diagnostic.
