# WC-103 · Anti-Bribery and Anti-Extortion

> **Part 37 — Integrity Branch** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 103/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** The classic offences, modernised for facilitation payments and chains

## Preamble

This document defines the bribery and extortion offences completely, abolishing the loopholes that survive in older codes.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Bribery defined

1.1 Active bribery: promising, offering or giving any undue advantage to a public official or candidate, directly or through intermediaries, for acting or refraining.

1.2 Passive bribery: requesting, agreeing to receive or accepting such advantage, including through family members or related entities.

1.3 The advantage is defined by function: money, goods, services, employment, contracts, forgiveness, discretion exercised in one's favour, quiet charity donations and sponsorship equivalents.

### Article 2 — Facilitation payments abolished

2.1 Small or routine facilitation payments are bribes; no de minimis carve-out exists.

2.2 Cultural or customary practice is no defence; the defence of duress exists only for genuine extortion, immediately reported.

2.3 Hospitality, gifts and travel connected to any official decision follow the zero rule of document 099.

### Article 3 — Extortion and demand-side

3.1 Extortion by officials - demanding advantages for lawful duties - is an aggravated corruption offence.

3.2 The coerced payer who reports promptly is protected and not charged, and may recover the payment.

3.3 Private-to-private bribery in commercial dealings is a distinct offence where damage flows to non-consenting owners.

### Article 4 — Chains and intermediaries

4.1 Using intermediaries, consultants, charitable foundations, agents or relatives is the same offence with the intermediary liable.

4.2 Ignorance of an intermediary's corruption is unavailable to principals with grossly negligent supervision.

4.3 Beneficial owners of bribing entities share criminal liability with the entity.

## Commentary

The abolition of facilitation payments follows the strictest modern statutes and the FCPA-Sapin convergence: every carve-out becomes the channel through which systems rot, because the small bribe trains the big one. Protecting the coerced payer who reports converts extortion victims into witnesses, which is the only practical detection route for demand-side corruption. Intermediary liability with gross-negligence exposure for principals closes the consultant structure through which most grand bribery is laundered.

## Notes for adopting states

- This Part is the zero-corruption architecture; it may be adopted alone as an organic statute where full constitutional reform is not yet possible.
- Build the registers before the courts: disclosure, beneficial ownership and open contracting are the foundation the prosecutions stand on.
- Expect resistance precisely where the old system was most profitable - that resistance is the diagnostic.
