# WC-085 · Commission Appointment and Tenure

> **Part 37 — Integrity Branch** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 085/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** How commissioners are chosen, protected and removed

## Preamble

This document defines the selection, protection and removal of the Integrity Commissioners so that no government can install or intimidate them.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Selection

1.1 The Commission has five members: the Chief Commissioner, two members with judicial or prosecutorial background, one forensic accountant or auditor, and one member from civil society.

1.2 Members are selected by an independent panel of retired appellate judges and integrity-council delegates after a public open call; finalists are heard in public parliamentary session; appointment is by the President without discretion.

1.3 One-time-only presidential refusal with reasons returns the file once.

### Article 2 — Terms

2.1 Members serve staggered, single, non-renewable eight-year terms, so no single government appoints a majority.

2.2 Salaries are set at the level of apex-court judges and never reduced.

2.3 Full-time incompatibility rules apply: no party membership, no private practice, no directorships.

### Article 3 — Removal

3.1 Removal only by the Constitutional Court for proven incapacity, grave misconduct or corruption, on referral by two thirds of Parliament or the Citizens' Oversight Assembly.

3.2 Suspension pending removal may be granted only by the Integrity Courts, for cause, thirty days at a time.

3.3 A removed member never serves in public office again.

### Article 4 — Vacancies and interim

4.1 Vacancies are filled within ninety days by the same panel procedure; the senior deputy acts in the interim with full powers but no policy changes.

4.2 Quorum rules keep the Commission functional at four members; below that, the Integrity Courts assume urgent caseload.

4.3 Staggering is preserved by appointing successors for the remainder of terms.

### Article 5 — Anti-Capture Amendment: Anti-Circularity Safeguard

5.1 At least two of the five members of the independent selection panel referred to in Article 1.2 shall be selected by random sortition from a certified pool of persons who have held no judicial, prosecutorial, or political office in the preceding ten years, so that the panel is not composed exclusively of appellate judges drawn from the same judiciary the Commission exists to check.

5.2 Removal under Article 3.1 on referral by two-thirds of Parliament requires, in addition, a reasoned finding by the Judicial Appointments Commission (05-judiciary/judicial-appointments.md, Article 5.5.6) that the referral is not retaliatory; this finding is published before any vote on removal proceeds.

## Amendment Note

*The original design in Articles 1–4 answers government capture of the Commission well, but leaves one circularity open: the selection panel in Article 1.2 is drawn from "retired appellate judges," and the judiciary that supplies them is itself subject to the capture risks addressed in 05-judiciary/judicial-appointments.md, Article 5.5.6. If that judiciary is compromised, a panel composed only of its alumni is not independent of the problem it is meant to check. Article 5.1 breaks the closed loop by mixing in sortition-selected members from outside the judicial and political class entirely. Article 5.2 answers the parallel risk that a broad-enough cross-party coalition in Parliament — which the "two-thirds" threshold in Article 3.1 assumes to be a safeguard — can in fact be exactly the kind of cartel this edition's Article 5.5.6 Design Note describes, united not by rivalry but by a shared interest in removing an inconvenient watchdog.*

## Commentary

Every serious regression story begins with the appointment capture of the integrity agency: packed panels, loyalists, revolving chairmen. The design answers with an independent panel, staggered single terms, salary floors, and judicial-only removal - the exact formula that kept supreme audit institutions alive under hostile governments. The public hearing of finalists borrows from judicial-appointment practice and turns candidate quality into a visible public fact. The interim rule denies the sabotage technique of leaving an agency headless and therefore immobile.
