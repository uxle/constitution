# WC-084 · The Integrity Commission

> **Part 04 — Integrity Branch - Zero Corruption** · Document **084 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** The operating agency: divisions, powers and duties

## Preamble

This document charters the Integrity Commission's four divisions and equips them with the powers that make corruption a high-risk, low-reward business.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Divisions

1.1 The Commission operates through: a Prevention Division that audits systems and designs controls; an Education Division that trains officials and informs citizens; an Investigations Division with full police powers; and a Prosecution Division that prosecutes corruption offences directly.

1.2 A Data Office fuses procurement, asset, company, customs and banking signals into risk models, governed by document 086 safeguards.

1.3 A Witness and Whistleblower Office administers rewards and protections under document 091.

### Article 2 — Core powers

2.1 The Commission may: require production of any document including bank, tax and company records under judicial confirmation within seventy-two hours; enter and search premises; arrest suspects; freeze accounts and assets; and operate covert surveillance under the warrant regime of document 027.

2.2 It may summon any person, including sitting officials, under the fast-track immunities procedure of document 102.

2.3 It may seize passports of flight-risk suspects for up to thirty days, renewable by the Integrity Courts.

### Article 3 — Direct prosecution

3.1 The Prosecution Division files and conducts corruption cases without the consent of any prosecutorial monopoly, ending the classic veto point of political prosecutors.

3.2 The general prosecutor retains parallel jurisdiction for non-corruption offences flowing from the same facts; conflicts are resolved by the Integrity Courts.

3.3 Plea cooperation and immunity-from-prosecution of minor participants require judicial approval and publication of reasons in aggregate.

### Article 4 — Obligations

4.1 The Commission answers every complaint in writing within fifteen days and publishes monthly statistics of intake, closure and duration.

4.2 Its annual report to Parliament details systemic weaknesses and names the institutions that failed to implement its prevention recommendations.

4.3 Failure to implement prevention recommendations within a statutory deadline is an integrity offence of the responsible official.

## Commentary

The decisive design choice is direct prosecution: comparative experience shows that where the anti-graft agency must hand cases to a politically-controlled prosecution service, the cases die in transfer. The fusion Data Office follows the success of pattern-detection in tax and customs administrations, where fraud is found by anomaly rather than complaint. Mandatory published statistics on case duration defeat the last refuge of corrupt systems, the investigation that formally exists but never ends.

## Notes for adopting states

- This Part is the zero-corruption architecture; it may be adopted alone as an organic statute where full constitutional reform is not yet possible.
- Build the registers before the courts: disclosure, beneficial ownership and open contracting are the foundation the prosecutions stand on.
- Expect resistance precisely where the old system was most profitable - that resistance is the diagnostic.
