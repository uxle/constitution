# WC-108 · Corruption Offenses: Definitions and Grading

> **Part 37 — Integrity Branch** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 108/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** The complete catalogue of corruption crimes with penalty classes

## Preamble

This document defines every corruption offence with grading into the penalty classes of Part 05, from petty bribery to grand corruption.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Catalogue

1.1 The corruption offences are: active and passive bribery; foreign official bribery; trading in influence; bribery of candidates and office-seekers; extortion by officials; embezzlement and misappropriation of public property; fraud against the treasury; abuse of office or discretion for gain; illegal enrichment under document 089; document falsification in official records; obstruction of integrity institutions; witness and source retaliation; procurement manipulation; conflict-of-interest profiteering; and laundering of corruption proceeds.

1.2 Each offence is defined in the penal document of Part 05 indicated by class; this document fixes names and grading.

1.3 Attempt and conspiracy are punished as completed offences.

### Article 2 — Grading

2.1 Petty corruption below three months' median wage: Class M with mandatory restitution and integrity training; public employment consequences follow regardless of amount.

2.2 Ordinary corruption: Class III to II felony by value and office.

2.3 Grand corruption - by officials above director level, amounts above fifty median annual wages, systemic schemes or harm to life, health or elections: Class I felony with the recovery and disqualification regimes.

### Article 3 — Aggravating and mitigating

3.1 Aggravated: emergency and disaster contexts, judicial or integrity-office abuse, abuse of aid or development funds, and recidivism.

3.2 Mitigating: full voluntary disclosure before discovery, complete restitution, and substantial cooperation with conviction of others.

3.3 Mitigation may never remove mandatory confiscation or disqualification.

### Article 4 — Consequences beyond prison

4.1 Mandatory disqualification from public office and public contracts for a term proportionate to class, from five years to lifetime.

4.2 Loss of honours, pensions forfeited above the contribution-funded part, and voidance of acts procured by the offence.

4.3 Forfeited proceeds follow the recovery priority of document 092.

## Commentary

A corruption catalogue with explicit grading does three jobs at once: it unifies the terminology that fragmented statutes scatter, it ties every offence to proportionate penalty classes, and it imports the doctrine of grand corruption into operational law. The consequence architecture beyond prison - disqualification, pension forfeiture, voidance - is where corruption is actually punished for incumbents, because careers and contracts matter more to them than months.

## Notes for adopting states

- This Part is the zero-corruption architecture; it may be adopted alone as an organic statute where full constitutional reform is not yet possible.
- Build the registers before the courts: disclosure, beneficial ownership and open contracting are the foundation the prosecutions stand on.
- Expect resistance precisely where the old system was most profitable - that resistance is the diagnostic.
