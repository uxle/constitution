# WC-098 · Lobbying Transparency

> **Part 37 — Integrity Branch** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 098/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Influence in the light: registers, footprints and cooling-off

## Preamble

This document regulates lobbying: registration, spending disclosure, a legislative footprint, and the ban on buying access.

## Articles

### Article 1 — The register

1.1 Professional lobbyists - in-house and agency - register before any advocacy, naming clients, subjects, bills and budgets.

1.2 Meetings with officeholders covered by the register are logged by the officeholder: date, participants, subject, position.

1.3 The register is public, free and machine-readable; acting while unregistered is an offence.

### Article 2 — The legislative footprint

2.1 Every bill publishes its footprint: which meetings were held with whom, and which submissions were received, at each stage.

2.2 Non-public drafts circulating in consultations are logged and published on introduction.

2.3 Ministers' and senior officials' diaries are published monthly under document 063.

### Article 3 — Access and gifts

3.1 Selling access is prohibited: paid meetings, sponsored travel, honoraria and think-tank funding in exchange for contact are offences for payer and receiver.

3.2 Gifts and hospitality to officeholders follow the zero threshold of document 099.

3.3 Charities and academia follow the register when advocacy is a paid activity.

### Article 4 — Cooling-off

4.1 Officeholders and senior officials may not lobby their former institution for five years; ministers for seven; procurement officials for five on their files.

4.2 Breaches void the lobbying privilege and are offences for both sides.

4.3 The register records cooling-off status to prevent accidental hiring into the ban.

## Commentary

Lobbying is legitimate and inevitable; invisible lobbying is where corruption incubates. The footprint device follows the EU transparency register's evolution and the US LARA-style state laws, and it answers the question every scandal raises: who was in the room when this clause was drafted. The five-to-seven-year cooling-off follows the Canadian regime, the longest well-tested, and voiding the privilege plus two-sided liability makes it enforceable in practice.

## Notes for adopting states

- This Part is the zero-corruption architecture; it may be adopted alone as an organic statute where full constitutional reform is not yet possible.
- Build the registers before the courts: disclosure, beneficial ownership and open contracting are the foundation the prosecutions stand on.
- Expect resistance precisely where the old system was most profitable - that resistance is the diagnostic.
