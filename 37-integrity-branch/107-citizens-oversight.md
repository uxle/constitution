# WC-107 · The Citizens' Oversight Assembly

> **Part 37 — Integrity Branch** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 107/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** The people's own integrity watchdog, selected by lot

## Preamble

This document creates a standing assembly of ordinary citizens with real powers to audit the integrity system itself.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Composition

1.1 The Citizens' Oversight Assembly comprises fifty members drawn annually by civic lottery, stratified by age, gender, region, education and income, from the volunteers and duty register.

1.2 Members are paid, trained in integrity practice, and barred from party or lobbying office for their term and three years after.

1.3 Members' names are public; their independence is protected by offence.

### Article 2 — Powers

2.1 The Assembly reviews: closed Integrity Commission case files where no charge followed, on a sampled and petition basis; the Commission's annual plan and budget proposal; systemic audit findings; and the integrity performance of institutions.

2.2 It hears complainants, whistleblowers and officials under confidentiality rules, with the power to require answers.

2.3 It issues public recommendations to the Commission, the Integrity Courts and Parliament, which must be answered in writing within sixty days.

### Article 3 — Checks on the watchdog

3.1 The Assembly may refer matters to the Integrity Courts and trigger the removal procedure for commissioners.

3.2 It cannot direct investigations or reveal sealed material; leaks are offences.

3.3 Its own operations are audited by the Auditor-General.

### Article 4 — Local assemblies

4.1 Regional and municipal oversight panels follow the same lottery model with local jurisdiction.

4.2 They review local procurement sampling, asset-disclosure anomalies and service quality.

4.3 Findings feed the national Integrity Index of document 290.

## Commentary

Who audits the auditor is the honest question about any integrity monopoly, and the answer here is the same random technology that legitimises juries: ordinary citizens with training, access and voice. The powers are calibrated - review, hearing, referral, trigger - without prosecutorial direction, preserving professional independence while ending self-certification. Local panels extend the same logic to the level where citizens personally experience corruption, which is where legitimacy is actually built.

## Notes for adopting states

- This Part is the zero-corruption architecture; it may be adopted alone as an organic statute where full constitutional reform is not yet possible.
- Build the registers before the courts: disclosure, beneficial ownership and open contracting are the foundation the prosecutions stand on.
- Expect resistance precisely where the old system was most profitable - that resistance is the diagnostic.
