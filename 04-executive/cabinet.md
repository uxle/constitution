# Section 4.3 — The Cabinet

> **Part 04 — The Executive** · Articles 4.3.1 – 4.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section organizes the Cabinet: the appointment of ministers from among members of the Parliament, collective responsibility, and the end of the Cabinet's mandate.*

## Article 4.3.1 — Appointment of Ministers

1. The President shall appoint and dismiss ministers on the proposal of the Prime Minister.
2. Ministers shall be members of the Parliament at the time of appointment.
3. The law may permit the appointment of a limited number of ministers who are not members of the Parliament; such ministers shall obtain a seat within six months or leave office.

## Article 4.3.2 — Collective Responsibility

1. The Cabinet shall decide the general policy of the Government.
2. Ministers shall bear collective responsibility for Cabinet decisions and shall defend them before the Parliament.
3. A minister who cannot support a Cabinet decision may resign; the resignation shall not suspend the decision.

## Article 4.3.3 — Deliberation and Records

1. The Cabinet shall be convened and chaired by the Prime Minister.
2. Its deliberations shall be confidential; its decisions shall be recorded in minutes.
3. The law may provide for Cabinet committees and for written procedure on routine matters.

## Article 4.3.4 — Portfolio Assignment

1. The Prime Minister shall allocate portfolios among the ministers and may reallocate them.
2. Every reorganization of portfolios shall be notified to the Lower House without delay.

## Article 4.3.5 — End of Mandate

1. The mandate of the Cabinet shall end on the first sitting of a newly elected Lower House, on the resignation or death of the Prime Minister, or on the loss of confidence.
2. A departing Cabinet shall manage only current business until a new Prime Minister is appointed.
3. During that period it shall not make major appointments or adopt measures of long-term effect.

## Design Note

*Collective responsibility gives the Parliament a single accountable government while leaving ministers real autonomy within their portfolios; the express right to resign disciplines the solidarity from within rather than abolishing it. The six-month rule for non-member ministers preserves parliamentary government against the gradual import of technocrats. The caretaker limits codify the widely shared convention that a departing government must not bind its successor.*
