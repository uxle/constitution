# Section 4.7 — Executive Accountability

> **Part 04 — The Executive** · Articles 4.7.1 – 4.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section makes the Government answerable to the Parliament through questions, committees, confidence, documents, and the personal responsibility of ministers.*

## Article 4.7.1 — Question Time

1. Ministers shall answer questions put by members of the Parliament.
2. The Lower House shall hold an oral question time at regular intervals fixed by its rules.
3. A minister shall not refuse to answer for reasons of convenience; refusal is permitted only where the law protects the information sought.

## Article 4.7.2 — Committee Scrutiny

1. Committees of the Parliament may summon ministers and public officials to appear before them.
2. Committees may require the production of documents, subject to Section 3.9 and to statutory protection of privacy and security.
3. A refusal shall be reasoned and may be reviewed by the House concerned.

## Article 4.7.3 — Confidence Responsibility

1. The Government shall hold the confidence of the Lower House.
2. The Lower House may call upon a minister to account for the minister's conduct.
3. The Prime Minister may put a question of confidence to the Lower House; its defeat shall entitle the Prime Minister to advise dissolution under Article 3.2.5 (Dissolution and Early Elections).

## Article 4.7.4 — Documents to Parliament

1. Ministers shall provide the Parliament with the information and documents necessary for its scrutiny.
2. Exceptions shall be fixed by law, shall be construed narrowly, and shall cover only Cabinet deliberations, individual privacy, and the security of the Republic.
3. The law shall provide an independent review of a refusal to disclose.

## Article 4.7.5 — Personal Responsibility

1. Each minister is personally responsible for the acts within the minister's portfolio and for the minister's own conduct.
2. Responsibility under clause 1 does not exclude the collective responsibility of the Cabinet.
3. Criminal liability of a minister shall not be barred by office; Sections 3.9 and 3.10 apply to ministers as members of the Parliament.

## Design Note

*Accountability is layered: oral questions for immediacy, committees for depth, confidence for survival, and personal responsibility so that collective cover cannot hide individual fault. The exceptions to document production are deliberately narrow and contestable, translating the transparency principles of Part 27 into enforceable parliamentary rights. The question of confidence survives alongside the constructive vote because a government that cannot legislate should be able to return its mandate to the voters.*
