# Section 4.8 — Emergency Powers

> **Part 04 — The Executive** · Articles 4.8.1 – 4.8.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section fixes the ordinary role of the executive within the emergency framework; the regime of the state of emergency itself is governed by Part 26.*

## Article 4.8.1 — Declaration and Countersignature

1. A state of emergency shall be declared by the President on the proposal of the Government.
2. The declaration shall require the countersignature of the Prime Minister.
3. It shall state the grounds, the territorial scope, and the measures intended, and shall be published without delay.
4. The police and the armed forces shall act under civilian authority during a state of emergency.

## Article 4.8.2 — Notification and Information of Parliament

1. The Government shall notify the Parliament of the declaration immediately and shall convene it without delay.
2. The Government shall report periodically to the Parliament on the measures taken, at intervals fixed by the emergency legislation.
3. The Parliament may at any time require information under Article 4.7.4 (Documents to Parliament).

## Article 4.8.3 — Compliance with Part 26

1. Measures taken in a state of emergency shall remain within the framework of Part 26.
2. The duration, approval, and renewal of the state of emergency shall follow the rules of Part 26.
3. The declaration shall lapse as provided by Part 26 unless approved by the Parliament.
4. Nothing in this Article extends the powers of the Government beyond those conferred by law.

## Article 4.8.4 — Accountability and Review

1. Acts of the executive under a state of emergency shall be subject to judicial review without delay.
2. The non-derogable core of rights under Section 1.1 shall remain inviolable.
3. Officials remain personally responsible for unlawful acts committed under a state of emergency.

## Design Note

*This Section is deliberately thin: Part 26 owns the emergency regime, so Part 04 states only who may declare, what must be told to the Parliament, and what must obey that regime. Countersignature prevents a ceremonial President from triggering emergency powers alone, and immediate judicial review reflects the settled lesson, embodied in the emergency constitution of the German Basic Law, that emergencies are precisely when courts must stay open.*
