# Section 4.1 — The President

> **Part 04 — The Executive** · Articles 4.1.1 – 4.1.7 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the President as head of state, elected by a joint sitting of the Parliament for a single term, with ceremonial duties, narrow reserve powers, and impeachment for grave violations.*

## Article 4.1.1 — Election

1. The President shall be elected by a joint sitting of both Houses.
2. A candidate shall be elected by a majority of all the members of both Houses.
3. If no candidate obtains that majority, a second round shall be held between the two leading candidates.

## Article 4.1.2 — Term of Office

1. The President shall serve a single term of six years.
2. The President shall not be eligible for re-election.
3. Before taking office, the President shall take an oath before the Constitutional Court to uphold this Constitution.

## Article 4.1.3 — Ceremonial Duties

1. The President shall represent the Republic at home and abroad.
2. The President shall confer the honours of the Republic as provided by law.
3. The President may address the Parliament and send messages to the nation on matters of common concern.

## Article 4.1.4 — Reserve Powers

1. The President may refer a bill back to the Parliament once, with written reasons, under Article 3.8.5 (Assent and Referral).
2. The President shall make appointments required to be made on the nomination of a body defined by this Constitution, and shall appoint judges on the binding recommendation of the Judicial Service Commission.
3. The President shall exercise no other power except as expressly conferred by this Constitution or by an Act.

## Article 4.1.5 — Countersignature

1. An act of the President shall require the countersignature of the Prime Minister in order to be valid.
2. Countersignature shall not be required where this Constitution provides otherwise.

## Article 4.1.6 — Impeachment

1. The President may be impeached for a grave violation of this Constitution, for treason, or for another grave offence against the Republic.
2. Indictment shall require a two-thirds majority of the members of the Lower House.
3. The Constitutional Court shall decide the charge; removal shall require the concurrence of two-thirds of its judges.
4. The President shall stand suspended while the charge is pending.

## Article 4.1.7 — Vacancy and Succession

1. The office shall become vacant on death, resignation, removal, or permanent incapacity.
2. The presiding officer of the Lower House shall act as President until a successor is elected.
3. A successor shall be elected under Article 4.1.1 (Election) within ninety days.

## Design Note

*The design places the President above daily politics: elected by the whole Parliament, limited to a single six-year term, and acting mainly through countersigned acts, like the neutral presidents of German and Italian practice. The single referral and the appointment powers are reserve powers that check but can never displace the parliamentary majority. Assigning the impeachment trial to the Constitutional Court keeps removal judicial rather than political, unlike bicameral trial models.*
