# Section 4.2 — The Prime Minister

> **Part 04 — The Executive** · Articles 4.2.1 – 4.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Prime Minister as head of government, holding the confidence of the Lower House and removable only through a constructive vote of no confidence.*

## Article 4.2.1 — Election and Confidence

1. The President shall propose to the Lower House a candidate for Prime Minister, after consulting the parliamentary groups.
2. The Lower House shall elect the Prime Minister by a majority of its members.
3. The President shall appoint the person elected.
4. If the Lower House does not elect a Prime Minister within twenty-one days after its first meeting following an election, or after the office falls vacant, the President shall dissolve the Lower House under Article 3.2.5 (Dissolution and Early Elections).
5. The Prime Minister shall be a member of the Lower House.

## Article 4.2.2 — Direction of Government

1. The Prime Minister shall head the Government and shall direct its policy.
2. The Prime Minister shall represent the Government in the Parliament.
3. The Prime Minister shall coordinate the work of the ministers, who shall retain responsibility for their portfolios.

## Article 4.2.3 — Countersignature

1. The Prime Minister shall countersign acts of the President, except where this Constitution provides otherwise.
2. An act countersigned under clause 1 shall be the responsibility of the Government.

## Article 4.2.4 — Constructive Vote of No Confidence

1. The Lower House may withdraw confidence in the Prime Minister only by electing a successor by the majority required in clause 2 of Article 4.2.1.
2. The election of the successor and the withdrawal of confidence shall occur in a single vote.
3. The President shall remove the Prime Minister and appoint the successor so elected.
4. A defeated motion shall not bar a later motion.

## Article 4.2.5 — Resignation and Succession

1. The Prime Minister may resign by notice to the President.
2. A Prime Minister whose resignation is accepted shall remain in office to manage current business until a successor is appointed.
3. During the temporary absence of the Prime Minister, a minister designated by the Prime Minister shall act in the office.

## Design Note

*The constructive vote obliges a hostile majority to agree on a replacement before it can remove the head of government — the mechanism the German Basic Law adopted to end the cabinet instability of earlier parliamentary systems. Formation begins with the President's proposal but is decided by the Lower House, which keeps the ceremonial head of state out of partisan bargaining. The twenty-one-day limit turns a prolonged formation crisis into a constitutional trigger for early elections rather than a hostage situation.*
