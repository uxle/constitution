# Section 4.4 — Ministries and Portfolios

> **Part 04 — The Executive** · Articles 4.4.1 – 4.4.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section keeps the map of the administration under law: the creation and abolition of ministries, the stability of portfolios, the coordination of government work, and ministerial accountability. It ties the machinery of government to the responsibility of ministers.*

## Article 4.4.1 — Creation and Abolition by Law

1. Ministries shall be created and abolished by Act of Parliament.
2. An Act establishing a ministry shall define its mandate and scope.
3. The Government may organize the internal structure of a ministry by decree under that Act.
4. Each ministry shall be headed by a minister, who shall be accountable for it under Article 4.4.4 (Accountability of Ministries).

## Article 4.4.2 — Stability of Portfolios

1. Portfolios shall be defined with sufficient stability to permit accountability and institutional memory.
2. A substantial reorganization of the ministries shall require an Act of Parliament.
3. The reasons for any reorganization shall be stated to the Lower House.

## Article 4.4.3 — Coordination Machinery

1. The Prime Minister shall coordinate the work of the ministries through the Cabinet and a Government Office.
2. The Government Office shall support the Cabinet, monitor the implementation of Cabinet decisions, and prepare their coordination.
3. The Cabinet may establish committees of ministers for the coordination of defined fields of policy.
4. The Government Office shall be headed by an official appointed by the Prime Minister.

## Article 4.4.4 — Accountability of Ministries

1. Each minister shall be accountable to the Parliament for the affairs of the minister's portfolio.
2. Each ministry shall have an accounting officer responsible for the legality and regularity of its expenditure, under Article 4.5.5 (Accountable Officers).
3. Ministries shall publish annual reports on their performance and shall present them to the Parliament.
4. The annual reports shall state the action taken on the findings of the Office of the Auditor-General.

## Design Note

*Requiring an Act to create or reshape ministries prevents a government from redrawing the administration to suit a coalition's convenience, while leaving internal organization to decree preserves day-to-day flexibility. Stability of portfolios is treated as a constitutional value because accountability to the Parliament presupposes a stable addressee: a minister cannot answer for a mandate that changes shape every season. Annual reporting and the accounting officer link each ministry into the audit architecture of Part 08, closing the circle from creation by statute to public explanation of results.*
