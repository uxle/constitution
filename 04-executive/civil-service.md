# Section 4.5 — The Public Service

> **Part 04 — The Executive** · Articles 4.5.1 – 4.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section organizes the Public Service of the Republic around merit, political neutrality, integrity, and protection from arbitrary dismissal.*

## Article 4.5.1 — Merit Recruitment

1. Admission to the Public Service shall be by open competition on the basis of merit, ability, and integrity.
2. Public employment shall be open to all citizens on equal terms.
3. The law shall regulate recruitment, advancement, and remuneration in the Public Service.

## Article 4.5.2 — Political Neutrality

1. Public servants shall act impartially and shall serve each successive Government with equal loyalty.
2. Public servants shall not use their office for partisan purposes.
3. The law may restrict the political activities of public servants in proportion to the sensitivity of their duties.

## Article 4.5.3 — Integrity Codes

1. The law shall provide codes of conduct for the Public Service.
2. Senior public servants shall declare their assets on terms comparable to those of Article 3.10.2 (Asset Disclosure).
3. The law shall protect persons who report wrongdoing within the Public Service in good faith.

## Article 4.5.4 — Protection from Arbitrary Dismissal

1. A public servant shall not be dismissed except for just cause established by a fair procedure.
2. A public servant shall be heard before dismissal and may appeal to a court.
3. Disciplinary measures shall be proportionate and reasoned.

## Article 4.5.5 — Accountable Officers

1. Each ministry and each public body shall have an accounting officer responsible for the legality and regularity of its expenditure.
2. Accounting officers shall answer to the committees of the Parliament and shall cooperate with the Office of the Auditor-General.

## Design Note

*A merit-based, neutral Public Service is the difference between a state that serves its laws and one that serves its incumbents; modern constitutions such as that of South Africa elevate public administration to constitutional rank for exactly this reason. Protection from arbitrary dismissal secures the neutrality the Section demands, since a servile service cannot tell the Government an unwelcome truth. The accounting officer figure welds administrative careers to parliamentary control of public money.*
