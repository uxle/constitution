# Section 4.6 — Executive Powers

> **Part 04 — The Executive** · Articles 4.6.1 – 4.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section defines the scope of executive power and its constitutional limits, including the regime for delegated legislation and judicial review of executive acts.*

## Article 4.6.1 — Scope of Executive Power

1. The executive power of the Republic shall be exercised by the Government, headed by the Prime Minister, in accordance with this Constitution and the law.
2. The powers of the President are governed by Section 4.1.
3. The executive shall faithfully implement this Constitution and the Acts of the Parliament.

## Article 4.6.2 — General Limits

1. The executive shall not impose or collect any tax except under an Act of the Parliament.
2. The executive shall not raise a loan except under statutory authorization.
3. The executive shall not order the detention of any person except under law and under judicial control.

## Article 4.6.3 — Delegated Legislation

1. The Government may make regulations and other instruments only under and within the limits of an Act of the Parliament.
2. Every instrument shall state its legal basis and the reasons for its issue.
3. An instrument shall not amend an Act or create an offence punishable by imprisonment.

## Article 4.6.4 — Parliamentary Oversight

1. Instruments of delegated legislation shall be laid before the Parliament without delay.
2. The Parliament may annul an instrument by resolution under its rules.
3. The law may provide for committees to review instruments before they take effect.

## Article 4.6.5 — Judicial Review of Executive Acts

1. Every act of the executive shall be subject to review by the courts for conformity with this Constitution and the law.
2. No Act may exclude such review in respect of constitutional questions.
3. Review under this Article is without prejudice to the jurisdiction of the Constitutional Court.

## Design Note

*Delegated legislation is inevitable in a modern state, but this Section hedges it with a legal basis, reasons, publication, and parliamentary annulment, so that delegation never becomes abdication. The express bans on executive taxation, borrowing, and detention state the classic rule-of-law boundary between government by law and government by order. The unwaivable reach of judicial review closes the last door through which a majority might otherwise walk past the courts.*
