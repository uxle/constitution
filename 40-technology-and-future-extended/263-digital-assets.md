# WC-263 · Crypto-Assets and Digital Money

> **Part 40 — Technology and Future Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 263/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Tokens, stablecoins and blockchain under honest finance rules

## Preamble

This document governs crypto-assets and distributed-ledger finance.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Classification

1.1 Assets classify as payment tokens, utility tokens, asset-referenced or e-money tokens, and security tokens - each to its regime.

1.2 Fully-backed stablecoins are e-money: reserve custody, redemption at par and daily disclosure are licence conditions.

1.3 Algorithmic unbacked stablecoins are prohibited as inherently deceptive.

### Article 2 — Market integrity

2.1 Exchanges and custodians are licensed with segregation of client assets, proof-of-reserves audits and capital.

2.2 Market abuse rules apply fully - insider dealing and manipulation including on-chain forms are document 132 offences.

2.3 AML rules follow document 105 with travel-rule compliance; privacy is balanced by lawful-investigation access with warrants.

### Article 3 — Consumer protection

3.1 Risk warnings, suitability checks and complaint channels are mandatory; aggressive influencer marketing is regulated.

3.2 Failure regimes assign orderly wind-down with client-asset priority.

3.3 No bailout: rescue never extends to token issuers' insolvency.

### Article 4 — Public ledger uses

4.1 Public registries may use DLT where it improves integrity - notarisation, procurement, voting audits - with open verification.

4.2 State e-currency follows document 189's cash-protective rules.

4.3 Energy-intensive consensus for new public uses is restricted by climate criteria under document 223.

## Commentary

The crypto chapter applies the corpus's oldest lesson to its newest assets: finance is honest when issuance is transparent, custody is segregated and abuse is crime - the same rules as banks, applied without fear or favour. Prohibiting algorithmic stablecoins follows their documented collapses; protecting client assets in failure follows every exchange bankruptcy. Public-ledger uses are welcomed exactly where they serve the transparency spine - procurement and registries.
