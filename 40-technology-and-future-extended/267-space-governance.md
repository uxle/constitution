# WC-267 · Space Governance

> **Part 40 — Technology and Future Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 267/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** The province of all: orbits, spectrum and celestial bodies

## Preamble

This document governs the state's space activities.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Principles

1.1 Space activities follow the Outer Space Treaty: exploration for all humankind, no national appropriation of celestial bodies, peaceful uses.

1.2 The state opposes weaponisation of space and supports debris-mitigation rules.

1.3 Spectrum and orbital slots are administered as scarce commons with transparent coordination.

### Article 2 — National activity

2.1 A space agency operates civilian programmes - earth observation, science, connectivity - with open data as default.

2.2 The state registers space objects, bears responsibility for national activities including private ones, and requires licence, insurance and debris plans.

2.3 Launch liability follows the liability convention with domestic recourse.

### Article 3 — Commercial

3.1 Commercial licences require safety, debris, spectrum and national-security review with published criteria.

3.2 Mega-constellation licensing assesses astronomy, debris and deorbit end-of-life duties.

3.3 Satellite earth-observation data sales respect international law and privacy limits on imaging.

### Article 4 — Benefit sharing

4.1 Earth-observation public-good data - disaster response, climate monitoring - are shared internationally.

4.2 Resource utilisation follows future international rules with benefit-sharing commitments; unilateral celestial property claims are void.

4.3 Space research results publish under document 031's open-access rules.

## Commentary

Orbit is the commons where tragedy-of-commons is measured in collision cascades, so the chapter internalises debris duty in every licence and deorbit in every end-of-life plan. Opposing weaponisation follows the corpus's abolitionist spine upward. Benefit-sharing clauses position the state on the right side of the resource-rush treaties now being written.
