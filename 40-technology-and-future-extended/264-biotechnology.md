# WC-264 · Biotechnology

> **Part 11 — Technology and the Future** · Document **264 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** Gene editing, synthetic biology and biosecurity

## Preamble

This document governs biotechnology within rights and safety limits.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Red lines

1.1 Germline editing for reproduction is prohibited; somatic therapies proceed under clinical-trial and ethics law.

1.2 Human cloning for reproduction and human-animal chimeras for organ harvesting are prohibited.

1.3 Non-consensual bio-interventions are crimes against bodily integrity under document 016.

### Article 2 — Governance

2.1 A bioethics council with pluralist membership reviews protocols and advises statutory rules.

2.2 Clinical applications follow the medicines regime of document 196 with gene-therapy vigilance.

2.3 Agricultural biotech follows the risk assessment and labelling of documents 214 and 217 with coexistence rules.

### Article 3 — Biosecurity

3.1 Dual-use research oversight with registry, risk tiers and international notification follows the pandemic-era lessons.

3.2 Gain-of-function experiments on dangerous pathogens require national ethics review and public register entries.

3.3 Biolabs are licensed, inspected and their inventories secured.

### Article 4 — Innovation access

4.1 Publicly funded biotech publishes data with patient-consent governance under document 258's commons.

4.2 Patent rules follow document 153 - no patents on natural genes; licensing for public-health needs.

4.3 Global access commitments accompany publicly-funded discoveries.

## Commentary

Biotech policy must be honest about both promises and dangers: cures and bioweapons ride the same science, so the chapter pairs research freedom with registries and tiered oversight. The germline red line follows the global consensus after the 2018 embryo-editing scandal. Commons publication and public-health licensing keep publicly-funded biology serving the many rather than becoming a toll booth.
