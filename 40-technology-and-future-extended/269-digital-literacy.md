# WC-269 · Digital Education and Literacy

> **Part 11 — Technology and the Future** · Document **269 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** The capability floor for digital citizenship

## Preamble

This document guarantees digital literacy as educational infrastructure.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Curriculum

1.1 Digital literacy is a compulsory curriculum strand from primary school: critical use, safety, rights, code basics and algorithmic awareness.

1.2 Media literacy - recognising manipulation, deepfakes and targeted influence - is embedded in civics under document 017.

1.3 Teacher training includes digital pedagogy with continuing development.

### Article 2 — Adult provision

2.1 Public adult digital training is free through libraries, municipalities and the learning accounts of document 197.

2.2 Elders receive targeted programmes with patient formats.

2.3 Employer co-funding of upskilling follows the levy rules of document 197.

### Article 3 — Tool access

3.1 Device and connectivity support for low-income students is a statutory programme.

3.2 Public computing points in libraries and municipal halls maintain assisted access.

3.3 Assistive technologies for disabled users are provided as of right.

### Article 4 — Public competence

4.1 The statistics office measures digital skills with published indicators by region and age.

4.2 Public-service design assumes and supports the least skilled user - no service may require skills beyond the literacy floor.

4.3 Digital-inclusion strategy reports annually to Parliament.

## Commentary

Digital rights without digital literacy are privileges for the connected, so the chapter builds the capability floor: curriculum, adult provision, devices and measurement. Designing public services to the floor - not the median - is the clause that keeps e-government from becoming a wall for the elderly and poor. Media literacy arrives as civics, because manipulation resistance is now a democratic survival skill.

## Notes for adopting states

- The technology chapters presume the data-protection framework; sequence document 026's statute before AI and platform rules.
- The algorithmic register can start small - every public body lists its five most consequential systems in year one.
- Digital-identity rollout must include the offline fallbacks from day one or the excluded will litigate rightly.
