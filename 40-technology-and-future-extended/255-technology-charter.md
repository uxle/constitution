# WC-255 · The Technology Charter

> **Part 11 — Technology and the Future** · Document **255 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** First principles for the digital age: human primacy, openness, security

## Preamble

This document states the constitutional principles governing all technology policy.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Primacy

1.1 Technology serves human beings; humans remain responsible for decisions that affect rights.

1.2 Human dignity and rights apply in full to digital life - the offline constitution is the online constitution.

1.3 No technology adoption may create unaccountable power over persons.

### Article 2 — Design duties

2.1 Public digital systems are accessible, multilingual, privacy-by-design and security-by-design.

2.2 Open standards and interoperability are defaults; source code of public systems is open subject to narrow security exceptions.

2.3 Data minimisation and purpose limitation are engineering requirements, not policy annexes.

### Article 3 — Oversight

3.1 An AI and Technology Authority registers consequential systems, audits them, and enforces the rules of this Part.

3.2 Parliament's technology committee reviews the register annually with public hearings.

3.3 Serious digital incidents are investigated with reports published.

### Article 4 — Innovation balance

4.1 Regulation is proportionate and innovation-friendly for low-risk uses; strict for high-risk uses with rights at stake.

4.2 Sandboxes with safeguards let startups test under supervision.

4.3 Public investment in sovereign capability - compute, models, skills - follows the utility rules of document 260.

## Commentary

The charter is the meta-rule of the Part: technology receives no exemption from the constitution and no vague deference either - it is registered, audited and classified by risk. The human-responsibility clause anchors every later document: automation may advise, but someone answerable decides. Sovereign capability in compute and models is treated as infrastructure because dependency on foreign black boxes became a strategic risk the corpus refuses to ignore.

## Notes for adopting states

- The technology chapters presume the data-protection framework; sequence document 026's statute before AI and platform rules.
- The algorithmic register can start small - every public body lists its five most consequential systems in year one.
- Digital-identity rollout must include the offline fallbacks from day one or the excluded will litigate rightly.
