# Amendment History

*About this annex: this annex records how the Constitution itself has changed and provides the template for recording every future change. It is part of the living governance of the text: an amendment that is not recorded here is not part of the authorized corpus.*

**Coverage note (added in the v1.0.1 editorial audit):** This annex covers Parts 00–30 only. Parts 31–42 — Government Eligibility, Animal and Plant Rights, Hills/Mountains and Geography, Low Violence and Public Safety, Clean Cities and Sanitation, Media Integrity, the Integrity Branch, Penal Code Extended, Security and International Extended, Technology and Future Extended, Economic Order Extended, and Society and Culture Extended (116 files, roughly a third of the Constitution) — were merged into this edition without corresponding updates here, contrary to `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s synchronization requirement. This gap is flagged, not yet fixed.

## The adoption record

Version 1.0.0 is the initial publication of the Constitution. It was adopted on **2026-08-31** and published as a complete corpus of **248 files**: 229 section files across the 31 numbered Parts, 7 root documents, 6 annexes, and 6 research notes. Within the Constitution's own narrative, the text is adopted by the people in a national referendum, having been deliberated upon and enacted by a freely elected constituent assembly, and it enters into force upon the proclamation of its ratification (Section 0.1). The transition from any prior legal order to this Constitution is governed by Section 30.4 (Transition Rules), which preserves existing law only insofar as it is consistent with the new text.

## How the Constitution may be changed

The amendment rules are deliberately tiered, and any future row in the history table below must identify which tier was used. Ordinary amendments require a two-thirds majority of each House (Section 25.1). Entrenched provisions — the republican and democratic form of the state, human dignity, equality, judicial independence, the non-derogable rights, and the amendment rules themselves, as listed in Section 30.5 — require the same two-thirds majorities plus a national referendum majority, and that referendum is mandatory by force of Article 6.7.3. Matters in the Schedules may be adjusted by simple majority, because they carry operational rather than constitutional weight. No amendment may be adopted during a state of emergency, and the emergency Part cannot be amended while the emergency lasts (Section 26.5).

## Rules for completing the table

Future contributors should follow five conventions. First, version numbers increase in whole steps for constitutional amendments and in minor steps for editorial corrections that change no norm; the version column records what was actually published. Second, the "Sections affected" column lists the section numbers touched, using the Part.Section scheme (for example, "Sections 3.2, 25.1"), so that the table doubles as an index of change. Third, the summary states the legal effect of the amendment in one sentence, not its political history. Fourth, the "Adopted by" column records the organ and majority — for example, a two-thirds majority of each House, or that majority plus a referendum majority for entrenched provisions. Fifth, a row is appended only after the adopting procedure has fully concluded and the amending text has been published as part of the corpus; an amendment is not "adopted" because it has been proposed, passed one House, or been announced.

Because the amendment rules are themselves entrenched, this annex cannot be used to smuggle change: any proposal to alter the tiers, the entrenched list, or the recording duties must clear the highest gate the Constitution provides. The table's plainness is the point. A reader who wishes to know whether a provision is original or amended compares the current text with the row that last touched its Section — nothing more is required.
