# Implementation Roadmap

*About this annex: this annex sets out a phased roadmap for bringing the Constitution into full operation, from entry into force to the first scheduled constitutional review. Milestones are relative to entry into force rather than tied to calendar dates, because the Constitution deliberately uses generic expressions of time.*

**Coverage note (added in the v1.0.1 editorial audit):** This annex covers Parts 00–30 only. Parts 31–42 — Government Eligibility, Animal and Plant Rights, Hills/Mountains and Geography, Low Violence and Public Safety, Clean Cities and Sanitation, Media Integrity, the Integrity Branch, Penal Code Extended, Security and International Extended, Technology and Future Extended, Economic Order Extended, and Society and Culture Extended (116 files, roughly a third of the Constitution) — were merged into this edition without corresponding updates here, contrary to `CONTRIBUTING.md`'s synchronization requirement. This gap is flagged, not yet fixed.

The roadmap serves two audiences. For the educator, it shows how a constitution's norms become a working state: institutions must exist before mandates can be exercised, and rights are justiciable from the first day even while implementing statutes lag behind. For the contributor, it identifies which Sections carry sequencing weight — the transition rules of Section 30.4, the establishment of courts and independent institutions, and the review mechanism of Section 25.4 — so that citations and classroom exercises can follow the same order.

| Phase | Focus | Key actions | Anchor sections |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Phase 0 | Entry into force | Proclaim ratification and bring the Constitution into force; preserve existing law only insofar as consistent; adopt interim measures; begin first appointments; confirm that all existing statutes and acts are subject to the supremacy clause and judicial review from day one. | Section 0.1; Section 0.8; Section 30.4 |
| Phase 1 | Institution building | Constitute the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court; establish the Judicial Service Commission with binding recommendations; appoint the Public Prosecutor; establish the independent institutions of Part 20 (Ombudsman, Human Rights Commission, Anti-Corruption Commission, Electoral Commission, Auditor-General, Statistics Authority, Information Commission); establish Central Bank governance, the independent fiscal council, and the competition authority; appoint the Boundary Commission. | Sections 5.2–5.5; Section 5.8; Sections 20.1–20.7; Section 8.4; Section 8.7; Section 9.2; Section 30.2 |
| Phase 2 | Legislation backlog | Enact the priority statutes that the Constitution mandates but leaves to ordinary law: the consolidated Criminal Code; data-protection and artificial-intelligence law; freedom-of-information procedures; social-protection frameworks; education and health financing laws; assess and conform the inherited statute book through the consistency rule of the transition provisions. | Section 14.1; Sections 17.2 and 17.4; Section 27.2; Section 22.1; Sections 11.2 and 12.1; Section 30.4 |
| Phase 3 | Devolution of functions | Elect Provincial Councils and Municipal councils under the Electoral Commission; transfer devolved competences under the subsidiarity principle; constitute Regions as cooperative groupings for shared services; establish equalization transfers and the no-unfunded-mandates rule in local finance; hold the first common local election day. | Sections 7.1–7.4; Section 7.5; Section 7.6 |
| Phase 4 | Fiscal frameworks | Institutionalize the annual budget adopted before the fiscal year; adopt the debt strategy and statutory sustainability limits; operate open, competitive procurement with its independent review body; publish monthly execution data; capitalize the sovereign wealth fund from resource revenues; align local borrowing limits with the national framework. | Sections 8.2–8.6; Section 13.8; Section 7.5 |
| Phase 5 | First constitutional review | Convene the Constitutional Review Commission at the constitutionally fixed interval; conduct public participation; process proposals through the ordinary and entrenched amendment tiers; consider the constitutional-convention route where an initiative matures; record any adopted change in the amendment history. | Section 25.4; Section 25.1; Section 25.2 |

Three sequencing principles explain the order. First, **institutions before mandates**: the courts, the Electoral Commission, and the Part 20 institutions must exist before the functions that depend on them — election administration, audit, data supervision — can lawfully be exercised. Second, **rights first**: nothing in the roadmap delays the justiciability of Part 01; the master limitation clause (Article 1.1.4) and the individual complaint (Article 5.3.4) operate immediately, even while Phase 2 legislation is outstanding, and courts apply existing law through the constitutional lens in the interim. Third, **review is scheduled, not improvised**: the fifteen-year review cycle of Section 25.4 is fixed in advance so that amendment pressure accumulates toward a legitimate, participatory moment instead of fragmenting into ad hoc change.

The phases overlap in practice, and the table records emphasis, not exclusivity. Local elections in Phase 3, for example, cannot wait for every Phase 2 statute; the Electoral Commission administers them under the constitutional integrity standards as soon as Provinces and Municipalities are constituted. What the roadmap forbids is reversal of dependencies: no fiscal framework without an Auditor-General, no devolution without an Electoral Commission, no review without the amendment tiers of Section 25.1.
