# Consolidated Legal Definitions

*About this annex: this annex collects the working vocabulary of the Constitution in one place so that educators and students can resolve every capitalized term without searching the corpus. It is a reading aid, not a source of law: the binding meaning of each term is fixed by the provision cited in the table, which would govern over any divergence here.*

The Constitution uses a deliberately small, load-bearing vocabulary. Institutions are capitalized exactly as defined — "the Lower House", never "the lower house" — while generic words carry their ordinary meaning unless a provision gives them a specific one. Each definition lives in the Section where its term does most of its work, and where one institution appears in two places — the Electoral Commission by function in Section 3.5 and by composition in Section 20.4 — the provisions cross-reference each other rather than duplicate definitions.

| Term | Meaning as used in this Constitution | Primary location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| the Republic | The unitary, democratic state whose Constitution this is; a neutral name for no real country. | Section 0.1; Article 2.1.1 |
| organ of the State | Any body or person exercising a public power or performing a public function under the Constitution or a law. | Article 1.1.3 |
| the Parliament | The bicameral supreme legislative authority, comprising the Lower House and the Upper House. | Article 3.1.1 |
| the Lower House | The confidence chamber: 400 members, four-year terms, proportional representation with open lists and a 3% national threshold. | Article 3.2.1 |
| the Upper House | The territorial chamber: three members per Province, elected by the Provincial Councils for staggered six-year terms, with delaying powers only. | Article 3.3.1 |
| the President | The head of state, elected by a joint sitting for a single six-year term, with ceremonial duties and narrow reserve powers. | Section 4.1 |
| the Prime Minister | The head of government: a member of the Lower House who must hold its confidence. | Article 4.2.1 |
| the Cabinet | The college of ministers appointed by the President on the Prime Minister's advice, bound by collective responsibility. | Article 4.3.1 |
| constructive vote of no confidence | A motion that displaces the Prime Minister only when the Lower House simultaneously elects a successor. | Article 4.2.4 |
| money bill | A bill whose sole purpose is taxation, appropriation, borrowing, or debt management, certified by the Lower House presiding officer subject to Constitutional Court determination. | Article 3.3.5 |
| the Constitutional Court | The highest organ for the protection of the Constitution: eleven judges on single non-renewable twelve-year terms, holding exclusive jurisdiction over constitutional questions. | Article 5.3.1 |
| the Supreme Court | The apex court of the general court system: final appeals and unification of case law. | Article 5.2.1 |
| the Judicial Service Commission | The independent commission that nominates judges through transparent, merit-based hearings; its recommendations bind the President. | Section 5.5 |
| the Public Prosecution Service | The prosecution authority, independent of the executive, headed by the Public Prosecutor serving a single seven-year term. | Article 5.8.1 |
| the Electoral Commission | The independent commission administering all national and local elections and referendums; its status is fixed in Part 03 and its composition in Part 20. | Section 3.5; Section 20.4 |
| the Central Bank | The independent monetary authority with the primary mandate of price stability and exclusive note issue. | Article 8.4.1 |
| the national currency | The money of the Republic; its name is left to statute, and its issue is monopolized by the Central Bank. | Article 8.4.3 |
| the Office of the Auditor-General | The independent supreme audit institution, auditing all public funds and reporting to Parliament. | Section 20.5; Article 8.8.2 |
| the Ombudsman | The independent institution investigating maladministration, on complaint from any person and on its own initiative. | Section 20.1 |
| the Human Rights Commission | The independent national human-rights institution for monitoring, education, and advice, on the Paris Principles model. | Section 20.2 |
| the Anti-Corruption Commission | The independent institution preventing and investigating corruption offenses, prosecuting jointly with the Public Prosecution Service. | Section 20.3 |
| the Information Commission | The single supervisor of both data protection and access to information, with order-making and fining powers subject to appeal. | Section 20.7 |
| the Statistics Authority | The independent authority guaranteeing the professional independence, quality, and confidentiality of official statistics. | Section 20.6 |
| Province | The primary subnational unit of the unitary state, governed by a directly elected Provincial Council. | Article 7.1.1 |
| Region | A cooperative grouping of Provinces for shared services, with voluntary composition and no separate sovereignty. | Article 7.2.1 |
| Municipality | The local tier of government, holding a constitutional right of self-government over local matters. | Article 7.3.1 |
| constitutional body | An institution created by the Constitution itself, creatable or abolishable only by constitutional amendment. | Section 29.1 |
| law of general application | A published legislative act of general scope; the only instrument by which rights may be limited under the master clause. | Article 1.1.4 |
| non-derogable right | A right listed in Article 1.1.5 that may never be suspended, even in a declared emergency; a purporting suspension is void from adoption. | Article 1.1.5 |
| state of emergency | The crisis regime declared by the President with countersignature, lapsing after 30 days unless Parliament approves, capped at six months per twelve-month period absent war. | Section 26.1 |
| entrenched provision | A provision listed in Section 30.5, amendable only by a two-thirds majority of each House plus a national referendum majority. | Section 30.5; Section 25.1 |
| referendum | A binding nationwide vote, triggered by Parliament, by citizens' initiative, or mandatorily for entrenched amendments. | Section 6.7 |
| independent fiscal council | The independent assessor of fiscal policy and of compliance with the statutory reference targets. | Article 8.7.3 |
| sovereign wealth fund | The constitutionally mandated fund receiving revenues from publicly owned natural resources for present and future generations. | Article 13.8.3 |
| peremptory norm (jus cogens) | A norm of international law recognized by the international community as a whole as non-derogable and supreme within the Republic's legal order. | Article 19.3.2 |

Three drafting habits explain the table's shape. First, definitions operate as interpretive rules: where a term is defined, the definition governs even against loose ordinary usage. Second, no definition is repeated; provisions that need a term cite it instead. Third, operational detail is left to statute — the fiscal year, the currency's name, and the number of Provinces — so this annex stays complete without inventing facts the Constitution declines to fix.
