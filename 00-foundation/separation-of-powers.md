# Section 0.5 — Separation of Powers

> **Part 00 — Foundation** · Articles 0.5.1 – 0.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section allocates the legislative, executive, and judicial functions to distinct organs and completes the allocation with checks, balances, and independent institutions.*

## Article 0.5.1 — Allocation of Powers

1. Legislative power vests in the Parliament; executive power in the President, the Prime Minister, and the Cabinet; judicial power in the courts.
2. Each organ shall exercise the functions this Constitution assigns to it and no others.
3. Functions may be shared or delegated only as this Constitution or a law adopted under it expressly provides.

## Article 0.5.2 — Checks and Balances

1. The allocation of powers is completed by the checks this Constitution establishes, including the confidence of the Lower House in the Prime Minister, the President's single power to refer a Bill back to Parliament, the Upper House's power to delay legislation, the review of laws by the courts, and the mutual accountability of the organs of the State.
2. No check shall be exercised so as to nullify the essential constitutional function of another organ.
3. Each organ shall cooperate in good faith so that the constitutional order remains functional.

## Article 0.5.3 — Independence of the Judiciary

1. The courts are subject only to this Constitution and to the law.
2. No organ of the State and no person may interfere with a pending judicial decision or give binding instructions to a judge in a case.
3. The tenure, appointment, and financial guarantees that protect judicial independence are stated in Part 05 (The Judiciary).

## Article 0.5.4 — Independent Institutions

1. The Ombudsman, the Human Rights Commission, the Anti-Corruption Commission, the Electoral Commission, the Office of the Auditor-General, the Statistics Authority, and the Information Commission shall be independent in the exercise of their mandates and are not subordinate to any other organ.
2. Their composition, tenure, and accountability are governed by Part 20 (Independent Institutions).
3. Their budgets shall secure their operational autonomy, and their reports shall be published.

## Article 0.5.5 — Limits of Delegation

1. The essential elements of the legislative function shall not be delegated to the executive; the executive shall not delegate to private persons decisions that bind the public; and adjudication shall not be withdrawn from the courts except as this Constitution expressly provides.
2. Every delegation shall be accompanied by parliamentary oversight and by clear standards limiting the discretion conferred.

## Design Note

*The Section assigns powers by function but immediately supplies concrete checks, reflecting the view, shared by most modern constitutions, that a catalogue of separations without checks is ornamental. Independent institutions receive constitutional rank as a fourth branch in function rather than in name, comparable to the independent administrative and electoral authorities that twentieth-century constitutions developed. The limits of delegation preserve the heart of each function while allowing the practical flexibility a modern state requires.*
