# Section 0.4 — Rule of Law

> **Part 00 — Foundation** · Articles 0.4.1 – 0.4.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the rule of law: legality, legal certainty, non-retroactivity, the prohibition of arbitrariness, equality before the law, and judicial protection.*

## Article 0.4.1 — Legality

1. Public power may be exercised only on the basis of a law adopted by a competent organ in the manner this Constitution prescribes.
2. Every restriction of a right, every imposition of a duty, and every withdrawal of a benefit requires such a legal basis.
3. Norms of general application shall be adopted by the Parliament, save where this Constitution confers a normative power on another organ.

## Article 0.4.2 — Legal Certainty and Accessibility

1. All laws shall be published, accessible, clear, and stable.
2. No norm shall take effect before its official publication.
3. A provision whose meaning a person of ordinary understanding cannot discern shall be construed to its narrower consistent effect.

## Article 0.4.3 — Non-Retroactivity

1. No person shall be convicted or punished for an act that did not constitute an offense when committed; a later law may reduce a penalty already imposed.
2. Laws shall operate prospectively, save where expressly provided otherwise for compelling reasons of general interest.
3. Retroactive civil or administrative effects are permissible only where necessary and consistent with legitimate expectations and Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Article 0.4.4 — Prohibition of Arbitrariness

1. Every exercise of public power shall rest on an adequate legal and factual basis and shall bear a reasonable relationship to its purpose.
2. A decision is arbitrary where it is taken without reasons, on irrelevant considerations, in bad faith, or in disregard of relevant considerations.

## Article 0.4.5 — Equality Before the Law

1. All persons are equal before the law and are entitled to its equal protection and benefit.
2. Public power shall be exercised without favor or disfavor toward any person or group, save for distinctions permitted by this Constitution.

## Article 0.4.6 — Judicial Protection and Legitimate Expectations

1. Every person whose rights or interests are affected by an exercise of public power has the right to an effective judicial remedy.
2. Legitimate expectations arising from definitive administrative decisions shall be protected; their override requires a legal basis and justification under Article 1.1.4.
3. Access to a remedy shall not be withdrawn, suspended, or rendered impracticable in fact or in law.

## Design Note

*This Section consolidates the rule of law in one place so that later Parts may invoke it without restating it, treating legality, certainty, and judicial protection as a single doctrine rather than several. The narrow retroactivity exceptions — the humane reduction of penalties and the rare civil case justified by compelling general interest — track settled comparative practice while keeping the general rule strict. The protection of legitimate expectations is included because administrative reliability is a dimension of the rule of law that judicial guarantees alone do not capture.*
