# Section 0.7 — Human Dignity

> **Part 00 — Foundation** · Articles 0.7.1 – 0.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section declares human dignity inviolable, makes it the source of all rights, and extends its protection to science and new technologies.*

## Article 0.7.1 — Inviolability of Human Dignity

1. Human dignity is inviolable. It is the supreme value of the Republic and the source of every right and duty this Constitution recognizes.
2. The dignity of every person shall be respected by all organs of the State and, where capable of application, by all persons and institutions.
3. No circumstance, including a state of emergency, may justify treating a person as a means to an end or as an object of disposal.

## Article 0.7.2 — Source of Rights and Duties

1. The rights of Part 01 (Rights and Freedoms) apply human dignity to particular fields; they reinforce one another and shall be read together.
2. Because dignity is universal, rights are universal and belong to every person without distinction.
3. The recognition of a right entails the recognition of the correlative duties of others and of the State.

## Article 0.7.3 — Prohibition of Degrading Treatment

1. No person shall be subjected to torture or to cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment.
2. Public institutions shall be organized and administered so as to prevent degradation, above all in places of detention.

## Article 0.7.4 — Dignity in Science and Technology

1. Scientific research and the application of new technologies shall respect human dignity; the human person shall be treated as an end and never merely as a means.
2. The Parliament shall regulate fields in which science or technology may impair dignity, including research involving human beings, the processing of biological and genetic data, and automated decision-making.
3. Such regulation shall not obstruct inquiry or innovation beyond what Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights) justifies.

## Article 0.7.5 — Respect for Dignity by Private Persons

1. Every natural and juristic person shall respect the dignity of others in their relations, where such application is capable of realization.
2. The State shall protect persons against degradation by private actors, including through the remedies provided in Part 16 (Civil Law).

## Design Note

*Placing dignity first, before liberty and equality, follows the ordering made famous by the Basic Law of Germany, drafted as a response to systematic degradation, and now shared by many constitutions written after 1990. The express treatment of science and technology is forward-looking: it anticipates disputes over biotechnology and automated decision-making that older texts left to interpretation. The trade-off is an open-textured standard that courts must concretize, accepted in exchange for a guarantee that will not become obsolete as circumstances change.*
