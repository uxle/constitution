# Section 0.6 — Democracy

> **Part 00 — Foundation** · Articles 0.6.1 – 0.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes representative, participatory, and direct democracy as the method of public life in the Republic.*

## Article 0.6.1 — Representative Democracy

1. The Republic is a representative democracy; the Parliament, elected by universal suffrage, represents the whole people.
2. Members of the Parliament represent the whole people and are bound only by their conscience.
3. Representatives remain accountable to the voters through periodic elections and through the scrutiny mechanisms of the Parliament.

## Article 0.6.2 — Free and Periodic Elections

1. Every citizen who has reached the age of eighteen years has the right to vote in elections and referendums.
2. Elections shall be free, secret, periodic, and fair, and shall be administered by the Electoral Commission established under Part 20 (Independent Institutions).
3. Voting is a civic duty; its discharge shall remain voluntary.

## Article 0.6.3 — Political Pluralism

1. Political parties may be formed freely, shall compete on equal terms, and shall conform to democratic principles in their internal organization.
2. A party may be prohibited only where its aims or conduct incite violence or seek to destroy the democratic order, in the manner and by the organ this Constitution provides.
3. Membership of a political party shall be voluntary and open, without discrimination.

## Article 0.6.4 — Civil Society and Participation

1. Associations, trade unions, and other organizations of civil society may be formed freely and may express views on public affairs.
2. The State shall provide opportunities for public participation in the preparation of legislation and of major policy, and shall consider the results in good faith.

## Article 0.6.5 — Direct Democracy

1. The people may act directly through national referendums in the cases and manner provided by Part 06 (Elections and Referendums).
2. A referendum result within its constitutional competence is binding and shall be implemented by the competent organs.
3. A referendum shall not be used to evade the amendment thresholds this Constitution establishes, including those protecting entrenched provisions under Part 30 (Schedules).

## Design Note

*The Section describes voting as both a civic right and a civic duty while leaving its discharge voluntary, a compromise that borrows the language of civic obligation without adopting compulsory voting. Representative, participatory, and direct democracy are given equal constitutional dignity, so that referendums supplement rather than replace parliamentary deliberation. The final safeguard, barring referendums from bypassing the amendment thresholds of Part 30 (Schedules), protects the entrenchment scheme that keeps core commitments beyond the reach of a single campaign.*
