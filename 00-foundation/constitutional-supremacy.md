# Section 0.8 — Constitutional Supremacy

> **Part 00 — Foundation** · Articles 0.8.1 – 0.8.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Constitution as supreme law, the invalidation of conflicting law, the duty to uphold it, conforming interpretation, and the hierarchy of norms.*

## Article 0.8.1 — Supreme Law

1. This Constitution is the supreme law of the Republic.
2. Any law, act, or practice inconsistent with it is without force or effect to the extent of the inconsistency.
3. Obligations arising outside this Constitution bind within the legal order only through and subject to this Constitution, as provided by law.

## Article 0.8.2 — Invalidation of Conflicting Law

1. Every court, in a case properly before it, shall refuse to apply a provision of law inconsistent with this Constitution.
2. The general invalidation of a provision, and other abstract questions of validity, belong to the Constitutional Court in the manner provided by Part 05 (The Judiciary).
3. A declaration of invalidity takes effect from the moment and in the manner the deciding court specifies, with due regard to legality and to reliance interests.

## Article 0.8.3 — Duty to Uphold the Constitution

1. Every organ of the State, every person exercising public power, and every member of an institution established by this Constitution shall uphold and defend this Constitution.
2. Obedience to a superior order shall not excuse a violation of this Constitution.
3. The persons designated by this Constitution or by law shall take an oath or solemn undertaking to respect the constitutional order.

## Article 0.8.4 — Conforming Interpretation

1. Every law shall, where its language permits, be read in the way that best conforms to this Constitution.
2. Where no conforming reading is reasonably possible, the deciding court shall decline to apply the provision to the extent of the inconsistency.
3. Conforming interpretation shall not be used to create obligations or prohibitions that the enacted text does not bear.

## Article 0.8.5 — Hierarchy of Norms

1. The Constitution prevails over statute; statute prevails over delegated legislation; delegated legislation prevails over by-laws and other subordinate norms.
2. A subordinate norm shall remain within the delegation from which it derives and consistent with the superior norm.
3. A conflict between norms of equal rank is resolved by the later or the more specific norm, subject to judicial control.

## Design Note

*The Section combines two techniques of constitutional supremacy often found separately: refusal of application by any court in a concrete case, in the diffuse-review tradition, and centralized abstract control by a Constitutional Court, in the continental tradition. Conforming interpretation is imposed as a duty before invalidation is reached, which reduces the disruptive consequences of striking down legislation. The hierarchy of norms makes explicit what many constitutions leave implied, helping students locate every norm within one ordered structure.*
