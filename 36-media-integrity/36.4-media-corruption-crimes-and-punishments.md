# Section 36.4 — Media Corruption: Crimes and Punishments

## Articles

**Article 36.4.1 (Bribery of Journalists or Editors).** Paying or accepting payment to suppress, distort, or fabricate news coverage is a corruption offense under Part 37, carrying licence revocation for the outlet and personal criminal liability for the individuals involved.

**Article 36.4.2 (Government Bribery of Media).** A government official who channels public funds or advertising to a media outlet in exchange for favorable coverage commits an aggravated corruption offense under Part 37, with mandatory referral to the Integrity Courts.

**Article 36.4.3 (Undisclosed State Propaganda).** State-funded content presented to the public as independent journalism, without disclosure of its government funding, is a disinformation offense under Article 36.3.

*Design Note: This Article is where Parts 36 and 37 (Integrity Branch) meet directly — media corruption is prosecuted exactly like any other corruption offense, with the same courts, penalties, and asset-recovery tools, rather than being treated as a separate or lesser category.*

**Cross-references:** 36.1, 36.2, 37-integrity-branch, 38-penal-code-extended


## Implementation Detail

**Article 36.4.4 (Penalty Schedule).** Bribery of journalists or editors (36.4.1): licence suspension of 1–5 years and personal fine up to twice the bribe value. Government bribery of media (36.4.2): mandatory referral to the Integrity Courts, minimum 2-year custodial sentence for the officials involved, and permanent disqualification under Article 31.7.
