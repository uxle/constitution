# Section 13.7 — Pollution

> **Part 13 — Environment and Natural Resources** · Articles 13.7.1 – 13.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section orders pollution control around prevention, the polluter-pays principle, strict liability for hazardous activities, and a duty of remediation.*

## Article 13.7.1 — Prevention First

1. Pollution shall be prevented or reduced at source, giving priority to prevention over remediation.
2. Discharges and emissions may be authorized only within limits set by law, applying the best available techniques and without unreasonable transference of pollution between environmental media.

## Article 13.7.2 — Polluter Pays

1. The costs of preventing, controlling, and remediating pollution shall be borne by the polluter.
2. Charges and levies may be imposed on emissions and discharges to internalize environmental costs.
3. Charges shall not confer any right to pollute beyond the limits set by law.

## Article 13.7.3 — Strict Liability

1. Operators of hazardous activities shall be strictly liable for environmental damage caused by those activities, without need to prove fault.
2. Operators of defined hazardous activities shall hold financial security adequate for the potential damage, as the law provides.

## Article 13.7.4 — Remediation Duty

1. Persons responsible for soil, water, or groundwater contamination shall remediate it to the standard required by law.
2. Where no responsible person can be identified or is unable to act, the State shall undertake remediation and may recover the costs from any responsible party later identified.
3. Contaminated sites shall be inventoried, and priority risks shall be addressed within time limits set by law.

## Article 13.7.5 — Hazardous Substances and Waste

1. Hazardous substances shall be registered, assessed, and, where unacceptable risks are found, restricted or prohibited.
2. Waste shall be managed according to the hierarchy of prevention, reuse, recycling, recovery, and safe disposal, and transboundary movements of hazardous waste shall be controlled by law.

## Design Note

*The section adopts the classical prevention-first ordering because remediation is always costlier and less complete than avoidance. Strict liability for hazardous activities, complemented by mandatory financial security, allocates the risk to those who create it and spare victims the near-impossible burden of proving fault in complex industrial operations. The State's backstop remediation duty guarantees that abandoned contaminated sites are not left to future generations, with cost recovery preserving the polluter-pays principle.*
