# Section 13.5 — Forests

> **Part 13 — Environment and Natural Resources** · Articles 13.5.1 – 13.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section requires sustainable forest management, protects old-growth forests, imposes a reforestation duty, and recognizes community forest rights.*

## Article 13.5.1 — Sustainable Forest Management

1. Forests shall be managed on a sustainable basis, balancing their ecological, climatic, economic, and social functions.
2. Forest management plans shall be based on scientific inventory and shall be reviewed periodically.
3. Clear-cutting shall be permitted only where the management plan establishes its necessity and provides for immediate restocking.

## Article 13.5.2 — Protection of Old-Growth Forests

1. Old-growth and primary forests shall be protected from commercial logging.
2. Exceptions may be provided by law only for overriding public interests, where no reasonable alternative exists and after the least damaging option has been chosen.

## Article 13.5.3 — Reforestation Duty

1. The State shall maintain a program of reforestation and forest restoration, giving preference to native and site-appropriate species.
2. Harvesting shall not exceed the regenerative capacity of the forests, and degraded forest areas shall be restored.
3. The State shall promote mixed-species stands adapted to the site and to projected climate conditions.

## Article 13.5.4 — Community Forest Rights

1. The law shall recognize and protect the rights of local communities to use forests sustainably for subsistence and customary purposes.
2. The rights of Indigenous peoples concerning forests and resources on their lands and territories shall be respected under Article 28.5.2 (Lands, Territories, and Resources).

## Article 13.5.5 — Forest Health

1. The State shall monitor forest health and shall maintain prevention and response measures against fire, pests, and disease.
2. Forest destruction through unlawful clearing shall be subject to restoration orders and penalties under Article 13.7.2 (Polluter Pays).

## Design Note

*Forest law occupies the junction of climate policy, biodiversity, and rural livelihoods, which is why sustainability is defined by function rather than by timber yield alone. The old-growth rule creates a categorical protection with a narrow, court-auditable escape clause, reflecting the near-irreversibility of primary-forest loss. Community forest rights are constitutionalized because local and Indigenous stewardship has proved among the most reliable defenses against illegal clearing.*
