# Section 13.6 — Biodiversity

> **Part 13 — Environment and Natural Resources** · Articles 13.6.1 – 13.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section protects species and habitats, establishes protected areas, forbids State action that causes extinction, and imposes restoration obligations.*

## Article 13.6.1 — Species and Habitat Protection

1. The State shall protect native wild species and their habitats, maintaining science-based lists of threatened species and habitats requiring strict protection.
2. Deliberate killing, capture, or disturbance of strictly protected species shall be prohibited save for narrowly defined exceptions in the law.

## Article 13.6.2 — Protected Areas

1. The State shall establish a coherent network of protected areas representing the diversity of ecosystems within the Republic.
2. Protected areas shall have management plans, designated authorities, and financing sufficient for their purposes.
3. The network shall be reviewed periodically for coherence and representativeness, and enlarged where science so requires.

## Article 13.6.3 — Prohibition of Extinction

1. No organ of the State shall adopt or authorize measures that it knows will cause the extinction of any species within the Republic.
2. Where significant harm to protected species or habitats is unavoidable, the authority shall apply the mitigation hierarchy: avoidance, reduction, and compensation of the residual harm.

## Article 13.6.4 — Restoration

1. The State shall adopt targets and programs for the restoration of degraded ecosystems, prioritizing habitats essential to threatened species.
2. Restoration obligations shall be imposed on those responsible for degradation in accordance with Article 13.7.2 (Polluter Pays).

## Article 13.6.5 — Invasive Species and Wildlife Trade

1. The State shall prevent, control, and where possible eradicate invasive alien species that threaten biodiversity.
2. Trade in protected species shall be regulated by law, including stricter national measures where necessary for conservation.

## Design Note

*The anti-extinction rule converts a moral intuition into an auditable constitutional command: a State organ cannot knowingly extinguish a species. Because extinction is irreversible, the clause admits no balancing against economic benefit, unlike ordinary environmental trade-offs, which remain governed by the mitigation hierarchy. Restoration duties complete the design, since protection alone cannot recover ecosystems already lost, and polluter-pays funding keeps the burden off the general taxpayer where a degrader can be identified. Restoration targets are set by law so that ambition is revisited by each Parliament rather than fixed for all time.*
