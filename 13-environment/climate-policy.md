# Section 13.2 — Climate Policy

> **Part 13 — Environment and Natural Resources** · Articles 13.2.1 – 13.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section commits the Republic to national climate goals set by law, carbon budgeting, adaptation planning, and a just transition for affected workers and regions.*

## Article 13.2.1 — Climate Goals

1. Parliament shall set by law national climate goals, including long-term reduction targets for greenhouse-gas emissions.
2. Goals shall be scientifically founded, publicly reasoned, and reviewed at fixed intervals.
3. Goals shall include interim milestones that permit progress to be measured and enforced.

## Article 13.2.2 — Carbon Budgeting

1. The law shall establish successive carbon budgets that quantify the total emissions permitted over defined periods.
2. The executive shall allocate sectoral pathways consistent with the budgets, report progress annually to Parliament, and explain any deviations.
3. An independent body shall assess whether policies are compatible with the budgets and shall publish its assessment.
4. Where a budget is projected to be exceeded, the executive shall present a corrective plan to Parliament within a defined period.

## Article 13.2.3 — Adaptation

1. The State shall adopt and periodically revise a national adaptation plan addressing climate risks to water, agriculture, infrastructure, settlements, and health.
2. Public infrastructure decisions shall take account of projected climate risks, consistent with Article 23.2.5 (Sustainability and Resilience).

## Article 13.2.4 — Just Transition

1. Measures taken to reduce emissions shall be accompanied by support for workers and regions whose livelihoods are affected, including retraining for displaced workers under Article 11.5.5 (Retraining for Displaced Workers), income support, and diversification programs.
2. The burdens of the transition shall be distributed in accordance with the principles of solidarity and equality.

## Article 13.2.5 — International Cooperation

1. The Republic shall pursue international cooperation on climate protection consistent with its obligations under international agreements.

## Design Note

*Climate duties are framed as procedural discipline rather than judicially commanded outcomes: Parliament sets binding goals, the executive must report and explain, and an independent assessor closes the credibility gap between promise and policy. The just-transition clause is constitutionalized because decarbonization concentrates costs on identifiable workers and regions, and transition programs that ignore them have historically failed politically. The design draws on framework climate-law models pioneered in several national statutes during the late 2010s.*
