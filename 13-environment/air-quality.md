# Section 13.3 — Air Quality

> **Part 13 — Environment and Natural Resources** · Articles 13.3.1 – 13.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section protects ambient air through binding standards, emission limits, a public monitoring network, and proportionate alert and restriction powers.*

## Article 13.3.1 — Ambient Standards

1. Parliament shall set by law binding ambient air quality standards protective of human health and the environment, implementing the general standards of Article 13.1.1 (Implementation Through Standards).
2. Standards shall specify limit values, averaging periods, and margins of tolerance.
3. Where a standard is persistently exceeded, the responsible authority shall report annually to Parliament on the causes and the corrective measures taken.

## Article 13.3.2 — Emission Limits

1. Installations, vehicles, and products may emit air pollutants only within limits set by law and, where required, under a permit.
2. Permits shall be granted through transparent procedures and shall be reviewed at defined intervals.
3. Emission limits for existing installations shall become stricter as the best available techniques evolve.

## Article 13.3.3 — Monitoring and Public Information

1. The State shall maintain a monitoring network measuring ambient air quality at representative locations.
2. Monitoring data shall be published continuously and in an open, machine-readable format.
3. Exceedance of an alert threshold shall trigger an automatic public warning without awaiting a separate decision.

## Article 13.3.4 — Air Quality Plans and Alerts

1. Where standards are exceeded, the responsible authority shall adopt an air quality plan with measures to achieve compliance within defined time limits.
2. During pollution episodes, the authority may order temporary, proportionate, and non-discriminatory restrictions on activities contributing to the exceedance, after public warning.

## Article 13.3.5 — Accountability

1. Authorities that persistently fail to adopt or execute compliant air quality plans may be compelled to act through the remedies of Article 13.1.4 (Access to Justice).

## Design Note

*Air quality law succeeds or fails on two design points: standards precise enough to be breached, and consequences that follow automatically from a breach. This section supplies both, adding continuous open monitoring so that exceedances are publicly known without litigation. Temporary episode restrictions are expressly non-discriminatory and time-limited, reflecting the lesson that emergency air measures have sometimes targeted the politically weakest activities first.*
