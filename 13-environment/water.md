# Section 13.4 — Water

> **Part 13 — Environment and Natural Resources** · Articles 13.4.1 – 13.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section secures access to safe drinking water and sanitation, protects water sources, and organizes water management by river basin with priority for domestic use.*

## Article 13.4.1 — Drinking Water and Sanitation

1. Everyone has the right of access to sufficient, safe, acceptable, and affordable water for personal and domestic use and to adequate sanitation services.
2. The State and the Municipalities shall progressively realize this right and shall ensure a basic minimum supply to every household.

## Article 13.4.2 — Priority of Uses

1. In the allocation and management of water resources, the satisfaction of essential domestic needs shall take priority over all other uses.
2. All other uses shall be authorized according to law, taking account of ecological requirements and the public interest.

## Article 13.4.3 — Protection of Sources

1. Drinking-water sources shall be protected by zones in which activities that may degrade water quality are restricted or prohibited.
2. Water bodies shall be managed to maintain or restore their ecological quality, applying the pollution rules of Article 13.7.1 (Prevention First).

## Article 13.4.4 — Basin Management

1. Water shall be managed according to river basins rather than administrative boundaries.
2. Regions and Provinces shall cooperate in basin planning, and Municipalities shall deliver local water and sanitation services within their general competence under Article 7.3.2 (General Competence and Services).

## Article 13.4.5 — Pricing and Affordability

1. Tariffs for water services may reflect the costs of provision, subject to:
   (a) affordability safeguards for low-income households; and
   (b) a prohibition on disconnection from the basic minimum supply where this would endanger health or dignity.

## Design Note

*Water is treated as both a right and a managed resource, which is why the section pairs an individual entitlement with basin-scale planning. Priority for essential domestic use gives courts and regulators a clear ranking rule for scarcity conflicts. Municipal delivery reflects the subsidiarity of the local tier, while basin planning corrects the mismatch between hydrology and administrative borders. The affordability safeguards acknowledge that cost-reflective pricing, left alone, can exclude the poorest households from an essential good.*

**Cross-references:** Article 7.3.2 (General Competence and Services); Article 13.7.1 (Prevention First); Article 7.2.2 (Competences).
