# Section 23.5 — Urban Development

> **Part 23 — Infrastructure** · Articles 23.5.1 – 23.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section directs sustainable urbanization through democratic land-use planning, participation, and the integration of housing policy.*

## Article 23.5.1 — Sustainable Urbanization

1. Urban development policy shall promote compact, mixed-use, and resource-efficient settlements, the reduction of sprawl, and adaptation to climate change.
2. Development shall give priority to the reuse of already-developed land and existing buildings where suitable.

## Article 23.5.2 — Land-Use Plans

1. Land use shall be determined by binding plans adopted under the procedures of Section 24.2 and applicable planning law.
2. Plans shall coordinate national, Provincial, and municipal interests; in case of conflict, the subsidiarity principles of Part 7 govern the allocation of competences.
3. Plans shall state the infrastructure, services, and transit connections required by the development they permit; land may not be released for development where the essential services that development presupposes cannot be secured.

## Article 23.5.3 — Participation

1. Residents shall be informed of draft plans in an understandable form and heard before their adoption, in accordance with Article 7.3.5 (Community Participation).
2. The reasoned response to objections shall be published together with the adopted plan.

## Article 23.5.4 — Housing Integration

1. Municipal land-use plans shall reserve land for housing adequate in quantity, location, and affordability, applying the housing policy of Section 22.4.
2. Social housing shall be integrated into residential areas and shall not be concentrated so as to create segregated districts.

## Article 23.5.5 — Public Space and Services

1. Urban plans shall secure public spaces, green areas, and access to everyday services within reasonable distance of dwellings, including for persons with disabilities in accordance with Section 22.3.
2. Planning shall reduce exposure of settlements to environmental hazards, applying the risk information produced under the environmental regime of Part 13.

## Design Note

*Urbanization is where abstract sustainability duties become visible in daily life, so this Section binds planning to compactness, participation, and social mix rather than to aesthetics. The integration duty responds to the failure mode of social housing policy in many countries, where large mono-tenure estates concentrated disadvantage. Linking municipal plans to the land-use regime of Section 24.2 keeps one coherent chain from constitutional land guarantees to the neighborhood plan.*

**Cross-references:** Article 7.3.5 (Community Participation); Article 24.2.1 (Planning by Law); Article 22.4.4 (Social Housing).
