# Section 23.4 — Public Infrastructure

> **Part 23 — Infrastructure** · Articles 23.4.1 – 23.4.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs the planning, selection, maintenance, and integrity of public infrastructure projects over their whole life cycle.*

## Article 23.4.1 — Life-Cycle Planning

1. Every public infrastructure project shall be planned over its full life cycle, covering the assessment of need, comparison of alternatives, construction costs, maintenance and operation, financing, and eventual decommissioning.
2. The method of appraisal shall be published, and the underlying assumptions of major projects shall be disclosed to the Parliament before funds are committed.
3. The realized costs and benefits of major projects shall be evaluated after completion, compared with the original appraisal, and the comparison published.

## Article 23.4.2 — Maintenance Duty

1. The State shall maintain public infrastructure in a condition fit for safe use, and shall provide for maintenance expenditure in the annual budget.
2. Authorities shall publish the condition of their infrastructure assets at defined intervals.
3. New construction of a class of assets shall not be approved while existing assets of the same class under the responsibility of the same authority fail published safety thresholds, unless the new construction serves to remedy that failure.

## Article 23.4.3 — Transparent Project Selection

1. Projects shall be selected according to published criteria of public benefit and cost, applied through the annual budget under Article 8.2.4 (Budget Transparency).
2. A pipeline of planned major projects shall be published in advance of each budget year.

## Article 23.4.4 — Procurement Integrity

1. Works, supplies, and services for public infrastructure shall be procured under the principles and integrity rules of Sections 8.5, including Article 8.5.3 (Integrity Rules).
2. Contracts shall not be split to evade review thresholds, and single-source awards shall be justified in writing and recorded.
3. Variations to contract price or duration during execution shall be documented and shall be reported to the Parliament where they materially change the appraisal under Article 23.4.1.

## Design Note

*Deferred maintenance is the quiet fiscal illusion of infrastructure policy: it improves today's accounts at the expense of tomorrow's safety. The bar on new construction in Article 23.4.2 is deliberately strong because maintenance has no natural constituency, whereas ribbon-cutting does. Life-cycle planning and published appraisal methods follow the practice of independent infrastructure authorities such as those used in Australia and the United Kingdom to depoliticize project selection.*
