# Section 23.6 — Critical Infrastructure

> **Part 23 — Infrastructure** · Articles 23.6.1 – 23.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section protects the continuity of essential services through identification, resilience standards, incident reporting, and tested continuity plans.*

## Article 23.6.1 — Identification

1. The Parliament shall by law define criteria for identifying critical infrastructure whose failure would endanger public safety, essential services, or the functioning of the State.
2. Operators of designated facilities shall be listed in a register maintained under security-appropriate conditions; the register shall be as public as protection allows.

## Article 23.6.2 — Resilience Standards

1. Operators of critical infrastructure shall assess risks, maintain redundancy for essential functions, and adopt security measures proportionate to the potential harm.
2. Standards shall be set with the participation of the operators concerned and shall be reviewed as threats evolve, in coordination with the cybersecurity regime of Part 17.
3. Operators shall map their dependencies on other critical infrastructure and on providers outside the Republic where essential functions rely on them, and shall record how those dependencies are secured.

## Article 23.6.3 — Incident Reporting

1. Operators shall report significant incidents to the competent authority without undue delay.
2. Reports concerning digital infrastructure shall follow the channels of Article 17.3.2 (Incident Reporting) and may be coordinated by the National CERT under Article 17.3.3 (The National CERT).

## Article 23.6.4 — Continuity Plans

1. Operators shall adopt continuity plans enabling the resumption of essential services after disruption, and shall test them through periodic exercises.
2. Constitutional organs and essential public services shall maintain continuity plans for their own functioning, including the preservation of essential records.

## Article 23.6.5 — Oversight

1. Compliance with this Section shall be subject to independent oversight, and findings shall be reported to the Parliament in general terms without disclosing exploitable details.
2. The oversight body shall evaluate the exercises conducted under Article 23.6.4 and shall recommend improvements to the standards of this Section.

## Design Note

*Resilience law walks a narrow line: too public, and the rules become a target map; too secret, and operators escape accountability. The compromise here follows the approach of the European Union's NIS framework of 2016 — public duties, security-classified details, aggregate parliamentary reporting. Extending continuity planning to constitutional organs themselves addresses a gap exposed by several real crises, where the legal order survived only because officials improvised.*
