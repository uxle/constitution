# Section 23.3 — Telecommunications

> **Part 23 — Infrastructure** · Articles 23.3.1 – 23.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section provides universal telecommunications service, independent spectrum management, and net neutrality as the operational basis of an open society.*

## Article 23.3.1 — Universal Service

1. Every person in the Republic shall have access to affordable basic telecommunications services, including internet access of sufficient quality for participation in public and economic life.
2. Coverage obligations may be imposed on providers to eliminate unequal access between regions, and the costs of such obligations shall be shared fairly within the sector.

## Article 23.3.2 — Spectrum and Numbering

1. Radio spectrum and numbering resources are public resources administered by an independent authority established by law.
2. Frequencies shall be allocated through open, non-discriminatory procedures published in advance; the grant of a frequency shall not confer any right to control content.
3. The authority shall publish its decisions with reasons and shall coordinate spectrum use with neighboring states as provided by Part 19.

## Article 23.3.3 — Net Neutrality

1. Providers of internet access shall treat all traffic equally, without blocking, slowing, or prioritizing content, applications, or services for payment or otherwise.
2. Reasonable traffic management is permitted only where it is necessary for network security, legal compliance, or the response to exceptional congestion, shall be non-discriminatory, and shall be disclosed publicly.
3. This Article implements the open-internet guarantee of Article 17.7.1 (The Open Internet).

## Article 23.3.4 — Privacy of Communications

1. Providers shall preserve the confidentiality of communications and shall process traffic and location data only as permitted under Article 17.2.1 (Principles of Processing).

## Article 23.3.5 — Competition

1. Regulation shall promote effective competition; providers holding significant market power shall meet access and interconnection obligations so that end users benefit from choice.

## Design Note

*Telecommunications is regulated because networks are natural monopolies and spectrum is scarce, yet the traffic itself belongs to users, not carriers — hence the strict neutrality rule in Article 23.3.3, aligned with the European Union's Open Internet Regulation of 2015. Separating spectrum management (infrastructure) from content governance (Part 21 and Part 17) prevents a licensing regime from becoming a censorship regime. Universal service is the telecommunications analogue of the universal service obligations in Section 23.1.*
