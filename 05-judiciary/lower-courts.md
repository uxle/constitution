# Section 5.4 — High Courts and District Courts

> **Part 05 — The Judiciary** · Articles 5.4.1 – 5.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section structures the general courts below the Supreme Court — the District Courts and the High Courts — and secures open justice, lay participation, and objective case allocation.*

## Article 5.4.1 — Court Structure

1. The general court system shall comprise the District Courts, the High Courts, and the Supreme Court.
2. The District Courts shall be the courts of first instance.
3. The High Courts shall hear appeals from the District Courts and shall try at first instance the categories of serious cases assigned to them by law.

## Article 5.4.2 — Jurisdiction of the High Courts

1. The High Courts shall exercise general jurisdiction within their districts.
2. Specialized administrative jurisdiction may be assigned by law to chambers of the High Courts.
3. The law shall fix the districts of the High Courts and of the District Courts.

## Article 5.4.3 — Open Courts and Lay Assessors

1. Hearings shall be public, and judgment shall always be pronounced in public.
2. A court may exclude the public only for reasons of public security, public order, or the protection of private life or of minors, by reasoned order.
3. There shall be no jury system. Lay assessors may participate in defined categories of cases as provided by law.

## Article 5.4.4 — Case Allocation

1. Cases shall be allocated to judges by objective, predetermined rules.
2. The allocation rules shall be published and shall not be altered with effect for a pending case.

## Article 5.4.5 — Specialized Chambers

1. The law may establish specialized chambers, such as for family, commercial, or labor matters, within the courts.
2. Specialization shall not create separate court systems outside the structure of this Section.

## Design Note

*A three-level general system keeps the paths of appeal short enough to be usable while concentrating serious first-instance work in the High Courts. Lay assessors offer civic participation without the rigidity of a jury system, on the pattern of the lay judges of German courts, and they are confined to categories fixed by law. Fixed allocation rules remove the judge-shopping temptation that corrodes both fairness and the appearance of fairness.*
