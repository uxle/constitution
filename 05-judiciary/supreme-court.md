# Section 5.2 — The Supreme Court

> **Part 05 — The Judiciary** · Articles 5.2.1 – 5.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Supreme Court as the apex of the general court system, orders its composition and panels, and empowers it to unify the case law.*

## Article 5.2.1 — Status and Jurisdiction

1. The Supreme Court shall be the highest court of the general court system.
2. It shall hear final appeals in civil, criminal, and administrative matters that do not fall within the exclusive jurisdiction of the Constitutional Court.
3. The law may require leave to appeal where a case raises no question of law of general importance.
4. Its decisions shall bind the parties and shall be treated as authoritative statements of the law by all courts.

## Article 5.2.2 — Composition

1. The number of judges of the Supreme Court shall be fixed by law and shall be an odd number.
2. Judges shall be appointed by the President on the binding recommendation of the Judicial Service Commission.
3. The judges shall elect a President of the Court from among themselves.

## Article 5.2.3 — Panels

1. The Court shall sit in panels of three or five judges.
2. A larger panel, and the Court sitting en banc, shall be constituted as provided by law for matters of principle.
3. Judgments shall state reasons and shall be published.

## Article 5.2.4 — Unification of Case Law

1. Where panels of the Court have decided the same question of law differently, the Court sitting en banc shall resolve the divergence.
2. A departure from a settled interpretation of the law shall require an en banc decision and published reasons.

## Article 5.2.5 — Practice Directions

1. The Court may issue practice directions on the conduct and management of proceedings.
2. Practice directions shall be published and shall not conflict with this Constitution or the law.

## Design Note

*Separating the Supreme Court from the Constitutional Court, in the manner of the Austrian and German models, keeps ordinary appeal justice and constitutional adjudication professionally distinct and prevents the constitutionalization of every dispute. The en banc mechanism lets the Court unify its own case law instead of allowing silent contradictions to accumulate across panels. Leave requirements concentrate the Court's authority where it matters: questions of law capable of guiding the whole system.*
