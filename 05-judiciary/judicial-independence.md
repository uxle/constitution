# Section 5.1 — Judicial Independence

> **Part 05 — The Judiciary** · Articles 5.1.1 – 5.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the independence of the courts and judges, their security of tenure and funding, and the strict conditions of removal from office.*

## Article 5.1.1 — Independence of Courts

1. The courts shall be independent and subject only to this Constitution and the law.
2. No organ of the State and no private person may give a binding instruction to a judge in a pending case.
3. The administration of the courts shall be organized so as to protect their independence.

## Article 5.1.2 — Independence of Judges

1. Judges shall be independent and impartial in the exercise of their office.
2. Judges shall decide cases according to law and conscience, without fear or favour.
3. Judges shall not hold an executive office or an office in a political party.

## Article 5.1.3 — Security of Tenure

1. Judges shall hold office until the end of the term for which they were appointed or until the retirement age fixed by law.
2. A judge shall not be transferred, suspended, or removed otherwise than as provided by this Section and by law.

## Article 5.1.4 — Financial Autonomy

1. The remuneration of judges shall be fixed by law and shall not be reduced during their term of office.
2. The budget of the judiciary shall be presented to the Parliament as a distinct item and shall be adequate for the proper administration of justice.

## Article 5.1.5 — Removal for Cause

1. A judge may be removed only for gross misconduct or permanent incapacity.
2. Removal shall be decided by the Judicial Service Commission by a two-thirds majority, after a proceeding in which the judge enjoys full rights of defence.
3. The decision shall be subject to review by the Constitutional Court.

## Design Note

*The salary guarantee is the oldest protection of judicial independence, familiar from Article III of the United States Constitution, and it matters as much as formal tenure: a judge who fears a pay cut is a dependent judge. Routing removal through the Judicial Service Commission, with constitutional-court review behind it, separates judges from the appetites of the government of the day. The distinct budget line prevents the same pressure from arriving through the appropriation route.*
