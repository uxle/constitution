# Section 5.9 — Legal Aid

> **Part 05 — The Judiciary** · Articles 5.9.1 – 5.9.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section secures state-funded legal aid as the companion of effective access to justice: mandatory in serious criminal cases and means-tested in civil matters.*

## Article 5.9.1 — Right to Legal Aid

1. A person who lacks sufficient means shall have the right to legal aid where this is necessary for effective access to justice.
2. Legal aid shall cover advice, representation, and the costs of proceedings as provided by law.

## Article 5.9.2 — The Legal Aid Office

1. The State shall maintain a Legal Aid Office to administer legal aid.
2. The Office shall be independent in the assessment of individual applications.
3. The law shall fix its organization and funding, which shall be sufficient to make the right under Article 5.9.1 effective.

## Article 5.9.3 — Mandatory Criminal Legal Aid

1. The State shall provide representation at its own expense where the interests of justice so require, and in particular where the person is detained or is charged with an offence punishable by imprisonment.
2. Representation under clause 1 shall be available from the first interrogation by the authorities.
3. No person shall be tried without counsel where the law makes representation mandatory.

## Article 5.9.4 — Civil Legal Aid

1. In civil and administrative matters, legal aid shall be means-tested.
2. Priority shall be given to matters affecting the basic conditions of life, such as family, housing, and social protection.
3. Legal aid shall be available in asylum procedures as provided by law.

## Article 5.9.5 — Quality and Independence of Providers

1. Providers of legal aid shall be qualified to the standard required by the matters concerned.
2. Providers shall act independently in the interest of the person aided.
3. The law shall provide for quality assurance of legal aid services.

## Design Note

*Legal aid converts the fair-trial guarantee from paper into practice; without counsel, the rights protected by Section 1.7 are themselves unenforceable, a lesson taught in the United States by Gideon v. Wainwright. Mandatory state-funded defence in serious criminal cases reflects a near-universal constitutional consensus, while means-tested civil aid with priority categories concentrates scarce resources where the basic conditions of life are at stake. An independent office shields individual eligibility decisions from the Government whose conduct may itself be at issue.*
