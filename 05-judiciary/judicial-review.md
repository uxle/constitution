# Section 5.6 — Judicial Review

> **Part 05 — The Judiciary** · Articles 5.6.1 – 5.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section distributes the power to refuse unconstitutional acts between all courts and the Constitutional Court, and fixes the effects of invalidation.*

## Article 5.6.1 — Power of All Courts

1. Every court shall refuse to apply an act of public authority that conflicts with this Constitution.
2. Where the constitutionality of an applicable act is doubtful and decisive for the case, the court shall stay the proceedings and refer the question to the Constitutional Court.

## Article 5.6.2 — Concrete Review

1. A party to a pending case may raise the unconstitutionality of an applicable act.
2. The court shall decide the objection, or shall refer the question as provided in clause 2 of Article 5.6.1.

## Article 5.6.3 — Abstract Review

1. The Constitutional Court shall review the conformity of laws with this Constitution also without a concrete case.
2. Access shall be open to the Government, to one-third of the members of either House, and to the Provincial Councils, as provided by law.

## Article 5.6.4 — Effects of Invalidation

1. A judgment of invalidation shall be reasoned and published.
2. The Constitutional Court shall determine whether the invalidation operates for the future only, and shall order the transitional arrangements necessary to protect legal certainty.

## Article 5.6.5 — No Ouster of Review

1. No Act of the Parliament and no other act may exclude the review of constitutional questions by the courts.
2. An interpretation of a provision that avoids its unconstitutionality shall be preferred.

## Design Note

*The design blends diffuse review — every court may refuse an unconstitutional act, as American practice since Marbury v. Madison teaches — with concentrated referral to the Constitutional Court, so that the decisive questions receive one authoritative answer. The ban on ouster clauses is the lock on the door: without it, a hostile majority could simply legislate itself out of judicial control. Prospective invalidation is kept as an exception, reserved for cases where immediate retroactivity would punish reliance on the old law.*
