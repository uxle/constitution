# Section 5.3 — The Constitutional Court

> **Part 05 — The Judiciary** · Articles 5.3.1 – 5.3.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Constitutional Court with eleven judges on single non-renewable twelve-year terms and vests in it the exclusive jurisdiction over constitutional questions.*

## Article 5.3.1 — Status and Composition

1. The Constitutional Court shall be the highest organ for the protection of this Constitution.
2. It shall consist of eleven judges.
3. It shall be distinct from the Supreme Court.

## Article 5.3.2 — Terms of Judges

1. Judges shall serve a single term of twelve years.
2. A term shall not be renewable.
3. Terms shall be staggered so that approximately one-third expire every four years; the initial terms shall be assigned by lot.
4. A judge shall cease to hold office at the end of the term, at the retirement age fixed by law, or upon removal under Article 5.1.5 (Removal for Cause).

## Article 5.3.3 — Exclusive Jurisdiction

1. The Constitutional Court shall have exclusive jurisdiction over constitutional questions.
2. It shall decide, in particular:
   (a) the conformity of laws and other acts of public authority with this Constitution;
   (b) disputes between organs of the State concerning their constitutional powers;
   (c) disputes between Provinces, and between a Province and the organs of the State;
   (d) appeals concerning the validity of elections and referendums;
   (e) the prohibition of political parties under Article 3.6.4 (Prohibition);
   (f) charges of impeachment against the President under Article 4.1.6 (Impeachment).

## Article 5.3.4 — Individual Complaints

1. A person who alleges the violation of a right of this Constitution by an act of public authority may complain to the Constitutional Court after exhausting the available remedies.
2. A complaint shall be admissible only where it raises a question of constitutional significance.

## Article 5.3.5 — Inter-Organ and Province Disputes

1. A dispute under clause 2 of Article 5.3.3 may be brought by an organ of the State or by a Provincial Council affected by it.
2. The Constitutional Court shall decide such disputes with binding effect.

## Article 5.3.6 — Effect of Decisions

1. Decisions of the Constitutional Court shall be reasoned and published.
2. They shall be binding on all organs of the State, all courts, and all persons.
3. Where the Court invalidates an act, the consequences shall follow Article 5.6.4 (Effects of Invalidation).

## Design Note

*Eleven judges on single, non-renewable twelve-year terms, staggered by lot, were chosen to outlast legislative cycles while removing any hope of reappointment as a political reward — the logic of Hans Kelsen's original Austrian design. Centralizing constitutional questions in one court prevents parallel verdicts from the general courts and keeps the individual complaint meaningful by tying it to the exhaustion of ordinary remedies. Its jurisdiction doubles as the peace treaty between the branches: organ disputes end here rather than in constitutional crisis.*
