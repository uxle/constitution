# Section 5.7 — Access to Justice

> **Part 05 — The Judiciary** · Articles 5.7.1 – 5.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees that justice is not only independent but reachable: timely proceedings, simplified procedures, proportionate fees, digital access, and interpretation.*

## Article 5.7.1 — Timely Justice

1. Proceedings shall be conducted within a reasonable time.
2. The courts shall manage cases actively and shall fix time limits for the completion of the steps in proceedings.
3. The law shall provide compensation where proceedings exceed a duration fixed by law and the delay is not attributable to the person affected.

## Article 5.7.2 — Simplified and Small-Claims Procedures

1. The law shall provide simplified procedures for small claims and for other suitable categories of cases.
2. Simplified procedures shall be decided promptly and with limited formality.
3. Self-represented parties shall receive support as provided by law.

## Article 5.7.3 — Fees and Waivers

1. Court fees shall be proportionate to the cost of the proceedings and to the means of the parties.
2. A party lacking sufficient means shall not be prevented from bringing a case by fees; the law shall provide for full or partial waiver.

## Article 5.7.4 — Electronic Filing and Remote Hearings

1. The courts shall provide for the electronic filing of documents.
2. Hearings may be held remotely where this serves the parties and the fairness of the proceedings.
3. Remote hearings shall respect the public character of proceedings under Article 5.4.3 (Open Courts and Lay Assessors).

## Article 5.7.5 — Interpretation and Accessibility

1. A party who cannot understand or follow the proceedings shall receive the assistance of an interpreter free of charge.
2. Court buildings and proceedings shall be accessible to persons with disabilities.

## Design Note

*The duties of access bind the courts themselves, not only the litigant's pocket: delay, formalism, and fees are all barriers that the Constitution orders removed. The reasonable-time guarantee draws on the settled doctrine of the European Convention on Human Rights, while electronic filing and remote hearings are subordinated to open justice so that efficiency never quietly privatizes the courtroom. Interpretation free of charge makes the promise of a fair trial real for those who cannot follow the language of the court.*
