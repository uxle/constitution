# Section 27.6 — Government Ethics

> **Part 27 — Transparency and Accountability** · Articles 27.6.1 – 27.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section completes the integrity framework with codes of conduct, ethics training, an independent ethics commission, and a public register of lobbyists.*

## Article 27.6.1 — Codes of Conduct

1. The Parliament shall adopt by law codes of conduct for persons exercising public functions, including ministers, civil servants, and local government members.
2. Each House of the Parliament enforces the code for its members under Article 3.10.1 (Duty of Integrity).
3. The codes shall give practical expression to the general duties of public authorities under Article 29.2.1 (General Duties of Public Authorities) and to this Part's integrity rules.

## Article 27.6.2 — Ethics Training

1. A person taking up a public function to which a code applies shall complete training on it within the period fixed by law.
2. Training shall be repeated periodically and shall include the handling of real conflict-of-interest cases.
3. Public authorities shall advise their staff on applying the codes in individual situations.

## Article 27.6.3 — The Independent Ethics Commission

1. An Act shall establish an independent ethics commission to advise, monitor, and enforce the codes of conduct.
2. Commission members shall be appointed for fixed terms through an open process and removed only for cause by a qualified majority of the Parliament.
3. The commission shall issue advisory opinions on doubtful cases, publish them in anonymized form, and refer suspected offenses to the Anti-Corruption Commission under Article 20.3.1 (Mandate).
4. The commission shall report annually to the Parliament on the state of public ethics.

## Article 27.6.4 — Register of Lobbyists

1. An Act shall establish a public register of persons who lobby decision-makers of the Republic professionally.
2. Registered lobbyists shall disclose their clients, the matters lobbied, and the organs approached at intervals fixed by law.
3. Decision-makers shall not meet an unregistered professional lobbyist on matters of legislation or public expenditure.
4. The register and disclosures shall be published in open data format under Article 27.1.2 (Proactive Publication).

## Article 27.6.5 — Advisory Opinions and Sanctions

1. Any person bound by a code may seek an advisory opinion from the ethics commission; a person acting in reliance on an opinion acts in good faith.
2. Breaches of a code attract graduated sanctions fixed by law, from reprimand to exclusion from public functions.
3. Sanctions are subject to judicial review.

## Design Note

*Codes, training, and an advisory organ convert integrity from a private virtue into a working system that a new official can follow, an approach refined in the public-service ethics regimes of the OECD states. The ethics commission is deliberately advisory in the first instance: cheap, fast, publishable advice prevents most breaches, while prosecution remains with the bodies of Part 20. The lobbyist register completes the picture, because influence flows through intermediaries as much as through bribes, and the meeting bar gives the register teeth without banning the profession. Anonymized opinions let the rules grow case law that every office can consult.*
