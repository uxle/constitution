# Section 27.3 — Asset Disclosures

> **Part 27 — Transparency and Accountability** · Articles 27.3.1 – 27.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section requires senior public officials to declare assets, income, and liabilities, makes declarations public with privacy safeguards, and attaches sanctions to false declarations.*

## Article 27.3.1 — Persons and Occasions of Disclosure

1. The following shall declare their assets, income, and liabilities: the President, the Prime Minister, ministers, members of the Parliament, judges, the Public Prosecutor, the Auditor-General, the heads and members of the Part 20 institutions, and such further senior officials as the law designates.
2. Persons who formally announce candidacy for an office listed in clause 1 shall declare before the election.
3. A declaration shall be filed on taking office, annually, and on leaving office.
4. Persons acting in such an office shall declare before acting, under Article 30.3.3 (Vacancies and Acting Capacity).

## Article 27.3.2 — Content and Verification

1. The declaration shall state the declarant's assets, income, and liabilities, and those of spouse and dependent children where the office may affect them, as fixed by law.
2. The declaration shall record gifts received in connection with the office.
3. The Anti-Corruption Commission shall verify declarations, cross-check them against tax, land, corporate, and financial records, and audit a proportion each year under Article 20.3.1 (Mandate).
4. Members of the Parliament declare under the registry of their House, under Article 3.10.2 (Asset Disclosure).

## Article 27.3.3 — Publication and Privacy Safeguards

1. Declarations shall be published in a public electronic registry, searchable free of charge.
2. Data whose publication would risk the safety or core privacy of the declarant or family, such as residence addresses and security arrangements, shall be withheld from publication but remain available to the verifying authorities.
3. The Information Commission shall supervise the balance between publication and privacy under Article 17.2.4 (Duties of Controllers).

## Article 27.3.4 — Sanctions

1. A false, incomplete, or late declaration shall attract administrative sanctions fixed by law.
2. A declaration shown to conceal illicit enrichment shall be reported to the Public Prosecution Service.
3. A person who fails to file a required declaration shall not take up the office; repeated failure may result in ineligibility for such offices for a period fixed by law.

## Article 27.3.5 — Registry and Retention

1. The Anti-Corruption Commission shall maintain the registry of declarations.
2. Declarations shall be retained for the period fixed by law after the office ends, to permit post-term verification.
3. Registry data shall be processed only for verification, publication, and integrity purposes.

## Design Note

*Asset disclosure is the cheapest anticorruption instrument available to a republic: it costs little, deters much, and converts unexplained wealth into a provable offense, which is why codes from the United Nations Convention against Corruption onward treat it as core infrastructure. The verification duty separates a real system from a filing ritual, and privacy safeguards are constitutional because family addresses in hostile hands have chilled candid disclosure. Ineligibility attaches to non-filing so that the sanction lands on the career, not merely the purse.*
