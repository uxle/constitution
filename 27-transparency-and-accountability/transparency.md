# Section 27.1 — Transparency

> **Part 27 — Transparency and Accountability** · Articles 27.1.1 – 27.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section makes openness the default posture of the State: proactive publication, public registers, and narrow limits on disclosure justified by the authority that withholds.*

## Article 27.1.1 — Openness by Default

1. Information held by a public authority is open to the public unless a limitation is justified under Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights) or under the specific exemptions of Section 27.2.
2. The authority that withholds information bears the burden of justifying the withholding.
3. Where an exemption applies only to part of a document, the remainder shall be disclosed.

## Article 27.1.2 — Proactive Publication

1. Public authorities shall publish, without awaiting a request: laws and regulations; budgets and execution data; contracts and their beneficiaries; the reasons for administrative decisions; and the registers maintained under this Part.
2. Publication shall be in open, machine-readable formats that permit reuse, consistent with Article 21.3.2 (Open-Data Default).
3. The categories of information subject to proactive publication shall be specified by law and reviewed periodically for adequacy.

## Article 27.1.3 — Limits on Openness

1. Openness may be limited only where necessary to protect privacy, public security, or another legitimate aim recognized by law, and only so long as necessary.
2. A limitation shall be reasoned, shall be the least restrictive available, and shall be reviewed whenever its grounds change.
3. The identity of officials and of decision-makers shall not be withheld as a matter of course.

## Article 27.1.4 — Registers of Interests

1. Public authorities shall maintain public registers of the declared interests of their office holders and decision-makers.
2. The registers shall record declared interests, recusals, and the treatment of gifts under Section 27.4.
3. A person who fails to declare a material interest shall be liable to the sanctions of Section 27.3 and Section 27.4.

## Article 27.1.5 — Timeliness, Formats, and Records

1. Proactively published information shall be current, and a delayed publication shall be reasoned.
2. Information shall be provided free of charge or at no more than the reasonable cost of reproduction.
3. Public authorities shall maintain records of their activities sufficient to make transparency effective, consistent with Article 21.3.4 (Records and Documentation).
4. Personal data published or released shall be processed consistently with Article 17.2.1 (Principles of Processing).

## Design Note

*Openness by default reverses the historical default of administration, which was secrecy until someone demonstrated a right to know; the burden of justification placed on the withholding authority is the operational heart of the reversal. The proactive publication list is drafted to include contracts and their beneficiaries because procurement is where public money most often meets private interests quietly, a lesson emphasized by open-government practice since the adoption of the first freedom-of-information statute in Sweden in 1766. Registers of interests tie this Section to the integrity regimes of Sections 27.3 and 27.4 so that disclosure is not merely available but located. The records duty closes the loophole by which a right of access survives while the files quietly disappear.*
