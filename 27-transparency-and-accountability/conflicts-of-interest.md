# Section 27.4 — Conflicts of Interest

> **Part 27 — Transparency and Accountability** · Articles 27.4.1 – 27.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section requires identification and registration of conflicts of interest, recusal from affected decisions, gift discipline, cooling-off periods for the revolving door, and their enforcement.*

## Article 27.4.1 — Identification and Declaration

1. A person exercising public functions shall avoid situations in which a personal interest could improperly influence, or reasonably appear to influence, the exercise of those functions.
2. A conflict of interest shall be declared without delay to the competent register under Article 27.1.4 (Registers of Interests).
3. The declaration shall describe the interest with the particularity needed to assess it.

## Article 27.4.2 — Recusal

1. A decision-maker with a declared or known personal interest in a matter shall not participate in its deliberation or decision.
2. The recusal and its reasons shall be recorded in the decision file, consistent with Article 3.10.3 (Recusal).
3. A decision taken in breach of recusal may be set aside by a court or by the competent review body.

## Article 27.4.3 — Gifts and Hospitality

1. A person exercising public functions shall not solicit or accept any gift, benefit, or promise connected with those functions beyond a token value fixed by law.
2. Gifts within the permitted value shall be entered in the register.
3. A benefit offered by a person seeking a decision from the public authority shall be refused and reported.

## Article 27.4.4 — The Revolving Door

1. A former Prime Minister, minister, or senior official shall not, for eighteen months after leaving office, accept employment, lobby, or act for consideration in matters within the person's former responsibility.
2. The law may extend the period for particular offices and impose longer incompatibilities on former members of independent institutions, under Article 20.4.2 (Qualifications and Incompatibilities).
3. During the cooling-off period, the person shall notify the competent authority of any employment offer within the sectors formerly supervised.

## Article 27.4.5 — Enforcement

1. The Anti-Corruption Commission shall monitor compliance and investigate suspected breaches, and the ethics commission under Section 27.6 shall issue advisory opinions on doubtful cases.
2. Breaches of this Section attract sanctions fixed by law, including fines, recovery of benefits, and exclusion from public functions for a period fixed by law.
3. Procurement and subsidy decisions affected by an undisclosed conflict shall be reviewed under Article 8.5.3 (Integrity Rules).

## Design Note

*The recusal rule protects the decision rather than punishing the person: an interested decision-maker may be honest, but the public cannot verify honesty from outside, so appearance is part of the harm, following the OECD standards for managing conflict of interest. The revolving-door clause is drafted narrowly, a fixed eighteen-month period with a subject-matter limit, because rules that are too broad strip retiring officials of their livelihoods and chill public service. The notification duty lets the authority police the boundary without prohibiting it, and reviewable procurement decisions close the loop between integrity rules and spent money.*
