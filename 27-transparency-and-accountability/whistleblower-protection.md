# Section 27.5 — Whistleblower Protection

> **Part 27 — Transparency and Accountability** · Articles 27.5.1 – 27.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section protects persons who report serious wrongdoing: protected disclosures, safe channels, prohibition of retaliation, confidentiality, and oversight of the channels.*

## Article 27.5.1 — Protected Disclosures

1. Any person may report, to a competent authority or to the public, serious wrongdoing known to the person in a work-related or official context: corruption and illicit enrichment; abuse of public power; violations of rights guaranteed by this Constitution; dangers to public health, safety, or the environment; obstruction of justice; and the concealment of any of these.
2. A disclosure in good faith and on reasonable grounds is protected under this Section.
3. A disclosure the person knows to be false receives no protection.

## Article 27.5.2 — Channels

1. Public and private employers shall maintain internal reporting channels, and an Act shall establish an independent external channel for reports internal channels cannot safely receive.
2. Reports concerning bodies exercising national security functions shall be made through the secure channel of Article 18.5.4 (Protected Disclosure Channel).
3. A reporter may choose the safest channel; internal reporting shall not be a condition of external reporting.

## Article 27.5.3 — Protection from Retaliation

1. Dismissal, demotion, harassment, exclusion, blacklisting, or any other detriment because of a protected disclosure is prohibited and void.
2. A person subjected to retaliation is entitled to reinstatement and full compensation for the harm.
3. In proceedings following a protected disclosure, the employer or authority bears the burden of proving that a measure was taken for reasons unrelated to the disclosure.

## Article 27.5.4 — Confidentiality and Anonymity

1. The identity of a reporter remains confidential unless the reporter consents to disclosure.
2. Channels shall accept anonymous reports where the law so provides and assess them on their content.
3. Identity may be revealed only by court order in proceedings where disclosure is strictly necessary for a fair trial, after the reporter has been heard.

## Article 27.5.5 — Oversight of Channel Integrity

1. The Ombudsman shall monitor the functioning and safety of the channels and report publicly on their integrity, under Article 20.1.1 (Mandate).
2. Obstruction of a channel or an attempt to identify a reporter is sanctioned by law.
3. The Ombudsman may refer channel shortcomings to the competent authority and shall publish the response.

## Design Note

*Whistleblowing is the reporting line of last resort: it surfaces what registers, audits, and inspections miss, which is why standards from the Council of Europe to the European Union treat reporter protection as an integrity system. Protection turns on good faith and reasonable grounds rather than being right, because demanding accuracy would silence the uncertain insiders who see most. The choice-of-channel rule and the burden reversal answer the two classic failures: forcing reporters back into the hierarchy they accuse, and formally neutral dismissals that are retaliation in fact. Placing oversight with the Ombudsman keeps the guardian independent of every body it watches, and the channel of Section 18.5 keeps the security sector inside the protection system.*
