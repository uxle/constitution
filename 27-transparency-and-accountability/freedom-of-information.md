# Section 27.2 — Freedom of Information

> **Part 27 — Transparency and Accountability** · Articles 27.2.1 – 27.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section fixes the right-of-access procedure: requests, timelines, narrow exhaustive exemptions, and Information Commission review.*

## Article 27.2.1 — Requests and Timelines

1. Any person may request information held by a public authority by any suitable means, without stating reasons, under Article 21.3.1 (Right of Access).
2. The authority shall acknowledge the request promptly and decide it within twenty days.
3. A single reasoned extension of up to ten days is permissible for requests of exceptional volume or complexity.
4. A request concerning an imminent threat to life or liberty shall be decided within forty-eight hours.

## Article 27.2.2 — Exhaustive Exemptions

1. Access may be refused only for the exemptions enumerated by law, and the list is exhaustive: protection of personal data; public security and defense; international relations; the prevention, investigation, and prosecution of offenses; third-party commercial confidentiality; and the internal deliberative space of the Cabinet.
2. Exemptions shall be construed narrowly, require demonstrated risk of concrete harm, and lapse when the harm is no longer probable.
3. Information shall be disclosed notwithstanding an exemption where the public interest clearly outweighs the harm, save where an Act precludes disclosure of that class of information.

## Article 27.2.3 — Partial Disclosure and Transfer

1. Where part of a record falls within an exemption, the remainder shall be disclosed.
2. A request within the competence of another authority shall be transferred without delay, and the applicant informed within seven days.

## Article 27.2.4 — Review by the Information Commission

1. A refusal, partial refusal, or failure to decide in time may be brought before the Information Commission by the requester.
2. The Commission shall examine the matter under Article 20.7.2 (Complaint Handling) and may order disclosure under Article 20.7.3 (Orders and Fines).
3. An order of the Commission, and a refusal to order disclosure, may be appealed to a court.
4. The Commission shall publish anonymized decisions clarifying the application of the exemptions.

## Article 27.2.5 — Assistance and Fees

1. Public authorities shall assist requesters, in particular persons with disabilities, persons who are illiterate, and persons lacking command of the official languages, under Article 5.7.5 (Interpretation and Accessibility).
2. Fees shall not exceed the reasonable cost of reproduction; the decision itself is free.
3. Requests shall not be refused because of their number alone; abusive volumes are managed by reasonable rules fixed by law.

## Design Note

*A short, exhaustive exemption list with a narrow construction most reliably distinguishes genuine secrecy regimes from convenient ones; open-ended exceptions are where freedom-of-information laws go to die. The public-interest override follows the strongest international formulations of the right of access, while the save for statutory secrecy classes respects genuine vetting and investigation confidentiality. Delegating review to the Information Commission, which also supervises data protection, balances the two regimes trading in the same material, and fixed timelines with an expedited life-and-liberty track convert the right into working procedure.*
