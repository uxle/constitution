# Section 11.4 — Universities

> **Part 11 — Education** · Articles 11.4.1 – 11.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section secures the institutional autonomy and self-governance of universities, the security of academic appointments, independent quality assurance, and the accountability that autonomy must answer.*

## Article 11.4.1 — Institutional Autonomy

1. Universities shall govern themselves, within the law, over their statutes, admissions, curricula, research agendas, appointment of academic staff, and management of resources, in fulfillment of Article 1.10.4 (Academic freedom).
2. Public authorities shall not issue instructions to universities concerning the content of research or teaching, nor intervene in their internal affairs except to enforce generally applicable law.

## Article 11.4.2 — Self-Governance

1. Each university shall adopt statutes establishing an academic senate responsible for academic affairs and a governing board responsible for finance, property, and long-term strategy.
2. Statutes shall provide for the election of the rector and of members of the university organs by the academic staff and the students.

## Article 11.4.3 — Academic Appointments and Tenure

1. Academic posts shall be filled by open competition on the basis of merit, conducted by committees in which scholars of the relevant discipline hold a majority, with published criteria and reasoned decisions.
2. The dismissal or other severe disadvantage of an academic staff member before the end of a term of office requires cause established through a fair procedure before an independent body, consistent with Article 11.6.3 (Protection of Individuals).

## Article 11.4.4 — Quality Assurance

1. An independent authority shall accredit universities and their programs against the national qualifications framework, shall publish its results, and shall periodically evaluate the quality of teaching and research.
2. No institution may offer a degree-bearing program without accreditation.

## Article 11.4.5 — Accountability for Public Funds

1. Universities shall publish audited accounts, admission and graduation statistics, quality reports, and the outcomes of external evaluations.
2. Public funding shall be allocated by transparent criteria, predictable over multi-year periods, in accordance with Article 11.7.3 (Transparent Reporting).
3. Public authorities may verify the lawful use of funds but may not use evaluation as a pretext for interference in scholarship or in the appointment of academic staff.

## Design Note

*Institutional autonomy follows the German Basic Law guarantee of research and teaching and the Magna Charta Universitatum of 1988, both of which treat self-governance as the precondition of free inquiry rather than as a privilege of the institution. Committee-based appointments with a scholarly majority adapt the continental university tradition that guards recruitment against political patronage. Pairing autonomy with mandatory audits and external quality evaluation follows the UNESCO Recommendation of 1997, which couples autonomy with responsibility. The accepted trade-off is that constitutional autonomy makes universities harder to steer, even when public priorities change quickly.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.10.4 (Academic freedom); Article 11.6.3 (Protection of Individuals); Article 11.7.3 (Transparent Reporting).
