# Section 11.2 — Compulsory Education

> **Part 11 — Education** · Articles 11.2.1 – 11.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section defines the compulsory period of basic education — free of charge, between the ages of six and fifteen — and the mutual duties of parents and the State in securing it.*

## Article 11.2.1 — Free Compulsory Basic Education

1. Every child between the ages of six and fifteen shall receive basic education, which shall be compulsory and free, in fulfillment of Article 1.10.1 (Right to education).
2. The compulsory period shall cover at least the whole of basic education and its continuation into the first stage of a secondary pathway.
3. The best interests of the child shall govern all decisions made under this section.

## Article 11.2.2 — No Fees

1. Instruction in public schools during the compulsory period shall be free of tuition and of charges for essential learning materials.
2. No child shall be excluded from, or treated differently within, a public school for inability to pay any charge.

## Article 11.2.3 — Duties of Parents

1. Parents and guardians shall ensure that children of compulsory school age attend, or receive, the education required by this section.
2. Parents may provide education at home or in non-school settings only where they register annually with the competent authority and the education meets the standards applicable to schools, and a refusal of registration may be appealed to an independent body.

## Article 11.2.4 — Duties of the State

1. The State shall provide a suitable school place, free of charge, within reasonable distance of the residence of every child of compulsory school age.
2. The State shall ensure that attendance duties are enforced in a manner consistent with Article 1.10.3 (Parental liberty and private education), beginning with outreach, remedial educational assistance, and access to social services.
3. Sanctions may be imposed only after support has failed or been refused, only by law, and never in a form that punishes a child for the conduct of others.

## Article 11.2.5 — Support for Disadvantaged Pupils

1. The State shall provide pupils whose families cannot meet related costs, and pupils with disabilities or other disadvantages, with the support necessary to participate on an equal basis, funded in accordance with Article 11.7.2 (Equitable Per-Pupil Funding).
2. Schools shall admit pupils without discriminatory barriers, including children who are pregnant or parenting, children of migrant families, and children returning after interrupted schooling.

## Design Note

*Free compulsory basic education is drafted as an immediately realizable obligation, following the reading of ICESCR Article 13 adopted in its General Comment 13, and the child-centered emphasis follows CRC Article 28. Home education is permitted but registered and inspected, tracking the European human-rights case law that conditions parental choice on compliance with State standards. Support-first enforcement borrows from welfare-based truancy models rather than penal ones. The accepted trade-off is that annual registration and verification of home education protect the child while burdening family autonomy.*
