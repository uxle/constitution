# Section 11.1 — The Education System

> **Part 11 — Education** · Articles 11.1.1 – 11.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section organizes the education system as a whole — its structure, the public duty to provide it, equality of access, the place of minority languages, and the standing of private institutions.*

## Article 11.1.1 — Structure of the System

1. The education system of the Republic shall comprise early childhood education, basic education, secondary education, vocational training, and higher education, arranged so that completion of each level opens access to the next.
2. Parliament shall establish by law the organization of public education, the allocation of responsibilities among national and local authorities, and the qualifications required of educators.
3. Movement between general and vocational pathways shall remain possible throughout the life of a learner.

## Article 11.1.2 — The Public Duty to Provide

1. The State shall maintain a comprehensive education system open to all, extending from early childhood through adult learning, in fulfillment of Article 1.10.1 (Right to education).
2. All education shall be directed to the aims stated in Article 1.10.2 (Aims of education), and Parliament shall establish national curriculum standards and a national qualifications framework applicable to public and private institutions alike.

## Article 11.1.3 — Equality of Access

1. No person shall be excluded from any public educational institution on a ground of discrimination prohibited by law, and the State shall remove barriers facing learners with disabilities, learners in rural or remote areas, and other disadvantaged learners.
2. An independent authority shall evaluate the quality of schools and institutions, publish its findings, and advise on improvement; inspection shall concern outcomes, safety, and the rights of learners, not the content of lawful instruction.

## Article 11.1.4 — Instruction in Minority Languages

1. The language or languages of instruction shall be prescribed by law, having regard to the linguistic composition of the Republic and the rights of persons belonging to minorities.
2. Persons belonging to minorities shall have adequate opportunities to learn their minority language and to receive instruction in it to the extent provided by law.

## Article 11.1.5 — Private Institutions

1. Persons and bodies may establish private educational institutions under conditions of transparency, non-discrimination, and standards no lower than those applicable to public institutions.
2. Public authorities may support private institutions that charge no tuition and admit pupils without discrimination.

## Design Note

*The structure follows the four-A scheme of availability, accessibility, acceptability, and adaptability developed under ICESCR General Comment 13, and the equal-standards rule for private institutions follows the Convention against Discrimination in Education of 1960. Instruction in minority languages implements the linguistic-identity guarantees of international minority-rights law within a unitary school system. A single independent quality authority keeps inspection focused on outcomes rather than on policing lawful viewpoints. The accepted trade-off is centralization: national standards secure comparability and equity but constrain local curricular experimentation.*
