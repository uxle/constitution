# Section 29.3 — Regulators

> **Part 29 — Public Institutions** · Articles 29.3.1 – 29.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section sets the constitutional standards for independent regulators: creation by law, independence and expertise, transparency, appeal, and coordination.*

## Article 29.3.1 — Creation by Law

1. An independent regulator shall be created by an Act that defines its mandate, its objectives, its powers, and the sectors or matters within its competence.
2. A regulator shall not be established by decree, and its core mandate shall not be altered except by law.
3. The independence and accountability of a regulator shall meet the standards of this Section.

## Article 29.3.2 — Independence and Expertise

1. The members of a regulator shall be appointed on merit through an open, published process, for fixed staggered terms, and may be removed only for cause by a qualified majority of the Parliament.
2. Members shall possess the expertise the mandate requires and shall observe the incompatibilities fixed by law.
3. The Government may adopt framework policy for a sector but shall not direct the decisions of a regulator in individual cases.
4. The funding of a regulator shall be adequate and shall not be reduced in a manner designed to obstruct its mandate.

## Article 29.3.3 — Transparency and Accountability

1. A regulator shall publish its rules, its reasoned decisions, and its guidance, and shall conduct consultations before rules of general application.
2. A regulator shall report annually to the Parliament on the exercise of its mandate.
3. Registers of licensed or supervised undertakings shall be public, subject to the limits of Section 27.1.

## Article 29.3.4 — Appeal

1. An individual decision of a regulator shall be subject to appeal to a court, consistent with Article 1.6.4 (Review and Remedies).
2. The law may provide for an internal reconsideration preceding the appeal, which shall not unreasonably delay access to the court.

## Article 29.3.5 — Coordination

1. Regulators whose mandates intersect shall coordinate to avoid duplication and conflicting requirements, and shall cooperate with the corresponding authorities of other States as the law provides.
2. The law may authorize joint proceedings and the mutual use of information within the limits of data protection.

## Design Note

*Independent regulators hold concentrated power over markets and speech, so this Section writes their constitution in miniature: created by law, staffed on merit, removable only by cause, and appealable like any other power, meeting the standard that the independent media regulator of Section 21.4 and the banking supervisor of Section 9.6 already satisfy. The framework-versus-cases distinction lets governments set policy without capturing decisions, which is the precise boundary most regulatory scandals test. Coordination duties respond to the modern reality that a single firm may face five regulators, and that conflicting demands are themselves a form of arbitrary power. Annual reporting to the Parliament keeps a technical mandate inside democratic visibility without subjecting it to daily politics.*
