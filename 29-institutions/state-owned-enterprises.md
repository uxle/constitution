# Section 29.4 — State-Owned Enterprises

> **Part 29 — Public Institutions** · Articles 29.4.1 – 29.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs state-owned enterprises: commercial objectivity, competitive neutrality, ownership transparency, governance and parliamentary reporting, and the law-bound creation and divestment of state holdings.*

## Article 29.4.1 — Commercial Objectivity

1. A state-owned enterprise shall be operated according to commercial principles unless an Act expressly assigns to it a public-service obligation.
2. A public-service obligation shall be defined as precisely as possible and shall be compensated on the terms fixed by law.
3. A state-owned enterprise shall not exercise public authority save where an Act confers it.

## Article 29.4.2 — Competitive Neutrality

1. The State shall ensure that a state-owned enterprise competes on equal terms with private undertakings, applying the competition rules of Article 9.2.5 (Concentrations and Public Undertakings) without exception.
2. A state-owned enterprise shall not receive privileged access to public finance, guarantees, land, information, or regulatory treatment.
3. Taxation and regulatory supervision shall apply to a state-owned enterprise on the same terms as to comparable private undertakings.

## Article 29.4.3 — Ownership Transparency

1. The State shall maintain a public register of its shareholdings and of the enterprises in which it holds a controlling interest, consistent with Article 9.4.4 (Transparency of Ownership).
2. The State shall publish annually a consolidated report on the performance, finances, and public-service obligations of state-owned enterprises.
3. The accounts of every state-owned enterprise shall be audited under Article 8.8.1 (Scope of Audit).

## Article 29.4.4 — Governance

1. The boards of state-owned enterprises shall be appointed on merit through open processes fixed by law and shall include independent members.
2. No organ of the State shall direct the individual transactions of a state-owned enterprise otherwise than through the board.
3. Ownership policy shall be set in writing and shall be reported to the Parliament.

## Article 29.4.5 — Creation and Divestment

1. A state-owned enterprise shall be created or wound up only by law.
2. A holding of the State shall be divested only by law, through a transparent and competitive process at a fair valuation, and the proceeds shall accrue to the public budget.
3. The conditions of employment of the staff of a divested enterprise shall be protected as the law provides.

## Design Note

*State-owned enterprises fail their publics in two opposite ways: as politicized employers that squander capital, or as private monopolies that quietly carry public privilege, and this Section aims at both failures at once. Competitive neutrality, developed in OECD practice, is the operative idea: the State competes where it chooses, but never referees with a thumb on the scale for its own team. The register and consolidated report make the state's commercial footprint visible to the Parliament, and the audit link brings the enterprises inside the scope of the supreme audit institution, which is where their money ultimately lives. Requiring divestment by law prevents the quiet privatization of public assets to well-placed buyers, and the employment clause keeps the process from visiting its costs on those least able to bear them.*
