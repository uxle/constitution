# Section 29.5 — Independent Agencies

> **Part 29 — Public Institutions** · Articles 29.5.1 – 29.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section sets the standards for independent agencies of the administration: establishment by law with a clear mandate, sunset and evaluation, accountability to the Parliament, merit staffing, and limits on delegation.*

## Article 29.5.1 — Establishment by Law

1. An independent agency may be created only by an Act that states its mandate, its functions, its funding, and the accountability attaching to it.
2. An agency shall be established only where a demonstrable public purpose is not better served by an existing authority, consistent with Article 29.3.1 (Creation by Law).
3. The mandate of an agency shall be interpreted strictly; powers beyond it may not be presumed.

## Article 29.5.2 — Sunset and Evaluation

1. Every agency shall be evaluated by the Parliament at intervals not exceeding ten years, on the basis of a published evaluation of its performance.
2. The continuation of an agency requires an affirmative decision of the Parliament; an agency whose evaluation shows that its purpose has lapsed shall be wound up.
3. The evaluation shall consider necessity, effectiveness, cost, and the effects on citizens and businesses.

## Article 29.5.3 — Accountability to the Parliament

1. An agency shall report annually to the Parliament on its activities and shall make its reports public.
2. The responsible committee may require reports, documents, and the attendance of the agency's head, consistent with Article 4.7.2 (Committee Scrutiny).
3. An agency shall answer questions and requests for information without delay.

## Article 29.5.4 — Merit Staffing

1. The staff of an agency shall be recruited by open competition on merit, consistent with Article 4.5.1 (Merit Recruitment).
2. Staff shall observe the political neutrality and integrity rules applicable to the civil service.
3. Recruitment shall include screening for conflicts of interest under Section 27.4.

## Article 29.5.5 — Limits on Delegation

1. An agency may delegate functions only as provided by law, within defined limits and under written conditions.
2. Essential functions, including final decision-making on individual cases, shall not be delegated outside the agency.
3. The responsible minister remains accountable to the Parliament for the agency's compliance with law, consistent with Article 0.5.5 (Limits of Delegation).

## Design Note

*Agencies are created to be technically insulated, which is exactly why they need the strongest accountability design: the sunset evaluation forces the Parliament to re-purchase each agency's existence with evidence rather than inherit it by inertia, a discipline borrowed from sunset statutes and periodic agency reviews in several administrative systems. The strict-mandate rule protects the citizen from the slow accretion of unrequested power, the standard pathology of expert bodies. Merit staffing ties agencies to the civil service's integrity culture while the conflict screening connects them to the integrity regime of Part 27. The delegation limits complete the design: insulation from politics must not become insulation from law, and the minister's residual accountability keeps a named person answerable for every agency's legality.*
