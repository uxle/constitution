# Section 29.2 — Public Authorities

> **Part 29 — Public Institutions** · Articles 29.2.1 – 29.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section imposes the general duties binding every public authority: legality, impartiality, proportionality, a service mandate, remedies for the individual, an administrative procedure act, and State liability.*

## Article 29.2.1 — General Duties of Public Authorities

1. A public authority shall act only on a legal basis and within the limits of its competence, consistent with Article 0.4.1 (Legality).
2. A public authority shall treat every person equally and impartially, decide matters objectively, and not pursue improper purposes.
3. A public authority shall observe proportionality in the means it employs and give reasons for its decisions, under Article 1.6.2 (Notice and Reasons).

## Article 29.2.2 — The Service Mandate

1. Public authorities shall provide services that are accessible, timely, courteous, and free of unnecessary formality.
2. The law shall fix service standards, including maximum processing times, for the principal public services.
3. Digital channels shall be provided under Article 17.6.1 (Voluntary Digital Identity), and no person shall suffer detriment for using a non-digital channel.

## Article 29.2.3 — Remedies and Complaints

1. Every person affected by a decision of a public authority has the right to its review, under Article 1.6.4 (Review and Remedies).
2. Every public authority shall operate a complaints procedure, and the Ombudsman's access shall not depend on its exhaustion, under Article 20.1.2 (Access and Standing).
3. A complaint shall be answered within a reasonable time fixed by law.

## Article 29.2.4 — The Administrative Procedure Act

1. The Parliament shall adopt by law an administrative procedure act codifying at a minimum: the conduct of proceedings, notice and hearing of affected persons, reasons, timelines, access to files, and the form and publication of decisions.
2. The administrative procedure act shall apply to all public authorities save where this Constitution provides otherwise.

## Article 29.2.5 — Liability of the State

1. The State is liable for the unlawful acts of its public authorities and officials to the same extent as a private person for fault, under Article 16.2.1 (Fault Liability).
2. The State may exercise recourse against an official who acted intentionally or with gross negligence.
3. Immunities of officials shall be confined to what is necessary for the functioning of the office.

## Design Note

*Every public body in the Republic is governed by one short grammar of legality, impartiality, and proportionality, so that a citizen need not learn a different legal culture for each counter, window, and portal. The service mandate is constitutionalized because administrative quality is not a luxury: processing times and courteous treatment determine whether rights are real for the person before the office. Mandating an administrative procedure act follows the codification tradition of the continental administrative-law codes, while leaving its details to legislation keeps the Constitution free of procedural minutiae. State liability on the private-law fault standard, with recourse limited to intent and gross negligence, balances compensation against the willingness of officials to decide at all.*
