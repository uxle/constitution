# Section 29.1 — Constitutional Bodies

> **Part 29 — Public Institutions** · Articles 29.1.1 – 29.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section defines the constitutional bodies of the Republic, reserves their creation and abolition to constitutional amendment, and protects their independence and funding.*

## Article 29.1.1 — Definition and Enumeration

1. Constitutional bodies are the organs established directly by this Constitution: the Parliament and its Houses; the President; the Government and the Cabinet; the courts; the Public Prosecution Service; the independent institutions of Part 20; the Provincial Councils and municipal organs of Part 07; and such other bodies as this Constitution establishes.
2. A body listed in clause 1 may be created, abolished, merged, or reconstituted only by amendment of this Constitution under Part 25 (Constitutional Process).

## Article 29.1.2 — Protection of Status

1. No Act may abolish a constitutional body, transfer its essential functions to a non-constitutional body, or dissolve it into an ordinary agency.
2. A change in the name, seat, or internal organization of a constitutional body shall not affect its status or independence.

## Article 29.1.3 — Funding Floors

1. The appropriations of constitutional bodies shall not be reduced below the level necessary for the independent performance of their functions.
2. A proposal to reduce the appropriation of an independent institution shall be reasoned in writing and shall state the effects on its mandate.
3. Where a constitutional body considers its funding inadequate for independence, it shall state so publicly in its annual report.

## Article 29.1.4 — Preservation of Independence Guarantees

1. Statutory reorganization may not dilute the guarantees of independence that this Constitution attaches to a constitutional body.
2. A court reviewing the legality of a reorganization shall give determinative weight to the purpose of the guarantees, consistent with Article 0.5.4 (Independent Institutions).

## Article 29.1.5 — Analogy for Newly Established Bodies

1. A body established by amendment acquires the status, funding protection, and independence guarantees appropriate to its function, as fixed by the amending text.
2. Until the amending text provides otherwise, the funding floor of Article 29.1.3 applies to the new body.

## Design Note

*Constitutional bodies are the load-bearing walls of the constitutional order, and this Section does for institutions what entrenchment clauses do for rights: it removes them from the reach of transient majorities. The funding floor addresses the modern, quiet form of abolition, starving an institution through the budget rather than repealing it, a risk the Auditor-General's budget independence in Article 20.5.2 was the first to answer. Allowing name and internal organization to change by statute keeps the rule from fossilizing administration, while the essential-functions test keeps the change from doing by surgery what abolition would do by axe. The analogy clause for newly established bodies lets future amendments create new constitutional bodies without drafting each funding guarantee from scratch.*
