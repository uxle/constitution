# Section 28.2 — The Elderly

> **Part 28 — Special Populations** · Articles 28.2.1 – 28.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section states the duties of all institutions toward older persons: dignity and autonomy in aging, participation, protection from abuse and neglect, adequate income and services, and respect for advance directives.*

## Article 28.2.1 — Dignity in Aging

1. Every older person has the right to age in dignity, with the fullest possible autonomy and independence.
2. Age shall not be a ground of unequal treatment inconsistent with Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination).
3. Public services shall be designed so that older persons can use them without dependence on third parties, unless the person chooses assistance.

## Article 28.2.2 — Participation

1. Older persons shall be enabled to participate in the social, cultural, economic, and political life of the Republic.
2. Public authorities shall consult the organizations of older persons on policies and services that affect them, including long-term care and pension design.
3. Segregation of older persons by age alone shall not be imposed by public bodies.

## Article 28.2.3 — Protection from Abuse and Neglect

1. The State shall protect older persons from physical, psychological, and financial abuse, and from neglect, whether in their homes or in institutions.
2. Persons and bodies in contact with older persons shall report suspected abuse to the competent authority as the law provides.
3. Support to manage the affairs of an older person shall follow the supported decision-making model of Article 16.8.2 (Supported Decision-Making) before any substitute measure is considered.

## Article 28.2.4 — Income and Services

1. The pension system shall prevent old-age poverty, consistent with Article 22.2.5 (Prevention of Old-Age Poverty).
2. Long-term care of adequate quality shall be available under Section 22.6 (Elder Care), with standards of staffing, dignity, and inspection fixed by law.
3. Care in institutions shall respect the private life, correspondence, and personal choices of the persons living there.

## Article 28.2.5 — Autonomy and Advance Directives

1. The free and informed consent of the person is required for medical treatment, consistent with Article 12.7.2 (Consent and Support).
2. An advance directive expressing an older person's wishes regarding future care shall be respected within the limits fixed by law.
3. A person appointed to assist under an advance directive shall act in the person's interest and shall be subject to review.

## Design Note

*The constitutional position of older persons is often left to pensions law alone; this Section instead frames aging as a matter of dignity, autonomy, and participation, following the direction of the international human-rights work on the rights of older persons. The financial-abuse clause addresses the most common and least prosecuted form of elder abuse, typically committed by persons closest to the victim, which is why the reporting duty extends to private bodies in regular contact. Supported decision-making is placed ahead of guardianship to keep the default of Section 16.8 intact even where capacity fluctuates. The advance-directive clause gives practical effect to autonomy at the stage of life when autonomy is most easily lost.*

**Cross-references:** Article 22.2.5 (Prevention of Old-Age Poverty); Article 16.8.2 (Supported Decision-Making); Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination).
