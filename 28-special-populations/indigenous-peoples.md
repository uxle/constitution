# Section 28.5 — Indigenous Peoples

> **Part 28 — Special Populations** · Articles 28.5.1 – 28.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section recognizes indigenous peoples and their rights: identity and institutions, lands and resources, free, prior, and informed consent, cultural heritage, and participation.*

## Article 28.5.1 — Recognition

1. The Republic recognizes the indigenous peoples within its territory, their distinct identities, cultures, languages, and traditional institutions.
2. Forced assimilation and the destruction of indigenous culture are prohibited.
3. Indigenous peoples shall not be displaced from their territories except under Article 28.5.2 and with full respect for their rights.

## Article 28.5.2 — Lands, Territories, and Resources

1. The lands, territories, and resources that indigenous peoples have traditionally owned, occupied, or used shall be recognized and protected, with secure tenure as the law provides.
2. The State shall share fairly the benefits arising from the use of natural resources on indigenous lands, and shall respect Article 0.2.5 (Intergenerational Responsibility) in their management.
3. Relocation of indigenous peoples requires their free, prior, and informed consent; where exceptionally given, it entitles them to compensation and return when the grounds for relocation cease.

## Article 28.5.3 — Consultation and Consent

1. The State shall consult indigenous peoples in good faith, through their representative institutions, on legislative and administrative measures that may affect them, before adoption.
2. Measures or projects substantially affecting indigenous lands, territories, or resources may proceed only with the free, prior, and informed consent of the peoples concerned, given without coercion and before authorization.
3. Consultation procedures shall be fixed by law, shall be accessible, and shall record how the outcome shaped the decision.

## Article 28.5.4 — Cultural Heritage

1. The languages, oral traditions, sacred sites, ceremonies, and traditional knowledge of indigenous peoples shall be protected by law.
2. Traditional knowledge shall not be used or commercialized without the consent of the people concerned; benefit-sharing follows Article 9.9.5 (The Public Domain) where protection expires.
3. Sacred sites shall be accessible to the communities concerned for their ceremonies.

## Article 28.5.5 — Participation and Institutions

1. Indigenous peoples may maintain and develop their own representative institutions and may participate in public decision-making through them.
2. The law may reserve representation for indigenous peoples in advisory bodies and provide for their participation in managing protected areas and natural resources.
3. Public authorities shall consult indigenous peoples on education and health policies affecting them.

## Design Note

*The free, prior, and informed consent standard is drawn from the United Nations Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples and ILO Convention No. 169, and this Section adopts its strong form: consent, not mere consultation, for measures touching lands and resources, the bases of indigenous self-perpetuation. Making consultation precede authorization prevents decisions being announced and then defended. The benefit-sharing and relocation clauses answer the two deepest historical grievances, resource extraction without return and removal without recourse, with structural rather than remedial rules, while recognition of traditional institutions respects the plurality of the Republic.*
