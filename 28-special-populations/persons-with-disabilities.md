# Section 28.3 — Persons with Disabilities

> **Part 28 — Special Populations** · Articles 28.3.1 – 28.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section states the duties of all institutions toward persons with disabilities: full equality, legal capacity, accessibility as a precondition, the reasonable-accommodation duty, and liberty and integrity.*

## Article 28.3.1 — Full Equality and Inclusion

1. Persons with disabilities enjoy every right of this Constitution without discrimination and have the right to inclusion in the life of the community.
2. Special measures to accelerate equality are admissible under Article 1.2.5 (Affirmative Measures) and are not discrimination.
3. Public authorities shall involve persons with disabilities and their representative organizations in the development of policies that affect them.

## Article 28.3.2 — Legal Capacity

1. Persons with disabilities enjoy legal capacity on an equal basis with others in all aspects of life.
2. Where a person requires assistance to exercise capacity, supported decision-making under Article 16.8.2 (Supported Decision-Making) shall be provided before any substitute measure is considered.
3. A substitute measure shall be proportionate, time-limited, and subject to periodic review by a court or independent body.

## Article 28.3.3 — Accessibility

1. The built environment, transport, information, communication, and public and private services open to the public shall be accessible to persons with disabilities, applying universal-design standards fixed by law.
2. Accessibility is a precondition of equality; a service inaccessible without justification is a service denied.
3. New public buildings, infrastructure, and digital services shall comply with the accessibility standards from their entry into service.

## Article 28.3.4 — Reasonable Accommodation

1. Public authorities and providers of goods and services available to the public shall provide reasonable accommodation to persons with disabilities, unless the accommodation would impose a disproportionate or undue burden.
2. The denial of reasonable accommodation constitutes discrimination.
3. The burden of demonstrating that an accommodation is disproportionate lies on the person or body refusing it.

## Article 28.3.5 — Liberty and Integrity

1. No person shall be deprived of liberty on the ground of disability alone.
2. Medical treatment requires the free and informed consent of the person; where consent cannot be given, supported consent shall be sought before a substitute decision, consistent with Article 12.7.3 (Least-Restrictive Care).
3. Any measure restricting the liberty or integrity of a person with disabilities shall be the least restrictive available, time-limited, and subject to prompt review.

## Design Note

*This Section follows the paradigm shift of the Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, which relocated disability from an object of charity to a question of equality and access, and it borrows that treaty's strongest tools. Making accessibility a precondition of equality and denial of accommodation a form of discrimination gives the two duties teeth: one applies before the fact through design standards, the other after the fact through courts. The legal-capacity clause ends the era in which a diagnosis could erase legal personhood, aligning this Section with the guardianship reform of Section 16.8. The liberty guarantee responds to the historical confinement of persons with disabilities on grounds of status rather than conduct, which modern review treats as among the most serious rights violations.*
