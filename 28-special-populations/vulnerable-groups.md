# Section 28.6 — Vulnerable Groups

> **Part 28 — Special Populations** · Articles 28.6.1 – 28.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section addresses intersectional disadvantage: recognition of compound and multiple discrimination, tailored measures, disaggregated data for policy, and impact assessment with consultation.*

## Article 28.6.1 — Intersectional Disadvantage

1. The State shall recognize that disadvantage may arise from the combination of grounds, such as sex, disability, age, ethnic origin, migration status, or poverty, and shall address that compound disadvantage in its policies.
2. The recognition of intersectional disadvantage under Article 1.2.3 (Intersectional Discrimination) applies to the design and evaluation of all public services.
3. Measures for vulnerable groups shall be designed with the participation of the groups concerned.

## Article 28.6.2 — Multiple Discrimination

1. A claim of discrimination may be based on the combination of two or more grounds.
2. Remedies shall reflect the cumulative harm of multiple discrimination rather than averaging its components.
3. Courts and equality bodies shall develop the doctrine of combined grounds consistently with Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination).

## Article 28.6.3 — Tailored Measures

1. Public authorities shall adopt targeted programs for groups facing intersectional disadvantage, including outreach, support services, and priority of access where justified.
2. Special measures shall be proportionate to the disadvantage addressed and shall be reviewed for necessity.
3. The minimum income strategy under Article 22.5.1 (National Poverty Strategy) shall take account of groups facing compound disadvantage.

## Article 28.6.4 — Disaggregated Data

1. Official statistics relevant to equality shall be disaggregated by the grounds of disadvantage, within the safeguards for statistical confidentiality of Article 20.6.3 (Confidentiality of Statistical Data).
2. The Statistics Authority shall coordinate the disaggregation standards across public bodies under Article 20.6.4 (Coordination of State Data Collection).
3. Individual data shall not be disaggregated in a form that permits identification, and respondents' self-identification shall govern.

## Article 28.6.5 — Assessment and Consultation

1. Laws and major policies with likely effects on vulnerable groups shall be assessed for those effects before adoption and evaluated after implementation.
2. Consultation shall reach the groups affected through their organizations and in accessible formats.
3. The results of assessments shall be published, and the reasoning for departing from their findings shall be stated.

## Design Note

*Intersectionality names a real phenomenon that single-ground equality law misses: the person disadvantaged as a woman, as a member of a minority, and as a person with disabilities is not thrice but differently disadvantaged. Allowing combined-ground claims follows the development of equality doctrine in several constitutional courts that abandoned additive models of discrimination. The data provisions are the quiet engine of the Section, since policy for compound disadvantage can only be as good as the statistics that reveal it, provided the firewall of Section 20.6 holds. Requiring assessment before adoption and evaluation after implementation converts good intentions into a reviewable record, the same discipline this Constitution applies to children in Section 28.1.*
