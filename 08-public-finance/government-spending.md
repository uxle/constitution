# Section 8.6 — Government Spending

> **Part 08 — Public Finance** · Articles 8.6.1 – 8.6.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs the execution of public spending: the authorization requirement, accounting officers, in-year reallocation, and publication of execution data.*

## Article 8.6.1 — No Expenditure Without Authorization

1. Expenditure of public funds shall be made only under an appropriation of the budget law or under another statutory authorization.
2. No organ may commit the Republic to expenditure without the authorization required by this Part.
3. Expenditure shall be economical and shall pursue the purpose for which it was appropriated.

## Article 8.6.2 — Accounting Officers

1. Each appropriation shall have an accounting officer, designated as provided by law, personally responsible for its lawful and economical execution.
2. An accounting officer shall refuse a payment ordered in breach of this Article and shall report the refusal to the Office of the Auditor-General and to the Parliament.
3. The responsibility of the accounting officer does not displace the political responsibility of the minister concerned.

## Article 8.6.3 — In-Year Reallocation

1. The budget law shall fix the rules under which funds may be moved between lines within an appropriation during the year.
2. Transfers between appropriations of different purposes, and any increase in total expenditure, require a supplementary budget law adopted under Article 8.2.3 (Parliamentary Adoption).
3. Reallocation shall be reported to the Parliament in the periodic execution reports.

## Article 8.6.4 — Publication of Execution Data

1. The Government shall publish monthly data on the execution of the budget, including revenues collected and expenditure by appropriation line.
2. Data shall be published in open data form, in a timely manner, and in a form capable of comparison with the adopted budget.
3. A final account of each fiscal year shall be laid before the Parliament and audited under Article 8.8.2 (Mandate).

## Design Note

*The authorization rule is made operational by naming a person — the accounting officer — who must obey the appropriation or refuse and report; diffuse responsibility is how unlawful spending survives audits. Strict limits on in-year reallocation protect the parliamentary bargain from administrative erosion, because every transfer beyond a line is a small second budget. Monthly publication converts execution from an annual surprise into a continuously observable process.*

**Cross-references:** Article 8.2.3 (Parliamentary Adoption); Article 8.8.2 (Mandate); Article 8.2.5 (Contingency Reserve).
