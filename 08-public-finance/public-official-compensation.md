# Section 8.9 — Compensation of Public Officials and Discretionary Funds (Anti-Capture Amendment)

> **Part 08 — Public Finance** · Articles 8.9.1 – 8.9.6 · Anti-Capture Amendment — added outside the original numbered sequence

*This Section ties the compensation of elected officeholders to the economic condition of the people they represent, and closes the discretionary-fund accountability gap left open by Articles 8.6 and 8.8 for funds administered by an individual officeholder rather than an institution.*

**Article 8.9.1 (Compensation Formula).** The base annual salary of a member of Parliament or comparable elected officeholder shall be fixed by law as a multiple, set by that law within a band of eight to twelve times, of the national average per-person, per-day income for the preceding fiscal year, as certified by the Statistics Authority (Part 20). The multiple in force, and the calculation underlying it, shall be published in full before it takes effect.

**Article 8.9.2 (No Self-Adjustment).** The multiple fixed under Article 8.9.1 may be changed only by a law adopted in a Parliament that will not itself sit in the term during which the change takes effect, so that no sitting member votes on their own compensation.

**Article 8.9.3 (Automatic Indexation).** Within the multiple set by Article 8.9.1, the salary figure is recalculated automatically each fiscal year against the certified national average per-day income and the certified GDP per capita of the preceding year, without requiring a fresh legislative act; recalculation is published as open data alongside the budget execution data of Article 8.6.4.

**Article 8.9.4 (Integrity of the Underlying Statistic).** The Statistics Authority's certification of GDP and per-day income figures under this Article is subject to the independence guarantees of its constituting Part and to audit by the Office of the Auditor-General (Article 8.8). Manipulation of the underlying statistic for the purpose of affecting compensation under this Article is an offense under Part 14 (Criminal Law) equivalent in gravity to financial fraud in public office.

**Article 8.9.5 (Discretionary Constituency Funds — Audit).** Any fund appropriated for discretionary use by an individual officeholder for constituency development or a comparable purpose is subject to independent audit by the Office of the Auditor-General every twelve months, published in open, machine-readable form, itemized by project and recipient contractor.

**Article 8.9.6 (Discretionary Constituency Funds — No Certification Without Verification).** A utilization certificate for a fund under Article 8.9.5 that lacks independent third-party verification of the underlying work does not satisfy the audit requirement of Article 8.8, and the corresponding allocation shall not be renewed for the following fiscal year until verified.

*Design Note: Fixing legislative compensation as a multiple of what the country's own people earn per day ties an officeholder's material interest to the economic condition of the electorate, rather than to a figure the officeholder's own institution sets for itself — which is the more common arrangement and the more common source of a visible gap between "public servant" and public. The band (eight to twelve times) is deliberately wide enough to preserve the legislative-independence rationale noted elsewhere in this edition — inadequate compensation invites a different, more informal kind of capture — while still moving the number out of pure self-discretion. Articles 8.9.5–8.9.6 close a separate but related gap: discretionary constituency funds are frequently subject to a formal audit requirement in name that in practice accepts self-certified utilization documents, which is not verification.*
