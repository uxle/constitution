# Section 8.1 — Taxation

> **Part 08 — Public Finance** · Articles 8.1.1 – 8.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section lays the constitutional foundations of taxation: the legality requirement, equity according to ability to pay, legal certainty, anti-abuse rules, and judicial review of assessments.*

## Article 8.1.1 — Legality of Taxation

1. Taxes, duties, and compulsory levies shall be imposed, altered, or abolished only by or under a law.
2. No executive organ may introduce a tax or levy of any kind; charges for specific services rendered require statutory authorization.
3. The elements of every tax — the persons liable, the base, the rate, and the exemptions — shall be defined in the law itself or by delegation clearly bounded by it.

## Article 8.1.2 — Equity and Ability to Pay

1. Taxation shall be apportioned according to economic capacity and shall be general; it shall not discriminate arbitrarily between persons in like circumstances.
2. The law shall take account of a minimum subsistence level that remains untaxed.
3. Tax benefits shall not be granted on grounds unrelated to the purposes of the tax system.

## Article 8.1.3 — Non-Retroactivity and Certainty

1. A tax law shall not apply to facts occurring before its entry into force.
2. Retroactive taxation is permitted only where it benefits the taxpayer or where a serious defect of legality is being remedied, and never beyond a short period fixed by law.
3. Tax law shall be drafted clearly, published before entry into force, and interpreted according to its purpose and the good faith of the taxpayer.

## Article 8.1.4 — Anti-Abuse Rules

1. The law may counter arrangements adopted for the principal purpose of avoiding tax, looking to their substance rather than their form.
2. Anti-abuse powers shall be exercised under published rules and shall not override legitimate planning based on the express terms of the law.
3. Tax information may be exchanged with foreign authorities under ratified agreements for the prevention of evasion.

## Article 8.1.5 — Review of Tax Assessments

1. A taxpayer may object to an assessment before the tax administration and may appeal to a court or an independent tribunal.
2. Unlawfully collected amounts shall be refunded without undue delay.
3. The law may provide for provisional collection pending review, subject to repayment of amounts found unlawful.

## Design Note

*No taxation without law is the oldest fiscal guarantee in constitutional history, and it is restated here absolutely, including against disguised levies. Legality keeps taxation the work of the elected Parliament; equity supplies the substantive standard by which those choices are measured. Non-retroactivity protects the reliability that long-term economic decisions require, while the anti-abuse clause acknowledges that formal legality alone cannot defeat engineered avoidance.*
