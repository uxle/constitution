# Section 8.4 — The Central Bank

> **Part 08 — Public Finance** · Articles 8.4.1 – 8.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Central Bank: independence, the primary mandate of price stability, exclusive note issue, instruments, and accountability.*

## Article 8.4.1 — Status and Mandate

1. The Central Bank shall be an independent constitutional institution and shall neither seek nor take instructions from the Government or any other organ of the State.
2. The primary objective of the Central Bank shall be to maintain price stability; without prejudice to that objective, it shall support the general economic policy of the Republic.
3. The Central Bank shall act as fiscal agent of the State, banker to credit institutions, and manager of the official foreign reserves.

## Article 8.4.2 — Governance

1. The Central Bank shall be directed by a governor and a governing board appointed for fixed terms longer than the parliamentary term and removable only for cause under a procedure fixed by law.
2. Members shall declare their interests and shall take no instructions from political bodies during their term.
3. The law shall ensure resources adequate for its mandate.

## Article 8.4.3 — Exclusive Note Issue

1. The Central Bank shall have the exclusive right to issue banknotes and coins of the national currency.
2. The banknotes and coins it issues shall be the only legal tender in the territory of the Republic, as provided by law.
3. The law shall protect the integrity of the currency and the security of banknotes and coins.

## Article 8.4.4 — Instruments and Financial Stability

1. The Central Bank shall determine its monetary policy and shall conduct open-market operations, set policy rates, and require minimum reserves.
2. Operations with the State, including temporary purchases and sales of securities, shall be conducted on market terms for monetary policy purposes and shall not constitute monetary financing under Article 8.3.4 (Prohibition of Monetary Financing).
3. The Central Bank shall contribute to financial stability and may act as lender of last resort to solvent institutions facing a liquidity shortage, under conditions fixed by law.
4. Emergency liquidity support shall be reported to the Parliament after the fact; where a bank must be resolved, Article 9.6.5 (Resolution of Failing Banks) applies.

## Article 8.4.5 — Accountability and Reporting

1. The Central Bank shall publish its decisions, a monetary policy report, and audited annual financial statements.
2. The governor shall report to the Parliament at least annually and shall answer its questions.
3. The accounts of the Central Bank shall be audited annually by an independent external auditor appointed by the Parliament.

## Design Note

*Independence with a single primary mandate follows the consensus that political time horizons systematically bias monetary policy toward inflation; accountability through publication and parliamentary reporting supplies the democratic legitimacy that independence withdraws from daily politics. The narrow market-terms exception reconciles the monetary-financing prohibition with routine open-market operations. Exclusive note issue makes the currency a public monopoly with a single accountable operator.*
