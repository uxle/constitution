# Section 8.8 — Audit of Public Funds

> **Part 08 — Public Finance** · Articles 8.8.1 – 8.8.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section organizes the audit of public funds by the Office of the Auditor-General: scope, mandate, access, reports to Parliament, and follow-up obligations.*

## Article 8.8.1 — Scope of Audit

1. All public funds shall be subject to audit, including the revenues, expenditures, assets, and liabilities of every organ of the State.
2. Funds administered outside the budget and entities in which the State holds a controlling interest shall be included.
3. Audit extends to recipients of public money with respect to the use of that money.

## Article 8.8.2 — Mandate

1. The Office of the Auditor-General shall audit:
   (a) the financial statements of audited entities for truth and fairness;
   (b) compliance of revenues and expenditures with law; and
   (c) the economy, efficiency, and effectiveness of public programs.
2. The Office performs these functions independently; its status and appointment are governed by Part 20 (Independent Institutions).
3. The Office shall apply recognized international auditing standards.

## Article 8.8.3 — Access and Cooperation

1. The Office of the Auditor-General shall have unrestricted access to the records, premises, and information of audited entities.
2. Officials shall furnish complete and truthful information; no organ may withhold records or instruct any person to withhold them.
3. Confidentiality duties owed to third parties shall not defeat audit access, subject to the lawful protection of specially protected data.

## Article 8.8.4 — Reports to Parliament

1. The Office shall report annually on the accounts of the State and on the results of audits, and may issue special reports on matters of importance.
2. Reports shall be laid before the Parliament, examined by a committee responsible for public accounts, and published without delay.
3. The Government shall not alter the findings of the Office.

## Article 8.8.5 — Follow-Up Obligations

1. An audited entity shall respond to findings with a corrective plan within the period fixed by law.
2. The Government shall report to the Parliament, at fixed intervals, on the implementation of corrective measures.
3. The Office shall verify implementation and shall report persistent non-compliance to the Parliament.

## Design Note

*Audit is the closing loop of the authorization chain: the Parliament appropriates, the administration spends, and the Office of the Auditor-General reports back on the Parliament's behalf. Financial, compliance, and performance auditing together answer not only whether money was lawful but whether it was worth spending. The follow-up clauses respond to the known weakness of audit institutions, which is not finding problems but getting them corrected.*
