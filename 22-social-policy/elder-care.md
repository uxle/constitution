# Section 22.6 — Elder Care

> **Part 22 — Social Policy** · Articles 22.6.1 – 22.6.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees dignified aging, a statutory long-term care system, and protection of older persons from abuse and neglect.*

## Article 22.6.1 — Dignity in Aging

1. Older persons have the right to live in dignity, to autonomy in their daily lives, and to participate in the social, cultural, and economic life of the Republic.
2. Support for older persons shall give priority to enabling independent living in a familiar environment for as long as the person wishes and it is compatible with safety and well-being.

## Article 22.6.2 — Long-Term Care

1. The Parliament shall by law establish a long-term care system covering assistance with daily activities, nursing care, and rehabilitation.
2. Needs shall be assessed according to uniform criteria, and entitlement shall not depend on the ability to pay.
3. Home-based and community-based services shall be developed alongside residential facilities, and the quality of both shall be subject to published standards and regular inspection.

## Article 22.6.3 — Protection from Abuse

1. The State shall protect older persons from violence, neglect, and financial exploitation, whether occurring in private homes, in care facilities, or elsewhere.
2. Professionals working with older persons shall have a duty to report suspected abuse to the competent authority, and reports shall be investigated without delay.
3. Care facilities shall be inspected at regular intervals by an authority independent of their operators, and inspection reports shall be published.

## Article 22.6.4 — Autonomy and Participation

1. Advance planning for care and representation, including powers of attorney and directives, shall be promoted and given legal effect before any substitute arrangement is considered.
2. Substitute decision-making for older persons shall follow the safeguards of Article 16.8.2 (Supported Decision-Making) and shall be subject to independent review.
3. Older persons shall be consulted on the design of care services intended for them, individually and through their representative organizations.

## Design Note

*Constitutionalizing long-term care answers the demographic arithmetic: in an aging society, care is no longer a residual welfare question but a core function of the state. The model statute approach keeps financing choices flexible while locking in universality of needs assessment. The safeguarding duties respond to documented patterns of under-reported elder abuse, and the preference for supported decision-making keeps this Section aligned with the disability paradigm of Section 22.3 rather than reverting to paternalism.*
