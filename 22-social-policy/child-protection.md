# Section 22.7 — Child Protection

> **Part 22 — Social Policy** · Articles 22.7.1 – 22.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section organizes child protection services implementing Sections 1.15 and 28.1, with family support first and removal only as a last resort.*

## Article 22.7.1 — Best Interests and Family Support

1. In all measures under this Section, the best interests of the child shall be paramount, consistently with Article 1.15.2 (Best Interests of the Child).
2. Protection services shall be organized so that families under strain receive support first: parenting assistance, counseling, respite care, material aid, and treatment for illness or addiction.
3. Support may not be withheld because a family has previously sought help.

## Article 22.7.2 — Reporting and Response

1. Professionals who work with children shall report suspected violence, exploitation, or neglect to the competent protection authority as provided by law.
2. Reports shall be assessed promptly by qualified staff; information shall be shared among authorities only to the extent necessary for the child's protection.
3. Where a child is in immediate danger, the authority may order urgent protective measures, which shall be submitted without delay to judicial confirmation.

## Article 22.7.3 — Removal as Last Resort

1. A child shall be separated from the family environment only where the child is subject to serious harm at home and no less intrusive measure can adequately protect the child.
2. Removal shall be decided or confirmed by a court, shall state its reasons in writing, and shall be limited to the shortest time necessary.
3. Contact with parents and siblings shall be maintained unless this would harm the child; placement shall, wherever possible, preserve the child's relations, schooling, and language.

## Article 22.7.4 — Review of Placement

1. Every placement outside the family shall be reviewed at defined intervals no longer than six months, with the child heard according to age and maturity under Article 1.15.3 (Participation of Children).
2. A plan for reunification with the family, or, where that is not possible, for a stable alternative placement, shall accompany every removal decision.
3. Affected persons may contest placement decisions before an independent body.

## Article 22.7.5 — Leaving Care

1. Young persons leaving public care shall receive support for housing, education, and employment until they can sustain an independent life, as provided by law.

## Design Note

*The family-support-first rule reflects the UN Guidelines for the Alternative Care of Children, which treat separation itself as a harm to be weighed. The six-month review ceiling responds to the drift found in many care systems, where placements persist without decisions. Comparatively, removal thresholds differ sharply across jurisdictions; this text fixes the test in general terms and leaves operational calibration to statute, keeping constitutional text out of case-by-case child welfare judgments.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.15.2 (Best Interests of the Child); Article 1.15.4 (Protection from Violence and Exploitation); Article 16.5.5 (State Support and Protection).
