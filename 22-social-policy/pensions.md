# Section 22.2 — Pensions

> **Part 22 — Social Policy** · Articles 22.2.1 – 22.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section organizes old-age security as a multi-pillar pension system subject to periodic sustainability reviews and protection of accrued rights.*

## Article 22.2.1 — Multi-Pillar System

1. The Parliament shall by law maintain a pension system comprising:
   (a) a basic pension, universal or residence-based, sufficient to prevent old-age poverty;
   (b) a mandatory contributory pillar with earnings-related benefits; and
   (c) voluntary supplementary savings under transparent prudential supervision.
2. Each pillar shall be governed by published actuarial assumptions, and its financing shall be distinguished from the ordinary annual budget.

## Article 22.2.2 — Sustainability Reviews

1. An independent review of the long-term sustainability of the pension system shall take place at least every five years.
2. The review shall examine demographic trends, contribution rates, benefit formulas, and the statutory retirement age, and shall publish its findings and recommendations in full.
3. Where the review identifies a funding gap over its projection horizon, the Parliament shall respond within two years by law, adjusting contributions, benefits, or both, and shall state publicly the reasons for its response.

## Article 22.2.3 — Protection of Accrued Rights

1. Pension entitlements already accrued shall not be reduced retroactively.
2. Adjustments to pension law may operate only prospectively and shall include transitional protection for persons within ten years of the statutory retirement age.
3. The basic pension under Article 22.2.1 shall in no case fall below the minimum income floor established under Section 22.5.

## Article 22.2.4 — Retirement and Continued Work

1. The statutory retirement age shall be set by law and may be adjusted only prospectively, following a sustainability review under Article 22.2.2.
2. The right to continue working beyond the retirement age shall be protected, and pension rules shall not operate as a prohibition on work.

## Article 22.2.5 — Prevention of Old-Age Poverty

1. The aggregate of basic pension, earnings-related benefits, and supplementary assistance shall secure every older person an income above the poverty line used for measurement under Section 22.5.
2. Obligations to contribute to long-term care shall not reduce a pension below that minimum; care shall be financed under Section 22.6.
3. Survivors' benefits shall be provided by law for dependent family members.

## Design Note

*Multi-pillar design follows the taxonomy popularized by international financial institutions in the 1990s, while the mandatory five-year review resembles automatic-balancing mechanisms pioneered in Sweden and Germany. The Constitution fixes the architecture but leaves parameters to ordinary law, so demography can be answered without constitutional amendment. The prospective-only rule for accrued rights reflects how constitutional courts in Germany and elsewhere have mediated between pension reform and legitimate expectations.*
