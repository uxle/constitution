# Section 17.1 — Digital Rights

> **Part 17 — Digital Law** · Articles 17.1.1 – 17.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section gives statutory effect to the digital rights of Sections 1.5 and 1.13: net neutrality, device privacy, lawful encryption, and protection from generalized surveillance, with remedies before the Information Commission and the courts.*

## Article 17.1.1 — Implementation

1. The Parliament shall implement the digital rights guaranteed by Section 1.13 (Digital Rights) and Section 1.5 (Privacy) by law.
2. Those rights bind the State and, where capable of application, private persons.

## Article 17.1.2 — Net Neutrality

1. Providers of internet access shall treat all traffic equally, without blocking, throttling, or discrimination by content, application, service, sender, or receiver, save for transparent, proportionate, and non-discriminatory management fixed by law.
2. Paid prioritization of traffic is prohibited.

## Article 17.1.3 — Device Privacy and Encryption

1. Access to a device or account shall observe the safeguards of Article 1.13.3 (Digital Privacy).
2. Every person may use encryption; the law shall not prohibit encryption for general use, weaken generally available encryption, or mandate means of circumventing it.

## Article 17.1.4 — No Generalized Surveillance

1. Indiscriminate or bulk surveillance is prohibited save under the strict conditions of Article 1.13.4 (Protection from Unlawful Surveillance).
2. Mandates to weaken the security of products or services for investigative convenience are prohibited.

## Article 17.1.5 — Remedies

1. A person whose digital rights are violated may complain to the Information Commission and to the courts; remedies shall include cessation, erasure, and compensation.
2. The Information Commission exercises its mandate under Part 20 (Independent Institutions).

## Design Note

*Net neutrality is constitutionalized as a duty of equal treatment for data packets; the layered statutory versions, such as the 2015 European Union regulation, are compressed into one enforceable command with a ban on paid prioritization, because discrimination in access replicates discrimination in speech. The encryption clauses reject mandatory backdoors as a matter of design: security weakened for one purpose is weakened for all, a trade this Part refuses to leave to administrative discretion. Complaints route through the Information Commission so that digital rights have a single, accountable supervisor.*
