# Section 17.5 — Algorithms and Transparency

> **Part 17 — Digital Law** · Articles 17.5.1 – 17.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section opens the public sector's algorithmic decisions to view: a public register of algorithms, a right of explanation, bias testing, audit access, and contestability.*

## Article 17.5.1 — Register of Algorithms

1. Public organs shall enter in a public register every algorithmic system used in decisions or services affecting the rights or interests of persons, stating its purpose, data categories, logic in summary form, human oversight, and evaluation results.
2. Entries shall be made before deployment and kept updated; narrow security exceptions shall be justified and shall lapse.

## Article 17.5.2 — Right of Explanation

1. A person affected by an algorithmic decision of a public organ may obtain an explanation of the decision's essential factors sufficient to contest it.
2. The explanation shall be intelligible without technical training and shall be given within a period fixed by law.

## Article 17.5.3 — Bias Testing

1. Public organs shall test algorithmic systems for bias before deployment and periodically thereafter, and shall publish the results.
2. A system that cannot be operated without unlawful discrimination shall not be used, consistent with Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination).

## Article 17.5.4 — Audit Access

1. The Information Commission and auditors designated by it shall have access to algorithmic systems, their data and documentation, and, where necessary for the audit, their source code, under protections of confidentiality.
2. Independent researchers may be granted supervised access as provided by law.

## Article 17.5.5 — Procurement and Contestability

1. Public procurement of algorithmic systems shall require compliance with this Section and shall not accept systems whose opacity defeats audit.
2. A person affected may contest an algorithmic decision before a public organ or a court and may require that the decision be remade by a person.

## Design Note

*Public-sector algorithms are singled out because the State does not merely use automation — it imposes it — and the audit trail must therefore be public by default rather than extracted by litigation; the register generalizes the municipal algorithm registers pioneered in cities such as Amsterdam and Helsinki. The explanation right is calibrated to contestability, not reverse engineering: enough to argue, not enough to game. Source-code access stays behind supervised audit because full public disclosure can conflict with security and with the rights of third parties.*
