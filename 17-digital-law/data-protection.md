# Section 17.2 — Data Protection

> **Part 17 — Digital Law** · Articles 17.2.1 – 17.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the statutory regime of data protection: processing principles, lawful bases and special categories, the rights of data subjects, the duties of controllers, and the dual supervision of the Information Commission.*

## Article 17.2.1 — Principles of Processing

1. Personal data shall be processed lawfully, fairly, and transparently, collected for specified and legitimate purposes, adequate for and limited to what is necessary, accurate, kept no longer than necessary, and secured against loss and unauthorized access.
2. Processing incompatible with the original purpose is prohibited save where authorized by law for weighty reasons; accountability for compliance lies with the controller.

## Article 17.2.2 — Lawful Bases and Special Categories

1. Processing requires a basis provided by law, including the consent of the data subject, a contract, a legal obligation, or another legitimate interest that does not override the rights of the data subject.
2. Sensitive data, including data concerning health, genetic and biometric data, and data revealing beliefs, sexuality, or ethnicity, may be processed only under a narrow legal basis carrying heightened safeguards.
3. The data of children shall be protected specifically, consistent with Article 1.15.1 (Rights of the Child).

## Article 17.2.3 — Rights of Data Subjects

1. Every person has the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, and portability, the right to object, and the right not to be subject to a decision based solely on automated processing that significantly affects the person, consistent with Article 1.6.5 (Impartiality and Automated Decisions).

## Article 17.2.4 — Duties of Controllers

1. Controllers shall implement data protection by design and by default, keep records of processing, notify personal-data breaches without undue delay to the Information Commission and, where the risk so requires, to data subjects, and assess the impact of high-risk processing before it begins.

## Article 17.2.5 — Supervision

1. The Information Commission shall supervise the protection of personal data and the right of access to information, with the powers, orders, and sanctions provided by law, subject to appeal.
2. Its status is stated in Part 20 (Independent Institutions); processing by public bodies is subject to the same principles save as expressly provided by law.

## Design Note

*The principles are stated as processing commands rather than aspirations, following the vocabulary that the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation has made the shared grammar of data protection: minimization, purpose limitation, and accountability. Special categories receive a separate lock because their misuse cannot be undone by later correction. Consolidating supervision in the Information Commission, which also polices access to information, economizes scarce expertise but obliges the Commission to balance the two values openly, as its establishing provisions require.*
