# Section 17.4 — Artificial Intelligence

> **Part 17 — Digital Law** · Articles 17.4.1 – 17.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs artificial intelligence: risk-based duties, prohibited practices including social scoring and real-time mass biometric identification, human oversight of significant decisions, transparency of public-sector use, and accountability.*

## Article 17.4.1 — Risk-Based Rules

1. The development, placing on the market, and use of artificial-intelligence systems shall be regulated according to the risk they pose to rights, safety, and democratic life.
2. High-risk systems shall be registered, tested, documented, and placed under effective human oversight before and during deployment, as provided by law.

## Article 17.4.2 — Prohibited Practices

1. Social scoring whereby the State or private actors evaluate the general trustworthiness of persons across unrelated contexts, with detrimental effects detached from the context of the data's origin, is prohibited.
2. Real-time mass biometric identification in publicly accessible spaces is prohibited save where strictly necessary and authorized by law for a defined purpose, such as the targeted search for a victim or a perpetrator of a serious offense, subject to judicial authorization and independent review.
3. Systems designed to materially distort behavior causing significant harm, and systems inferring emotions in workplaces and schools, are prohibited as provided by law.

## Article 17.4.3 — Human Oversight

1. A decision that significantly affects a person's rights or obligations shall not be taken solely by an artificial-intelligence system; a person shall review and decide, consistent with Article 1.6.5 (Impartiality and Automated Decisions).
2. Oversight persons shall be competent and empowered to dissent, and shall not be subject to incentives that make oversight nominal.

## Article 17.4.4 — Transparency of Public Use

1. A public organ that uses artificial intelligence in a service or decision shall inform the persons affected, shall disclose the system's role, its logic in summary form, and the safeguards applied, and shall register the system consistent with Article 17.5.1 (Register of Algorithms).

## Article 17.4.5 — Accountability

1. Liability for harm caused by artificial-intelligence systems follows the general law of Part 16 (Civil Law); the law may ease proof where causation is technically opaque.
2. Providers and deployers of high-risk systems shall keep logs sufficient to establish accountability after significant incidents.

## Design Note

*The Section borrows the risk-tiering of the European Union's Artificial Intelligence Act but constitutionalizes only the hard core — prohibitions, oversight, transparency — leaving technical detail to legislation that can keep pace with the technology. Social scoring is banned by its structure, cross-context evaluation with detached detriments, rather than by name, so the rule does not age with the term. Human oversight is made effective by the incentive clause, because a review button that cannot say no is not oversight.*
