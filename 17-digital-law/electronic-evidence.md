# Section 17.8 — Electronic Evidence

> **Part 17 — Digital Law** · Articles 17.8.1 – 17.8.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section sets the standards for electronic evidence in legal proceedings: judicially authorized extraction, authentication and integrity, certified forensic methods, defense access, and preservation.*

## Article 17.8.1 — Extraction Under Authorization

1. Data held in information systems shall be extracted for use in proceedings only under the authorization required by Article 15.2.1 (Warrant Requirement) or under another judicial decision.
2. Extraction shall be confined to the data categories authorized; incidental data shall be secured and dealt with under Article 15.2.3 (Digital Searches).

## Article 17.8.2 — Authentication and Integrity

1. Electronic evidence is admissible where its authenticity and integrity are established by the means provided by law, including cryptographic hash verification, electronic signatures, and system documentation.
2. Any alteration, whether accidental or deliberate, shall be disclosed and shall affect the weight and the admissibility of the evidence.

## Article 17.8.3 — Certified Forensics

1. Forensic examination of data shall follow certified methods and shall be performed by qualified persons who record every step of the examination.
2. Working copies shall be used for analysis; originals shall be preserved under seal.

## Article 17.8.4 — Defense Access

1. The defense shall receive copies of electronic evidence in a usable form and may engage an independent expert to verify the extraction and the analysis, with representation at public expense where required by Article 5.9.3 (Mandatory Criminal Legal Aid).

## Article 17.8.5 — Preservation and Cross-Border Data

1. Expedited preservation of data may be ordered to prevent its loss, consistent with Article 14.7.4 (Investigative Powers).
2. Data located abroad shall be obtained through the channels provided by law and by treaty.

## Design Note

*Digital evidence differs in volatility, not in principle: the chain-of-custody duty of Section 15.4 is met here with hashing and working-copy discipline, which make tampering detectable rather than merely forbidden. The defense-access article exists because extraction is a one-way technical operation in which the defense has no physical presence; a usable copy and an independent expert restore the equality of arms. Cross-border data is routed through treaty channels, following the design of the Budapest Convention on Cybercrime, rather than through unilateral reach.*

**Cross-references:** Article 15.2.1 (Warrant Requirement); Article 15.2.3 (Digital Searches); Article 14.7.4 (Investigative Powers).
