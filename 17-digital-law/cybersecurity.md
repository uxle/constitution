# Section 17.3 — Cybersecurity

> **Part 17 — Digital Law** · Articles 17.3.1 – 17.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section organizes cybersecurity: security-by-design duties, incident reporting, an independent national computer emergency response team, the obligations of critical operators, and the limits that protect encryption and communications.*

## Article 17.3.1 — Security by Design

1. Operators of network and information systems shall take technical and organizational measures proportionate to the risk, by design and by default.
2. The law shall set minimum duties for providers of essential services and of digital services, graded by risk.

## Article 17.3.2 — Incident Reporting

1. Operators shall report incidents with significant impact to the competent authority without undue delay.
2. Reports shall be used for the security purpose for which they are given and shall not of themselves expose the operator to liability beyond the express terms of the law.

## Article 17.3.3 — The National CERT

1. An independent national computer emergency response team shall detect, analyze, and coordinate responses to threats and incidents.
2. The CERT shall cooperate with operators, other authorities, and international counterparts; its powers of intervention in private systems shall be fixed by law and shall be proportionate.

## Article 17.3.4 — Critical Operators

1. Operators of critical infrastructure shall maintain continuity plans, resilience testing, and duties of cooperation, consistent with Section 23.6 (Critical Infrastructure).
2. The law may combine or separate the supervision of critical infrastructure and of cybersecurity, provided that accountability for each remains identifiable.

## Article 17.3.5 — Limits

1. Nothing in this Section authorizes the weakening of encryption or the general monitoring of communications, consistent with Article 17.1.3 (Device Privacy and Encryption) and Article 1.5.3 (Confidentiality of Communications).
2. Security duties shall be enforced with regard to the state of the art and to the size of the operator.

## Design Note

*Cybersecurity is treated as an infrastructure duty rather than a police power: the CERT coordinates defense, while coercive powers over private systems stay behind statutory authorization and proportionality, in line with the investigative limits already fixed for cybercrime in Article 14.7.4 (Investigative Powers). The safe-harbor logic of incident reporting follows the practice by which regulators coax disclosure out of operators who would otherwise hide breaches. The limits clause repeats the encryption guarantee here because security mandates are where privacy rules are most quietly eroded.*
