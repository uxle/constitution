# Section 17.7 — Internet Governance

> **Part 17 — Digital Law** · Articles 17.7.1 – 17.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section commits the Republic to the open internet: no internet shutdowns save narrowly under the emergency regime, multi-stakeholder participation in governance, content rules confined by law, and digital inclusion.*

## Article 17.7.1 — The Open Internet

1. The Republic shall preserve an open, free, and secure internet as an infrastructure of expression, commerce, education, and participation, consistent with Article 1.13.1 (Access to the Internet).
2. Measures affecting the network shall be necessary, proportionate, and reviewable.

## Article 17.7.2 — No Internet Shutdowns

1. No organ of the State shall order the disconnection or the degradation of internet access, in whole or in part, for any region, service, or population.
2. An exception exists only under the emergency regime of Part 26 (Emergency and Crisis), only by a measure strictly required by the exigencies, only for the shortest necessary duration, and only with immediate judicial review; a general or indefinite shutdown shall in no case be ordered.

## Article 17.7.3 — Multi-Stakeholder Participation

1. The State shall develop internet policy with the participation of users, industry, the technical community, civil society, and the Provinces, through open consultation as provided by law.

## Article 17.7.4 — Content Governance

1. Content removal or blocking may be required only by law, only by a specific decision, and only subject to review, consistent with Article 1.13.2 (Online Expression).
2. A general duty to monitor content may not be imposed on intermediaries.

## Article 17.7.5 — Digital Inclusion

1. The State shall promote affordable access, digital literacy, and the participation of persons with disabilities and of rural and low-income communities in digital life.

## Design Note

*Shutdowns are addressed by an absolute rule with a single narrow valve because they are, in practice, collective punishment of the networked public; international human-rights bodies have repeatedly found that the blanket access restrictions governments invoke rarely survive a necessity test. The valve runs through the emergency regime of Part 26 rather than through administrative discretion, and general, indefinite disconnection is excluded by definition. The multi-stakeholder clause imports the working method of internet governance bodies so that policy rests on the parties who run and use the network.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.13.1 (Access to the Internet); Article 1.13.2 (Online Expression); Article 17.1.2 (Net Neutrality).
