# Section 17.6 — Digital Identity

> **Part 17 — Digital Law** · Articles 17.6.1 – 17.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section regulates the state digital identity: strictly voluntary use, privacy by design, equivalent channels for non-users, interoperability on open standards, and supervision by the Information Commission.*

## Article 17.6.1 — Voluntary Digital Identity

1. The State may provide a digital identity that enables secure authentication and selective disclosure of attributes.
2. Use of the digital identity shall be voluntary; no person shall be required to use it in order to exercise a right, to access a service, or to fulfil an obligation.

## Article 17.6.2 — Privacy by Design

1. The system shall minimize data, shall support the disclosure of single attributes without revealing more, and shall not create a central record of a person's transactions; authentication shall be distinguishable from identification.
2. Biometric templates used for identity shall be stored so as to resist extraction and reuse.

## Article 17.6.3 — No Detriment for Non-Users

1. Public organs shall maintain equivalent non-digital channels; a person who does not use the digital identity shall suffer no delay, cost, or disadvantage.
2. Private persons shall not be obliged by law to rely exclusively on the state digital identity; competing identity systems may be accredited under open standards.

## Article 17.6.4 — Interoperability

1. The digital identity shall operate on published and open standards and shall be interoperable with accredited private identity systems and, as provided by law, with identity systems of other states.

## Article 17.6.5 — Oversight

1. The Information Commission shall supervise the digital-identity system; its architecture assessments, audits, and incident reports shall be published.

## Design Note

*Voluntariness is the load-bearing wall: identity systems drift toward de facto compulsion when offline channels decay, so equivalence for non-users is a duty of the same rank as the system itself. The design clause — selective disclosure, unlinkability, no central transaction log — draws on the privacy engineering of decentralized identity models rather than on any single national design, including that of Estonia. Distinguishable authentication without identification is what keeps an identity credential from becoming a trail of breadcrumbs.*
