# Section 33.1 — Mountain and Hill Conservation

## Articles

**Article 33.1.1 (Special Terrain Status).** Mountains, hills, and highland ecosystems are recognized as ecologically sensitive terrain requiring rules distinct from lowland land-use law, given their role in water catchment, biodiversity, and erosion control.

**Article 33.1.2 (Slope-Based Building Limits).** Regions must set slope-based construction limits (the steeper the land, the stricter the limit) to prevent landslide risk and habitat loss, reviewed by independent geological survey.

**Article 33.1.3 (Watershed Protection).** Any hill or mountain range that forms a river's watershed receives heightened protection under Part 13 (Water Governance), since damage upstream harms every community downstream.

**Article 33.1.4 (Sacred and Cultural Sites).** Mountains and hills of religious or cultural significance to a community receive additional protected status at that community's request, safeguarded from commercial development without consent.

*Design Note: Highland terrain is disproportionately fragile and disproportionately under-regulated in most existing legal systems, precisely because damage there is invisible until a landslide or a dried-up river makes it visible downstream. This section exists to regulate the cause, not just respond to the consequence.*

## Implementation Detail

**Article 33.1.5 (Slope Bands).** Construction is unrestricted below a 10% grade, permit-only with mitigation between 10–25%, and prohibited above 25% except for essential public infrastructure with independent geological sign-off.
