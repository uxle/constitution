# Section 24.4 — Farmers' Rights

> **Part 24 — Agriculture and Land** · Articles 24.4.1 – 24.4.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section protects fair farm income, seed autonomy subject to biosafety, participation in agricultural policy, and generational succession.*

## Article 24.4.1 — Fair Income and Market Access

1. The State shall promote conditions in which farmers obtain a fair standard of living from their activity, in accordance with the fair remuneration principle of Article 1.9.2 (Fair Remuneration) adapted to self-employed producers.
2. Farmers shall have access to markets without unjustified barriers, and public quality schemes shall be open to all producers meeting the published criteria.

## Article 24.4.2 — Seed Autonomy

1. Farmers may save, use, exchange, and sell farm-saved seed of their own harvest, subject to biosafety requirements and to the intellectual property rules of Part 9.
2. Prohibitions or fees shall not be designed so as to eliminate the practice of seed saving, and traditional varieties shall remain lawful.
3. Rules protecting plant varieties shall balance the interests of breeders with the conservation of agricultural biodiversity.

## Article 24.4.3 — Biosafety

1. Rules on seeds, plant health, and organisms shall protect human, animal, and plant health and the environment, and shall be based on scientific evidence and risk assessment.
2. Biosafety measures shall be applied without arbitrary discrimination between domestic and imported products of equivalent safety.

## Article 24.4.4 — Participation and Succession

1. Farmers and their organizations shall be consulted in the preparation of agricultural legislation and programs, consistently with Article 0.6.4 (Civil Society and Participation).
2. The State shall support the transfer of farms between generations, including early handover, training of successors, and taxation that does not force the sale of viable farms.

## Design Note

*Farmers' rights rarely appear in constitutions as such; they are assembled here from labor, property, and participation guarantees that self-employed producers otherwise receive unevenly. Seed autonomy is the most contested clause internationally — plant variety protection systems from the 1961 Paris convention onward restrict what farmers may reuse — so the text protects the practice as a floor while leaving the commercial framework to law. Succession support addresses the structural aging of the farming population, which no production subsidy corrects.*
