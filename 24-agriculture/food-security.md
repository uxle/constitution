# Section 24.3 — Food Security

> **Part 24 — Agriculture and Land** · Articles 24.3.1 – 24.3.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees access to adequate food for all and prepares the Republic against supply crises through strategic reserves.*

## Article 24.3.1 — Access to Adequate Food

1. The State shall pursue a food security policy ensuring for every person the availability, accessibility, and nutritional adequacy of food.
2. Food security policy shall link production under Section 24.1 with social protection under Part 22, since hunger within the Republic is in the first place a question of purchasing power and distribution.
3. Public nutrition policy shall be based on independent scientific advice and shall promote truthful consumer information under Article 1.14.2 (Truthful Information).

## Article 24.3.2 — Strategic Reserves

1. The State shall maintain reserves of essential foodstuffs and agricultural inputs sufficient for foreseeable supply disruptions, financed through the budget.
2. Reserves shall be rotated so that stocks remain usable, and their size, composition, and management shall be published annually.
3. Reserves may be released to stabilize supply in scarcity; releases shall be non-discriminatory and shall not be used to manipulate markets for the benefit of particular traders.

## Article 24.3.3 — Crisis Response

1. Measures against severe food shortages, including distribution controls and priority allocations, shall follow the emergency and disaster regimes of Part 26 and shall remain temporary, proportionate, and non-discriminatory.
2. Extraordinary burdens imposed on producers or distributors during a food crisis shall be compensated as provided by law.

## Article 24.3.4 — Monitoring

1. The Government shall monitor the security of food supply continuously and shall report to the Parliament annually on production, stocks, prices, and nutritional outcomes.
2. Monitoring data shall inform the renewal of agricultural support programs under Section 24.1 and the reserves policy under Article 24.3.2.

## Design Note

*Food security has two distinct failure modes: scarcity of supply and poverty amid plenty. The text directs constitutional attention to both — reserves for the first, the minimum income floor of Section 22.5 for the second — because stockpiles alone do not feed those who cannot pay. Publication duties for reserves respond to historical experience with opaque stock management, which invited speculation and corruption; transparency here is itself a stabilizer.*
