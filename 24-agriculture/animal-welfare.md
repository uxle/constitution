# Section 24.5 — Animal Welfare

> **Part 24 — Agriculture and Land** · Articles 24.5.1 – 24.5.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section recognizes animals as sentient beings and requires welfare standards in their keeping, transport, and slaughter, with an enforcement authority.*

## Article 24.5.1 — Sentience and Duty of Protection

1. Animals are sentient beings; the State shall protect their welfare in keeping, care, transport, and slaughter.
2. No animal shall be subjected to avoidable pain, suffering, or fear; any use of animals shall be justified by a legitimate human purpose and shall apply the least harmful practicable methods.
3. Where human rights and interests conflict with animal welfare, restrictions shall be prescribed by law, pursue a legitimate aim, and be proportionate, in accordance with the method of Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Article 24.5.2 — Welfare Standards

1. The Parliament shall by law set standards for the keeping, husbandry, transport, and slaughter of animals, appropriate to each species.
2. Standards shall be reviewed in the light of scientific and veterinary evidence and of experience under Article 13.1.5 (Monitoring and Enforcement).
3. Education and training in animal welfare shall be provided for persons who keep or handle animals professionally.

## Article 24.5.3 — Enforcement Authority

1. A competent authority shall enforce animal welfare law, with powers of inspection, seizure in urgent cases, and the imposition of sanctions.
2. Inspections shall be conducted on a risk basis and may be unannounced; inspectors shall have the qualifications prescribed by law.
3. Persons may report suspected violations to the authority, and such reports shall be assessed without disclosing the reporter's identity where protection is required.

## Article 24.5.4 — Scientific Use of Animals

1. The use of animals in research and testing shall be permitted only where no alternative exists, the number of animals is reduced to the necessary minimum, and the conditions meet the standards of this Section.

## Design Note

*The recognition of sentience follows the leading formulations in the German Basic Law of 2002 and the Lisbon Treaty, which opened ordinary legislation to animal welfare as a public interest rather than a private hobby of owners. The three-R rule for scientific use — replacement, reduction, refinement — is paraphrased in Article 24.5.4 because research use is the hardest case for proportionality. Constitutional text stays principle-level; species-by-species detail belongs to law that can track veterinary science.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights); Article 13.1.5 (Monitoring and Enforcement).
