# Section 24.2 — Land Use

> **Part 24 — Agriculture and Land** · Articles 24.2.1 – 24.2.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section requires land-use planning by law, secures tenure for all holders, and confines expropriation to the guarantees of Section 1.8.*

## Article 24.2.1 — Planning by Law

1. Land use shall be determined only on the basis of plans adopted by law or under law by democratically legitimized bodies.
2. Planning shall pursue the balanced development of urban and rural areas, the protection of agricultural land, nature, and cultural heritage, and the efficient use of land as a finite resource.
3. Land-use planning is an exclusive public function; binding plans shall not be produced by private bodies.

## Article 24.2.2 — Secure Tenure

1. All forms of tenure — ownership, leasehold, customary and communal holdings, and use rights — shall be recognized and protected against arbitrary interference.
2. The State shall maintain a public land register establishing the legal position of land, and registration shall be open to inspection.
3. Tenure shall not be terminated except on the basis of law, for reasons made known to the holder, and with the possibility of review.

## Article 24.2.3 — Expropriation

1. Expropriation of land shall take place only under the conditions of Article 1.8.2 (Expropriation): for a public purpose, on the basis of law, and against fair compensation determined in advance.
2. The intended public purpose shall be substantiated in the expropriation decision, and the land shall be used for that purpose; if it is not, the former holder shall have a right of repurchase as provided by law.
3. Disputes over compensation and purpose shall be open to full judicial review under Article 1.8.4 (Compensation and Review).

## Article 24.2.4 — Balanced Development

1. Rural areas shall be served with infrastructure, services, and connectivity comparable in quality to urban areas, consistent with Section 23.1 and Section 23.5.
2. Planning shall prefer the reuse of developed land over the conversion of farmland and open space.

## Design Note

*The land register in Article 24.2.2 carries the weight of centuries of property practice: secure, public records are the cheapest anti-corruption device in land administration. Limiting expropriation by cross-reference rather than restatement keeps one single test — Section 1.8 — instead of parallel regimes that invite forum shopping. The repurchase sanction responds to the common abuse of expropriating for a stated purpose and then diverting the land to private development.*
