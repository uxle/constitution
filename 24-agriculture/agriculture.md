# Section 24.1 — Agriculture

> **Part 24 — Agriculture and Land** · Articles 24.1.1 – 24.1.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes a sustainable agriculture policy, support programs tied to public goods, research and extension, and fair supply chains.*

## Article 24.1.1 — Sustainable Agriculture Policy

1. The State shall pursue an agriculture policy that keeps land productive over generations, protects soil, water, and biodiversity, and strengthens the resilience of farming to climate and market shocks.
2. Agricultural activity shall remain compatible with the environmental duty of the State under Article 1.12.2 (Duty of the State).

## Article 24.1.2 — Support Programs

1. Public support to agriculture shall be predictable over multiple years and shall be directed to the provision of public goods and to the stability of farm incomes.
2. Support shall not reward practices that cause avoidable environmental harm; support conditions shall be proportionate to the administrative capacity of small farms.
3. Support programs shall be evaluated periodically and the evaluations published.

## Article 24.1.3 — Research and Extension

1. The State shall maintain agricultural research and an independent advisory service available to farmers, including on sustainable methods and farm management.
2. Agricultural data collected by public bodies shall be made available to farmers and researchers in aggregated form, respecting the confidentiality rules of Section 20.6.

## Article 24.1.4 — Fair Supply Chains

1. Unfair trading practices that abuse bargaining power in agricultural and food supply chains shall be prohibited by law.
2. Farmers may form producer organizations and, in accordance with Part 10, associations to negotiate the terms of sale.
3. Competition law applies to the food sector under Article 9.2.1 (Protection of Competition); processing and retail markets shall be monitored for market power at the expense of producers and consumers.

## Design Note

*Agricultural constitutions historically swung between productivism and protectionism; this text chooses a third anchor, paying for public goods rather than volume. Tying support to sustainability answers the well-documented failure of acreage and headage subsidies to arrest environmental decline. The fair-supply-chain clause reflects statutory reforms adopted in several European states after investigations showed persistent margin squeeze on primary producers, and it deliberately permits collective negotiation despite general competition law.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.12.2 (Duty of the State); Article 9.2.1 (Protection of Competition).
