# Section 24.6 — Fisheries

> **Part 24 — Agriculture and Land** · Articles 24.6.1 – 24.6.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs fishing through science-based quotas, transparent licensing, protection of small-scale fishers, and marine conservation.*

## Article 24.6.1 — Science-Based Quotas

1. Fishing opportunities shall be set at levels that maintain or restore fish stocks above biomass levels capable of producing the maximum sustainable yield, on the basis of the best available independent scientific advice.
2. Total allowable catches and the allocation among fleet segments shall follow long-term management plans published in advance.
3. Scientific advice and the decisions taken shall be published, and any departure from the advice shall state its reasons.

## Article 24.6.2 — Transparent Licensing

1. Fishing licenses shall be granted, renewed, and withdrawn according to published criteria, in the manner of Article 13.8.2 (Transparent Extraction Licensing).
2. A public register shall record the holders of licenses and quotas; hidden transfers shall have no legal effect.
3. Fees shall reflect the public value of the resource and shall not be levied so as to exclude small operators from viability.

## Article 24.6.3 — Small-Scale Fishers

1. The State shall secure a fair share of fishing opportunities for small-scale and coastal fishers, whose operations often carry the least environmental impact per unit landed.
2. Support under Section 24.1 shall be accessible to small operators without disproportionate administrative burden.

## Article 24.6.4 — Marine Conservation

1. Marine ecosystems shall be protected through measures including closed areas and seasons, gear restrictions, and the reduction of bycatch and discards.
2. The State shall protect the marine environment in fulfillment of Article 1.12.2 (Duty of the State) and shall cooperate internationally on shared stocks, consistent with Part 19.

## Design Note

*Fisheries collapse is the classic tragedy of the commons, and the constitutional answer here is procedural: bind the quota decision to published scientific advice and require reasons for any departure, as done in Norway and under the reform of the European Union's common fisheries policy in 2013. The register of quota holdings targets quota laundering through informal leasing. Recognizing small-scale fishers acknowledges evidence that fleet concentration delivers neither the best conservation nor the strongest coastal communities.*

**Cross-references:** Article 13.8.2 (Transparent Extraction Licensing); Article 1.12.2 (Duty of the State).
