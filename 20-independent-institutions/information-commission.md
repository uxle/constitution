# Section 20.7 — The Information Commission

> **Part 20 — Independent Institutions** · Articles 20.7.1 – 20.7.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Information Commission as the single supervisor of data protection and of access to information, defines its complaint-handling role, and subjects its orders and fines to judicial appeal.*

## Article 20.7.1 — Mandate

1. The Information Commission shall supervise compliance with data protection law and with the right of access to information held by public bodies.
2. The Commission shall issue guidance, monitor practice, and promote awareness of both regimes.
3. Guidance shall be published and may include codes of recommended practice for public and private bodies.

## Article 20.7.2 — Complaint Handling

1. Any person may complain to the Commission of an alleged violation of data protection or of the right of access to information.
2. The Commission shall examine complaints impartially and within a reasonable time and shall inform the complainant of the outcome with reasons.

## Article 20.7.3 — Orders and Fines

1. The Commission may order compliance, including the correction or deletion of data, the disclosure of information, and the cessation of unlawful processing, and may impose fines as provided by law.
2. Orders and fines are subject to appeal to a court, consistent with Article 1.6.4 and Article 5.6.1.
3. The Commission may conduct audits and inspections of public and private bodies within its mandate.

## Article 20.7.4 — Independence and Reporting

1. The Commission is independent; its head serves a fixed term and may be removed only for cause by a supermajority of the Parliament.
2. The Commission shall report annually to the Parliament, including on systemic weaknesses in transparency and data protection.

## Design Note

*Merging data protection and access-to-information oversight in one commission recognizes that the two regimes trade in the same material and are frequently misunderstood in the same breath; several jurisdictions have converged on this model. Because the Commission both orders and enforces, appeal to a court is the indispensable check, keeping an independent regulator inside the rule of law it polices. Annual reporting on systemic weaknesses turns case-by-case supervision into a public account of how open, and how private, the Republic actually is.*
