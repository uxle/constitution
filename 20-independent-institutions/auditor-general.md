# Section 20.5 — The Office of the Auditor-General

> **Part 20 — Independent Institutions** · Articles 20.5.1 – 20.5.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Office of the Auditor-General as an independent supreme audit institution, defines the Auditor-General's single term and supermajority appointment, and secures operational and budgetary independence.*

## Article 20.5.1 — Status and Appointment

1. The Office of the Auditor-General is the independent supreme audit institution of the Republic, established under Article 0.5.4.
2. The Auditor-General is appointed by the Parliament by a supermajority as fixed by law, for a single term, and may not be reappointed.
3. The Auditor-General may be removed before the end of the term only for cause, by the same supermajority, and subject to judicial review.

## Article 20.5.2 — Operational and Budgetary Independence

1. The Office determines its audit program, methods, and staffing independently.
2. The Office's budget proposal shall be transmitted to the Parliament without alteration by the executive and shall not be reduced below the level necessary to perform its functions.
3. Neither the executive nor any public body may direct or restrict an audit.
4. Staff of the Office act independently and take instructions only from within the Office.

## Article 20.5.3 — Access and Powers

1. The Office shall audit all public revenue, expenditure, assets, and liabilities, including bodies in which the State holds a controlling interest.
2. The Office has full access to the records, premises, and information of audited entities; obstruction shall be sanctioned by law.

## Article 20.5.4 — Reporting and Follow-Up

1. The Office shall report its findings to the Parliament and shall publish its reports, consistent with Article 4.7.4.
2. Audited entities shall respond to findings and implement follow-up within reasonable times; the Office shall track implementation and report on it annually.

## Design Note

*A single non-renewable term frees the Auditor-General from cultivating reappointment, the same insulation given to constitutional court judges; the supermajority appointment ensures the choice is not a government patronage prize. The budget rule removes the classic chokehold of executive control over audit funding. Publication and a tracked follow-up duty matter as much as audit power itself, because findings that are never answered rarely change how public money is spent.*
