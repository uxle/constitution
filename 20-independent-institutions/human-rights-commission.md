# Section 20.2 — The Human Rights Commission

> **Part 20 — Independent Institutions** · Articles 20.2.1 – 20.2.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Human Rights Commission to monitor, promote, and advise on human rights, with the independence, pluralism, and resources that credible national human rights institutions require.*

## Article 20.2.1 — Mandate

1. The Human Rights Commission shall monitor the observance of human rights, promote awareness and education, and advise public authorities and the Parliament.
2. Its mandate covers every right guaranteed by this Constitution and every binding human rights obligation of the Republic.
3. The Commission shall pay particular attention to persons in detention and in other settings where rights are most at risk.

## Article 20.2.2 — Independence and Pluralism

1. The Commission shall be independent of the executive, pluralist in composition, adequately and predictably funded, and transparent in its work.
2. Members shall be appointed through an open process for fixed terms and may be removed only for cause by a qualified majority of the Parliament.

## Article 20.2.3 — Participation in Proceedings

1. The Commission may submit observations as amicus curiae to any court, including the Constitutional Court, on questions of human rights.
2. The Commission may assist complainants, refer matters to the competent authorities, and intervene in proceedings where the law so provides.

## Article 20.2.4 — Periodic Reports

1. The Commission shall publish periodic reports on the state of human rights in the Republic at the intervals fixed by law, and shall report annually to the Parliament.
2. Public authorities shall cooperate with the Commission and shall respond to its recommendations, consistent with Article 1.1.3.
3. The Commission may require from public bodies the information necessary for its monitoring; a refusal shall be justified in writing.

## Design Note

*The independence, pluralist composition, adequate funding, and broad mandate required here restate the standards set for national human rights institutions by the Paris Principles, adopted by the United Nations General Assembly in 1993. An amicus role lets the Commission bring system-wide knowledge into individual cases without displacing the parties or the courts. Periodic public reporting is the institution's chief instrument: it converts scattered monitoring into a published account against which the State can be judged.*
