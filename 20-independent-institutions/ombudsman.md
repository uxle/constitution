# Section 20.1 — The Ombudsman

> **Part 20 — Independent Institutions** · Articles 20.1.1 – 20.1.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Ombudsman as an independent investigator of maladministration, accessible to every person, empowered to investigate on complaint and on own initiative, and reporting to the Parliament.*

## Article 20.1.1 — Mandate

1. The Ombudsman shall investigate maladministration by public authorities, including unlawfulness, delay, error, abuse of power, discrimination, discourtesy, and failure to give reasons.
2. The Ombudsman acts independently of the executive and answers to the Parliament.
3. The Ombudsman may propose legislative and administrative reforms arising from investigations.

## Article 20.1.2 — Access and Standing

1. Every person may complain to the Ombudsman without charge and by any suitable means; complaints may be made confidentially.
2. The Ombudsman may open an investigation on own initiative.
3. Access to the Ombudsman shall not depend on the exhaustion of other remedies.
4. The Ombudsman shall be accessible to persons in detention and in other closed settings, including through confidential correspondence.

## Article 20.1.3 — Powers of Investigation

1. The Ombudsman may require public authorities to produce documents and information and may question officials, including under oath.
2. Classified material shall be reviewed under special procedures that preserve its confidentiality without excluding the Ombudsman.
3. Obstruction of an investigation shall be sanctioned by law.

## Article 20.1.4 — Recommendations and Reports

1. The Ombudsman may recommend that an authority remedy the maladministration; the authority shall reply within a reasonable time, stating the action taken or the reasons for taking none.
2. Where no adequate remedy follows, the Ombudsman shall report the matter to the Parliament.
3. The Ombudsman shall publish an annual report and may publish special reports on systemic failures, consistent with Article 1.6.1.
4. Reports shall be laid before the Parliament without alteration by the executive.

## Design Note

*The Ombudsman descends from the Swedish model of an officer of Parliament who audits administration on behalf of the citizen. Its recommendatory power is deliberate: persuasion backed by publicity and by report to Parliament often corrects administration faster than litigation, and at no cost to the complainant. Own-initiative jurisdiction converts isolated complaints into systemic reform, which is the office's principal public value.*
