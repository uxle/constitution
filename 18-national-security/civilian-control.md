# Section 18.3 — Civilian Control

> **Part 18 — National Security and Defense** · Articles 18.3.1 – 18.3.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section secures democratic control of the armed forces through a civilian Minister of Defense, command of the President within policy set by the Parliament, and continuous parliamentary scrutiny.*

## Article 18.3.1 — Civilian Minister of Defense

1. The Minister of Defense shall be a civilian.
2. The Minister of Defense is accountable to the Parliament for the armed forces and the defense administration and shall answer questions and appear before committees under the standing rules. 
3. Serving officers may not hold ministerial office or exercise ministerial authority; the chain of command remains distinct from the government of the day.

## Article 18.3.2 — The President as Commander-in-Chief

1. The President is commander-in-chief of the armed forces.
2. The President exercises this command within defense policy established by the Parliament and through the Minister of Defense; the modalities of operational command shall be regulated by law.
3. The command authority of the President does not displace the responsibility of the executive to the Parliament for defense matters.

## Article 18.3.3 — Parliamentary Defense Committee

1. A standing committee of the Parliament shall scrutinize defense policy, budgets, procurement, and deployments. 
2. The committee shall be informed in advance, to the fullest extent consistent with the safety of the Republic, of any intended deployment of the armed forces outside the territory of the Republic.
3. The committee and its members may access classified information under security procedures fixed by law; secrecy shall not be used to defeat the committee's essential functions.

## Article 18.3.4 — Deployment Abroad

1. The armed forces shall be deployed outside the territory of the Republic only with the prior authorization of the Parliament, given by law or by resolution.
2. In urgent cases of imminent danger to citizens abroad or of humanitarian rescue, the President may order a temporary deployment; it shall be reported to the Parliament without delay and lapses unless approved within fourteen days.
3. International agreements governing deployments abroad require approval under Article 19.2.2.

## Design Note

*Civilian control is implemented here at three levels: a civilian minister with personal accountability, a head of state whose command is bound by parliamentary policy, and a committee empowered to see what it must in order to judge. The urgent-deployment exception is drawn narrowly and paired with mandatory reporting and rapid sunset, a design used to reconcile rescue operations with the parliamentary war power. Conditioning foreign deployments on approval keeps the decision to use force where the Constitution places the decision to make war.*
