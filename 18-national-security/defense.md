# Section 18.1 — Defense

> **Part 18 — National Security and Defense** · Articles 18.1.1 – 18.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes defense as a sovereign duty of the Republic, gives the armed forces an exclusively defensive orientation, and places defense law, policy, and resources under constitutional and democratic control.*

## Article 18.1.1 — Purpose of Defense

1. The Republic shall maintain armed forces to defend its sovereignty, territorial integrity, and constitutional order against armed attack.
2. Defense is a sovereign duty of the Republic and shall be exercised exclusively under this Constitution and law enacted by the Parliament, to which the armed forces remain subordinate at all times.

## Article 18.1.2 — Prohibition of Wars of Aggression

1. The Republic renounces war as an instrument of national policy and shall not wage a war of aggression.
2. The armed forces may be employed outside the territory of the Republic only in individual or collective self-defense or in operations authorized by the Parliament.
3. The structure, doctrine, training, and armament of the armed forces shall remain defensive in character.

## Article 18.1.3 — Defense Policy and Resources

1. Defense policy shall be determined by Act of the Parliament and implemented by the executive under civilian direction.
2. The executive shall present an annual defense report to the Parliament covering readiness, deployments, and expenditure.
3. Defense expenditure shall be appropriated through the annual budget adopted by the Parliament; no armed force shall be raised, maintained, or deployed except pursuant to appropriation and authorization by law.

## Article 18.1.4 — Conscription and Alternative Service

1. Compulsory military service may be imposed only by or under an Act of the Parliament of general application.
2. A person who refuses armed service on grounds of conscience or religion shall have the right to alternative service of equivalent duration and comparable burden, of a civilian or non-combatant character.
3. Alternative service:
   (a) shall not be designed or administered as punishment; and
   (b) shall carry no civil or criminal disability beyond its performance.

## Article 18.1.5 — Support to Civil Authorities

1. The armed forces may assist civil authorities in natural disasters and other grave crises when civilian means are insufficient.
2. Such assistance requires the request of the competent civilian authority, remains under civilian direction at all times, and lasts only as long as the need persists.
3. Assistance shall not include policing tasks unless a state of emergency has been declared under Part 26 and an Act so provides.

## Design Note

*The renunciation of wars of aggression follows constitutions that entrench a defensive orientation as supreme law, while expressly preserving the inherent right of individual and collective self-defense. Making defense policy, money, and doctrine matters for the Parliament converts symbolic civilian primacy into enforceable control. The alternative-service guarantee protects conscientious objection without allowing blanket exemption from civic duty.*
