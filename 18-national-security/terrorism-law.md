# Section 18.7 — Terrorism Law

> **Part 18 — National Security and Defense** · Articles 18.7.1 – 18.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section disciplines counter-terrorism: narrow offense definitions, unchanged ordinary evidence and procedure, an absolute ban on coercive interrogation, reviewable administrative measures, and sunset clauses.*

## Article 18.7.1 — Narrow Definitions

1. Terrorism offenses shall be defined by Act with precision and limited to serious acts intended to cause death, serious bodily injury, grave destruction of property, or grave disruption of essential services, committed with the aim of intimidating the public or compelling a public authority.
2. An offense shall not be established by the political purpose of the alleged offender alone; advocacy, protest, dissent, industrial action, and the ordinary work of journalists shall not constitute terrorism offenses, consistent with Article 14.9.2.

## Article 18.7.2 — Ordinary Evidence and Procedure

1. The ordinary rules of evidence and criminal procedure apply in full to the investigation and prosecution of terrorism offenses; special tribunals are prohibited.
2. Evidence derived from torture or from cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment shall be excluded absolutely, whatever its apparent reliability.
3. Pre-trial detention in terrorism cases shall not exceed the ordinary limits and remains subject to Article 1.7.4.

## Article 18.7.3 — No Torture or Degrading Interrogation

1. No person shall be subjected to torture or to cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment in the investigation of terrorism, in whatever circumstances and by whatever authority.
2. Interrogation shall be recorded throughout; coercion, threats, and inducements are prohibited and taint every consequence derived from them.

## Article 18.7.4 — Administrative Measures and Listing

1. Preventive administrative measures, including designation and listing of persons or organizations, require a statutory basis, notice, and an effective opportunity to contest the measure before a court, consistent with Article 1.6.4.
2. Secrecy shall not deprive a person of the substance of a fair hearing; a court may use a summary procedure to protect genuinely sensitive evidence.

## Article 18.7.5 — Sunsets and Review

1. Special measures departing from ordinary law shall be adopted for a defined period and renewed only by the Parliament after an evaluation of their necessity and proportionality.
2. A public review of the impact of special measures on rights and on security shall be conducted at the intervals fixed by law, and its findings shall be laid before the Parliament.

## Design Note

*Terrorism statutes fail most often through vagueness, and this Section responds by importing the narrow definitions discipline of ordinary criminal law. Keeping ordinary evidence and procedure intact rejects the recurrent claim that security justifies separate courts or lowered standards; the absolute exclusion of torture-derived evidence guards the integrity of verdicts as much as the dignity of suspects. Sunset and review clauses acknowledge that exceptional powers, once enacted, rarely repeal themselves.*
