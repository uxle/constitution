# Section 18.5 — National Security Oversight

> **Part 18 — National Security and Defense** · Articles 18.5.1 – 18.5.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section creates the machinery of oversight for security and intelligence bodies: a cleared parliamentary committee, an independent Inspector-General, annual reporting, and a protected disclosure channel.*

## Article 18.5.1 — Parliamentary Oversight Committee

1. A special committee of the Parliament shall oversee the intelligence services and other bodies exercising national security functions.
2. The committee shall receive reports, examine activities, and may require the attendance of and answers from the heads of services.
3. Members shall hold security clearance under law; the committee shall publish its reports with only narrowly confined redactions for genuinely sensitive material.

## Article 18.5.2 — Inspector-General for Security and Intelligence

1. An independent Inspector-General shall audit compliance of security and intelligence bodies with law, investigate complaints, and inspect operations.
2. The Inspector-General is appointed in the manner provided by law for a single term and may be removed only for cause by a qualified majority of the Parliament.
3. The Inspector-General may access any information, document, or premises of the services; obstruction is sanctioned by law.

## Article 18.5.3 — Annual Reporting

1. Each service shall submit an annual report on its activities to the committee and to the Inspector-General.
2. A public version of each report shall be laid before the Parliament each year, with omissions limited to what the committee approves as genuinely sensitive.

## Article 18.5.4 — Protected Disclosure Channel

1. A secure channel shall receive disclosures from staff and contractors of security and intelligence bodies concerning serious unlawful conduct, with protection against retaliation consistent with the whistleblower regime under Part 27.
2. Disclosures shall be examined by the Inspector-General; knowingly false or malicious disclosures receive no protection.
3. No person shall be penalized for a disclosure made in good faith through the channel established under this Article.

## Design Note

*Secrecy is legitimate in security work, but institutions clothed in secrecy must be answerable to institutions empowered to look. Pairing a cleared parliamentary committee with a full-time Inspector-General combines political accountability with expert, continuous audit, a combination favored in several mature oversight systems. Annual public reporting disciplines services without publishing what endangers them. The protected channel addresses the reality that the gravest abuses are most often revealed from within.*
