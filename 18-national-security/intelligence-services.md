# Section 18.4 — Intelligence Services

> **Part 18 — National Security and Defense** · Articles 18.4.1 – 18.4.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section defines the legal basis of intelligence services, subjects intrusive collection to judicial warrants, prohibits political surveillance, and guarantees remedies for victims.*

## Article 18.4.1 — Mandates Defined by Law

1. Intelligence services may be established and may exercise powers only on the basis of an Act of the Parliament that defines their mandate, powers, and limits.
2. Each service shall have a single statutory purpose, such as foreign intelligence or domestic security, stated with precision.
3. Intelligence services shall not exercise police powers of arrest, search of persons, or prosecution.

## Article 18.4.2 — Judicial Warrants for Intrusive Collection

1. Intrusive collection, including interception of communications and surveillance of persons, premises, or devices, requires prior judicial authorization upon a showing of necessity and proportionality, consistent with Article 1.5.5.
2. Authorization shall specify the person, place, means, and duration and may be renewed only on a fresh showing.
3. Collection without prior authorization is permitted only where life or grave public safety is at immediate risk; it shall be submitted without delay for retrospective judicial confirmation, and data not confirmed shall be deleted.

## Article 18.4.3 — No Political Surveillance

1. Intelligence services shall not collect information on persons or organizations because of their political opinions, religious beliefs, lawful association, or lawful journalism.
2. Collection concerning a lawful organization is permissible only on individualized suspicion of an offense within the statutory mandate and shall be reported to the oversight committee established under Section 18.5.

## Article 18.4.4 — Remedies for Victims

1. A person who believes an intelligence service has acted unlawfully toward them may complain to the Inspector-General under Article 18.5.2 and to the courts.
2. Courts shall afford an effective remedy, including suppression of unlawfully obtained information and just compensation.
3. Where full disclosure of evidence would endanger the safety of the Republic, a court may use a summary procedure; the substance of the complaint shall nonetheless be independently tested.

## Design Note

*Defining each service by a single statutory purpose counters mandate creep, the historical engine of domestic spying. Requiring judicial warrants before intrusive collection mirrors the constitutional standard for intrusions on communications and prevents oversight from becoming self-review. The prohibition of political surveillance protects the associational and expressive life that Article 1.3 and Article 1.4 guarantee. Summary procedures preserve both secrecy and fairness: the State may withhold evidence, but never the judge.*
