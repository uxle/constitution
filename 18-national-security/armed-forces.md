# Section 18.2 — The Armed Forces

> **Part 18 — National Security and Defense** · Articles 18.2.1 – 18.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs the composition of the armed forces, the constitutional oath, the rights and duties of service members, the strict limits on internal deployment, and military discipline.*

## Article 18.2.1 — Composition and Recruitment

1. The armed forces consist of the land, maritime, air, and such other components as an Act of the Parliament establishes.
2. Service is voluntary unless compulsory service has been introduced under Article 18.1.4.
3. Recruitment shall proceed by merit and be open to all who meet uniform and objective requirements fixed by law, without discrimination.

## Article 18.2.2 — Oath to the Constitution

1. Every member of the armed forces shall, upon entry into service, swear or solemnly affirm an oath to respect and defend this Constitution, the laws, and the democratic order of the Republic.
2. The text of the oath shall be prescribed under Part 30.
3. Orders contrary to this Constitution, to the laws, or to the rules of international law applicable to armed conflict shall not be obeyed; obedience to a manifestly unlawful order does not excuse the person who obeys it.

## Article 18.2.3 — Rights and Duties of Service Members

1. Service members retain their fundamental rights save for limitations strictly required by military service and consistent with Article 1.1.4.
2. Service members have the right to vote, to lawful remuneration and rest, and to petition public authorities.
3. Service members may join professional associations recognized by law; bargaining over operational and command matters is excluded.

## Article 18.2.4 — Internal Deployment

1. The armed forces may be deployed within the territory of the Republic only by or under an Act of the Parliament and only where civilian forces are insufficient to counter an armed threat to public safety or a grave disaster.
2. Internal deployment:
   (a) shall be proportionate and temporary;
   (b) shall remain under civilian command at all times; and
   (c) shall lapse when the need ends.
3. Measures affecting individual liberty during internal deployment remain subject to Article 1.7.4.

## Article 18.2.5 — Discipline and Criminal Jurisdiction

1. Disciplinary power shall be exercised under law, with a right to independent review of sanctions.
2. Serious criminal offenses by service members shall be tried before the ordinary courts; military tribunals, if any are established by law, may hear only minor service-related offenses and must satisfy Article 1.7.1.
3. The non-derogable core of fair-trial rights under Article 1.7.5 applies to every proceeding affecting a service member.

## Design Note

*An oath addressed to the Constitution, not to a person or party, gives every member a textual ground for refusing unlawful orders. Constitutions differ on whether the military may police at home; this Section permits internal deployment only by statute, in necessity, and under civilian command. Sending serious crimes to the ordinary courts preserves uniform fair-trial standards while minor discipline stays with the chain of command.*
