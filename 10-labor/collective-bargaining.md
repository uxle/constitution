# Section 10.7 — Collective Bargaining

> **Part 10 — Labor** · Articles 10.7.1 – 10.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section builds the machinery of negotiated industrial relations: a duty to bargain in good faith, the binding force of collective agreements, extension mechanisms, and the peaceful strike right with essential-services limits.*

## Article 10.7.1 — Duty to Bargain in Good Faith

1. Employers and the organizations of workers shall bargain in good faith over terms and conditions of employment, shall exchange the information necessary for meaningful negotiation, and shall not refuse to bargain because another representative organization also exists.
2. The State shall maintain, with the participation of the social partners, machinery for conciliation and mediation of collective disputes, available promptly and free of charge.

## Article 10.7.2 — Binding Force of Agreements

1. Collective agreements bind the parties, who shall ensure their observance, and apply to the workers they cover for the period and scope they define.
2. During the term of an agreement, industrial action concerning its subject matter is barred between the parties.
3. Compulsory arbitration is prohibited except in essential services narrowly defined by law or where the parties consent.

## Article 10.7.3 — Extension of Agreements

1. The law may extend a collective agreement to an entire sector where the parties to it are demonstrably representative.
2. Extension shall require published reasons and shall not impair the entitlement of unorganized workers to standards no less favorable than the statutory floors.

## Article 10.7.4 — The Right to Strike

1. Workers and their organizations have the right to strike peacefully in defense of their occupational and social interests, in fulfillment of Article 1.9.3 (Trade unions and collective bargaining).
2. The strike shall be exercised in accordance with the procedures of the organizations concerned, and lockouts are subject to equivalent limitations.
3. No person shall suffer criminal penalty for peaceful participation in a lawful strike.

## Article 10.7.5 — Essential Services

1. In essential services, whose scope shall be narrowly defined by law, the right to strike shall be exercised through minimum service arrangements agreed between the parties or, failing agreement, fixed by an independent body.
2. Persons deprived of the right to strike in essential services shall have access to impartial arbitration.

## Design Note

*Good-faith bargaining and the extension power follow ILO Convention No. 154 and Recommendation No. 163, which treat extension as legitimate where representativeness is shown and reasons are published. The strike right is drafted peacefully and precisely because it is the only sanction workers hold against unilateral employer power, while the essential-services exception, narrowly defined and paired with arbitration, follows the settled approach of the ILO supervisory bodies. The accepted trade-off is the extension mechanism itself: it spreads agreed terms across a sector but can entrench incumbent bargaining agents.*
