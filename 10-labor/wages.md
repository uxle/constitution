# Section 10.3 — Wages

> **Part 10 — Labor** · Articles 10.3.1 – 10.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section secures remuneration as the core of just conditions of work: a living-wage orientation, a national minimum wage set through tripartite negotiation, equal pay, and the protection of payment.*

## Article 10.3.1 — Living-Wage Orientation

1. Every worker has the right to remuneration sufficient to provide a decent living for the worker and the family of the worker, in fulfillment of Article 1.9.2 (Just conditions of work).
2. The State shall pursue this objective progressively through wage-setting machinery, social transfers, and fiscal policy, and shall review adequacy against the cost of living at regular intervals.

## Article 10.3.2 — The National Minimum Wage

1. Parliament shall maintain a national minimum wage, fixed by law after negotiation in which the representative organizations of employers and workers participate on terms of equality with the Government.
2. The minimum wage shall take full account of the needs of workers and of economic factors, shall be adjusted at regular intervals, and shall not be reduced by private agreement.
3. Its coverage shall extend to every worker, including those paid by piece, task, or output, and to workers in every sector without exception.

## Article 10.3.3 — Equal Pay

1. Workers shall receive equal remuneration for work of equal value, including benefits and occupational pension rights, without discrimination on any ground, consistent with Article 1.2.3 (Equality of women and men).
2. Pay secrecy clauses are void, and workers and their representatives shall have access to the information necessary to verify compliance with this Article.

## Article 10.3.4 — Timely Payment

1. Wages shall be paid regularly, in the national currency or by transfer of equivalent value, and in full; payment in kind may be permitted only to the extent authorized by law and shall not replace the monetary wage.
2. Deductions shall be lawful only as prescribed by law and shall never reduce pay below the statutory minimum.
3. Claims for wages earned before the opening of insolvency proceedings shall enjoy priority in those proceedings as provided by law.

## Article 10.3.5 — Enforcement of Wage Claims

1. Claims for unpaid wages shall be enforceable through a speedy and inexpensive procedure, with limitation periods that do not extinguish remuneration earned by work.
2. Unlawful nonpayment shall attract surcharges payable to the worker, and repeated nonpayment shall constitute an offense.

## Design Note

*The section tracks ILO Conventions No. 95 on wage protection, No. 100 on equal remuneration, and No. 131 on minimum-wage fixing, and absorbs the living-wage aspiration adopted by the ILO in 2024. Because a constitution cannot fix an amount responsibly, the design constitutionalizes the process — tripartite negotiation, regular adjustment, insolvency priority — while leaving the figures to machinery responsive to economic conditions. The accepted trade-off is that the minimum wage floors the market but cannot by itself guarantee a living wage everywhere.*
