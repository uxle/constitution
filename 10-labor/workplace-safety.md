# Section 10.5 — Workplace Safety

> **Part 10 — Labor** · Articles 10.5.1 – 10.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section guarantees safe and healthy working conditions: the duty of employers, the machinery of inspection, the right of workers to remove themselves from danger, and occupational health services.*

## Article 10.5.1 — The Safe-Workplace Duty

1. Every worker has the right to safe and healthy working conditions, including protection from physical, chemical, biological, and psychosocial hazards, in fulfillment of Article 1.9.2 (Just conditions of work).
2. Every employer shall, so far as reasonably practicable, provide workplaces, equipment, and systems of work that are safe and without risk to health, together with information, training, and personal protective equipment at the expense of the employer.
3. The State shall formulate and periodically re-examine a national policy on occupational safety and health in consultation with the organizations of employers and workers.

## Article 10.5.2 — Risk Assessment and Information

1. Employers shall assess risks systematically, record and report occupational injuries and diseases, and maintain emergency procedures.
2. Workers and their representatives shall be informed of hazards, consulted on protective measures, and entitled to appoint safety representatives and committees without loss of pay.

## Article 10.5.3 — The Inspection Authority

1. Parliament shall establish an inspection authority for occupational safety and health, empowered to enter workplaces, order remediation, halt dangerous activity, and impose penalties.
2. The authority shall coordinate with the general labor inspectorate under Article 10.2.4 (Labor Inspection) and shall publish the aggregate results of its inspections.

## Article 10.5.4 — Right to Refuse Dangerous Work

1. A worker who has reasonable justification to believe that continuing work presents an imminent and serious danger to life or health may remove himself or herself from that danger and shall suffer no adverse consequence for doing so.
2. No worker may be required to return while the danger persists, except to take measures necessary to protect others.

## Article 10.5.5 — Occupational Health Services

1. The law shall provide for occupational health services entrusted with preventive advice, workplace surveillance, and the monitoring of the health of workers.
2. A system of compensation for employment injuries and occupational diseases, covering medical care, income support, and rehabilitation, shall be maintained by law.

## Design Note

*The architecture follows ILO Convention No. 155 and the European framework directive on safety and health at work, with the removal right recognized by both as the ultimate personal safeguard when prevention fails. The reasonably-practicable standard avoids an absolute liability that would be unworkable across industries, but the design pairs it with a dedicated inspection authority so that the standard cannot become a shield for negligence. Consultation rights convert workers from objects of protection into participants in it. The accepted trade-off is judgment-laden reasonableness review instead of strict liability.*
