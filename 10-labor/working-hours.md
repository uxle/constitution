# Section 10.4 — Working Hours

> **Part 10 — Labor** · Articles 10.4.1 – 10.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section limits the time a person may be required to work and guarantees rest: daily and weekly limits, paid annual leave, controlled overtime, and protection in night work.*

## Article 10.4.1 — Limits on Working Time

1. Ordinary working hours shall not exceed eight hours a day or forty hours a week, and total hours including overtime shall not exceed the maximum fixed by law.
2. These limits apply to every person in an employment relationship, including managerial, agricultural, and domestic workers, subject only to exceptions proportionate to genuine operational need.

## Article 10.4.2 — Rest Periods

1. Every worker is entitled to daily rest of not less than eleven consecutive hours and to an uninterrupted weekly rest period of not less than twenty-four hours.
2. Work exceeding six consecutive hours shall be interrupted by a rest break of a duration fixed by law.

## Article 10.4.3 — Paid Annual Leave

1. Every worker is entitled to paid annual leave of not less than four weeks, in fulfillment of Article 1.9.2 (Just conditions of work), in addition to public holidays.
2. Leave shall be genuine rest and may not be replaced by payment in lieu, except upon termination of employment.
3. Entitlement to leave shall not depend on a minimum period of service beyond that reasonably required to accrue leave.

## Article 10.4.4 — Overtime

1. Overtime shall be voluntary except where a lawful and narrowly defined necessity, such as an accident or force majeure, compels it, and shall attract compensation at a premium rate fixed by law.
2. Overtime shall be counted, recorded, and compensated whether performed at the workplace or remotely.

## Article 10.4.5 — Night Work and Flexibility

1. Night workers shall be entitled to reduced hours, periodic health assessment, and additional protection as prescribed by law.
2. The law may authorize flexible arrangements, including averaging of hours over a reference period, only where collective agreements provide safeguards and health monitoring remains in force.
3. Every worker shall have the right to disconnect from digital means of contact outside working hours, and derogations under this Article shall be reviewed periodically and shall not become the general rule.

## Design Note

*The baseline follows ILO Convention No. 1 of 1919, which fixed the eight-hour day, and the European Union Working Time Directive of 2003, whose rest-period logic the right to disconnect extends to digital work. The four-week leave floor constitutionalizes what international labor standards treat as the minimum for genuine rest, immune from waiver under Article 10.2.1 (Non-Waivable Floors). Counting remote overtime closes the loophole that home working once opened. The accepted trade-off is flexibility: averaging periods are tolerated in exchange for collective safeguards and periodic review.*
