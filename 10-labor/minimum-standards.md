# Section 10.2 — Minimum Standards

> **Part 10 — Labor** · Articles 10.2.1 – 10.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section fixes the non-waivable floor of labor protection and guarantees its reach to the workers most often excluded: minors, domestic and informal workers, and migrants.*

## Article 10.2.1 — Non-Waivable Floors

1. The standards established in this Part are minimum standards and shall not be diminished by contract, agreement, or waiver, except in favor of the worker.
2. Collective agreements may improve upon, but may not reduce, the standards of this Part; any term to the contrary is void and replaced by the statutory minimum.
3. The level of protection in force shall not be reduced by amendment.

## Article 10.2.2 — Working Minors

1. Parliament shall prescribe a minimum age for admission to employment, not below the end of compulsory schooling under Section 11.2 and in no case below fifteen years.
2. The worst forms of child labor, such as slavery, trafficking, debt bondage, and commercial sexual exploitation, are prohibited absolutely, consistent with Article 1.9.2 (Just conditions of work) and Article 1.15.3 (Protection from harm).
3. Persons above the minimum age but below eighteen years may work only under conditions protecting their safety, health, and schooling, and hazardous work by young persons is prohibited.

## Article 10.2.3 — Domestic and Informal Workers

1. Domestic workers, home workers, and workers in the informal economy shall enjoy the standards of this Part, including limits on hours, rest periods, and a written statement of terms.
2. The State shall pursue the progressive formalization of informal work, extending registration, social security coverage, and inspection without penalizing the workers concerned.
3. Migrant workers shall enjoy treatment no less favorable than national workers in respect of remuneration and conditions of work, and no employer shall retain the identity documents of a worker.

## Article 10.2.4 — Labor Inspection

1. The State shall maintain a system of labor inspection, adequately resourced and staffed by trained officials independent of improper influence, with free access to workplaces at any time without prior notice.
2. Inspectors may require remediation, impose administrative penalties, and refer violations for prosecution; inspection results shall be published in aggregate.

## Article 10.2.5 — Review of Standards

1. Parliament shall review the adequacy of the statutory floors at intervals not exceeding five years, with the participation of the organizations of employers and workers.
2. The review shall consider productivity, prices, and the composition of the labor force, and shall publish its findings and reasons.

## Design Note

*The floor follows the ILO Declaration on Fundamental Principles and Rights at Work, with the child-labor prohibitions tracking Conventions No. 138 and No. 182 and the coverage clauses adapting Convention No. 189 on domestic workers, historically the least inspected workforce. The inspectorate follows Convention No. 81, whose insistence on unannounced access recognizes that violations thrive on notice. The accepted trade-off is gradual formalization rather than immediate prohibition of informal work, which would drive vulnerable workers into hiding.*
