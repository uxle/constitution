# Section 26.1 — State of Emergency

> **Part 26 — Emergency and Crisis** · Articles 26.1.1 – 26.1.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs declaration, approval, duration, and review of the state of emergency.*

## Article 26.1.1 — Grounds and Declaration

1. A state of emergency may be declared only where war, armed conflict, widespread public danger, or grave catastrophe threatens the life of the nation and ordinary measures are demonstrably inadequate.
2. The President shall declare it on the proposal of the Government and with the countersignature of the Prime Minister.
3. The declaration shall state its grounds, territorial scope, intended measures, and the provisions of ordinary law affected.
4. The declaration shall be published immediately and communicated to the Parliament, which shall convene without delay.

## Article 26.1.2 — Approval and Duration

1. The declaration lapses thirty days after publication unless the Parliament approves it by a majority of the members of each House.
2. The Parliament shall vote on the declaration within seven days.
3. Approval and each renewal may be given for up to thirty days.
4. The total period shall not exceed six months in any twelve-month period unless war is authorized under Article 26.4.1 (Authorization of War).

## Article 26.1.3 — Proportionate and Reasoned Measures

1. Emergency measures shall be adopted only by a law or a ministerial order founded on a law, confirmed by the Parliament within seven days; the reasons for every measure shall be published upon adoption.
2. Every measure shall satisfy Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights) as the least restrictive means strictly required by the exigencies.
3. A measure applies only within its stated scope and duration and ceases with its grounds.
4. Internet access may be restricted only within the exception of Article 17.7.2 (No Internet Shutdowns).

## Article 26.1.4 — Immediate Judicial Review

1. Every declaration and emergency measure is subject to immediate judicial review; no law may exclude or delay it.
2. Any affected person may apply without charge; the Constitutional Court decides challenges to the declaration with priority.
3. The courts shall remain open, and their ordinary jurisdiction shall not be curtailed.
4. A court shall set aside any measure exceeding the grounds or outlasting the danger.

## Article 26.1.5 — Termination

1. The President shall terminate the state of emergency without delay once the grounds have ceased; the Parliament may terminate it at any time.
2. Upon termination, every emergency measure lapses automatically and ordinary law resumes in full.
3. Termination shall be published immediately.

## Article 26.1.6 — Reporting and Transparency

1. The Government shall report to the Parliament at intervals not exceeding fourteen days, stating which measures remain in force and why.
2. A public register of emergency measures, their legal bases, and duration shall be maintained.

## Design Note

*The declaration, exigency, and notification requirements restate the derogation standard of Article 4 of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights. The six-month ceiling, the open courts, and countersignature treat emergency powers as a loan from ordinary law for which the requesting Government must answer.*
