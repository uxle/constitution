# Section 26.4 — War Powers

> **Part 26 — Emergency and Crisis** · Articles 26.4.1 – 26.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section allocates the war powers: the Parliament authorizes war, the President directs operations, and the law of armed conflict protects persons in the Republic's power.*

## Article 26.4.1 — Authorization of War

1. A state of war shall not be declared and war shall not be waged except with the authorization of the Parliament by a majority of the members of each House, consistent with Article 18.1.2 (Prohibition of Wars of Aggression).
2. The authorization shall state its purpose, legal basis, geographic scope, and the maximum duration of hostilities.
3. The President directs operations as commander-in-chief within the defense policy established by the Parliament, under Article 18.3.2 (The President as Commander-in-Chief).

## Article 26.4.2 — Major Deployments

1. The armed forces shall be deployed outside the Republic only with the prior authorization of the Parliament under Article 18.3.4 (Deployment Abroad).
2. A deployment ordered in urgent necessity shall be reported to the Parliament without delay and lapses unless approved within fourteen days.
3. Deployment within the Republic follows Article 18.2.4 (Internal Deployment).

## Article 26.4.3 — Duration and Termination

1. A war authorization shall be given for a limited period and renewed only by the Parliament.
2. The Parliament may terminate the state of war at any time by majority vote of the members present in each House.
3. Upon cessation of hostilities, the Government shall report to the Parliament and the state of war shall end by proclamation.
4. Derogations from ordinary law during war are admissible only under Section 26.1 and within the limits of Section 26.5.

## Article 26.4.4 — Reporting and Oversight

1. The Government shall report on the conduct of hostilities at regular intervals and immediately upon the Parliament's request.
2. The standing parliamentary defense committee shall be informed to the fullest extent consistent with the safety of the Republic, under Article 18.3.3 (Parliamentary Defense Committee).
3. Every deployment decision shall be documented and available to the committee.

## Article 26.4.5 — Protection of Civilians and Prisoners

1. The rules of international law applicable to armed conflict bind the Republic and every member of its armed forces.
2. The Republic shall protect and treat humanely: civilians; persons hors de combat and prisoners of war; and the wounded and shipwrecked, who shall be collected and cared for.
3. Grave breaches of the law of armed conflict shall be investigated and prosecuted under the Republic's criminal law.
4. Independent humanitarian organizations shall be permitted to perform their functions in areas under the Republic's control, under conditions fixed by law.

## Design Note

*Concurrent parliamentary majorities keep the gravest decision a republic takes with its widest mandate, while the commander-in-chief directs operations rather than choosing their purposes. The fourteen-day sunset on urgent deployments ensures necessity never ripens into a standing war power. The humanitarian clauses entrench the Geneva Conventions because the treatment of prisoners and civilians is where the temptation to except oneself from law is strongest.*
