# Section 26.3 — Pandemics

> **Part 26 — Emergency and Crisis** · Articles 26.3.1 – 26.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs the public-health emergency regime: graduated escalation, limits on coercive measures, scientific advice, compensation, and continuity of essential services.*

## Article 26.3.1 — Graduated Response

1. The ordinary public-health powers of Section 12.2 (Public Health) apply first and shall be used wherever they suffice.
2. A public-health emergency may be declared only where those powers are demonstrably inadequate, and only under Article 26.1.1 (Grounds and Declaration).
3. The declaration shall state the nature of the risk, the measures contemplated, the territorial scope, and the anticipated duration.
4. The declaration lapses, and is approved and renewed, under Article 26.1.2 (Approval and Duration).

## Article 26.3.2 — Limits on Coercive Measures

1. The following measures may be imposed only by law, on the best available evidence, and only where necessary and proportionate to the risk:
   (a) isolation;
   (b) quarantine;
   (c) mandatory examination or treatment; and
   (d) restrictions on movement or assembly.
2. Such measures shall be the least restrictive available, shall be time-limited, and shall be subject to prompt review by a court or an independent body.
3. Coercive measures shall not discriminate and shall serve no purpose other than the protection of health.
4. The special protection of health data under Article 12.6.1 (Specially Protected Data) applies without exception during a public-health emergency.

## Article 26.3.3 — Independent Scientific Advice

1. Measures with major public-health impact shall rest on the advice of the scientific body established under Article 12.2.4 (Scientific Advice and Transparency).
2. The advice received, the evidence relied upon, and the reasons for departing from it shall be published.
3. Dissenting scientific opinions shall be recorded and published with the advice.

## Article 26.3.4 — Compensation for Extraordinary Burdens

1. A person bearing an extraordinary and disproportionate burden imposed for the protection of public health is entitled to compensation as the law provides.
2. Extraordinary burdens include compulsory exclusion from work and the closure of a lawful business.
3. Claims shall be decided within a reasonable time and are subject to judicial review.

## Article 26.3.5 — Continuity of Essential Services

1. During a public-health emergency, public authorities shall secure the continuity of healthcare, care in institutions, education, and the supply of food and essential goods.
2. Restrictions within care facilities shall respect the dignity of the persons confined and their contact with the outside world.
3. In-person education shall be interrupted only for the shortest necessary period, and distance learning shall be provided meanwhile.

## Design Note

*The graduated structure makes escalation the exception: ordinary communicable-disease powers come first, and the emergency regime is reserved for the rare situation they cannot manage. The science provisions make transparency the price of coercion, since published advice and recorded dissent let the public judge whether measures track the evidence. Compensation borrows the old quarantine-compensation tradition and converts it into a general principle of public-health solidarity.*
