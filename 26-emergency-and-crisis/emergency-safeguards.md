# Section 26.5 — Emergency Safeguards

> **Part 26 — Emergency and Crisis** · Articles 26.5.1 – 26.5.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section entrenches the limits of every crisis regime: non-derogable rights, continuing oversight, expiry of measures, the amendment freeze, and accountability after termination.*

## Article 26.5.1 — Non-Derogable Rights

1. The rights listed in Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights) may not be derogated from in any circumstance, in war as in peace.
2. Beyond that list, a right may be limited in an emergency only under Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights), to the extent strictly required by the exigencies.
3. A provision purporting to suspend a non-derogable right is void from adoption, and no organ of the State may act upon it.

## Article 26.5.2 — Habeas Corpus and Judicial Access

1. The right to bring habeas corpus proceedings remains available in full at all times.
2. Every person detained under emergency powers shall be brought promptly before a judge and informed of the reasons in a language the person understands.
3. Secret places of detention are prohibited; every place of detention remains subject to judicial access and to inspection by the Ombudsman and the Human Rights Commission.

## Article 26.5.3 — Continuing Parliamentary and Institutional Oversight

1. The Parliament shall review the continuation of the emergency at every renewal and may attach conditions to approval.
2. The Ombudsman and the Human Rights Commission retain their full mandates during an emergency and shall report publicly on the observance of rights, under Article 20.2.4 (Periodic Reports).
3. Obstruction of oversight institutions shall be sanctioned by law.

## Article 26.5.4 — Expiry of Emergency Measures

1. Every emergency measure lapses automatically when the emergency ends, without repeal.
2. An emergency measure shall not acquire permanence by repetition; its provisions may be re-enacted as ordinary law only through the full legislative process, and emergency legislation shall remain formally distinct from ordinary law.

## Article 26.5.5 — Amendment Freeze

1. During a state of emergency or war, no amendment of this Part, of Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights), or of the entrenched provisions of Section 30.5 may be introduced, adopted, or proclaimed.
2. The restriction in clause 1 subsists until six months after termination of the emergency.
3. A purported amendment in breach of this Article is void.

## Article 26.5.6 — Accountability After Termination

1. Within six months after termination of an emergency, an independent review of the measures taken shall be laid before the Parliament and published.
2. A person subjected to a measure unlawful under this Constitution is entitled to compensation without prejudice to any other remedy.
3. Officials remain personally responsible for unlawful acts committed under emergency powers, under Article 4.8.4 (Accountability and Review).

## Design Note

*This Section makes the emergency powers of the preceding Sections acceptable: everything not expressly non-derogable is derogable, and everything derogable is temporary, reported, and reviewable. The amendment freeze and its six-month tail prevent a government from manufacturing an emergency in order to rewrite the constitution, while post-termination review and compensation follow the truth-seeking model of modern commissions of inquiry.*
