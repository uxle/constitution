# Section 1.14 — Consumer Rights

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.14.1 – 1.14.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the safety of goods and services, truthful information, fair contract terms, and accessible redress for consumers.*

## Article 1.14.1 — Safe Goods and Services

1. Every person has the right to goods and services that are safe and that conform to the standards provided by law.
2. Producers, importers, and suppliers are liable, under the conditions provided by law, for damage caused by defective goods or unsafe services.
3. Where a product or service proves dangerous after placement on the market, the supplier shall inform the public and, where required by law, withdraw it.

## Article 1.14.2 — Truthful Information

1. Consumers have the right to accurate and complete information on the essential characteristics, price, and terms of goods and services.
2. Advertising shall not mislead; claims shall be verifiable; and practices that materially distort a consumer's decision-making are prohibited as provided by law.
3. Information shall be provided in plain language, and labeling requirements shall be set by law.

## Article 1.14.3 — Fair Contract Terms

1. Contract terms that have not been individually negotiated shall be fair; a term is unfair where it causes a significant imbalance in the parties' rights and obligations to the detriment of the consumer.
2. An unfair term shall not bind the consumer, without prejudice to the validity of the remainder of the contract.

## Article 1.14.4 — Accessible Redress

1. Consumers have access to complaints procedures, to small-claims and simplified procedures, and to collective actions provided by law.
2. Redress shall be effective, proportionate, and dissuasive, and procedural costs shall not exceed what the value of the claim reasonably permits.
3. The general law of contracts, within which these guarantees operate, is stated in Part 16 (Civil Law).

## Design Note

*Consumer rights are constitutionalized to anchor a field otherwise scattered across commercial codes, with the anchor deliberately modest: safety, truthful information, fair standard terms, and an accessible remedy. The unfair-terms rule borrows the significant-imbalance standard developed in comparative consumer law, which targets adhesion contracts without displacing ordinary contract doctrine. Collective redress is included because individual losses are typically small while aggregate harm is large, which makes litigation economics a matter of access to justice.*
