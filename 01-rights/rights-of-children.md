# Section 1.15 — Rights of Children

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.15.1 – 1.15.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the rights of the child: best interests as the paramount consideration, participation, protection from violence and exploitation, and non-derogable essentials.*

## Article 1.15.1 — Rights of the Child

1. Every child has the rights of this Section without discrimination of any kind, irrespective of the child's or the parents' status, including birth, nationality, or residence status.
2. A child is a person below the age of eighteen years.

## Article 1.15.2 — Best Interests of the Child

1. In all actions concerning a child, whether by public or private institutions, the best interests of the child shall be a paramount consideration.
2. Proceedings affecting a child shall be adapted to the child's age and situation and shall be decided without undue delay.

## Article 1.15.3 — Participation of Children

1. A child capable of forming views has the right to express those views freely in matters affecting the child, and due weight shall be given to them according to age and maturity.
2. The child's views shall be heard, directly or through a representative, in judicial and administrative proceedings affecting the child.

## Article 1.15.4 — Protection from Violence and Exploitation

1. Every child has the right to protection from all forms of violence, neglect, exploitation, and trafficking, and from work that jeopardizes health, education, or development, consistent with Article 1.9.4 (Prohibition of Child Labor).
2. The State shall protect the child from corporal punishment and from other cruel or degrading forms of discipline.
3. Protection services implementing this Section are stated in Part 22 (Social Policy), and the duties of all institutions toward children in Part 28 (Special Populations).

## Article 1.15.5 — Non-Derogable Essentials

1. Every child has the right, from birth, to a name, to registration, and to acquire a nationality, consistent with Part 02 (Citizenship and Migration).
2. Every child has the right to family care or, where the family is unavailable, to alternative care consistent with the child's dignity and development.
3. The protections essential to the child's life, survival, and development may not be derogated from.

## Design Note

*The best-interests standard is made paramount, not merely primary, following Article 3 of the Convention on the Rights of the Child, so that competing public interests cannot outweigh the child where the child's interests are central. Participation rights are paired with protection rights, reflecting the modern understanding of children as subjects of rights rather than objects of care. The non-derogable essentials — a name, registration, a nationality, and care — secure the conditions of legal personhood that a stateless or undocumented child cannot assert alone.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.9.4 (Prohibition of Child Labor); Article 2.1.4 (Prevention of Statelessness); Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights).
