# Section 1.11 — Healthcare Rights

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.11.1 – 1.11.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees access to healthcare services, unconditional emergency care, progressive realization, and immediate minimum core obligations.*

## Article 1.11.1 — Right of Access to Healthcare Services

1. Every person has the right of access to healthcare services, including preventive, curative, and rehabilitative care.
2. Access shall be on a non-discriminatory basis and shall be organized so that distance, cost, and administrative complexity do not defeat the right.
3. The organization, financing, and quality standards of the health system are stated in Part 12 (Health).

## Article 1.11.2 — Emergency Care

1. A person in need of urgent care shall receive it without discrimination of any kind and without prior inquiry into ability to pay or administrative status.
2. Facilities receiving public funds shall maintain the emergency capacity that law requires of them, and private facilities shall not refuse urgent care on grounds of payment.

## Article 1.11.3 — Progressive Realization

1. The State shall take deliberate, concrete, and targeted steps, to the maximum of its available resources, toward the full realization of the right of access to healthcare.
2. A retrogressive measure, meaning a reduction of protection below its existing level, is permissible only where it is temporary, necessary, non-discriminatory, and justified under Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Article 1.11.4 — Minimum Core Obligations

1. At a minimum, the State shall ensure access to primary healthcare, to essential medicines, and to maternal and child health services for everyone within its territory, and shall prioritize underserved regions.
2. The minimum core is immediate and is not subject to progressive realization; any shortfall shall be identified and remedied with priority.

## Design Note

*The Section distinguishes the immediate minimum core from the progressively realizable remainder, a structure taken from international economic and social rights doctrine and tested in litigation over health rights. Emergency care is made immediate and unconditional because its denial is irreversible in a way that waiting-list delay is not. The bar on retrogression, subject to strict justification, prevents the slow erosion of achieved protection that would otherwise hollow out the right without ever formally repealing it.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights); Article 1.2.1 (Equality Before and Under the Law).
