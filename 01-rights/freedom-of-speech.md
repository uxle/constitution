# Section 1.3 — Freedom of Expression

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.3.1 – 1.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees freedom of expression, information, and the media, protects political speech at the core, prohibits prior restraint, and secures assembly and association.*

## Article 1.3.1 — Freedom of Expression and Opinion

1. Every person has the right to hold opinions without interference and to seek, receive, and impart information and ideas of every kind, orally, in writing, in print, in art, or through any media, regardless of frontiers.
2. No one shall be compelled to express or to declare an opinion.

## Article 1.3.2 — Freedom of Information and the Media

1. The media shall be free from censorship, from suspension, and from administrative control.
2. Editorial independence shall be protected, and the law may promote media pluralism and transparency of ownership.
3. Public bodies shall publish information proactively as provided by law, in addition to the right of access under Part 21 (Media and Information).

## Article 1.3.3 — Political Speech

1. Expression on political and public affairs occupies the core of this freedom and enjoys the widest protection, including criticism of public authorities, satire, and advocacy of lawful change.
2. Public officials shall tolerate greater scrutiny than private persons and may invoke their reputation only within the limits of Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Article 1.3.4 — Prohibition of Prior Restraint

1. No licensing, registration, or prior approval shall be required for the publication of print or online media.
2. A court may restrain publication in advance only where the harm is grave and imminent, the restraint is no broader than necessary, and the test of Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights) is satisfied.

## Article 1.3.5 — Peaceful Assembly and Association

1. Every person has the right to assemble peacefully and to form and join associations, including political parties and trade unions.
2. Notification schemes for assemblies shall serve public-safety purposes only and shall not operate as a requirement of permission.

## Design Note

*The prohibition of prior restraint is constitutionalized rather than left to media law because censorship by delay is the form of restriction hardest to remedy after the fact; the narrow judicial injunction exception keeps orders available for grave and imminent harm. Placing political speech at the core instructs courts to calibrate scrutiny by subject matter, an approach familiar from comparative free-expression doctrine. Assembly is paired with expression because, in practice, the public square is where speech acquires civic force.*
