# Section 1.9 — Labor Rights

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.9.1 – 1.9.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees freedom of association in labor matters, fair remuneration, the prohibition of forced and child labor, and the right to strike.*

## Article 1.9.1 — Freedom of Association in Labor Matters

1. Every worker and every employer has the right to form and join organizations of the person's own choosing to promote and defend occupational interests.
2. Organizations of workers and of employers may federate and may affiliate internationally; no one may be compelled to join or to remain outside.

## Article 1.9.2 — Fair Remuneration

1. Every worker has the right to remuneration sufficient for a decent standard of living for the worker and the worker's family.
2. Women and men shall receive equal pay for work of equal value; the standards implementing this Section are stated in Part 10 (Labor).

## Article 1.9.3 — Prohibition of Forced Labor

1. Slavery, servitude, and forced labor are prohibited and may not be derogated from, in accordance with Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights).
2. "Forced labor" does not include work required in lawful detention under conditions consistent with this Constitution, service of a military character where lawfully imposed with a civilian alternative, or civic obligations of a general nature.

## Article 1.9.4 — Prohibition of Child Labor

1. Work by children is prohibited below the minimum age provided by law and, at any age, in conditions harmful to health, safety, or development.
2. The worst forms of child labor are prohibited absolutely, and the protection of working minors is a duty of the State and of employers.

## Article 1.9.5 — Right to Strike

1. Workers have the right to strike in defense of their occupational interests, exercised peacefully and as provided by law.
2. The law may restrict the strike right in essential services only to the extent necessary to protect the life, personal safety, or health of the population, and only with a substitute mechanism for the settlement of disputes; restrictions remain subject to Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Design Note

*Labor rights are stated as fundamental rights rather than as directives of social policy, which entitles them to the full protection of the master limitation clause and to horizontal application against private employers. The definition of forced labor incorporates the internationally recognized exceptions — penal work, military service with a civilian alternative, and ordinary civic duties — so that the prohibition targets compulsion rather than obligation as such. The strike right is paired with a mandatory substitute mechanism in essential services, preserving bargaining power without holding the population hostage.*
