# Section 1.2 — Equality

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.2.1 – 1.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees equality before and under the law, prohibits discrimination on listed and open grounds, and disciplines affirmative measures.*

## Article 1.2.1 — Equality Before and Under the Law

1. All persons are equal before the law and entitled to its equal protection and equal benefit.
2. Equality under the law requires that like cases be treated alike and that different cases be treated differently only on reasonable and objective grounds.

## Article 1.2.2 — Prohibition of Discrimination

1. The State shall not discriminate, directly or indirectly, against any person on any ground, including race, color, ethnic or social origin, nationality, sex, gender, pregnancy, marital or family status, language, religion or belief, political or other opinion, disability, age, property, birth, sexual orientation, gender identity, or other status.
2. Discrimination is direct where a measure treats a person less favorably on a listed ground; it is indirect where an apparently neutral measure places persons sharing a listed ground at a particular disadvantage without justification under Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Article 1.2.3 — Intersectional Discrimination

1. Discrimination arising from the combination of two or more grounds shall be recognized and remedied as such.
2. The State shall collect, consistent with the privacy of persons, the information needed to detect and remedy intersectional disadvantage.

## Article 1.2.4 — Equality of Women and Men

1. Women and men are equal in dignity and in rights; the State shall remove obstacles to the full enjoyment of this equality, including in public employment and public appointments.
2. Measures protecting maternity or the health of mothers shall not constitute discrimination.

## Article 1.2.5 — Affirmative Measures

1. Measures may be adopted to advance persons or groups disadvantaged by past or continuing discrimination.
2. Such measures shall pursue the removal of disadvantage, shall be reasonable, and shall lapse when their purpose is substantially achieved; they remain subject to Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).
3. Affirmative measures shall not result in the permanent exclusion of any person from the exercise of a right.

## Design Note

*The open formula "other status" keeps the catalogue current, while the express recognition of intersectional discrimination reflects a development that most constitutional texts still omit. Affirmative measures are permitted but disciplined: they must target demonstrated disadvantage and remain subject to the master limitation clause, which prevents temporary correction from hardening into permanent preference. The distinction between equality before the law and equality under the law preserves both the formal and the substantive dimension.*
