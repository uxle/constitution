# Section 1.10 — Education Rights

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.10.1 – 1.10.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the right to education, free and compulsory basic education, equal access, and parental choice within public standards.*

## Article 1.10.1 — Right to Education

1. Every person has the right to education directed to the full development of the human personality and to the strengthening of respect for human rights and freedoms.
2. The State shall progressively make higher education and vocational training accessible to all on the basis of capacity.
3. The structure of the education system and its financing are stated in Part 11 (Education).

## Article 1.10.2 — Free and Compulsory Basic Education

1. Basic education shall be free of charge and compulsory for the period provided by law.
2. No child shall be excluded from basic education for lack of means, and the State shall remove economic barriers, including through material support.
3. Public basic education shall be available in every Province and shall reach children in rural and remote areas on equal terms.

## Article 1.10.3 — Equal Access

1. Access to educational institutions shall be without discrimination on any ground.
2. The State shall take measures to enable learners with disabilities and learners belonging to minorities to participate in education on an equal basis with others, including instruction in minority languages.

## Article 1.10.4 — Parental Choice

1. Parents may choose non-public education for their children, provided that the institutions chosen conform to the standards the State sets for the equivalent public stage and do not infringe the rights of the child.
2. The State may subsidize non-public institutions that meet public standards; a subsidy shall not condition admission on grounds prohibited by Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination).

## Design Note

*The right is layered: basic education is guaranteed immediately and free of charge, while higher education is governed by progressive realization, matching the capacity-based phrasing of international educational-rights law. Parental choice is preserved but subordinated to public standards and to the rights of the child, which prevents education from becoming a vehicle for the denial of other constitutional rights. This allocation anchors the non-negotiable core while leaving the design of the school system to Part 11 (Education).*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.2.1 (Equality Before and Under the Law); Article 1.15.2 (Best Interests of the Child); Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).
