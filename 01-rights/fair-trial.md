# Section 1.7 — Fair Trial

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.7.1 – 1.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the fair-trial rights of every person: an independent tribunal, the presumption of innocence, defense rights, habeas corpus, and a non-derogable core.*

## Article 1.7.1 — Right to a Fair Trial

1. In the determination of civil rights and obligations or of any criminal charge, every person is entitled to a fair and public hearing within a reasonable time by an independent and impartial tribunal established by law.
2. The public may be excluded only by order of the tribunal, only to protect minors, privacy, or the integrity of justice, and only to the extent necessary.

## Article 1.7.2 — Presumption of Innocence

1. Every person charged with an offense shall be presumed innocent until proven guilty according to law in a final judgment.
2. The burden of proof lies on the prosecution; the benefit of the doubt belongs to the accused; and public authorities shall not present a person as guilty before judgment.

## Article 1.7.3 — Rights of the Accused

1. In the determination of any criminal charge, the accused shall have, at minimum:
   (a) to be informed promptly, in a language the accused understands, of the nature and cause of the charge;
   (b) to adequate time and facilities for the preparation of the defense;
   (c) to defend in person or through counsel of choice, with state-funded legal aid where the interests of justice so require;
   (d) to examine witnesses against the accused and to obtain the attendance of witnesses for the defense; and
   (e) to the free assistance of an interpreter where needed.
2. The accused may not be compelled to testify or to confess guilt.

## Article 1.7.4 — Habeas Corpus and Liberty

1. Every person deprived of liberty shall be informed promptly of the reasons and shall be brought before a judge without undue delay.
2. Every detained person has the right to challenge the lawfulness of the detention before a court, which shall decide promptly and order release where the detention is unlawful.

## Article 1.7.5 — Non-Derogable Core

1. The guarantees that constitute the core of fair-trial rights, including habeas corpus and the right to an independent and impartial tribunal, may not be derogated from, in accordance with Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights).
2. In a state of emergency, the remaining fair-trial guarantees may be adjusted only under Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights) and never below the non-derogable core.

## Design Note

*The Section follows the structure of Article 6 of the European Convention on Human Rights but states the guarantees as direct constitutional commands and names habeas corpus expressly as non-derogable, closing the gap through which emergency regimes have historically eroded personal liberty. The presumption of innocence is extended to pre-judgment statements by public authorities, reflecting its function as a shield against the court of public opinion as well as the court of law. The prohibition of compelled testimony completes the accusatorial design.*
