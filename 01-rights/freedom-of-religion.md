# Section 1.4 — Freedom of Conscience and Religion

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.4.1 – 1.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees freedom of conscience and religion, mandates State neutrality, protects free exercise and the autonomy of religious communities, and declares the freedom non-derogable.*

## Article 1.4.1 — Freedom of Conscience and Religion

1. Every person has the right to freedom of conscience, thought, and religion, including the freedom to change religion or belief and to hold no belief.
2. This freedom may be manifested individually or in community, in public or in private, through worship, teaching, practice, and observance.
3. No person shall be coerced in matters of conscience or religion.

## Article 1.4.2 — Non-Establishment and Neutrality

1. The Republic has no state religion and shall maintain neutrality toward all religions and beliefs.
2. Public institutions shall neither privilege nor burden any religion or belief, and public office shall be open without religious test.
3. Cooperation with religious communities is permissible only where it serves a public purpose open to all on equal terms and does not confer religious authority on the State.

## Article 1.4.3 — Free Exercise

1. Religious communities may establish and maintain institutions, schools, charities, and places of worship, and may regulate their own religious affairs.
2. Restrictions on the manifestation of religion or belief are permissible only under Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights); the inner freedom of belief admits no limitation.

## Article 1.4.4 — Institutional Autonomy of Religious Communities

1. Religious communities shall determine their membership, doctrine, ministry, and worship without State interference, subject to generally applicable laws protecting rights and public order.
2. Religious instruction in public schools shall be voluntary, and alternatives of equal standing shall be available.

## Article 1.4.5 — Non-Derogation

1. Freedom of conscience and religion may not be derogated from, even during a state of emergency, in accordance with Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights).
2. Emergency measures shall not single out religious communities for restriction except under the ordinary test of Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Design Note

*The Section pairs non-establishment with institutional autonomy so that neutrality toward religion is protected in both directions: the State must not command religion, and religious communities must not be absorbed by the State. The absolute protection of inner belief, with manifestation alone subject to limitation, tracks the distinction drawn in comparative doctrine between the internal and external forums of conscience. The express non-derogation clause removes any doubt that this freedom survives emergencies intact.*
