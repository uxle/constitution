# Section 1.1 — Bill of Rights

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.1.1 – 1.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section defines the nature, application, limitation, and non-derogable core of the rights in this Part.*

## Article 1.1.1 — Nature and Purpose of Rights

1. The rights and freedoms of Part 01 (Rights and Freedoms) are inherent in human dignity and belong to everyone within the jurisdiction of the Republic.
2. The enumeration of particular rights does not deny other rights inherent in human dignity.

## Article 1.1.2 — Vertical and Horizontal Application

1. This Part binds all organs of the State and all persons and institutions exercising public power.
2. A provision of this Part also binds natural and juristic persons in their relations with one another to the extent that the right is capable of such application.

## Article 1.1.3 — Duties of Organs of State

1. All organs of the State shall respect, protect, promote, and fulfill the rights of this Part.
2. Respect requires non-interference; protection requires preventing interference by others; promotion and fulfillment require positive measures, within available resources where qualified.

## Article 1.1.4 — Limitation of Rights

1. The rights of this Part may be limited only by a law of general application, and only to the extent that the limitation is reasonable and justifiable in an open and democratic society founded on human dignity, equality, and freedom.
2. A limitation is reasonable and justifiable only if it satisfies every element of this test:
   (a) legality — it is prescribed by a published law, precise enough to regulate conduct, and adopted by a competent organ;
   (b) legitimate aim — it pursues an objective recognized by this Constitution or that law;
   (c) suitability — it is capable of achieving that objective;
   (d) necessity — there is no less restrictive means; and
   (e) proportionality stricto sensu — the benefit to the general interest outweighs the harm to the right.
3. The organ relying on a limitation bears the burden of justifying it.
4. Nothing in this Article authorizes derogation from the rights listed in Article 1.1.5 (Non-Derogable Rights).

## Article 1.1.5 — Non-Derogable Rights

1. The following rights may not be derogated from in any circumstance, including an emergency declared under Part 26 (Emergency and Crisis):
   (a) the right to life;
   (b) freedom from torture and from cruel, inhuman, or degrading treatment or punishment;
   (c) freedom from slavery and from forced labor;
   (d) freedom of conscience and religion;
   (e) the core of fair-trial rights, including habeas corpus; and
   (f) the principle of legality in criminal law.
2. A provision that purports to suspend any of these rights is void from its adoption.

## Design Note

*A single master limitation clause replaces per-right lists, so every restriction must pass one transparent test in a predictable sequence. The non-derogable list condenses the protections that international human-rights law treats as beyond suspension, as in Article 4 of the ICCPR and the regional emergency clauses. The burden of justification lies on the State, correcting the asymmetry of information between citizen and administration.*
