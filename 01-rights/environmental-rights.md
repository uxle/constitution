# Section 1.12 — Environmental Rights

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.12.1 – 1.12.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the right to a healthy environment, the duty of the State to protect it, and standing and remedies for its enforcement.*

## Article 1.12.1 — Right to a Healthy Environment

1. Every person has the right to an environment that is not harmful to health or well-being, including clean air, safe and sufficient water, healthy ecosystems, and a stable climate.
2. The right belongs to present and future generations alike and may be asserted before a serious threat is fully realized.

## Article 1.12.2 — Duty of the State

1. The State shall protect the environment, prevent pollution and ecological degradation, promote conservation, and secure ecologically sustainable development while promoting justifiable economic and social development.
2. The State shall apply a precautionary approach where there is a risk of serious or irreversible harm.
3. Environmental policy implementing this Section is stated in Part 13 (Environment and Natural Resources).

## Article 1.12.3 — Standing and Access to Justice

1. Any person, and any association acting in the interest of environmental protection, may seek review or other relief where this right or its implementation is threatened or breached, without needing to show injury beyond the interest relied upon.
2. Proceedings shall be expeditious, and the law may provide that costs do not deter meritorious environmental claims.

## Article 1.12.4 — Remedies and Enforcement

1. Courts may grant preventive orders, restoration orders, compensation, and any other effective remedy in environmental matters.
2. The standards adopted under Part 13 (Environment and Natural Resources) shall be interpreted in light of this Section.

## Design Note

*The right is framed to be enforceable before catastrophe: standing is granted at the threshold of serious threat and extends to associations, which corrects the structural mismatch between diffuse environmental harm and the individualized conception of injury. The substantive content — air, water, ecosystems, climate — is listed so that courts are not left to invent the baseline. Implementation is deliberately routed to Part 13 (Environment and Natural Resources), keeping this Section judicial rather than administrative in character.*
