# Section 1.5 — Privacy

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.5.1 – 1.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section protects the privacy of the person, home, communications, and personal data, and subjects intrusions to judicial authorization.*

## Article 1.5.1 — Right to Privacy

1. Every person has the right to privacy, which includes the person's body, home, papers, communications, image, and personal data.
2. Privacy is protected against interference by the State and, where capable of application, by private persons.

## Article 1.5.2 — Inviolability of the Home

1. The home is inviolable; no entry or search shall take place without the consent of the occupant or a judicial warrant.
2. A warrant shall issue only on reasonable grounds, shall identify the place and the purpose of the search, and shall be executed as provided by law; the criminal-procedure regime is stated in Part 15 (Criminal Procedure).

## Article 1.5.3 — Confidentiality of Communications

1. The confidentiality of letters, telephone, electronic, and all other communications shall be respected.
2. Interception or recording is permissible only under a judicial warrant that satisfies Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights), for a defined purpose, and for a defined period.

## Article 1.5.4 — Personal Data and Informational Self-Determination

1. Every person has the right to informational self-determination: to know what personal data concerning the person is processed, by whom, and for what purpose; to obtain rectification; and to a remedy against unlawful processing.
2. Personal data shall be processed fairly and lawfully, only with consent or another basis provided by law; oversight belongs to the Information Commission, and the statutory regime is stated in Part 17 (Digital Law).

## Article 1.5.5 — Judicial Authorization of Intrusions

1. Searches, surveillance, and other intrusive measures shall be authorized or reviewed by a court, shall be proportionate to a legitimate aim, and shall be documented.
2. A person subjected to an unlawful intrusion has the right to be informed of it once notification no longer endangers a lawful purpose of investigation, and to an effective remedy.

## Design Note

*The Section constitutionalizes the warrant requirement for the home and for communications so that the cornerstone safeguards of criminal procedure cannot be diluted by ordinary legislation. Informational self-determination is recognized as a distinct dimension of privacy, an approach inspired by the German census decision of 1983 and now reflected in comprehensive data-protection regimes. The duty to inform the subject of an intrusion once secrecy is no longer needed balances investigative effectiveness against the right to know.*
