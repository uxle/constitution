# Section 1.6 — Due Process

> **Part 01 — Rights and Freedoms** · Articles 1.6.1 – 1.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees fair administrative action — notice, reasons, hearing, impartiality, and review — for every public decision affecting rights or interests.*

## Article 1.6.1 — Fair Administrative Action

1. Every person has the right to fair administrative action in every decision of a public authority that affects the person's rights, interests, or legitimate expectations.
2. Fairness applies to decisions taken under statute, under delegated authority, or in the exercise of any other public function.

## Article 1.6.2 — Notice and Reasons

1. Before a decision that may adversely affect a person is taken, the authority shall give notice of the matter and of the applicable criteria.
2. A written decision shall state its reasons in sufficient detail to enable the person to understand it and to consider a remedy.

## Article 1.6.3 — Hearing and Representation

1. A person affected shall have a reasonable opportunity to present the person's case, to comment on adverse evidence, and, where the decision is serious, to be assisted or represented.
2. The complexity of the matter and the stakes for the person shall determine the degree of formality required.

## Article 1.6.4 — Review and Remedies

1. A person affected by an administrative decision has the right to seek its review, first where an internal appeal is provided and ultimately before a court.
2. Review shall be available without regard to the value or importance of the matter, and remedies shall be effective, proportionate, and timely.

## Article 1.6.5 — Impartiality and Automated Decisions

1. No person shall take a decision in which the person has a personal interest; decision-makers shall act without bias.
2. A decision that significantly affects a person's rights may be prepared with the support of automated systems but shall not be taken solely by automated means; a human decision-maker shall review the case at the person's request.

## Design Note

*Making fair administrative action a free-standing constitutional right, rather than a by-product of judicial review, owes much to the South African model and gives citizens a positive claim to notice, reasons, and hearing before decisions are taken. The graduated approach to formality keeps the right practical: more procedure where the stakes are high, less where they are modest. The automated-decision clause responds to a risk older texts could not foresee, requiring human review rather than prohibiting assistance by systems.*
