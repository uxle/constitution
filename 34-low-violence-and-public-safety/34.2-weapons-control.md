# Section 34.2 — Weapons Control

## Articles

**Article 34.2.1 (Licensing Principle).** Ownership of weapons capable of lethal force is subject to licensing that includes the psychological-fitness principles of Article 31.3, adapted for civilian applicants, plus safe-storage requirements.

**Article 34.2.2 (Prohibited Categories).** Weapons whose primary design purpose is mass casualty have no lawful civilian use anywhere in the territory; regional variation is permitted only for traditional, sporting, and defensive categories, not for this prohibited category.

**Article 34.2.3 (Registry and Tracing).** A world-linked weapons registry allows tracing of any weapon used in a crime back to its licensed owner, supporting both prevention and prosecution.

*Design Note: This Article sets a floor (no mass-casualty weapons, mandatory licensing with fitness screening) while leaving the harder question of ordinary civilian firearm policy to regional law, since cultures vary enormously and reasonably on this point.*

**Cross-references:** 34.1, 31.3, 38-penal-code-extended


## Implementation Detail

**Article 34.2.4 (Licence Renewal).** Civilian weapons licences are reviewed every three years, incorporating the psychological-fitness screening standard of Article 31.3 adapted for civilian risk assessment.
