# Section 34.3 — Violence Prevention and Rehabilitation

## Articles

**Article 34.3.1 (Early Intervention).** Schools (Part 11) and health systems (Part 12) must maintain early-identification and support programs for children and adults showing risk factors for future violence, framed as care rather than surveillance.

**Article 34.3.2 (Restorative Options).** Wherever consistent with victim safety, restorative-justice processes (38-penal-code-extended/restorative-justice.md) are offered as an alternative or supplement to incarceration for non-severe violent offenses.

**Article 34.3.3 (Reentry Support).** Formerly incarcerated persons are guaranteed housing, employment, and health-access support on release, consistent with 38-penal-code-extended/parole-and-reentry.md, because failed reentry is one of the strongest predictors of repeat violence.

*Design Note: Prevention and rehabilitation are treated here as safety infrastructure, on the same constitutional footing as roads or hospitals — not as leniency, but as the most effective known lever for reducing future harm.*

**Cross-references:** 34.1, 38-penal-code-extended, 11-education, 12-health


## Implementation Detail

**Article 34.3.4 (Coverage Target).** Early-identification and support programs under Article 34.3.1 must be available in 100% of public schools within 8 years of adoption, with interim coverage targets of 50% by year 4.
