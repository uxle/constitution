# Section 34.1 — The Low-Violence Constitutional Policy

## Articles

**Article 34.1.1 (Constitutional Commitment).** The State commits, as a matter of constitutional principle rather than ordinary policy, to the sustained reduction of violence — interpersonal, domestic, communal, and state — as measured by publicly reported metrics reviewed annually.

**Article 34.1.2 (Prevention Before Punishment).** Government budgets must demonstrate investment in violence-prevention (education, mental health access, poverty reduction, community mediation) proportionate to, and not merely as an afterthought to, spending on policing and punishment.

**Article 34.1.3 (State Violence Constrained).** The State itself is bound by the strictest proportionality standard when using force — police and military use of force is governed by the standards in Part 39 (policing) and Part 05/38 (use-of-force liability) — because a constitution that asks citizens to be less violent must first bind its own institutions.

**Article 34.1.4 (Annual Violence Report).** An independent Violence Reduction Commission publishes an annual report on trends, causes, and government performance against this Article, submitted to the Legislature and made public.

*Design Note: "Low violence" is deliberately framed as a metric-driven, budget-linked commitment rather than a slogan — the annual report in 34.1.4 is what keeps it enforceable rather than aspirational.*

## Implementation Detail

**Article 34.1.5 (Reporting Metrics).** The annual Violence Reduction Commission report (34.1.4) tracks, at minimum: homicide rate, domestic-violence incidence, youth-violence incidence, incarceration rate, and reoffending rate, each benchmarked against the prior five-year rolling average.

**Article 34.1.6 (Budget Ratio Target).** Government budgets must show prevention-oriented spending (education, mental health, poverty reduction, mediation) reaching parity with enforcement spending within 10 years, on a published annual glide path.
