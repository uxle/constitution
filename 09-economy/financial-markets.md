# Section 9.7 — Financial Markets

> **Part 09 — The Economy** · Articles 9.7.1 – 9.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section secures the freedom of capital markets and imposes the disclosure, integrity, and investor-protection rules on which their functioning depends.*

## Article 9.7.1 — Freedom of Capital Markets

1. The issuance, trading, and transfer of securities and other financial instruments shall be free, subject to the authorization and conduct requirements of this section.
2. No restriction may be imposed on capital markets except by law for investor protection, market integrity, monetary policy, or public security.

## Article 9.7.2 — Disclosure

1. An offer of securities to the public shall be preceded by a prospectus containing the information necessary for investors to assess the issuer and the securities.
2. Issuers of traded instruments shall publish periodic financial reports audited to statutory standards, and material information shall be disclosed to the market in a timely and equal manner.
3. Persons responsible for a prospectus containing false or misleading statements shall be liable to compensate investors who acquired securities in reliance on it.

## Article 9.7.3 — Market Integrity

1. Insider dealing, unlawful disclosure of inside information, and market manipulation shall be prohibited by law.
2. Market abuse shall be sanctioned by administrative fines or criminal penalties proportionate to the gravity of the conduct, without prejudice to the civil liability of wrongdoers toward injured parties.

## Article 9.7.4 — The Securities Regulator

1. An independent authority shall authorize, supervise, and sanction participants in financial markets, publish its rules and reasoned decisions, and report annually on the state of the markets.
2. Its members shall be appointed for fixed terms on grounds of competence and integrity and may be removed only for cause by a procedure prescribed by law.
3. The regulator shall cooperate with the supervisory authority for banks under Article 9.6.2 (Prudential Supervision) in the oversight of financial conglomerates.

## Article 9.7.5 — Investor Protection

1. Providers of investment services shall act honestly, fairly, and professionally in the best interest of their clients, shall assess the suitability of the products they offer, and shall communicate with clients in a clear and non-misleading manner.
2. Investors shall have access to effective collective remedies for losses caused by violations of this section, without prejudice to Article 5.6.5 (Effective Remedies).

## Design Note

*The disclosure philosophy follows the United States securities tradition of sunlight as the best disinfectant, operationalized through prospectus and periodic-reporting duties, while the integrity rules mirror modern market-abuse statutes. Suitability duties and collective redress answer the information asymmetry that disclosure alone leaves open between professionals and households. An independent regulator with fixed-term members completes the design, mirroring the institutional guarantees of the competition and banking authorities. The accepted trade-off is compliance cost, borne disproportionately by smaller issuers.*
