# Section 9.2 — Competition

> **Part 09 — The Economy** · Articles 9.2.1 – 9.2.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section establishes the protection of competition as a constitutional economic principle and entrusts its enforcement to an independent competition authority.*

## Article 9.2.1 — Protection of Competition

1. The Republic shall maintain an economic order founded on free and undistorted competition, which shall serve the welfare of consumers and the efficient allocation of resources.
2. Parliament shall enact rules preserving competition as the primary coordinator of markets, in conjunction with the sectoral provisions of this Part.
3. No organ of the State may adopt measures that eliminate competition in a market except as expressly authorized by a law serving a public interest and no broader than that interest requires.

## Article 9.2.2 — The Competition Authority

1. There shall be an independent competition authority, separate from the ministries, which shall investigate infringements, order remedies, and impose fines proportionate to the gravity and duration of an infringement.
2. The authority shall act upon its own initiative or upon complaint, shall decide with published reasons, and shall be subject to full judicial review of both findings and remedies.
3. Its members shall be appointed for fixed terms on grounds of competence and integrity and may be removed only for cause by a procedure prescribed by law.

## Article 9.2.3 — Prohibited Agreements

1. Agreements between undertakings, decisions of associations of undertakings, and concerted practices that prevent, restrict, or distort competition shall be prohibited by law.
2. The law may exempt an agreement that improves production or distribution and affords consumers a fair share of the resulting benefit, provided that the essentials of competition are not eliminated.

## Article 9.2.4 — Abuse of Dominance

1. The abuse by one or more undertakings of a dominant position in a market shall be prohibited.
2. Abuse shall include, in particular, exploitative pricing, exclusionary conduct toward competitors and suppliers, and discrimination between trading parties without objective justification.

## Article 9.2.5 — Concentrations and Public Undertakings

1. A concentration between undertakings that would significantly impede effective competition may be prohibited or approved subject to conditions by the competition authority, under a transparent and evidence-based procedure.
2. Public undertakings and enterprises granted special or exclusive rights shall be subject to the same competition rules as private undertakings, without prejudice to Article 9.3.2 (Legal Monopolies).

## Design Note

*The architecture follows the two-pillar prohibition of anticompetitive agreements and abuse of dominance consolidated in European Union competition law, with merger review added as a third safeguard against structural foreclosure. An independent authority, with fixed-term members and published decisions, guards enforcement against capture by incumbent firms and political interference. Objective-justification and exemption clauses preserve space for efficiency defenses. The accepted trade-off is that bright-line prohibitions may occasionally condemn efficiency-enhancing conduct.*
