# Section 9.1 — Economic Freedom

> **Part 09 — The Economy** · Articles 9.1.1 – 9.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section guarantees the economic liberty of every person — freedom of contract, occupation, and enterprise — as the foundation of the social-market economy of the Republic, subject only to limits of general application imposed by law.*

## Article 9.1.1 — Economic Liberty

1. Every person shall be free to engage in economic activity, to choose and practice an occupation, and to found, acquire, and operate an enterprise alone or with others.
2. These freedoms bind every organ of the State and every person exercising public authority.
3. They may be limited only by a law of general application, never by individual discretionary measures.

## Article 9.1.2 — Occupation and Enterprise

1. Access to an occupation may be made subject to licensing or qualification requirements only where the law pursues a legitimate aim and the requirements are proportionate and applied without discrimination.
2. Qualifications obtained in other jurisdictions shall be recognized to the extent prescribed by law.
3. No enterprise may be transferred from its owner against the will of the owner except under the expropriation guarantees of Article 1.8.2 (Expropriation).

## Article 9.1.3 — Freedom of Contract

1. Parties shall be free to determine the content and terms of their contracts.
2. The law may restrict freedom of contract to protect consumers, employees, tenants, and other structurally weaker parties, and to safeguard overriding public interests.
3. Standard terms offered for general use shall be subject to statutory content control, and terms creating excessive imbalance to the detriment of the weaker party may be declared void.

## Article 9.1.4 — The Social-Market Economy

1. The economic order of the Republic is a social market economy, in which free initiative and competition are reconciled with solidarity and the protection of workers and the environment.
2. Private ownership of the means of production is the general rule; the State may own or operate an enterprise only under a law serving a public purpose.
3. Public and private enterprises shall compete on equal terms, and no enterprise shall enjoy preferential treatment or exemption from generally applicable law by reason of public ownership.

## Article 9.1.5 — Limits and Judicial Review

1. Economic activity may be regulated by law for the protection of public health and safety, consumers, employees, the environment, competition, and the stability of the financial system.
2. Every restriction of economic freedom shall be general, proportionate, and non-retroactive, and shall remain open to effective judicial review.
3. Where a restriction amounts to expropriation, just and prompt compensation is owed under Article 1.8.2 (Expropriation).

## Design Note

*These articles adopt the ordoliberal insight that the State should shape a competitive order rather than direct production from above. The German Basic Law guarantees of occupational freedom and property, and the business-freedom clause of the European Charter of Fundamental Rights, supply the comparative models. The trade-off is proportionality review of economic regulation, protecting liberty at some cost in regulatory uncertainty.*
