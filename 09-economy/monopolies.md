# Section 9.3 — Monopolies

> **Part 09 — The Economy** · Articles 9.3.1 – 9.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section governs the exceptional cases in which a single supplier may be tolerated, imposing authorization, conduct, and sunset duties on legal and natural monopolies alike.*

## Article 9.3.1 — Monopolies Exceptional

1. No person may acquire or maintain a monopoly position otherwise than through superior performance in competition.
2. A law may confer a monopoly only where a public interest cannot adequately be secured through competition, and only for a scope and duration no broader than that interest requires.
3. Every monopoly conferred by law shall be subject to the duties of this section without further enactment.

## Article 9.3.2 — Legal Monopolies

1. A monopoly conferred by law shall carry obligations of universal service, equal treatment, price transparency, and open accounting, fixed by the authorizing law and supervised by the competent regulator.
2. The continuation of every legal monopoly shall be reviewed at least every five years against its stated justification.
3. A monopoly shall not be extended, by practice or by interpretation, to adjacent markets beyond its statutory scope.

## Article 9.3.3 — Universal Service

1. Where a service of general economic interest would not be supplied on commercially viable terms throughout the territory of the Republic, Parliament shall secure its provision through a regulated universal-service obligation.
2. Such an obligation shall be imposed by law, shall be precisely defined, and shall be compensated transparently where it exceeds what a normal commercial operator would bear.

## Article 9.3.4 — Essential Facilities

1. An undertaking controlling infrastructure that cannot feasibly be duplicated and that is indispensable to competing suppliers shall grant access on fair, reasonable, and non-discriminatory terms.
2. Terms of access shall be published or filed with the competent regulator, and disputes over access shall be decided within a time limit fixed by law.
3. Where the technical structure of a network makes duplication uneconomic, Parliament shall separate the operation of the network from competitive supply, as provided for energy networks under Article 23.2.2 (Independent Regulation).

## Article 9.3.5 — Remedies and Review

1. Where the justification for a monopoly ceases to exist, the monopoly shall be terminated and competitive conditions restored without avoidable delay.
2. The competition authority may order structural remedies, including divestiture, where conduct or regulation cannot restore effective competition by lesser means.
3. Every decision under this section shall be reasoned, published, and open to full judicial review.

## Design Note

*This section codifies the essential-facilities doctrine developed in American and European Union case law, together with the practice of time-limited concessions for public utilities found in continental administrative law. The five-year review and the duty to restore competition convert a monopoly from a permanent privilege into a conditional trust. Universal service is honored through transparent, precisely defined obligations rather than informal cross-subsidies. The accepted trade-off is that regulated monopolies invite capture and underinvestment, risks checked here by sunset review, structural remedies, and judicial oversight.*
