# Section 9.5 — Small Business

> **Part 09 — The Economy** · Articles 9.5.1 – 9.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section commits the Republic to a regulatory environment in which small and medium-sized enterprises can form, operate, and grow, without weakening the protection owed to workers, consumers, and the environment.*

## Article 9.5.1 — Proportionate Regulation

1. Parliament shall define the categories of small and medium-sized enterprises by objective criteria and shall review the definition periodically.
2. Legislative and regulatory proposals shall be assessed for their impact on small enterprises before adoption, and administrative burdens shall be scaled to the size and risk of the enterprise.
3. Simplified procedures may be provided for the smallest enterprises, provided that the level of protection owed to workers, consumers, and the environment is not reduced.

## Article 9.5.2 — One-Stop Administration

1. The State shall maintain a single point of contact through which enterprises may complete registrations, declarations, and reporting obligations owed to public authorities.
2. Information required once by one public authority shall be made available to other public authorities without fresh demands, in accordance with data protection law.

## Article 9.5.3 — Prompt Payment

1. Payment terms in commercial transactions shall be fair, and late payment shall carry interest and recovery costs fixed by law.
2. The State and its organs shall pay their commercial invoices within the period fixed by law, and failure to do so shall attract the same interest and recovery costs as private late payment.

## Article 9.5.4 — Promotion Programs

1. The State may support access by small enterprises to credit, guarantees, training, advice, and markets through transparent, temporary, and non-discriminatory programs.
2. Every support program shall state its objectives and shall be evaluated against them before its renewal.
3. Support shall not amount to state aid that distorts competition within the meaning of Article 9.2.1 (Protection of Competition).

## Article 9.5.5 — Equal Treatment in Markets

1. Public procurement shall not impose requirements exceeding the subject matter of the contract and shall not be structured to favor large incumbent suppliers, consistent with Article 8.5.1 (Principles).
2. Self-employed persons and economically dependent contractors shall enjoy the protections extended to them by law on equal terms with comparable enterprises.
3. Nothing in this section shall confer on any enterprise an exemption from generally applicable law.

## Design Note

*The section translates the think-small-first principle into constitutional form: impact assessment before adoption, burdens scaled to risk, and a single administrative entry point. Prompt-payment rules borrow from late-payment statutes that treat the State as the most consequential debtor of small suppliers. Support programs are framed as temporary and evaluable so that promotion does not harden into protectionism. The accepted trade-off is differentiated regulatory burdens, which add administrative complexity and invite boundary disputes over enterprise size.*
