# Section 9.6 — Banking

> **Part 09 — The Economy** · Articles 9.6.1 – 9.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section regulates the business of banking through licensing, prudential supervision, deposit protection, and resolution, with supervision independent of the Government and the Central Bank's own mandate remaining governed by Section 8.4.*

## Article 9.6.1 — Licensing of Banks

1. No person may conduct banking business, meaning the taking of deposits or similar repayable funds from the public, without a license issued under law.
2. Licenses shall be granted to applicants satisfying solvency, fitness, and governance requirements, and may be withdrawn only for cause after a fair procedure.

## Article 9.6.2 — Prudential Supervision

1. Banks shall maintain capital, liquidity, and large-exposure limits fixed by law, calibrated to internationally recognized prudential standards.
2. An independent supervisory authority shall supervise banks continuously, examine them on site, and require the information necessary to assess their soundness.
3. Banking groups shall be supervised on a consolidated basis, and information exchanged between supervisors shall remain confidential.

## Article 9.6.3 — Independence of Supervision

1. Neither the Government nor any other organ of the State may instruct the supervisory authority in the exercise of its powers over individual institutions.
2. The supervisory authority shall cooperate with the Central Bank in matters of financial stability without prejudice to the institutional mandate of the Central Bank under Article 8.4.1 (Status and Mandate).

## Article 9.6.4 — Deposit Protection

1. A deposit insurance scheme shall protect depositors up to a statutory limit and shall be financed by contributions of credit institutions rather than by the general budget.
2. The scheme shall pay out promptly upon the unavailability of deposits, and the statutory limit shall be reviewed periodically.

## Article 9.6.5 — Resolution of Failing Banks

1. A failing bank shall be resolved under a special statutory regime that preserves critical functions, allocates losses to shareholders and creditors in the order of priority fixed by law, and protects insured depositors.
2. Resolution costs shall not be borne by public funds except as a last resort, after the resources of the bank are exhausted, and only subject to repayment obligations.
3. Emergency liquidity support shall remain subject to Article 8.4.4 (Instruments and Financial Stability).

## Design Note

*The capital, liquidity, and consolidated-supervision clauses track the Basel framework, principally Basel III, while the resolution and bail-in rules draw on the European Banking Union's Single Resolution Mechanism. Deposit insurance follows the post-crisis consensus on meaningful coverage and rapid payout, financed by the industry rather than the taxpayer. Supervision is deliberately separated from governmental instruction to resist forbearance toward politically connected banks. The accepted trade-off is that higher prudential standards constrain credit supply, accepted here in exchange for reduced systemic risk.*
