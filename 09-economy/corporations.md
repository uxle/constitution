# Section 9.4 — Corporations

> **Part 09 — The Economy** · Articles 9.4.1 – 9.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section provides the constitutional basis for incorporation, limited liability, the duties of those who direct corporations, and the transparency that the corporate form owes to the public.*

## Article 9.4.1 — Incorporation by Law

1. Every person shall be free to establish, join, and manage corporations, cooperatives, and other forms of enterprise under general laws of equal application.
2. Incorporation shall not require case-by-case permission of the State, and no corporate form shall be reserved to any class of persons.
3. Cooperatives and mutual societies shall be recognized by law and may not be dissolved or deprived of their assets except by law and for cause.

## Article 9.4.2 — Limited Liability

1. A corporation validly formed shall possess legal personality distinct from its members, and the liability of members shall be limited to their contribution.
2. The corporate form may not be used to defeat obligations imposed by law, to commit fraud, or to evade responsibility for wrongs; the law may disregard it in such cases.

## Article 9.4.3 — Directors' Duties

1. Directors shall manage the corporation with due care and loyalty to its lawful purpose and shall account for their management to the members.
2. Directors shall not accept benefits from third parties in connection with their office and shall disclose conflicts of interest to the supervisory organ.
3. The law may require undertakings above a size fixed by law to provide employees with information, consultation, and participation rights consistent with Article 1.9.2 (Just conditions of work).

## Article 9.4.4 — Transparency of Ownership

1. Corporations shall keep accounts according to uniform standards, shall have them audited where prescribed by law, and shall file them in a public register.
2. The natural persons who ultimately own or control a corporation shall be recorded in a register of beneficial owners accessible to the competent authorities, and to the public to the extent provided by law.

## Article 9.4.5 — Dissolution and Insolvency

1. Corporations shall be supervised by the competent registries and the courts, which may order dissolution for persistent abuse of the corporate form.
2. Dissolution shall not extinguish obligations owed to creditors, employees, or the public treasury.
3. The insolvency of a corporation shall be governed by Article 9.8.1 (Access to Insolvency Proceedings) and the articles that follow it.

## Design Note

*The incorporation model chosen here is the registration system, which treats company formation as a right subject to general law, in preference to concession systems that leave formation to administrative grace. Beneficial-ownership registers follow the transparency standards promoted by the Financial Action Task Force against money laundering. Directors' duties are stated as care and loyalty, with employee participation left to statutory calibration. The accepted trade-off is that limited liability externalizes part of business risk onto creditors and society, a cost offset here by transparency, audit, and abuse doctrines.*
