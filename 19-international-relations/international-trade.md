# Section 19.5 — International Trade

> **Part 19 — International Relations** · Articles 19.5.1 – 19.5.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section subjects trade policy to parliamentary law, requires sustainability and labor chapters in trade agreements, and preserves domestic remedies alongside the Republic's trade obligations.*

## Article 19.5.1 — Trade Policy by Law

1. Trade policy, including tariffs and measures affecting external trade, shall be based on an Act of the Parliament, consistent with Article 9.10.2.
2. Trade policy shall serve prosperity while upholding the labor, environmental, and consumer standards of the Republic.
3. Trade measures shall not discriminate among persons or goods except as the law of the Republic and its international obligations provide.

## Article 19.5.2 — Sustainability and Labor Chapters

1. Trade agreements of the Republic shall include chapters on environmental protection and labor rights whose commitments are no lower than the corresponding standards of the law of the Republic.
2. No trade agreement shall require the Republic to reduce protections below the level of its law at the time of signature.
3. The executive shall assess the social and environmental impact of a draft agreement and lay the assessment before the Parliament before approval.

## Article 19.5.3 — Parliamentary Oversight of Negotiations

1. The executive shall present a negotiating mandate for a significant trade agreement to the Parliament before negotiations begin.
2. The Parliament shall receive progress reports and the text of a draft agreement sufficiently before signature and approval to permit informed deliberation.

## Article 19.5.4 — Remedies and Trade Defense

1. Domestic remedies, including judicial review of acts implementing trade agreements, remain available to every person, consistent with Article 4.6.5.
2. Trade defense measures may be adopted under law and only in conformity with the obligations of the Republic, consistent with Article 9.10.5.

## Design Note

*Trade agreements function as law for exporters, workers, and consumers, which is why this Section routes tariffs and mandates through Parliament and requires texts to arrive early enough for real scrutiny. Sustainability and labor chapters respond to the criticism that trade liberalization can outrun social protection; the no-regression clause is a floor, not a ceiling. Preserving domestic remedies ensures that international economic commitments never become a shield against review of the acts that implement them.*
