# Section 19.4 — Diplomatic Relations

> **Part 19 — International Relations** · Articles 19.4.1 – 19.4.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs the Republic's diplomatic and consular relations, the protection of citizens abroad, and the status of international organizations to which the Republic belongs.*

## Article 19.4.1 — Missions and Privileges

1. The Republic shall establish and maintain diplomatic missions and consular posts in the manner provided by law.
2. The privileges and immunities of missions, of their personnel, and of international organizations recognized by the Republic apply as provided by treaty and by law, subject to reciprocity.
3. Missions shall be staffed and resourced so as to provide the protection and services required by Articles 19.4.2 and 19.4.3.

## Article 19.4.2 — Protection of Citizens Abroad

1. Every citizen has the right to the protection and assistance of the missions of the Republic while abroad.
2. A citizen detained abroad shall be informed without delay of the right to have the detention notified to a consular officer of the Republic and to communicate with that officer.
3. The Republic shall maintain the consular capacity necessary to make this protection effective.

## Article 19.4.3 — Consular Services

1. Missions shall provide consular services, including emergency assistance, documentation, and notification of next of kin.
2. Emergency consular assistance shall be accessible to citizens regardless of means; fees, where charged, shall be reasonable and uniform.

## Article 19.4.4 — International Organizations

1. The Republic may join international organizations only by treaty approved under Article 19.2.2.
2. Organizations to which the Republic belongs shall act within their mandates; the constitutional functions of the organs of the Republic are unaffected by membership.
3. Members of staff of the Republic seconded to international organizations retain their rights as citizens and servants of the Republic as provided by law.

## Design Note

*Diplomatic law is largely codified by treaty, so this Section fixes constitutional anchors rather than restating rules: reciprocity, statutory regulation of privileges, and a personal right of citizens to consular protection, which some constitutions treat as a genuine individual right. The detention-notification rule reflects the practical reality that detained citizens abroad are powerless without prompt contact. The membership clause preserves constitutional functions against erosion by institutional loyalty to international bodies.*
