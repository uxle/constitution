# Section 19.2 — Treaties

> **Part 19 — International Relations** · Articles 19.2.1 – 19.2.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section allocates treaty powers between the executive and the Parliament, conditions domestic application on publication, and involves the Parliament in withdrawal.*

## Article 19.2.1 — Negotiation and Signature

1. The executive negotiates and signs treaties on behalf of the Republic.
2. Before signature of a treaty within a category stated in Article 19.2.2, the executive shall inform the foreign affairs committee of the Parliament of the text and of its intended effect.

## Article 19.2.2 — Parliamentary Approval

1. Treaties that regulate the political relations of the Republic, relate to matters of legislation, impose significant financial obligations, affect the territory of the Republic, concern human rights, or transfer sovereign powers require the approval of the Parliament expressed in an Act before ratification.
2. Approval may be given subject to reservations, declarations, or conditions; ratification may proceed only in conformity with any condition of approval.
3. Agreements excluded from this Article shall not circumvent it by fragmenting a significant commitment into smaller instruments.

## Article 19.2.3 — Publication as Condition of Application

1. A treaty binds the Republic internationally upon ratification in accordance with international law.
2. A treaty shall be applied domestically only after its publication in the official gazette.
3. No treaty of the Republic shall be secret; negotiated texts shall be made public, save for narrowly confined and time-limited annexes approved by the Parliament.

## Article 19.2.4 — Withdrawal and Denunciation

1. Withdrawal from, or denunciation of, a treaty within a category stated in Article 19.2.2 requires the approval of the Parliament.
2. Where urgent necessity makes immediate withdrawal unavoidable, the executive shall report to the Parliament without delay and shall fulfill the steps required by international law.
3. Domestic provisions implementing a treaty remain effective only so long as the Republic remains bound and shall be amended conformingly after withdrawal.

## Design Note

*Treaty practice worldwide divides between systems requiring approval for broad categories and systems reserving it for select subjects; this Section takes the categorical approach because treaties quietly remake domestic law. The publication rule turns the maxim that secret treaties are enemies of democracy into a condition of enforceability, echoing post-war constitutional settlements. Involving the Parliament in withdrawal closes the loophole by which a state could achieve by exit what it could not achieve by entry.*
