# Section 2.1 — Citizenship

> **Part 02 — Citizenship and Migration** · Articles 2.1.1 – 2.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes citizenship of the Republic, its acquisition at birth and by descent, its equal standing, and the civic rights and duties it carries.*

## Article 2.1.1 — Citizenship of the Republic

1. There is a single citizenship of the Republic, held on equal terms by all citizens.
2. Citizenship is the gateway to the political rights of this Constitution; it is acquired, held, and lost only as this Section and Sections 2.6 and 2.7 provide.

## Article 2.1.2 — Citizenship by Birth

1. Every child born in the territory of the Republic is a citizen by birth.
2. A child born in the territory to parents serving a foreign state in an official capacity is not a citizen by birth, unless the child would otherwise be stateless.
3. A newborn found in the territory whose parents are unknown is presumed, until the contrary is shown, to have been born in the territory.

## Article 2.1.3 — Citizenship by Descent

1. A child born outside the territory to a citizen parent is a citizen by descent, subject to registration as provided by law.
2. The law shall keep registration accessible and shall not create a risk of statelessness.

## Article 2.1.4 — Prevention of Statelessness

1. No measure of law or administration shall render a person stateless.
2. Where a person would otherwise be stateless, the State shall grant citizenship in the manner provided by law, subject only to safeguards against fraud consistent with Article 2.7.2 (Revocation for Fraud).

## Article 2.1.5 — Civic Rights and Duties

1. Citizens have the right to vote and to stand for public office as provided by this Constitution and by law.
2. Citizens shall uphold this Constitution, comply with the law, and pay the taxes lawfully imposed.
3. The duties of citizenship shall not diminish any right this Constitution guarantees.

## Design Note

*The birth rule is deliberately generous — every child born in the territory is a citizen — with a narrow diplomat exception and a foundling presumption, a combination that makes statelessness by birth practically impossible within the Republic. Equality of citizenship forecloses graduated or tiered civic status, which history shows to be corrosive of both equality and loyalty. The pairing of civic rights with civic duties in one Article signals that membership is reciprocal without converting duties into conditions of rights.*
