# Section 2.5 — Refugees

> **Part 02 — Citizenship and Migration** · Articles 2.5.1 – 2.5.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the protection regime for recognized refugees: equal status, rights to work and education, durable solutions, and integration support.*

## Article 2.5.1 — Status of Refugees

1. A person recognized as a refugee shall be granted protection in the Republic and shall enjoy every right of this Constitution and the additional rights of this Section.
2. Refugees shall not be discriminated against on any ground, and their status shall be documented without charge.
3. Identity and travel documents shall be issued to refugees as provided by law.

## Article 2.5.2 — Rights to Work and Education

1. Recognized refugees have the right to work and to engage in self-employment on the same terms as lawfully resident non-citizens under Article 2.3.4 (Rights of Lawfully Resident Non-Citizens).
2. The children of refugees have the right to education on the same terms as the children of citizens, including basic education free of charge under Article 1.10.2 (Free and Compulsory Basic Education).
3. Refugees have access to healthcare on the terms applicable to lawfully resident non-citizens.

## Article 2.5.3 — Durable Solutions

1. The State shall pursue durable solutions for refugees, including voluntary return in safety and dignity, local integration, and, in cooperation with international organizations, resettlement of refugees from abroad.
2. Return shall occur only where it is genuinely voluntary; no refugee shall be pressured to return.

## Article 2.5.4 — Integration Support

1. The State shall provide language instruction, employment services, and civic orientation to enable refugees to participate in society.
2. Support shall be structured toward self-reliance and shall not be administered so as to produce prolonged dependence or marginalization.

## Design Note

*The Section treats refugee protection as a regime with an end: durable solutions — voluntary return, local integration, or resettlement — are constitutionalized so that camps and temporary status cannot become a warehouse without exit. Rights to work and education are the operational core, since exclusion from both is the chief cause of prolonged dependency. The framing follows the international refugee regime while leaving quotas, procedures, and program design to ordinary law.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination); Article 2.3.4 (Rights of Lawfully Resident Non-Citizens); Article 1.10.2 (Free and Compulsory Basic Education).
