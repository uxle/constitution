# Section 2.6 — Naturalization

> **Part 02 — Citizenship and Migration** · Articles 2.6.1 – 2.6.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section governs residence-based naturalization: reasonable integration requirements, non-discrimination, and reasoned decisions subject to review.*

## Article 2.6.1 — Right to Apply for Naturalization

1. A non-citizen who has lawfully and habitually resided in the Republic for the period provided by law may apply for citizenship by naturalization.
2. The residence period shall be reasonable, shall be the same for all applicants, and may be reduced only on fair and published conditions.
3. No application shall be refused on a ground not provided by law.

## Article 2.6.2 — Integration Requirements

1. The law may require knowledge of a national language and of the basic constitutional order, tested without discrimination.
2. Requirements shall be reasonable and proportionate: the standard shall be attainable by a person of ordinary diligence, shall accommodate age and disability, and shall not operate to exclude applicants on grounds prohibited by Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination).

## Article 2.6.3 — Non-Discrimination in Naturalization

1. Naturalization shall be decided on uniform criteria for all applicants, without regard to national or ethnic origin, religion, or any ground listed in Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination).
2. Fees shall be moderate and shall not operate as a barrier to applicants of modest means.

## Article 2.6.4 — Decision and Review

1. An application shall be decided within a reasonable time by a reasoned decision.
2. An unsuccessful applicant may seek review of the decision before a court.
3. A successful applicant shall take a solemn undertaking to respect the constitutional order; the undertaking imposes no duty beyond those this Constitution already establishes.

## Design Note

*The Section constitutionalizes the process rather than the threshold, fixing uniform, proportionate, and reviewable standards while leaving the residence period to legislation, a division that has allowed comparative systems to adjust integration policy without reopening the constitution. The attainability standard for civic-knowledge tests responds to the documented risk that such tests become covert filters. The solemn undertaking is drafted as a reaffirmation of existing duties, not as a source of new obligations.*
