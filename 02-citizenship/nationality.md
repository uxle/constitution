# Section 2.2 — Nationality

> **Part 02 — Citizenship and Migration** · Articles 2.2.1 – 2.2.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the single nationality of the Republic, the registration of births, proof of status, and the independence of nationality from marriage.*

## Article 2.2.1 — Single Nationality

1. The nationality of the Republic is single and uniform throughout the territory.
2. No Province, Region, or Municipality may confer, condition, or deny nationality or any element of civic status.
3. The State shall maintain a reliable register of nationality and citizenship, accessible for correction as provided by law.

## Article 2.2.2 — Registration of Births

1. Every birth shall be registered promptly, without charge, and in the manner provided by law; the duty to register rests on the State as much as on the parents.
2. The register may record only the data required to establish status and identity, and its treatment of data is subject to Article 1.5.4 (Personal Data and Informational Self-Determination).
3. Where a birth is registered late, a procedure shall confirm the facts without penalizing the child.

## Article 2.2.3 — Proof of Status

1. Every citizen shall be issued, on request and without charge, a document evidencing citizenship, and an application shall be decided within a reasonable time.
2. Doubt about a person's status shall be resolved by the interpretation most favorable to citizenship and least likely to cause statelessness.

## Article 2.2.4 — Nationality and Marriage

1. Neither marriage nor its dissolution shall affect the nationality of a spouse.
2. The law may shorten the residence period required for the naturalization of a spouse on fair and published conditions, without distinction between women and men and without regard to the spouse's origin.

## Design Note

*Uniform nationality prevents the fragmentation of civic status along regional lines and reinforces the unitary design of Part 00 (Foundation). Free and prompt birth registration is treated as a constitutional duty because unregistered births are the principal gateway to de facto statelessness, and the late-registration rule shifts its burdens away from the child. The marriage-neutrality rule reflects the international principle that women and men hold nationality independently of marital status. Proof of status complements registration by ensuring that citizenship can always be exercised, not merely held.*
