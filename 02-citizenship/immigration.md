# Section 2.3 — Immigration

> **Part 02 — Citizenship and Migration** · Articles 2.3.1 – 2.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section allocates competence over entry and residence, guarantees lawful migration channels and family unity, and defines the rights of lawfully resident non-citizens and the due process of removal.*

## Article 2.3.1 — Legislative Competence

1. The entry, residence, and removal of non-citizens are governed by law adopted by the Parliament.
2. The executive shall apply that law; administrative discretion shall be structured by published criteria and shall remain subject to review.

## Article 2.3.2 — Lawful Migration Channels

1. The law shall provide clear, accessible, and non-discriminatory channels for lawful entry and residence, including for work, study, family, and protection.
2. A non-citizen admitted under a lawful channel is entitled to the rights attached to that status and to every right of this Constitution that is not reserved to citizens.

## Article 2.3.3 — Family Unity

1. The family is entitled to protection, and measures affecting entry or residence shall be interpreted and applied so as to preserve family unity.
2. A resident may apply for the admission or continued residence of close family members; a refusal is subject to the test of Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights) in light of the family's rights.

## Article 2.3.4 — Rights of Lawfully Resident Non-Citizens

1. Non-citizens lawfully resident enjoy treatment equal to that of citizens in work, social security, housing, education, and healthcare, except where this Constitution reserves a matter to citizens.
2. A distinction between citizens and lawfully resident non-citizens is permissible only for political rights and such public functions as this Constitution or a law reserves to citizens; every other distinction requires justification under Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights).

## Article 2.3.5 — Due Process in Removal

1. No non-citizen shall be removed except by a reasoned decision under law, communicated in a language the person understands, and subject to review.
2. Removal shall not take place while review is pending where the person invokes Article 2.4.2 (Non-Refoulement).
3. Detention pending removal shall be judicially supervised, shall last no longer than necessary, and shall not be punitive.

## Design Note

*The Section allocates migration control to ordinary legislation while embedding due-process and equality constraints in the Constitution, so that a politically volatile field remains governed by law rather than by ad hoc executive practice. The equality clause draws a principled line — political rights for citizens, civil and social rights for everyone lawfully present — which mirrors the comparative consensus on resident non-citizens. The suspensive effect of a refoulement claim protects the absolute character of Article 2.4.2 without freezing the migration system.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.1.4 (Limitation of Rights); Article 1.2.2 (Prohibition of Discrimination); Article 2.4.2 (Non-Refoulement).
