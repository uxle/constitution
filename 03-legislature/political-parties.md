# Section 3.6 — Political Parties

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.6.1 – 3.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section guarantees the free formation and democratic organization of political parties and permits their prohibition only on narrow grounds.*

## Article 3.6.1 — Freedom of Formation

1. Citizens may form political parties freely.
2. Parties shall not require prior authorization. Registration with the Electoral Commission shall serve only the administration of elections.
3. Parties shall participate in the formation of the political will of the people.

## Article 3.6.2 — Internal Democracy

1. The statutes of a party shall provide for the periodic election of its governing bodies and for the participation of its members in its decisions.
2. Candidate selection shall follow transparent and democratic procedures, consistent with open-list elections under Article 3.2.2 (Electoral Principle).
3. The statutes of a party shall be public.

## Article 3.6.3 — Transparency of Financing

1. Parties shall keep proper accounts of their income and expenditure.
2. Parties shall publish audited accounts annually and shall disclose donations in accordance with Article 3.7.1 (Disclosure of Donations).

## Article 3.6.4 — Prohibition

1. A party may be prohibited only where its statutes or its conduct aim to abolish the democratic order by violence, or where it systematically incites violence against persons or groups.
2. Prohibition shall be pronounced only by the Constitutional Court.
3. Measures short of prohibition shall be preferred where they can avert the threat effectively.

## Article 3.6.5 — Parliamentary Groups

1. Members may associate in parliamentary groups under the rules of each House.
2. The rules shall secure the rights of small groups and of members who belong to no group.
3. Public funding of parliamentary groups shall follow the rules of Article 3.7.4 (Public Funding).

## Design Note

*The narrow exception for violent anti-democratic parties, known from the constitutional practice of Germany, is confined to violence, requires a judicial pronouncement, and is softened by a preference for lesser measures, so that it cannot become a tool against lawful dissent. Regulating parties' internal democracy is justified by their constitutional role in forming the political will of the people, a role no ordinary private association shares.*
