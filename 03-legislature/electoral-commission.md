# Section 3.5 — The Electoral Commission

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.5.1 – 3.5.4 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section states the constitutional status, independence, and exclusive electoral role of the Electoral Commission within the parliamentary setting.*

## Article 3.5.1 — Constitutional Status

1. The Electoral Commission shall be an independent constitutional institution.
2. The Commission shall not form part of the Government, the Parliament, or any other organ of the State.
3. The Commission shall perform its functions in the public interest and with impartiality.
4. The Commission shall be self-governing in its administrative affairs under this Constitution and the law.

## Article 3.5.2 — Independence

1. The Commission shall exercise its functions independently and shall not be subject to direction in individual matters.
2. Its members shall serve fixed terms and may be removed only for cause in the manner provided for the independent institutions under Part 20.
3. The Parliament shall appropriate resources sufficient for the independent performance of the Commission's functions.

## Article 3.5.3 — Exclusive Administration

1. The Commission shall have exclusive competence to organize, conduct, and supervise elections to the Lower House and the Upper House.
2. The Commission shall maintain the integrity of the electoral process for parliamentary elections, including the registration of voters and of candidates.
3. No other organ may interfere with the conduct of an election while it is underway.

## Article 3.5.4 — Accountability and Cooperation

1. The Commission shall report annually to the Parliament on the administration of parliamentary elections.
2. All organs of the State shall provide the Commission with the information and assistance it requires for the discharge of its functions.
3. Decisions of the Commission shall state reasons and shall be subject to appeal under Part 06.

## Design Note

*Placing the Commission outside the three classic branches prevents the incumbent Government from refereeing the very contest that could replace it. This Section states only the Commission's status and its role in parliamentary elections; composition, tenure, and general accountability are fixed uniformly with the other independent institutions in Part 20, so that one coherent constitutional design governs all of them.*
