# Section 3.8 — The Legislative Process

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.8.1 – 3.8.6 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section orders the path of a bill from introduction through committee scrutiny and public consultation to passage, presidential assent, and publication.*

## Article 3.8.1 — Introduction of Bills

1. Bills may be introduced by the Government, by a member of either House, or by a committee of either House.
2. A citizens' initiative may lead to the introduction of a bill under the conditions of Part 06.

## Article 3.8.2 — Committee Scrutiny

1. Every bill shall be referred to a committee of the House of introduction before its second reading.
2. Committees may hold public hearings, invite evidence, and assess the financial, social, and environmental impact of a bill.
3. A committee shall report to the House with a recommendation or with amendments.

## Article 3.8.3 — Readings and Public Consultation

1. Each bill shall pass through three readings in each House.
2. Drafts of Government bills shall be published for public consultation for a period fixed by law before the second reading.
3. A House may shorten a stage only for urgency, recording the reasons in the minutes.

## Article 3.8.4 — Joint Passage

1. A bill shall be passed by both Houses in the same text.
2. Where the Upper House delays an ordinary bill or a money bill, the procedures of Articles 3.3.4 (Powers over Ordinary Bills) and 3.3.5 (Powers over Money Bills) shall apply.

## Article 3.8.5 — Assent and Referral

1. A passed bill shall be presented to the President for assent.
2. The President shall assent within thirty days or shall refer the bill back to the Parliament once, with written reasons.
3. If the Parliament re-adopts the bill in the same text, the President shall assent within fifteen days.
4. A referral under this Article may be exercised once in respect of each bill.

## Article 3.8.6 — Publication and Entry into Force

1. An Act shall be promulgated and published in the official gazette without delay.
2. No Act shall enter into force before its publication.
3. An Act may postpone its entry into force to a later day fixed in the Act itself.

## Design Note

*Committee-centered scrutiny with published consultation inserts expertise and public voice before votes are disciplined, while the urgency exception is deliberately narrow and must be minuted. The single presidential referral is a pause, not a veto: the Parliament overcomes it by simple re-adoption, a design close to the deferential reference powers found in some parliamentary constitutions, such as that of Ireland. Publication as a condition of entry into force completes the principle of legality on the procedural side.*
