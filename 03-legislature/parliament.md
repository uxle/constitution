# Section 3.1 — The Parliament

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.1.1 – 3.1.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Parliament of the Republic as the supreme legislative authority and orders its composition, sessions, decisions, and presiding officers.*

## Article 3.1.1 — Legislative Power

1. The legislative power of the Republic shall be vested in the Parliament.
2. The Parliament shall consist of two Houses, the Lower House and the Upper House.
3. The Parliament shall be the supreme legislative authority of the Republic, subject only to this Constitution.
4. No organ of the State may exercise legislative power except as conferred by this Constitution or delegated by an Act of Parliament.

## Article 3.1.2 — Composition

1. The Lower House shall comprise 400 members elected directly by the people.
2. The Upper House shall comprise three members for each Province, elected by the Provincial Councils.
3. Members of each House shall represent the whole people of the Republic.
4. Members shall not be bound by instructions and shall be guided by their conscience and by this Constitution.

## Article 3.1.3 — Sessions and Calendars

1. Each House shall assemble without convocation on the day fixed by law and shall sit for such periods as its business requires.
2. Each House shall adopt an annual legislative calendar and shall make it public.
3. Sittings shall be public. A House may sit in closed session only for reasons of public security or the protection of private life, under its rules and with the reasons recorded in the minutes.

## Article 3.1.4 — Quorum and Decisions

1. Each House may act only if a majority of its members is present.
2. Decisions shall be taken by a majority of the votes cast, unless this Constitution requires a different majority.
3. If the votes are equally divided, the proposal shall be rejected.
4. Voting shall be personal. The rules of each House shall provide for recorded votes at the request of a fraction of the members present.

## Article 3.1.5 — Presiding Officers

1. Each House shall elect a presiding officer and deputy presiding officers from among its members.
2. The presiding officer shall maintain order, apply the rules of the House, and represent the House in its external relations.
3. Joint sittings of both Houses shall be presided over by the presiding officer of the Lower House.

## Design Note

*Bicameralism is deliberately asymmetric: the Lower House is the confidence chamber from which the Government flows, while the Upper House gives the Provinces a delaying voice without an absolute veto. The equal-division rule withholds a casting vote from any presiding officer, preserving neutrality in the chamber where government is made and unmade. Public sittings with narrow, recorded exceptions follow the presumption of transparency that runs through the whole Constitution.*
