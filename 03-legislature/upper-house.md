# Section 3.3 — The Upper House

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.3.1 – 3.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section establishes the Upper House as the chamber of the Provinces, orders its staggered election, and confines its powers to the delay of legislation.*

## Article 3.3.1 — Composition

1. The Upper House shall consist of three members for each Province.
2. The number of Provinces shall be fixed by law within the principles of Part 07.
3. Members shall represent the interests of their Province and the people of the Republic.

## Article 3.3.2 — Election by Provincial Councils

1. The members for each Province shall be elected by its Provincial Council by secret ballot.
2. Provincial law shall govern the method of election, subject to the principles of free and equal election.
3. The Upper House shall not be dissolved.

## Article 3.3.3 — Staggered Terms and Vacancies

1. Members shall serve a term of six years.
2. One member of each Province's delegation shall be elected every second year, so that the terms are staggered.
3. A vacancy shall be filled for the remainder of the term by election under clause 2 of Article 3.3.2.

## Article 3.3.4 — Powers over Ordinary Bills

1. The Upper House may postpone its assent to an ordinary bill for up to ninety days by a resolution stating its objections.
2. If the Lower House re-adopts the bill after the expiry of the delay, the bill shall be deemed passed.
3. The Upper House shall have no power to defeat an ordinary bill.

## Article 3.3.5 — Powers over Money Bills

1. The Upper House may delay a money bill for up to twenty-one days.
2. A bill whose sole purpose is the imposition, alteration, or repeal of a tax, an appropriation, a loan, or the management of public debt shall be a money bill.
3. The presiding officer of the Lower House shall certify money bills, subject to a final determination by the Constitutional Court.

## Design Note

*Three members per Province give each territorial unit an equal voice inside a unitary state, and the two-year staggering of six-year terms provides continuity while preventing wholesale turnover. The ninety-day delay for ordinary bills, similar to the delaying power of the Irish second chamber, allows reconsideration without enabling obstruction. The shorter twenty-one-day window for money bills protects the financial primacy of the confidence chamber, where the budget's political authors sit.*
