# Section 3.10 — Member Integrity and Anti-Corruption

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.10.1 – 3.10.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section imposes duties of integrity on members of the Parliament — disclosure, recusal, and accountability — and links the Houses to the Anti-Corruption Commission.*

## Article 3.10.1 — Duty of Integrity

1. Members shall exercise their functions in the public interest.
2. A member shall not solicit or accept any gift, benefit, or promise offered in connection with the member's functions.
3. Each House shall adopt a code of conduct and shall enforce it.

## Article 3.10.2 — Asset Disclosure

1. A member shall declare the member's assets and liabilities upon taking office, annually, and upon leaving office.
2. Declarations shall be filed in a public registry maintained under the code of conduct.
3. A member shall also declare assets of the member's household where they may be affected by the member's functions.

## Article 3.10.3 — Recusal

1. A member who has a personal interest in a matter before the House shall disclose that interest to the House.
2. The member shall withdraw from the deliberation and the vote on that matter.
3. The disclosure and the withdrawal shall be recorded in the minutes.

## Article 3.10.4 — Sanctions and Expulsion

1. A House may impose graduated sanctions on a member for a serious breach of the code of conduct.
2. Expulsion requires a two-thirds majority of the members of the House.
3. Expulsion shall terminate the mandate and shall entail loss of eligibility to sit in that legislature.

## Article 3.10.5 — Cooperation with the Anti-Corruption Commission

1. The Houses shall refer suspected offences of corruption to the Anti-Corruption Commission and to the Public Prosecution Service.
2. Parliamentary privilege shall not bar the investigation or prosecution of corruption offences.
3. The Anti-Corruption Commission may report to the House concerned on integrity matters, within the limits of individual privacy and the presumption of innocence.

## Design Note

*Public asset disclosure converts abstract integrity into checkable fact, and recusal rules protect legislative decisions from private capture. The express statement that privilege never bars corruption prosecutions closes the most abused loophole in parliamentary immunity systems, a loophole likewise closed in the asset-declaration regimes of several Latin American constitutions. Expulsion is deliberately demanding — a two-thirds majority — so that it cannot be misused to purge political opponents for mere dissent.*

## Anti-Capture Amendment — Continuity of Investigation Across Party Affiliation

**Article 3.10.6 (No Closure by Reason of Affiliation).** An investigation or prosecution referred under Article 3.10.5 shall not be closed, stayed, reclassified, or deprioritized by reason of the member's change of political party or parliamentary bloc. A closure in breach of this Article is void and is automatically referred for judicial review by the Constitutional Court.

**Article 3.10.7 (Public Case-Status Register).** The Anti-Corruption Commission shall publish, quarterly, a register of every member and former member under active investigation or prosecution, showing the case's opening date, present status, and the member's party affiliation at each relevant date. Publication under this Article does not itself constitute a finding of guilt and is without prejudice to the presumption of innocence.

*Design Note (Amendment): The most common route around the integrity provisions of this Section is not repeal but neglect — a file that is never formally closed but is never advanced either, timed to a change of political alignment. Article 3.10.6 makes such neglect legally void rather than merely regrettable; Article 3.10.7 makes the pattern, if it exists, visible to the public rather than dependent on investigative journalism to surface it case by case.*
