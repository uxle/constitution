# Section 3.9 — Parliamentary Privileges

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.9.1 – 3.9.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section protects freedom of speech in parliamentary proceedings and grants limited immunity, while confining both privileges to the protection of the institution.*

## Article 3.9.1 — Freedom of Speech in Proceedings

1. A member shall not be held liable in any proceeding, court, or tribunal outside the House for a vote given, a statement made, or a report presented in the proceedings of the House.
2. The protection shall extend to the publication of the proceedings by order of the House.
3. The protection shall not extend to statements repeated or published outside the proceedings.

## Article 3.9.2 — Limited Immunity

1. A member may not be detained or prosecuted for an act performed in the exercise of the mandate, except when apprehended in the commission of a serious offence.
2. Immunity shall lapse when the mandate ends. It shall not bar investigation, indictment, or trial after the mandate has ended.

## Article 3.9.3 — Purpose and Limits

1. Privileges exist to protect the function of parliamentary representation, not the personal interests of members.
2. No privilege may be invoked to shield corruption, fraud, or other serious wrongdoing.
3. Privileges shall be construed narrowly.

## Article 3.9.4 — Waiver and Review

1. The House concerned may lift immunity at the reasoned request of the Public Prosecution Service, by a majority of its members.
2. A member may waive the member's own privilege.
3. A decision refusing to lift immunity may be reviewed by the Constitutional Court for abuse of power.

## Article 3.9.5 — Internal Order and Contempt

1. Each House shall control its own proceedings and may discipline conduct that obstructs them.
2. Sanctions for contempt shall be proportionate, reasoned, and recorded.
3. The rules of each House shall provide for review of disciplinary decisions by an internal body before recourse to the courts.

## Design Note

*The guarantee protects institutional boldness: a member must be able to speak the truth about the powerful without fear of suit, and the House must be able to publish what is said. Modern drafting, following the narrower immunities of recent parliamentary constitutions such as the German Basic Law, confines protection to the proceedings themselves and expressly excludes its use as a corruption shield. The final review of refusals to lift immunity keeps the privilege from becoming a partisan veto over prosecution.*
