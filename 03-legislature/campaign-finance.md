# Section 3.7 — Campaign Finance

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.7.1 – 3.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section secures the transparency and integrity of the financing of political parties and election campaigns, from private donations to audited public funding.*

## Article 3.7.1 — Disclosure of Donations

1. Parties and candidates shall keep records of every donation received.
2. They shall report the sources and amounts of donations to the Electoral Commission at the intervals and in the form fixed by law.
3. The Commission shall publish the reports, protecting the identity of small donors as provided by law.

## Article 3.7.2 — Limits on Contributions

1. The law shall fix the maximum amount that any person may donate to a party or candidate within a calendar year.
2. The law may fix limits on campaign expenditure.
3. Limits shall be proportionate to the protection of equal opportunity in elections and shall not effectively prevent political participation.

## Article 3.7.3 — Prohibited Donations

1. Anonymous donations above a de minimis amount fixed by law shall be prohibited and shall be surrendered to the State.
2. Donations from foreign States or from entities controlled by them shall be prohibited.
3. Donations from public bodies and from entities holding contracts with the State shall be regulated by law to prevent the conversion of public resources into party advantage.

## Article 3.7.4 — Public Funding

1. Public funding of parties may be provided by law.
2. Public funding shall be audited, shall follow a transparent formula, and shall not be allocated to reward the incumbent Government.
3. Eligibility for public funding shall be conditional on compliance with the disclosure duties of this Section.

## Article 3.7.5 — Oversight and Sanctions

1. The Electoral Commission shall oversee compliance with this Section.
2. Sanctions shall be graduated and proportionate, from warnings to the forfeiture of public funding and, for grave violations, referral to the Public Prosecution Service.
3. Sanctions shall be subject to judicial review.

## Design Note

*Disclosure is treated as the primary tool: sunlight on donations deters capture at a lower cost to political speech than sweeping spending bans. The Canadian model of disclosure combined with contribution limits illustrates this balance in settled practice. Public funding is made conditional on transparency so that State money cannot subsidize opacity, and enforcement is left to the same independent body that administers the elections themselves.*
