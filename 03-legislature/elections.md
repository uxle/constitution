# Section 3.4 — Elections to Parliament

> **Part 03 — The Legislature** · Articles 3.4.1 – 3.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This Section fixes the timing of parliamentary elections, the mandate and vacancy of members, and the administration and validity of elections to both Houses.*

## Article 3.4.1 — Timing of Elections

1. Ordinary elections to the Lower House shall be held every fourth year.
2. Elections to the Upper House shall be held every second year in accordance with Article 3.3.3 (Staggered Terms and Vacancies).
3. Election dates shall be fixed by law. Early elections shall be held only under Article 3.2.5 (Dissolution and Early Elections).

## Article 3.4.2 — Term and Mandate

1. The mandate of a member shall begin upon validation of the member's election and shall end upon the first sitting of a newly elected House, or upon earlier cessation under this Section.
2. The mandate shall be exercised freely. No member may be recalled by any body otherwise than as provided by this Constitution.
3. Members shall declare their assets in accordance with Article 3.10.2 (Asset Disclosure).

## Article 3.4.3 — Vacancies and By-Elections

1. A vacancy in the Lower House shall be filled by replacement from the party list under Article 3.2.4 (Vacancies).
2. A vacancy in the Upper House shall be filled by election by the Provincial Council concerned, for the remainder of the term.
3. Where a by-election is required, the Electoral Commission shall conduct it without delay.

## Article 3.4.4 — Administration and Validity

1. The Electoral Commission shall have exclusive competence to organize and conduct elections to both Houses.
2. The Commission shall certify the results and shall publish them together with the counting records.
3. The validity of parliamentary elections shall be determined, on appeal, by the Constitutional Court under Part 06.

## Article 3.4.5 — Loss of Seat

1. A member shall lose the seat upon loss of eligibility, upon resignation accepted by the House, upon expulsion under Article 3.10.4 (Sanctions and Expulsion), or upon a final conviction of an offence that entails such loss by law.
2. Decisions on the loss of a seat shall be subject to judicial review.

## Design Note

*This Section deals only with mandate mechanics — timing, vacancy, and validity — while the electoral system itself is governed by Part 06; the division keeps chamber rules and electoral rules separately amendable. Replacing members from the same list, rather than by new votes, respects the choice the electorate already made and keeps representation continuous. Final judicial determination of validity, instead of self-judgment by the Houses, follows the principle that electoral disputes demand an independent arbiter.*
