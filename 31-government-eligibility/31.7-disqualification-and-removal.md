# Section 31.7 — Disqualification and Removal

## Articles

**Article 31.7.1 (Grounds).** An officeholder is disqualified for: a sustained, appeal-exhausted failure of Articles 31.2–31.4; a criminal conviction under Part 37 (corruption) or Part 38 (serious crimes); or a finding of gross violation of the rights in Part 01.

**Article 31.7.2 (Process).** Disqualification is decided by the Integrity Courts (37.9), never by a simple vote of political rivals, to prevent the process itself from becoming a tool of persecution.

**Article 31.7.3 (Right of Reply and Appeal).** Every disqualification proceeding guarantees the officeholder a public hearing, legal representation, and one appeal to the Constitutional Court.

**Article 31.7.4 (Interim Succession).** On disqualification, an interim officeholder — never a family member of the disqualified person, to avoid dynastic capture of the vacancy — serves until a fresh, fully-vetted successor is certified.

*Design Note: A removal power strong enough to remove the corrupt must be built so it cannot be turned against the honest. Independent adjudication (37.9) and guaranteed appeal are the two safeguards doing that work here.*

**Cross-references:** 31.1–31.5, 37 (Integrity Branch), 38 (Penal Code Extended), 05-judiciary/constitutional-court.md


## Implementation Detail

**Article 31.7.5 (Hearing Timeline).** A disqualification hearing must be scheduled within 30 days of referral and concluded within 90 days, to prevent indefinite suspension being used as an informal removal tool.

**Article 31.7.6 (Interim Successor Term).** An interim officeholder under Article 31.7.4 serves no longer than 180 days before a fully certified successor must take office.
