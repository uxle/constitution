# Section 31.7 — Disqualification and Removal

## Articles

**Article 31.7.1 (Grounds).** An officeholder is disqualified for: a sustained, appeal-exhausted failure of Articles 31.2–31.4; a criminal conviction under Part 37 (corruption) or Part 38 (serious crimes); or a finding of gross violation of the rights in Part 01.

**Article 31.7.2 (Process).** Disqualification is decided by the Integrity Courts (37.9), never by a simple vote of political rivals, to prevent the process itself from becoming a tool of persecution.

**Article 31.7.3 (Right of Reply and Appeal).** Every disqualification proceeding guarantees the officeholder a public hearing, legal representation, and one appeal to the Constitutional Court.

**Article 31.7.4 (Interim Succession).** On disqualification, an interim officeholder — never a family member of the disqualified person, to avoid dynastic capture of the vacancy — serves until a fresh, fully-vetted successor is certified.

*Design Note: A removal power strong enough to remove the corrupt must be built so it cannot be turned against the honest. Independent adjudication (37.9) and guaranteed appeal are the two safeguards doing that work here.*

**Cross-references:** 31.1–31.5, 37 (Integrity Branch), 38 (Penal Code Extended), 05-judiciary/constitutional-court.md


## Implementation Detail

**Article 31.7.5 (Hearing Timeline).** A disqualification hearing must be scheduled within 30 days of referral and concluded within 90 days, to prevent indefinite suspension being used as an informal removal tool.

**Article 31.7.6 (Interim Successor Term).** An interim officeholder under Article 31.7.4 serves no longer than 180 days before a fully certified successor must take office.

## Anti-Capture Amendment — Interim Accountability Pending Trial

**Article 31.7.7 (Interim Suspension on Framing of Charges).** Where a competent court has framed charges — not merely registered a complaint or first information report — against a sitting officeholder for an offense carrying a sentence of five years or more, the officeholder is suspended from office pending trial. Suspension is not disqualification and carries no finding of guilt.

**Article 31.7.8 (Safeguard Against Misuse).** To prevent Article 31.7.7 from becoming a tool of harassment, the framing of charges against a sitting officeholder must be reviewed within thirty days by a sessions judge for prima facie sufficiency; a suspension found to rest on an insufficient framing is void from the outset, and the officeholder is restored with full back pay.

**Article 31.7.9 (Fast-Track Trial).** A trial under Article 31.7.7 shall proceed on a day-to-day basis and conclude within two years of the framing of charges. Failure to meet this deadline is reported by the trial court to the Judicial Appointments Commission as a systemic failure; it is not, by itself, a ground for release of the officeholder from suspension.

*Design Note (Amendment): Article 31.7.1 disqualifies only upon conviction, which is correct for permanent removal — a conviction should mean something and must not be presumed. But conviction can take years, sometimes longer than a term of office, leaving a charged officeholder fully empowered throughout. This Amendment adds a proportionate, reversible interim step — suspension on the framing, not mere filing, of charges — so the multi-year gap between accusation and conviction is not, in effect, a multi-year gap in accountability.*

**Cross-references:** 31.7.1–31.7.6; 05-judiciary/judicial-appointments.md (Article 5.5.6).
