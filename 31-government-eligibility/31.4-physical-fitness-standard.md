# Section 31.4 — Physical & Health Fitness Standard

## Articles

**Article 31.4.1 (Baseline Standard).** Officeholders must meet a baseline standard of capacity to perform the duties of the office, assessed functionally — can the person fulfil the responsibilities, with reasonable accommodation — never by a checklist of diagnoses.

**Article 31.4.2 (Reasonable Accommodation).** Any physical or sensory disability must first be assessed for reasonable accommodation before any question of ineligibility arises; a wheelchair user, a blind candidate, or a candidate with a chronic condition is not, by that fact, unfit.

**Article 31.4.3 (Emergency Capacity Review).** For the very highest offices (Head of Government, Head of State's governing deputies), a periodic review confirms the office-holder retains the capacity to respond to genuine emergencies, with the same appeal rights as 31.3.3.

**Article 31.4.4 (Prohibition on Abuse).** As with 31.3.5, this standard may never be used as a pretext for political removal; any disputed finding goes to the Integrity Courts, not to the officeholder's political rivals.

*Design Note: History offers as many examples of physically vigorous leaders causing catastrophic harm as of frail ones governing wisely. This Article is deliberately narrow — functional capacity for the specific duties, nothing more — to avoid becoming a tool of ableism or political convenience.*

**Cross-references:** 31.1, 31.3, 41-disability rights, 26-emergency-and-crisis


## Implementation Detail

**Article 31.4.5 (Review Frequency).** The baseline functional-capacity review occurs at candidacy, then every five years in office, or immediately upon a documented emergency-relevant incapacitating event, whichever comes first.
