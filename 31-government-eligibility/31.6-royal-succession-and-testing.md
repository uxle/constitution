# Section 31.6 — Royal Succession and the Testing of Heirs

## Articles

**Article 31.6.1 (The Crown Is Ceremonial).** The Crown, held by hereditary succession under 04-executive/head-of-state.md, is a ceremonial and unifying office with defined reserve powers (dissolution deadlock-breaking, mercy, and constitutional guardianship) but does **not**, by itself, direct ministries, courts, or legislation.

**Article 31.6.2 (Heirs Are Candidates for Governing Office).** Should any member of the Royal Family wish to hold an actual governing office — a ministry, a governorship, a judgeship, a seat in the Assembly — they must clear Articles 31.2–31.4 exactly as any other citizen, with no expedited track and no private examiners.

**Article 31.6.3 (Separation of Crown and Cabinet).** No serving Head of Government or Minister may simultaneously be the Sovereign Monarch, preserving the separation of powers of Section 0.5 and preventing the concentration your ancestors' worst monarchies suffered from.

**Article 31.6.4 (Education of Heirs).** Heirs to the Crown receive the same governance-studies curriculum (31.2.2) as any candidate, from childhood, not as a courtesy but as a constitutional duty — an uneducated monarch is as much a design flaw as an uneducated minister.

*Design Note: This Article is the hinge between "king" and "constitutional government run by tested professionals" — it keeps the unifying, continuity-giving symbol of a Crown while making sure no one governs by blood alone. If a purely elected head of state is preferred instead of a hereditary Crown, this section and 04-executive/head-of-state.md would need to be revised together.*

**Cross-references:** 04-executive/head-of-state.md, 31.1, 0.3 (Sovereignty), 0.5 (Separation of Powers)


## Implementation Detail

**Article 31.6.5 (Age of First Testing).** Heirs first sit the age-appropriate governance-studies curriculum assessment at 16, and may not be certified for any governing office (as opposed to ceremonial Crown succession) before age 21 and full completion of Articles 31.2–31.4.
