# Section 31.3 — Psychological Fitness Test

## Articles

**Article 31.3.1 (Purpose).** The test exists to screen for traits with a demonstrated record of causing governmental harm — unchecked impulsivity, pathological dishonesty, disregard for the welfare of others, and susceptibility to corruption — never to screen for ordinary mental illness, neurodivergence, or personal struggle, which are not disqualifying in themselves.

**Article 31.3.2 (Administering Body).** The test is designed and scored by an independent panel of clinical psychologists and ethicists under the Integrity Branch, insulated from the government of the day, using validated, peer-reviewed instruments rather than ad hoc questioning.

**Article 31.3.3 (Repeatability & Appeal).** A failed test may be retaken after a minimum interval, and every result may be appealed to the Integrity Courts (Part 37/38). No candidate may be permanently barred without an appeal opportunity.

**Article 31.3.4 (Confidentiality).** Results are confidential; only the pass/fail certification, not the underlying clinical detail, is made public, to protect candidates' dignity while preserving public accountability for the outcome.

**Article 31.3.5 (Non-Discrimination).** This test shall never be used to exclude candidates on the basis of disability, neurodivergence, or a treated and stable mental health history unrelated to governance risk; doing so is itself a violation of Article 1.2 (Equality) and is subject to challenge before the Constitutional Court.

*Design Note: This is the most sensitive Article in the whole Constitution and the one most easily abused by a future government wanting to disqualify opponents. Article 31.3.5 and the independence guarantee in 31.3.2 exist specifically to prevent that misuse; any amendment weakening either is subject to the entrenchment rule in 0.5.*

**Cross-references:** 31.1, 31.5, 1.2 (Equality), 41-disability rights sections, 05-judiciary/constitutional-court.md


## Implementation Detail

**Article 31.3.6 (Retest Interval).** A failed test may be retaken no sooner than 90 days later, with a maximum of three attempts per election cycle.

**Article 31.3.7 (Panel Composition).** Each candidate is assessed by a panel of at least three clinicians, none of whom may share a political party affiliation with the candidate, with panel membership rotated randomly from a certified national roster to prevent targeting.
