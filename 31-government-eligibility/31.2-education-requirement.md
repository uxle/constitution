# Section 31.2 — Education Requirement

## Articles

**Article 31.2.1 (Minimum Credential).** No candidate may be certified eligible for governing office without a recognized qualification in public administration, constitutional law, public policy, economics, or an equivalent field of governance studies, awarded by an accredited institution.

**Article 31.2.2 (Governance Studies Curriculum).** The curriculum must cover, at minimum: constitutional structure, budgeting and public finance, human rights law, environmental and animal-welfare law, criminal justice principles, and the history of governmental failure (corruption, atrocity, and collapse case studies), so that officeholders understand not only how to govern but how governments go wrong.

**Article 31.2.3 (Equivalency Pathway).** A candidate without a formal degree may qualify through an equivalency examination of the same rigor, so that eligibility depends on demonstrated knowledge, not on wealth or access to universities.

**Article 31.2.4 (Continuing Education).** Sitting officeholders must complete refresher governance education at least once every five years as a condition of Article 31.5 re-certification.

*Design Note: The requirement is deliberately about governance competence, not general academic prestige — a brilliant surgeon or engineer with no training in public administration is not, by that fact alone, qualified to run a ministry.*

## Implementation Detail

**Article 31.2.5 (Recognized Institutions).** The Governance Eligibility Board maintains a public list of accredited institutions and equivalency-exam centers in every region, reviewed every three years, with a mandatory minimum of one free public option per 2 million residents.

**Article 31.2.6 (Equivalency Exam Structure).** The equivalency exam (31.2.3) consists of a written constitutional-law and public-finance section (60%) and an applied case-study section (40%), scored by at least two independent examiners with a documented disagreement-resolution procedure.
