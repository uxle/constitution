# WC-253 · Neutrality and Non-Aggression

> **Part 10 — Security and International Order** · Document **253 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** The state's posture toward wars it does not join

## Preamble

This document governs neutrality policy for states choosing that posture.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Status

1.1 A state may adopt permanent neutrality by organic law: no participation in wars between others, no military alliances.

1.2 Neutrality never means indifference to international law: document 139's crimes remain everyone's business.

1.3 Armed neutrality - defence capacity without alliance - is the compatible model.

### Article 2 — Duties

2.1 Impartial treatment of belligerents under the law of neutrality; humanitarian duties stand above.

2.2 Sanctions compliance under UN decisions overrides strict neutrality - the Charter's primacy.

2.3 Neutrality law binds private actors: mercenarism and war profiteering from neutral territory are crimes.

### Article 3 — Services

3.1 Good offices, mediation and hosting negotiations are the neutral's classical role, funded and staffed professionally.

3.2 Geneva-convention duties - wounded, prisoners, civilians - apply fully.

3.3 Neutrality never shields war criminals: document 139's jurisdiction applies.

### Article 4 — Change of posture

4.1 Abandoning neutrality follows the amendment-grade procedure with referendum.

4.2 Collective-security participation under UN command is compatible with neutrality.

4.3 Armed attack suspends neutrality by the Charter's inherent right of self-defence.

## Commentary

Neutrality is an optional posture, not a value in itself, and the chapter renders it honest: no mercenary profiteering behind the shield, no war-criminal shelter, full Charter compliance. The good-offices role converts neutrality from passivity into service. Making the posture changeable only by referendum protects it from quiet erosion by executive practice.
