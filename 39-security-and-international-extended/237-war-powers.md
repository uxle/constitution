# WC-237 · War Powers and Use of Force

> **Part 10 — Security and International Order** · Document **237 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** The decision to fight: authorisation, limits, reporting

## Preamble

This document governs the decision to use armed force abroad and its limits.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Authorisation

1.1 Armed force abroad requires prior parliamentary authorisation by majority, specifying mission, scope, duration and rules of engagement.

1.2 Urgent action in collective self-defence under the UN Charter is permitted with immediate parliamentary convocation within seventy-two hours and ratification within thirty days.

1.3 Secret warfare is prohibited: no unauthorised covert kinetic operations.

### Article 2 — Limits

2.1 Operations follow international humanitarian law absolutely; torture, targeted killings outside combat law, and indiscriminate weapons are prohibited.

2.2 Mission creep requires new authorisation; extensions beyond one year require fresh votes.

2.3 War in violation of the Charter is the crime of aggression with command liability under document 139.

### Article 3 — Reporting

3.1 Monthly classified reporting to the oversight committee with quarterly public summaries.

3.2 Civilian casualty counts are kept, reported to Parliament and published in aggregate - no secret ledgers.

3.3 The audit court reviews operational costs.

### Article 4 — After

4.1 Deployments end by parliamentary decision or mission completion; withdrawal is as lawful as deployment.

4.2 Veterans receive the care and recognition guarantees of document 017 analogues in Part 09.

4.3 Lessons-learned reviews are published within two years.

## Commentary

War powers are where executives everywhere maximised and parliaments atrophied, so the chapter restores the authorisation machinery with clocks and mission definitions that resist drift. Mandating casualty counting follows the practice of the most accountable militaries - what is counted is restrained. The prohibition of secret warfare answers the documented decade of unauthorised covert campaigns that made the authorisation power decorative.
