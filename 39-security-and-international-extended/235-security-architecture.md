# WC-235 · The Security Architecture

> **Part 39 — Security and International Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 235/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Map of the security sector and its civilian constitution

## Preamble

This document lays out the whole security sector - military, police, intelligence, emergency - under civilian constitutional control.

## Articles

### Article 1 — The map

1.1 The security sector comprises the defence forces under document 236, the police under documents 238-239, the intelligence services under document 237, the civil protection under document 241, and the cyber defence under document 242.

1.2 All security bodies are established by statute, mission-bounded, and subject to parliamentary oversight, judicial control and integrity jurisdiction.

1.3 No armed body exists outside this map; militias and private armies are criminal organisations under document 128.

### Article 2 — Civilian control

2.1 The executive directs, Parliament authorises and funds, courts review, and the Integrity Branch polices the sector.

2.2 Security appointments above command threshold require parliamentary confirmation with public hearings.

2.3 Uniformed personnel are barred from party politics; the armed forces vote but do not campaign.

### Article 3 — Rights in uniform

3.1 Personnel of the sector hold the rights of Part 02 with the service-law adaptations of statute.

3.2 Conscientious objection, complaint channels and union-adjacent representation exist in all services.

3.3 Commands violating the law of armed conflict are refused and reported - the duty of document 007.

### Article 4 — The sector budget

4.1 Security budgets follow full transparency under document 175 with classified annexes controlled by the audit court.

4.2 Procurement follows document 098 with security-graded publication - never total opacity.

4.3 Off-budget security spending is prohibited absolutely.

## Commentary

The security sector concentrates the state's coercive power, so the chapter draws its constitutional cage: statutory existence, mission bounds, quadripartite control by parliament, courts, executive and integrity. Classifying procurement annexes to the audit court rather than to ministers is the compromise that protects secrets from enemies without protecting them from auditors - the single most important clause in security integrity.

## Notes for adopting states

- Security-sector reform starts with parliamentary oversight and budget transparency before any new capabilities.
- Asylum procedure and border management are adoptable as statute packages with immediate effect.
- War powers and intelligence warrants require the surveillance court; build it first.
