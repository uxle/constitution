# WC-244 · Cyber Defence

> **Part 10 — Security and International Order** · Document **244 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** Defending the digital state without becoming a surveillance state

## Preamble

This document governs military and security cyber capabilities.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Mission

1.1 Cyber defence protects critical infrastructure, state systems and elections under national command.

1.2 Offensive cyber capabilities exist by statute under document 236's war-powers regime - never for domestic intelligence.

1.3 The military cyber command is separate from the domestic cybersecurity agency of document 261.

### Article 2 — Rules

2.1 Operations follow international humanitarian law: distinction, proportionality and precaution apply in cyberspace.

2.2 Critical civilian services - hospitals, water, power - are protected objects; attacking them is a war crime.

2.3 Hack-back by private actors is prohibited; attribution standards are evidentiary and published in aggregate.

### Article 3 — Coordination

3.1 A national cyber centre fuses threat intelligence across defence, intelligence, police and the CSIRT network.

3.2 Mandatory reporting of significant incidents follows document 261 with voluntary-sharing incentives.

3.3 Election systems follow the integrity regime of documents 052-053 with defended-in-depth audits.

### Article 4 — Limits

4.1 Domestic surveillance by military cyber tools is prohibited absolutely.

4.2 Stockpiled vulnerabilities follow an equities process: disclosure by default, retention only for defined necessity, reviewed by the surveillance court.

4.3 The oversight committee of document 237 reviews operations quarterly.

## Commentary

Cyber defence must protect the digital state without swallowing the private digital sphere, and the chapter draws that line in doctrine: military tools abroad, agency tools at home, one court above both. The equities process for vulnerabilities follows the reform consensus after the Shadow Brokers era - hoarded exploits leak, and citizens pay. Protecting hospitals and water systems as protected objects states the humanitarian law rule the sector needed in writing.
