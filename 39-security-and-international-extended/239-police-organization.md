# WC-239 · The Police

> **Part 10 — Security and International Order** · Document **239 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** Organisation, independence and community accountability

## Preamble

This document establishes policing as civilian, local and accountable.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Organisation

1.1 Policing is organised nationally for serious and organised crime and regionally-municipally for community policing, under a single professional statute.

1.2 Police independence in individual investigations from political direction is guaranteed; priorities are set by law and community plans.

1.3 Municipal police follow national standards under document 072.

### Article 2 — Recruitment and training

2.1 Selection includes psychological screening, integrity vetting and community representation targets.

2.2 Training emphasises de-escalation, law, human rights and first aid; annual in-service requalification includes these.

2.3 Higher education pathways and civilian specialists professionalise investigations.

### Article 3 — Powers

3.1 Stop, search, arrest and detention follow documents 017 and 025 with proportionality and recording.

3.2 Equipment: body-worn cameras for uniformed interaction with strict retention; lethal weapons rules under document 016; military-grade equipment requires statutory authorisation and public disclosure.

3.3 Facial recognition and predictive policing follow document 026's prohibitions and human-in-the-loop rules.

### Article 4 — Accountability

4.1 Independent police complaint bodies investigate serious incidents with full powers and publish outcomes.

4.2 Every use of force is reported; statistics publish quarterly by unit and demographics.

4.3 The Integrity Commission polices police corruption with priority; internal affairs exists but never closes corruption files alone.

## Commentary

Policing succeeds by consent, and consent is built by independence in cases, visibility in force statistics and real complaint machinery. The chapter routes corruption in policing directly to the Integrity Commission because internal affairs historically buried it. Camera retention rules and demographic force statistics are the transparency couple that changed behaviour wherever mandated.
