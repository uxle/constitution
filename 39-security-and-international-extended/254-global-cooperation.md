# WC-254 · Global Cooperation and International Organisations

> **Part 10 — Security and International Order** · Document **254 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** The state as a good citizen of the world

## Preamble

This document governs membership and conduct in international organisations.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Membership

1.1 The state participates in the UN system and regional organisations as a duty of cooperative statehood.

1.2 Membership transfers follow document 243's referendum rule; contributions are budgeted transparently.

1.3 Candidacies for international bodies follow merit processes with published nomination files.

### Article 2 — Standards

2.1 The state implements international standards through domestication with gap reporting to Parliament.

2.2 International organisation inspections and reviews are accepted with follow-up regimes.

2.3 Peer-review obligations are honoured on schedule - good citizenship is measurable.

### Article 3 — Reform

3.1 The state advocates transparency, budget integrity and accountability reform in the bodies it joins.

3.2 International organisation immunity is functional, never personal for crimes.

3.3 Whistleblower protection applies to seconded staff under document 091's channels.

### Article 4 — People

4.1 National participation in international civil service is supported with career paths.

4.2 Youth delegate and civil-society delegations accompany the state to major conferences.

4.3 International organisation failures affecting citizens are litigated with the state's diplomatic support.

## Commentary

Small and medium states live by the rules of institutions, so the chapter makes good citizenship deliberate: timely implementation, accepted reviews, honoured peer obligations, and reform advocacy inside the clubs. Treating organisation immunity as functional-only protects the corpus's accountability principle from the international layer. Career support builds the national presence through which influence actually flows.
