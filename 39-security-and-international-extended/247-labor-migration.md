# WC-247 · Labour Migration

> **Part 10 — Security and International Order** · Document **247 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** Economic channels: demand-driven, rights-protected

## Preamble

This document governs labour migration channels and migrant worker rights.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Channels

1.1 Labour migration follows published shortage lists with points-based selection and employer sponsorship duties.

1.2 Seasonal and circular schemes are statutory with wage-parity and housing standards.

1.3 Regional mobility agreements facilitate movement with rights portability.

### Article 2 — Employer duties

2.1 Sponsors guarantee contracts, wages and conditions; violations bar future sponsorship with publication.

2.2 Recruitment fees charged to workers are prohibited - employer pays; recruitment agencies are licensed.

2.3 Gangmaster and exploitative intermediaries follow document 131's trafficking regime.

### Article 3 — Worker rights

3.1 Migrant workers hold all labour rights of document 017 with complaint channels regardless of status.

3.2 Visa portability after a period ends dependence on single employers.

3.3 Social security is portable by treaty and accrues from day one.

### Article 4 — Regularisation and controls

4.1 Regularisation programmes follow statutory criteria where exploitation or long residence equities exist.

4.2 Employer sanctions follow document 131 for knowing exploitation; workers are protected from prosecution for status in labour claims.

4.3 Migration statistics publish annually with labour-market impact assessments.

## Commentary

Labour migration's documented abuses run through one dependency: the visa tied to a single employer, so portability after a period and employer-pays recruitment are the two structural fixes. Worker complaints surviving status is the enforcement key - undocumented silence is the exploiter's business model. These rules also serve domestic workers: wage parity stops migration being a wage-suppression instrument.
