# WC-240 · Policing Standards and Use of Force

> **Part 39 — Security and International Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 240/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** The rules of engagement at home: force, protest, custody

## Preamble

This document sets the operational standards of policing.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Use of force

1.1 Force is lawful only where necessary, proportionate and preceded by de-escalation; the ladder - presence, dialogue, control, restraint, intermediate, lethal - is mandatory.

1.2 Chokeholds and neck restraints are prohibited; shooting at vehicles and warning shots are prohibited.

1.3 Every lethal-force event triggers the independent investigation of document 016.

### Article 2 — Protest policing

2.1 Assemblies follow document 020's facilitation duty; communication with organisers is documented and command decisions logged.

2.2 Kettling and indiscriminate dispersal are prohibited; tear gas and water cannon need commander-level authorisation, medical presence and are banned against children and elders.

2.3 Plain-clothes officers identify themselves at arrest; snatch squads are prohibited.

### Article 3 — Custody

3.1 Detention follows document 025's clocks; custody records, medical screening, CCTV and lawyer access are mandatory.

3.2 Interrogation recording is complete; torture and coerced statements are excluded under document 121.

3.3 Vulnerable detainees - minors, disabled, intoxicated, mental-health crises - receive specialist handling with health co-response.

### Article 4 — Data and surveillance

4.1 Camera footage is retained by rule with access logs; delete-on-schedule is the default.

4.2 Watchlists require criteria, review and judicial confirmation; secret watchlists are void.

4.3 Ethnic profiling is prohibited; stop-data publishes for antidiscrimination audit under document 030.

## Commentary

The standards chapter writes the hard operational lessons of the last decades into rules rather than manuals: force ladders, protest facilitation, custody cameras, watchlist criteria. Banning neck restraints and warning shots follows the reforms that cut deaths in custody. Health co-response for crisis calls answers the documented reality that police became the default mental-health service without training for it.
