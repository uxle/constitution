# WC-133 · Trafficking and Modern Slavery

> **Part 38 — Penal Code Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 133/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Exploitation as an offence with victim status, not immigration status

## Preamble

This document defines trafficking, slavery and forced labour with victim protection as the first design goal.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Offences

1.1 Trafficking in persons - recruitment, transport, harbouring or receipt by force, fraud, coercion, deception or abuse of vulnerability for exploitation - is Class I.

1.2 Slavery, servitude, forced labour, debt bondage, forced marriage, organ trafficking and sale of persons are specific Class I offences.

1.3 Exploitation of a victim's prostitution or of forced criminality is included; consent of the victim is no defence.

### Article 2 — Victim status

2.1 Identified victims receive residence status for the duration of proceedings and a path to permanent status for cooperating victims and unaccompanied children.

2.2 No prosecution of victims for unlawful acts committed under coercion, including immigration offences.

2.3 Protection includes safe housing, medical and psychological care, legal aid, translation and, where desired, family reunification.

### Article 3 — Detection and duty

3.1 Mandatory vigilance duties for supply chains above statutory turnover: risk mapping, audits, disclosure and remediation under Part 08 labour rules.

3.2 First responders - police, labour inspection, health, coast guard, border officers - follow national identification standards with presumption of victimhood for minors.

3.3 Victim compensation from the offender and a state fund of last resort, payable within one year of claim.

### Article 4 — Structure

4.1 Organised trafficking follows document 128's aggravated regime with full asset recovery.

4.2 Labour exploitation in agriculture, construction, domestic and care work is inspected under the labour inspectorate's powers of entry.

4.3 Public procurement excludes suppliers with unresolved slavery findings.

## Commentary

Trafficking law's historical failure was treating victims as immigration offenders, which delivered them back to their exploiters and destroyed prosecutions; the reflection-period residence rule and non-punishment principle fix that at the root. Supply-chain vigilance follows the strongest modern transparency statutes, because demand-side liability is where the economics turn. The state compensation fund with a one-year clock answers victims' years-long wait that functioned as denial.
