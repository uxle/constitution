# WC-135 · Corporate and White-Collar Crime

> **Part 38 — Penal Code Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 135/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Legal persons on trial: liability, sanctions and compliance

## Preamble

This document completes corporate criminal liability with sanctions that bite assets, licences and existence.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Liability

1.1 Legal persons are liable for offences by organs and agents acting in their interest within their powers, and for supervision failures that enabled the offence.

1.2 Public-interest entities, SOEs, NGOs and public authorities can be liable as offenders.

1.3 Liability does not replace individuals: parallel prosecution of decision-makers is mandatory where evidence supports it.

### Article 2 — Sanctions

2.1 Fines calibrated to turnover and gain, minimum multiples of benefit; confiscation in full; debarment from public contracts and licences; publication of judgment and remediation plans.

2.2 Compliance monitors and judicial supervision for systemic offenders; dissolution for predators whose business model is crime.

2.3 Victim restitution precedes every state claim.

### Article 3 — Compliance and leniency

3.1 Effective, pre-existing compliance programmes mitigate individuals' exposure and sanctions, but never remove confiscation or restitution.

3.2 Leniency for first confessors of cartels and bribery follows the competition model with published criteria.

3.3 Audited self-reporting with full remediation earns deferred prosecution under the conditions of document 085.

### Article 4 — Professional enablers

4.1 Lawyers, accountants, auditors, notaries and company agents who enable crimes are individually liable with professional consequences.

4.2 Professional privilege does not cover crime-fraud participation.

4.3 Bars and institutes operate fitness rules mirroring the code under oversight of the Ombudsman.

## Commentary

Corporate crime is where corruption and ordinary crime meet the balance sheet, and fines-as-cost-of-business is the documented failure mode; turnover-based minima, monitors and dissolution change that arithmetic. The parallel-prosecution mandate for individuals answers the all-too-familiar corporate conviction with zero humans charged. Enabler liability with the crime-fraud exception ends the professional assist that laundered everything from tax schemes to trafficking.

- Decriminalisation lists take effect on day one; exoneration of prior convictions follows document 134's automatic procedure.
