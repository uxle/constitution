# WC-119 · Abolition of the Death Penalty

> **Part 05 — Justice and Penal Law** · Document **119 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** The absolute, unamendable end of state killing

## Preamble

This document implements the abolition of the death penalty in all cases, and defines the substitute maximum penalty.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Abolition

1.1 No court, tribunal, military commission or any other organ may impose or execute the death penalty for any offence, in war or peace.

1.2 Existing death sentences commute to the maximum Class I term with the review rules of document 116.

1.3 This article belongs to the unamendable core and no referendum may restore it.

### Article 2 — Extradition and transfer

2.1 No extradition, expulsion, surrender or transfer to a death-penalty jurisdiction without binding assurances in writing that the penalty will neither be sought nor imposed, verified at diplomatic level.

2.2 Assurance breaches are treaty violations triggering return of the person.

2.3 Life means life with review: the substitute sanction follows document 116, never any pseudo-executive substitute.

### Article 3 — No functional equivalents

3.1 Death penalty by other names - deliberate medical neglect to death, lethal prison conditions, unofficial disappearances - is murder under the homicide article with official aggravation.

3.2 War or emergency powers do not revive capital jurisdiction; military codes conform within one year.

3.3 Public officials cooperating with foreign executions face Class I liability.

### Article 4 — Memory and remedy

4.1 Historic wrongful executions are acknowledged, files opened to families, and compensation paid to descendants on statutory criteria.

4.2 Commemoration and data collection on historic use are a research duty of the Justice Ministry.

4.3 No statute of limitation applies to the associated tortures and frame-ups.

## Commentary

Abolition is total, unamendable, and extended to functional equivalents because states that lose the formal penalty have sometimes kept the substance through neglect and disappearances. The extradition-assurance regime follows European practice with the added teeth of return-on-breach. Acknowledging historic executions is included deliberately: credible justice systems audit their worst history rather than bury it, and families of the wrongfully hanged hold real claims.

## Notes for adopting states

- Penal reform requires the sentencing council and the training of document 294 before new codes bite.
- Commutation of existing death sentences and records-sealing schedules are part of adoption, not afterthoughts.
- Decriminalisation lists take effect on day one; exoneration of prior convictions follows document 134's automatic procedure.
