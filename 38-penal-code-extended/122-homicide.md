# WC-122 · Homicide and Offences Against Life

> **Part 05 — Justice and Penal Law** · Document **122 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** Murder, manslaughter, infanticide and assistance at peril

## Preamble

This document defines the offences against life with clear gradations and the duty to rescue.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Murder

1.1 Murder is intentional killing, or intentional infliction of grievous harm foreseeably causing death, with Class I penalty.

1.2 Premeditation, cruelty, killing for concealment of another offence, killing an on-duty official, and domestic-context killings are aggravating circumstances.

1.3 Mass killing and killing with terror purpose carry the terrorism aggravation of document 129.

### Article 2 — Manslaughter

2.1 Manslaughter is unintentional killing by unlawful or grossly negligent act, Class III to II by circumstances.

2.2 Reckless killing in traffic, construction and industry follows the corporate-crime rules where systemic failure is present.

2.3 Provocation reduces class only under the strict limits of document 113.

### Article 3 — Infanticide and end of life

3.1 Killing a newborn by a mother in the disturbance of childbirth or lactation is a distinct reduced offence with treatment priority.

3.2 Assisted dying is regulated, not criminalised, under the health law of Part 09 with safeguards; killing outside those safeguards is homicide.

3.3 Withdrawal of futile treatment with consent is lawful medical practice, never homicide.

### Article 4 — Duty to rescue

4.1 Failure to help a person in evident peril, where assistance is possible without serious risk, is a misdemeanour.

4.2 Professionals on duty bear a heightened duty.

4.3 Good Samaritan clauses protect honest helpers from liability for imperfect rescue.

## Commentary

The homicide article keeps the murder-manslaughter line on intent and gross negligence rather than result alone, which is where prosecutorial manipulation of tragedy historically lives. The infanticide recognition follows English and Indian precedent, treating the disturbance of childbirth as a real medical category rather than a sentimental excuse. Duty-to-rescue follows the French-Belgian tradition: cheap to comply, powerful for social trust, with protection for honest attempts.
