# WC-125 · Domestic and Gender-Based Violence

> **Part 05 — Justice and Penal Law** · Document **125 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** A specialist regime: protective orders, risk assessment, integrated support

## Preamble

This document creates the specialist regime for domestic and gender-based violence, from first call to long-term safety.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Offences and grading

1.1 Domestic violence is a distinct offence and an aggravating context for all offences in this Part, covering physical, sexual, psychological, economic violence and coercive control between partners, family members, cohabitants and exes.

1.2 Coercive control - patterns of isolation, surveillance, micro-regulation of life and threats - is an offence without a single assault.

1.3 Repeat patterns escalate class; strangulation is grave injury.

### Article 2 — Protective orders

2.1 Ex parte emergency orders within twelve hours of request, confirmed within seventy-two hours by the court with a hearing; duration up to two years renewable.

2.2 The aggressor is removed from the home; firearms and licences are suspended immediately.

2.3 Breach is an offence with GPS monitoring as an enforcement condition.

### Article 3 — Risk and response

3.1 Standardised risk assessment at every police contact; repeat visits logged on the national risk map.

3.2 One-stop support centres: police, medical, forensic, legal, shelter and financial services co-located with a single case manager.

3.3 Victims never bear protection costs.

### Article 4 — Children and workplace

4.1 Children who witness violence are recognised as victims with support and placement rules under document 023.

4.2 Employers provide paid safety leave and job protection; relocation and address confidentiality programmes operate by statute.

4.3 Stalking follows document 129's harassment rules with the same protective apparatus.

## Commentary

Domestic violence is where violence hides in plain sight for years, and the regime concentrates on the two moments that matter: the first call, with risk assessment and same-day removal of the aggressor, and the long tail, with integrated support and renewal of orders. Removing the aggressor rather than the victim reverses the historical design that punished victims with homelessness. Coercive control as a standalone offence follows England and Wales 2015 and several European codes, which finally reached violence that never bruises.
