# WC-118 · Parole, Release and Reentry

> **Part 38 — Penal Code Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 118/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Release as a supervised right with support, not a lottery

## Preamble

This document governs release preparation, parole decision and reentry support so that punishment ends at the gate.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Release preparation

1.1 From one third of the term, every prisoner has a statutory reintegration plan: housing, work, treatment, documents and a named probation officer.

1.2 Temporary release on licence is used to test reentry, with revocation rules and no new punishment for the original offence.

1.3 Parole hearings are adversarial, with counsel, evidence, victim input and reasons.

### Article 2 — Parole decision

2.1 Parole is granted at two thirds of term as the rule, unless proven current dangerousness on structured, validated criteria.

2.2 Refusal requires written reasons and a re-listing within one year; lifetime parole as a structure is prohibited.

2.3 Parole boards are mixed professional-citizen bodies under the judicial appointments logic.

### Article 3 — Reentry support

3.1 The state provides identity documents, a bridge allowance, health coverage continuity and work-placement priority under statutory right.

3.2 Housing for released persons is a duty of the municipality; homelessness at the gate is a system failure, reportable.

3.3 Voting, work rights and licences restore automatically at release; only court-ordered specific restrictions survive.

### Article 4 — Probation

4.1 Probation is social work with authority: caseload caps, training and dual mandate of support and enforcement.

4.2 Technical violations answer with graduated responses; custody only for repeated or serious breach.

4.3 Records of supervision are sealed on completion under document 133.

## Commentary

Recidivism is manufactured at the gate: no documents, no housing, no work and no coverage produce the first new offence within months, and the corpus treats each as a statutory entitlement rather than charity. Parole at two-thirds as the rule with proven dangerousness as the exception reverses the discretionary parole lottery documented in punitive systems. Automatic restoration of civil rights ends the civil death that some systems impose for life after sentence completion.
