# WC-137 · Rights of Victims

> **Part 38 — Penal Code Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 137/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Recognition, information, participation, protection, compensation

## Preamble

This document makes victims rights-holders in the penal process, not bystanders to it.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Recognition and standards

1.1 Victims of crime are recognised as rights-holders, including indirect victims and the dependents of the deceased.

1.2 Minimum standards: respect, recognition, information, support, protection, participation and compensation, in every case.

1.3 Repeat and vulnerable victims receive priority treatment without filing heroics.

### Article 2 — Information and participation

2.1 Victims are informed of rights at first contact, of case status at every stage, and of release or escape of the offender in time to act.

2.2 Victim statements are part of the process: impact statements at sentencing, opinions on conditional release, and the right to review non-prosecution decisions under the criteria of document 085.

2.3 Interpretation, translation of key decisions and dedicated contact persons are statutory.

### Article 3 — Protection

3.1 Risk assessment at every contact; protective orders under documents 126 and 131; separate waiting spaces, shielded testimony and pseudonymity where fear is real.

3.2 Intimidation and retaliation are offences; contact bans are enforced with location monitoring.

3.3 Children, elders, disabled and trafficked persons have specialist pathways with guardians.

### Article 4 — Compensation and restorative access

4.1 Restitution by the offender has first claim on assets before the state; the state compensation fund pays within one year where the offender cannot.

4.2 Restorative processes are offered under document 117's consent rules.

4.3 Support services - shelters, crisis lines, trauma care - are funded as statutory services with national coverage.

## Commentary

Systems that forget victims lose public cooperation and with it their evidence base; the corpus treats victims as the process's load-bearing participants, with information, voice and protection rather than ceremony. The state compensation fund with a one-year clock answers the documented decade-long waits that functioned as second victimisation. Prioritising restitution over state claims aligns the recovery machinery of Part 04 with justice rather than treasury optics.

## Notes for adopting states

- Penal reform requires the sentencing council and the training of document 294 before new codes bite.
- Commutation of existing death sentences and records-sealing schedules are part of adoption, not afterthoughts.
- Decriminalisation lists take effect on day one; exoneration of prior convictions follows document 134's automatic procedure.
