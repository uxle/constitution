# WC-124 · Sexual Offenses

> **Part 38 — Penal Code Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 124/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Consent as the core, protection of complainants, expert procedure

## Preamble

This document defines sexual offences around consent, with protective procedure and modern evidence rules.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Rape and assault

1.1 Rape is non-consensual penetration, by any body part or object, of vagina, anus or mouth; consent is free, specific, revocable and present in mind throughout.

1.2 Consent is absent where obtained by force, threat, coercion, abuse of authority, incapacitation, fraud as to the act, or where the person cannot consent by age, capacity or status.

1.3 No corroboration requirement, no resistance requirement, no marital exemption, no recent-complaint requirement; the claimant's testimony is evidence like any other.

### Article 2 — Other offences

2.1 Sexual assault on non-penetrative contact, sexual coercion, exploitation of dependents, patients and prisoners by caregivers and officials, and sexual offences against children with full protection rules.

2.2 Age of protection: sexual acts with persons below sixteen are offences per se, with close-in-age tolerance of two years for consensual adolescent contact.

2.3 Offences against children carry Class I grading with treatment orders.

### Article 3 — Procedure

3.1 Specialised prosecutors, victim advocates, recorded evidence-in-chief, protective cross-examination through the judge, closed session at the claimant's choice.

3.2 Complainant's sexual history, style and prior relationship with third parties is inadmissible except on relevance to consent decided by the judge in camera.

3.3 Rape myths are excluded as reasoning; judgments citing them are appellate error.

### Article 4 — False allegations

4.1 Knowingly false reporting is an offence, prosecuted at the Class M level with the strict proof required of all offences.

4.2 The risk of discouraging genuine complaints is managed by trial transparency rules and non-disclosure of complainant identity.

4.3 Lawyers filing pressure complaints in custody matters are disciplined.

## Commentary

The consent-centred definitions follow the 2010s reform wave in England, Wales, Germany and the Nordic countries, replacing coercion-centred codes that silenced most victims. Procedure is where these cases historically failed, so the protections are structural: judge-filtered cross, advocate support, rape-myth exclusion. False allegations remain criminal but at modest gravity and strict proof, reflecting the evidence that they are rare and that their politicisation chills genuine complaints.
