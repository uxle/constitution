# WC-120 · Prohibition of Torture and Ill-Treatment

> **Part 05 — Justice and Penal Law** · Document **120 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** The absolute ban with exclusion, investigation and command liability

## Preamble

This document implements the absolute prohibition of torture with the full enforcement apparatus: exclusion, investigation, redress and command responsibility.

## Articles

### Article 1 — The ban

1.1 No torture, cruel, inhuman or degrading treatment or punishment, by any person, at any time, for any reason, including emergency, war, orders and necessity of state.

1.2 The definition includes mental suffering and pharmacological, environmental and systemic techniques; consent is no defence.

1.3 No derogation, no exception, no balancing.

### Article 2 — Exclusion

2.1 Statements and evidence obtained by torture or coercion are inadmissible in any proceeding, against anyone, including co-accused.

2.2 Derivative evidence from torture is inadmissible; from lawful covert sources is assessed independently.

2.3 The state bears the burden of proving lawful origin where the defence raises a real risk of coercion.

### Article 3 — Investigation and redress

3.1 Every credible allegation triggers a prompt, independent investigation by the Integrity Commission and ordinary prosecutors jointly.

3.2 Victims receive reparation: rehabilitation, compensation, satisfaction and guarantees of non-repetition.

3.3 Command responsibility extends to superior officers and ministers who knew or should have known and failed to act.

### Article 4 — Custody safeguards

4.1 Custody records, medical examination on entry and exit, lawyer access within the first hour, full recording of interrogations, and CCTV coverage with tamper liability are mandatory.

4.2 Secret detention sites and ghost detainees are high constitutional crimes with international notification duty.

4.3 Diplomatic assurances against torture do not permit transfer where risk is real.

## Commentary

The absolute ban is completed by the machinery that makes it real, because torture survives precisely in the procedural dark: unrecorded interviews, camera gaps, medical exams that never happen. Full recording and entry-exit medical exams are the cheapest and most effective documented prevention. Command liability and the prohibition of secret detention answer the two institutional structures through which modern torture systems were built.
