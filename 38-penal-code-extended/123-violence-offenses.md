# WC-123 · Violence Offenses

> **Part 38 — Penal Code Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 123/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Assault, coercion, endangerment and hate-crime enhancement

## Preamble

This document defines non-fatal violence with bodily-integrity grading and the hate-crime enhancement.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Assault and battery

1.1 Battery is unlawful physical force or harmful contact; aggravated by weapon, group, ambush, vulnerability of the victim or on-duty status of the target.

1.2 Grading by harm: Class M for transient injury; Class III for serious injury; Class II for grievous or permanent injury; Class I where death results under document 127's rules.

1.3 Consent validates contact in sport, medicine and ordinary life; consent never legitimises grievous harm.

### Article 2 — Coercion and threats

2.1 Threats of crime, coercion of will, stalking and intimidation are offences from Class M up, with protection orders available to victims.

2.2 Threats against officials, journalists and witnesses are aggravated and prosecuted by the Integrity Commission.

2.3 Swatting, doxxing and organised harassment campaigns are aggravated forms.

### Article 3 — Endangerment

3.1 Endangering life by reckless conduct: dangerous driving, fire-setting, leaving hazards, Class III to II.

3.2 Failure of custodial duty toward children and dependents is aggravated endangerment.

3.3 Arson and building violations with risk to occupants follow the environmental-crime stacking where land or habitat is harmed.

### Article 4 — Hate-crime enhancement

4.1 Where any offence is selected by prejudice against protected characteristics under document 016, penalty class rises by one step.

4.2 Bias motive is proved as a selection factor, not as a separate ideology trial.

4.3 Organised hate violence is treated as organised crime under document 128.

## Commentary

The hate-crime enhancement follows the model that works in practice: selection motive as an aggravator on the base offence, not a separate speech crime, which protects expression while punishing targeted violence. Threats against journalists and witnesses are deliberately routed to the Integrity Commission, because they are the standard instruments of corruption's self-defence. Grading violence by harm keeps proportionality anchored to the victim's body, not to the offender's biography.
