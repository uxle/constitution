# WC-134 · Environmental Crime and Ecocide

> **Part 38 — Penal Code Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 134/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Crimes against nature: pollution, wildlife, water, and ecocide

## Preamble

This document criminalises serious environmental harm with personal liability for decision-makers and ecocide at the top of the scale.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Offences

1.1 Unlawful pollution of air, water, soil or sea causing serious harm: Class II; with death or widespread damage: Class I.

1.2 Unlawful waste trafficking, dangerous-substance dumping, ozone and climate-system sabotage, and destruction of protected habitats or species are specific offences.

1.3 Ecocide - unlawful or wanton acts committed with knowledge of substantial likelihood of severe, widespread or long-term environmental damage - is a Class I international crime under document 139.

### Article 2 — Corporate and personal

2.1 Corporate environmental crime follows the liability rules of document 138 with restoration orders, receiver remedies for repeat offenders, and site clean-up funds.

2.2 Directors and officers who directed, tolerated or concealed are personally liable, Class III to I by gravity.

2.3 False monitoring data and bribery of inspectors follow the corruption catalogue with aggravation.

### Article 3 — Enforcement tools

3.1 Environmental inspectors hold entry, sampling, seizure and closure powers with judicial confirmation within forty-eight hours.

3.2 Restoration-first remedies: courts order clean-up, habitat rebuilding and species recovery with bonds and deadlines.

3.3 Standing follows document 022 with NGOs and affected communities; class mechanisms apply.

### Article 4 — Scale and repetition

4.1 Cumulative and repeated violations aggregate to systemic offence grading.

4.2 Transboundary harm triggers international cooperation under document 105.

4.3 Sanctions publish per-company records feeding procurement debarment.

## Commentary

Environmental crime is typically a business model with a fine line item, so the article attaches restoration-first orders, bonds, receivers and debarment - the instruments that convert pollution into a bad investment. Personal director liability is the check on the corporate shield that made executives indifferent to compliance. Ecocide, defined with the Stop Ecocide drafting discipline, completes the ladder for the gravest organised destruction of nature.
