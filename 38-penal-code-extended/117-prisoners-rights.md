# WC-117 · Prisoners' Rights and Prison Regime

> **Part 38 — Penal Code Extended** · Source: World Constitution v1.0 corpus, document 117/300 (adapted) · This edition v1.0.1
> [README](../README.md) · [Glossary](../GLOSSARY.md)

**Subject.** Prisons as rehabilitation institutions with enforceable rights

## Preamble

This document makes the prison a rights-bearing rehabilitation institution with inspection, minimum standards and sanctions for keepers.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Residual rights

1.1 Prisoners retain all rights not necessarily removed by the sentence: dignity, safety, health, family contact, expression, religion, education, complaint and vote.

1.2 The aim of the regime is preparation for release from day one.

1.3 Cell space, light, sanitation, food and outdoor time meet minimum standards set by statute and treaty body guidance.

### Article 2 — Regime

2.1 A full day of activity: work paid at public rates with savings, education, vocational training, treatment and exercise.

2.2 No forced labour, no private leasing of convict labour, no profit from prisoner communication pricing.

2.3 Solitary confinement: maximum 15 consecutive days, 22 minimum hours out of cell, never for minors, pregnant women, or the mentally ill, never as punishment, only as documented last resort for safety.

### Article 3 — Oversight

3.1 Independent prison boards of judges and citizens visit unannounced, with private prisoner interviews and published reports.

3.2 The Ombudsman handles complaints with access to everything; retaliation is an offence.

3.3 Prison statistics - population, turnover, incidents, solitary days - publish quarterly.

### Article 4 — Keepers' liability

4.1 Torture, ill-treatment and failure of care in custody are offences of document 121 with command responsibility.

4.2 Every death in custody triggers an independent investigation with a public inquest verdict.

4.3 Cover-ups, camera gaps and record losses are aggravated offences, presumed against the institution.

## Commentary

Prisons are the state's most total power over persons, and their corruption is physical rather than financial: contraband economies, brutality and neglect. The article ends the three worst practices by explicit text: private convict leasing, solitary-as-punishment, and unexplained camera gaps, each documented as the signature of abuse. Paying work at public rates with savings at release is the Nordic device that converts sentence time into reintegration capital.

## Notes for adopting states

- Penal reform requires the sentencing council and the training of document 294 before new codes bite.
- Commutation of existing death sentences and records-sealing schedules are part of adoption, not afterthoughts.
- Decriminalisation lists take effect on day one; exoneration of prior convictions follows document 134's automatic procedure.
