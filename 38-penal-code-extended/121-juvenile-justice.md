# WC-121 · Juvenile Justice

> **Part 05 — Justice and Penal Law** · Document **121 / 300** · World Constitution (WC) v1.0
> [Index](../INDEX.md) · [README](../README.md) · [Sources](../SOURCES.md)

**Subject.** A separate, welfare-first system for children in conflict with the law

## Preamble

This document builds the juvenile system: age of criminal responsibility, special procedure, custody as last resort and sealing.

## Articles

### Article 1 — Age and scope

1.1 Criminal responsibility begins at fourteen; below it, no prosecution, only child-welfare measures.

1.2 Fourteen to eighteen falls within juvenile jurisdiction in all matters, whatever the offence gravity.

1.3 Juvenile status is by age at act, and measures continue until twenty-one at most.

### Article 2 — Procedure

2.1 Specialised judges, prosecutors and defenders trained in child development; parents present; closed hearings; child-friendly language and timing.

2.2 Diversion is the presumption for all but gravest violence: restorative conferencing, education, treatment.

2.3 No child is tried in adult court, ever; grave offences add resources, not a change of forum.

### Article 3 — Sanctions and custody

3.1 Juvenile custody is a last resort for proven grave violence, in small educational facilities near family, for the shortest period, reviewed every six months.

3.2 Maximum juvenile measure: eight years, with reentry support to twenty-one.

3.3 No juvenile record enters general police databases; sealing is automatic at majority plus two years.

### Article 4 — Welfare safety-net

4.1 Status offences - truancy, curfew, running away, alcohol - are not crimes.

4.2 Children in the justice system retain education, health and family rights fully.

4.3 Detained children receive schooling, and detention never interrupts their curriculum year.

## Commentary

The corpus fixes responsibility at fourteen following the median of advanced systems and CRC guidance, because lower ages criminalise poverty and school failure rather than culpability. Diversion presumption plus sealed records answers the documented criminogenic effect of early justice contact, which is among the strongest findings in the field. Keeping all juveniles in juvenile court with added resources for grave cases ends the transfer practice that produced the worst of both worlds.
