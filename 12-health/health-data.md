# Section 12.6 — Health Data

> **Part 12 — Health** · Articles 12.6.1 – 12.6.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section treats health data as a specially protected category, permitting secondary use only by law with strict safeguards and supervision by the Information Commission.*

## Article 12.6.1 — Specially Protected Data

1. Data concerning health, including genetic data, constitute a specially protected category of personal data.
2. Processing of health data shall be permitted only on a lawful basis that is specifically provided for by law and accompanied by appropriate safeguards, consistent with Article 1.5.4 (Personal Data and Informational Self-Determination).

## Article 12.6.2 — Primary Use in Care

1. Health data may be processed for the direct care of the patient on the basis of consent or another lawful basis defined by law.
2. Every patient shall have access to the health data concerning the patient and may request their rectification.

## Article 12.6.3 — Secondary Use

1. Secondary use of health data for research, planning, or public health shall be permitted only where:
   (a) the purpose is defined by law;
   (b) anonymized or pseudonymized data are used wherever the purpose allows;
   (c) an ethics review has approved the processing; and
   (d) safeguards prevent results from being used to the detriment of the persons concerned.

## Article 12.6.4 — Supervision

1. The Information Commission shall supervise the processing of health data within its general mandate under Article 20.7.2 (Mandate).
2. Processing systems shall meet security, integrity, and access-control standards set by law, and significant breaches shall be reported and published.

## Article 12.6.5 — Interoperability and Portability

1. Health information systems shall be interoperable to ensure continuity of care, and records shall be portable when a patient changes provider.
2. Technical design shall implement data minimization and purpose limitation as required by Article 17.2.1 (Principles of Processing).

## Design Note

*Health data receive a stricter regime than ordinary personal data because their misuse carries irreparable dignitary harm and because patients cannot realistically refuse care to refuse processing. The constitutional choice is a dual structure: free flow within direct care, and tightly conditioned secondary use reserved to statute with ethics review. Tying supervision to the Information Commission avoids creating a parallel regulator while signaling that health data are not merely another database.*

**Cross-references:** Article 1.5.4 (Personal Data and Informational Self-Determination); Article 17.2.1 (Principles of Processing); Article 20.7.2 (Mandate).
