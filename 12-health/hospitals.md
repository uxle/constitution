# Section 12.3 — Hospitals

> **Part 12 — Health** · Articles 12.3.1 – 12.3.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section sets licensing and governance standards for hospitals and guarantees the patient rights of dignity, information, consent, and a second opinion.*

## Article 12.3.1 — Licensing and Standards

1. Hospitals may operate only under a license granted by law and shall comply with binding standards on staffing, hygiene, equipment, and quality of care.
2. An inspection authority shall verify compliance, may order corrective measures, and may suspend or withdraw licenses for persistent or grave breaches.

## Article 12.3.2 — Governance of Public Hospitals

1. Public hospitals shall have governing boards that operate transparently and account for the use of public funds.
2. Boards shall include members appointed on merit, and the law shall provide for representation of the communities the hospital serves.

## Article 12.3.3 — Patient Rights

1. Every patient has the right to be treated with dignity and respect for privacy.
2. Every patient has the right to receive comprehensible information about diagnosis, treatment options, risks, and prognosis.
3. Medical intervention requires the free and informed consent of the patient, consistent with the inviolability of human dignity under Article 0.7.1 (Inviolability of Human Dignity), save where the law provides for treatment without consent in narrowly defined circumstances.
4. Patients shall have access to their medical records and to effective complaint procedures.

## Article 12.3.4 — Second Opinion

1. Before a major intervention with significant risks, the patient shall be entitled to request a second opinion, and reasonable time shall be allowed for obtaining it without endangering health.

## Article 12.3.5 — Continuity of Care

1. Discharge from hospital shall be planned with the patient and with providers of follow-up care.
2. No patient shall be discharged earlier than medically appropriate for reasons of cost or administrative convenience.

## Design Note

*Licensing with an inspection authority gives the constitutional health guarantee an operational grip on institutional quality, while the patient-rights clause codifies dignity, information, and consent at the point of care. The second-opinion right is deliberately limited to major interventions to protect it from trivialization and to respect urgent clinical timelines. The anti-premature-discharge rule addresses a well-documented failure mode of cost-pressured hospital systems without dictating clinical judgment.*
