# Section 12.4 — Medical Profession

> **Part 12 — Health** · Articles 12.4.1 – 12.4.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section entrusts licensing and professional discipline to independent professional chambers, protects ethics and continuing competence, and reconciles conscientious objection with the duty of referral.*

## Article 12.4.1 — Professional Chambers

1. Parliament shall establish by law independent professional chambers for the health professions.
2. Chambers shall license practitioners, maintain registers, and set professional standards within the framework of the law.
3. Chambers shall be independent of government direction in the exercise of their licensing and standard-setting functions.

## Article 12.4.2 — Ethics and Standards

1. Chambers shall adopt codes of ethics binding on their members, including duties of care, confidentiality, and honest dealing with patients.
2. Practitioners shall maintain their competence through continuing education as the law requires.

## Article 12.4.3 — Conscientious Objection

1. A practitioner may refuse to perform a lawful intervention on grounds of conscience, provided that:
   (a) the refusal does not create a real risk to the patient's life or health in an emergency; and
   (b) the practitioner refers the patient promptly, in writing where practicable, to an available willing provider.
2. The State shall ensure that access to services within the coverage scheme does not depend on the conscientious convictions of individual practitioners.

## Article 12.4.4 — Discipline

1. Chambers shall adjudicate professional misconduct through procedures that guarantee notice, a hearing, and reasons, and their decisions shall be subject to concrete review under Article 5.6.2 (Concrete Review).
2. Sanctions shall be proportionate and shall include, where appropriate, conditions on practice, suspension, or removal from the register.

## Article 12.4.5 — Qualifications and Mobility

1. Chambers shall recognize qualifications on the basis of transparent criteria and may require assessment of competence.
2. The scope of practice of each health profession shall be defined by law.

## Design Note

*Independent professional chambers reconcile two risks: government control of medicine and unaccountable guild power. They receive licensing and standard-setting authority because clinical judgment is best regulated by peers, but their discipline procedures are constitutionally tethered to due process and judicial review. The conscientious-objection clause follows the referral-duty model: conscience is respected, yet the patient's lawful access to care does not collapse with the convictions of a single practitioner, and emergencies admit no refusal.*
