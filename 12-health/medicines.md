# Section 12.5 — Medicines

> **Part 12 — Health** · Articles 12.5.1 – 12.5.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section governs the quality, safety, and availability of medicines, protecting generic competition and securing access to essential medicines against pricing abuse.*

## Article 12.5.1 — Regulation of Medicines

1. An independent medicines authority shall evaluate the quality, efficacy, and safety of medicines and grant marketing authorizations on the basis of scientific evidence.
2. Decisions shall be reasoned, published, and subject to judicial review.
3. Members of the medicines authority shall declare conflicts of interest and shall not hold interests in the industry that they regulate.

## Article 12.5.2 — Essential Medicines

1. Parliament shall provide by law for a national list of essential medicines.
2. Public health facilities shall ensure the availability of essential medicines within the coverage scheme established under Article 12.1.2 (Public Financing Floor).

## Article 12.5.3 — Generic Competition

1. The law shall provide expeditious and reliable procedures for the authorization of generic and biosimilar medicines after patent or regulatory protection ends.
2. Marketing authorizations shall not be used to prolong exclusive rights beyond their legal term.

## Article 12.5.4 — Access and Pricing Abuse

1. Practices that abuse a dominant position to deny or restrict access to essential medicines are unlawful and may be sanctioned under competition law.
2. Where a patent is abused to obstruct the supply of an essential medicine, the State may apply the exceptions and compulsory-license measures permitted under Article 9.9.4 (Compulsory Licenses).

## Article 12.5.5 — Pharmacovigilance

1. The medicines authority shall operate a post-market surveillance system, including adverse-event reporting, recalls, and withdrawal of unsafe products.
2. Serious safety information shall be communicated to professionals and the public without delay.
3. The medicines authority shall publish an annual report on the safety and availability of medicines on the market.

## Design Note

*The section treats medicines as a chokepoint of the health guarantee: a formally free system fails if the pharmacy shelf is empty or unaffordable. The essential-medicines list, generic-authorization procedures, and competition safeguards work together, while the public-health licensing lever remains a last resort consistent with the constitutional balance of intellectual property. Post-market surveillance closes the loop, since safety evidence evolves after authorization.*

**Cross-references:** Article 9.9.4 (Compulsory Licenses); Article 12.1.2 (Public Financing Floor); Article 1.14.1 (Safe Goods and Services).
