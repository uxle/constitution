# Section 12.7 — Mental Health

> **Part 12 — Health** · Articles 12.7.1 – 12.7.5 · Adopted — v1.0.0

*This section guarantees parity between mental and physical health and strictly conditions involuntary treatment on consent, least-restrictive means, and independent review.*

## Article 12.7.1 — Parity of Esteem

1. Mental healthcare shall be given parity with physical healthcare in financing, in the coverage scheme, and in the planning of services.
2. The State shall develop community-based mental health services so that treatment is available close to where people live and work.

## Article 12.7.2 — Consent and Support

1. Treatment for mental illness requires the free and informed consent of the person, consistent with the right to personal integrity under Article 28.3.5 (Liberty and Integrity).
2. The law shall provide for supported decision-making so that diminished capacity does not automatically remove a person's voice in treatment decisions.

## Article 12.7.3 — Least-Restrictive Care

1. Involuntary treatment or confinement may be ordered only where:
   (a) a qualified professional has found a serious risk to the health or safety of the person or of others arising from a mental illness; and
   (b) no less restrictive alternative is adequate.
2. Involuntary measures shall be proportionate, time-limited, and reviewable, and shall be accompanied by care aimed at restoring autonomy.

## Article 12.7.4 — Independent Review

1. Every involuntary measure shall be subject to prompt review by a court or independent tribunal, and to periodic re-examination.
2. The person concerned shall have the right to be heard, to be represented, and to receive legal aid under Article 5.9.1 (Right to Legal Aid).

## Article 12.7.5 — Dignity in Facilities

1. Facilities providing mental healthcare shall respect the dignity, privacy, and family contact of patients.
2. Seclusion and restraint may be used only to prevent imminent harm, for the shortest time possible, and shall be recorded and reviewed.

## Design Note

*Mental health is separated from general hospital law because its distinctive risk is the exercise of State and clinical power over persons rather than institutions. The parity clause targets the chronic underfunding of psychiatric services; the least-restrictive standard and independent review translate the general right to bodily integrity into psychiatry-specific safeguards. Supported decision-making reflects the shift from substitute decision-making toward preserving the legal agency of persons with impaired capacity.*

**Cross-references:** Article 28.3.5 (Liberty and Integrity); Article 28.3.2 (Legal Capacity); Article 5.9.1 (Right to Legal Aid).
